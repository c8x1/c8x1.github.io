发面的时辰

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年10月23日·3分钟阅读

北道翻山，骡队一趟要走三个星期，褡裢里装的口粮就是这种烙得瓷实、耐放的饼，走山的人管它叫过山饼。沙爷的骡队天亮出山，谁的饼赶得上他这一趟，给价都从优。爹去岁没了，聂家的招牌还挂在门楣上，欠磨头的账却没跟着爹去；磨头放过话，当天就能带人来搬我家的门板。黄昏沙爷的单子到了：六百只过山饼，天亮装袋，给的价能让账平一半。我把瓦罐里最后一把铜钱数了两遍。

The north road goes over the mountain; a mule train needs three weeks for the crossing, and the rations in the saddlebags are these hard-baked, keepable buns that the mountain men call crossing cakes. Old Sha's mules leave at dawn, and whoever's buns ride his train get the better price. Father died last year. The Nie sign still hangs over our door, but the debt to the miller did not go with him, and the miller had said outright that he could bring men to carry off our door boards that same day. At dusk Sha's order came: 600 crossing cakes, bagged by dawn, at a price that would cover half the debt. I counted the last handful of copper coins in the clay jar twice.

我抱着瓦罐挨家去敲门，开得了口的都开到了。二更的时候，长案后头坐了十二个女人，邻家又应下借三只炉子，连自家的一只，一共四只。揉面、下剂、整形，头两双手省下的是几个时辰，到第十双、第十二双，省下的只剩一炷香。到三更，六百只饼坯全在案上排齐了队，比爹在的哪一夜都早。我数着麻袋，只当这一夜已经赎回来了。

I carried the clay jar from door to door and asked at every house that could be asked. By the second watch, twelve women sat behind the long table, and the neighbor had agreed to lend three ovens, so that with our own there were four. Kneading, portioning, shaping — the first two pairs of hands saved whole hours; by the tenth pair and the twelfth, the saving had shrunk to a single incense stick. By the third watch, all 600 dough rounds stood in ranks along the table, earlier than on any night when Father was alive. I counted out the sacks and told myself the night was already bought back.

然后就轮不到人了。女人们烤着手等，炉膛里养着火，劈好的柴够烧三天。案上的面自己长自己的，谁也替不了它——发面的时辰是面团自己的。我又叫醒两家男人来劈柴，那点活转眼就完，夜却一寸也没短。后来我才明白：能装进麻袋的数目，饼坯排齐那会儿就已经定下了；往后的钟点，没有一件由人做主。

After that it was no longer up to us. The women warmed their hands and waited, the fires were banked in the ovens, and the chopped wood was enough for three days. The dough on the table rose on its own, and no one could rise for it — the hours the dough keeps belong to the dough. I woke the men of two more households to chop wood; the work was done in a blink, and the night had not shortened by an inch. Only later did I understand: the number that would go into the sacks was settled the moment the rounds stood in ranks, and of all the hours that followed, not one was ours to command.

邱婶隔着热气替我着急：「麻袋一扎，谁看得出瘪的？掺一半进去，过山的人三个星期后才骂得回来，你的债那时候早平了。」我掀了一屉先试：出炉的饼又白又死，掂在手里像块河石。火没有欠我什么，面里缺的那些，哪只炉子也借不出来。那屉饼我掰碎喂了鸡，回来坐下，守着案上的六百只走完它们自己的路。

Aunt Qiu called to me through the steam, anxious on my account. "Once the sack is tied, who can spot a flat one? Mix half of them in; the mountain men won't curse their way back for three weeks, and your debt would be settled long before then." I pulled out one tray to test: the buns came from the oven white and dead, and weighed in the hand like river stones. The fire owed me nothing; whatever the dough lacked, no oven could lend. I crumbled that tray for the chickens, came back and sat down, and kept watch over the 600 on the table while they finished the road that was theirs.

天光时候，四只炉子吐出来的还不到三百。货场上沙爷的人点数，货郎的陈饼补了缺，账房先生笔一勾：「聂家，短过一半。」沙爷隔着骡子看我，说饼是好饼，数目是数目；明年雪一化，单子照旧六百。晌午磨头来，收了欠账的一半，看了看剩下的那一半，说下月再算。我家的门板还在门上。

By first light the four ovens had turned out fewer than 300. In the depot yard Sha's men counted the sacks, a peddler's older buns made up the shortage, and the clerk flicked his brush: "The Nie shop, short by more than half." Sha looked at me across his mules and said the buns were good buns, but a number is a number; when the snow melts next year, the order will stand at 600 all the same. At noon the miller came, collected half of what we owed, looked at the half that remained, and said next month would do. Our door boards are still on the door.

剩下的饼近晌才出齐，赶集的人站在街边吃，半价卖到日头当顶。工钱付清，罐底还剩几枚。夜里我对账：爹和我要熬两个通宵的活计，十二个女人三更就做完了；整炉六百只，天光时候却连一半也没装进袋。人手买得来，炉子也借得来；这一夜的账上，头一回记下一样铜钱买不动的东西。

The last of the buns came out close to noon, and market folk ate them standing along the street; I sold them at half price until the sun stood at its height. The wages were paid, and a few coins were left in the bottom of the jar. That night I went over the accounts: work that would have kept Father and me two whole nights had been finished by the twelve women before the third watch, yet of the full 600, not even half had gone into the sacks by first light. Hands could be bought and ovens borrowed; on that one night's ledger there appeared, for the first time, something that copper could not buy.

注解：阿姆达尔定律（Amdahl's law）——优化一处给整体带来的提速，被这一处实际占掉的时间框住；快不起来的那一段给整夜定了底，人手再多也越不过这个底。这则寓言只记了一夜的账——它管不到头天晚上就先起面的人，也管不到索性换了饼的做法的人。

Note: Amdahl's law — the speedup that improving one part buys for the whole is hemmed in by the share of time that part actually takes; the stretch that cannot be hurried sets the floor for the whole night, and no number of hands can come in under it. This fable keeps the accounts of a single night only — it has nothing to say about the one who set her dough to rise the evening before, nor about the one who changed her way of making buns altogether.
