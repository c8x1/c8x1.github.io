风的那一份

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年10月15日·4分钟阅读

鹰嘴渡的北岸立着一根石桩，是老辈人凿的，正对着石埂上唯一的豁口。桩底下的石滩上有两样印子：一样在下游三十步，一小块滩皮磨得发白，是一条船的船底三十多年里回回擦在同一处磨出来的；另一样在口子上下散开百步，深深浅浅十几道白痕，没有两道落在同一个地方。如今撑渡的是秀枝，她的船回回落在桩前三五步，涨水也落。两岸的人夸这丫头手上带准数，知道那两样印子来历的人，不接这句话。

On the north bank of the Eagle-Beak crossing stands a stone stake, cut by the old generations, set square against the only gap in the stone ridge. Below it the shingle carries two marks. One is thirty paces downstream: a small patch of gravel worn pale, where one boat's bottom had scraped the same spot every crossing for thirty-some years. The other is scattered a hundred paces up and down the mouth, a dozen pale streaks deep and shallow, no two of them in the same place. The ferry now is Xiuzhi's, and her boat lands within three or five paces of the stake every time, flood or no flood. Both banks praise the girl for having a sure hand. The people who know where those two marks came from do not answer.

早年撑渡的是老关。他师父传他一句口诀：船头咬住鹰嘴石，篙不换肩。靠着这句口诀，他的船回回落在桩前。他上手第七年，上游发了一场大水，主溜往南摆了半里。打那以后，船还是那条船，口诀还是那句口诀，落点却齐齐往下游挪了三十步，一步不多，一步不少，从来只朝这一个方向差。他拿步子量过，认下了：差三十步，上岸多走三十步，他认得起。有人劝他改，他不改——认下的差是个准数，他闭着眼也知道船落在哪；口诀一改，他就再说不准自己的船落在哪。两岸的人也认这个理：平水的日子，多走三十步算不得什么。只有一样，那块滩在口子下头，涨起水来跟整片苇障一起沉到水底，滩边的柳碴子成了挂船的地方，所以老例是大涨封渡，只有产妇和咽气的人破例。

In the old years the ferryman was Guan. His master had left him one rule: hold the bow on Eagle-Beak Rock, and never shift the pole. By that rule his boat had landed before the stake every crossing. In his seventh year on the water a great flood came down from upstream, and the main current shifted half a li toward the south bank. The boat was the same boat, the rule the same rule, but the landing moved thirty paces downstream — not one pace more, not one pace less, and never in any other direction. He paced it out and accepted it: thirty paces of walking ashore, and he could afford that. When men urged him to change the rule he would not — a miss you have accepted is a sure number; blindfolded, he still knew where his boat would be. Change the rule, and he could no longer say where his own boat would land. Both banks accepted his logic: on calm water, thirty paces of walking was nothing. Only, that patch lay below the mouth, and when the river rose it went under with the whole reed bank, and the old willow stumps along it became a place that catches boats; so the old rule was that high water closes the crossing, with an exception only for women in labor and the dying.

水生是老关的外甥，十六岁上船，看不惯这个。他说舅的船连滩上的石头都认得船底。他撑船眼睛只钉在石桩上，手上见推就还：水往东搡一把，他往西还一篙。水平的日子，他有两回正正把船头搭在桩根上，全渡口没第二个人有过这个数。可他的落点东一天西一天：今儿桩上十步，明儿桩下四十步。老河集的人后来说：坐老关的船，知道落在哪，就是得走三十步；坐水生的船，兴许一步不走，兴许走半天，落哪儿得问水。水生每一趟都落得有道理——那天的水确实那么推——可一趟有一趟的道理，合起来没个准。

Shuisheng was Guan's nephew, sixteen when he took the pole, and he had no patience with any of it. He said the stones on his uncle's landing patch knew the boat's bottom by heart. He steered with his eyes nailed to the stake and answered every push the water gave: the river shouldered him east, he paid it back a stroke to the west. On calm days, twice, he laid the bow exactly against the root of the stake, a thing no one at the crossing had ever done. But his landings wandered: ten paces above the stake one day, forty below it the next. The people of Laoheji put it this way: on Guan's boat you know where you land, and you know you will walk thirty paces; on Shuisheng's boat you may not walk a step, and you may walk half the morning — where you land, you must ask the water. Every crossing of his had its own logic — the water that day had truly pushed so — but one crossing's logic served only itself, and taken together they made no pattern.

那年七月，河涨了。半夜里苇寨来人磕门，寨里一个媳妇难产，要请老河集的齐婆婆。齐婆婆披衣出门，只说了一句：「夜里涨水，只坐老关的船。」水生那年十七，说他眼睛尖，齐婆婆摇头：「你那条船，落哪儿得问水。」难产犯在老例的例外上，老关解了缆。那一夜的水，是十几年来最高的一场。船头咬住鹰嘴石，篙不换肩，船落下去，落在回回不差的那块滩上——大水把那块滩淹在了柳碴子底下。船帮豁开，齐婆婆抱着药箱在黑水里呛了几口，苇寨的人打着火把拿绳索把两个人拖上岸，药箱沉了，齐婆婆的腿在碴子上豁开一道口子。消息过晌午才传回南岸：大人保住了，小的没能等到人。老关把破船拖上滩，从那夜起再没摸过篙。没有人说他撑错——他一步都没有多差。

In the seventh month the river rose. At midnight men from Weizhai hammered at the ferry gate: a woman in the village was in hard labor, and they had come for Granny Qi, the midwife of Laoheji. Granny Qi threw her coat over her shoulders and said one thing: "At night in flood water I ride Guan's boat and no other." Shuisheng was seventeen that year; he said his eyes were quicker. Granny Qi shook her head: "On your boat, where we land we must ask the water." A hard labor fell within the old rule's exception; Guan cast off. The water that night was the highest in more than ten years. The bow held Eagle-Beak Rock, the pole never shifted shoulders, and the boat came down and landed on the patch it had never once missed — the flood had drowned that patch under the old willow stumps. The gunwale split. Granny Qi swallowed black water with the medicine chest in her arms before the men of Weizhai, out with torches and ropes, hauled the two of them ashore; the chest sank, and her leg opened a gash on a stump. The news came back across the river past noon: the mother was saved, the child could not wait for anyone. Guan dragged the broken boat up the shingle and never touched a pole again. No one said he had steered it wrong — he had not missed by one pace more than ever.

八月里又涨了一回水，渡口只剩水生敢开。头两趟他都抢进了口子，一趟偏上，一趟偏下，苇寨的人捏着汗没言语。第三趟送盐，半河里横过来一阵风，他把那阵风当成了溜，一篙还出去，船横着拍在口子上头的石梁上。船和盐都留在了石梁底下，水生的右臂叫船帮别住，抬回家就再没能抬过肩。那年冬天，秀枝落了地。两岸的老辈人在渡口议了一天，把老例里那条例外也抹了：大涨封渡，多大的事也不开。老关搬进滩边的草棚，守着水看了三年，没提过一句口诀，人就没了。平水的渡口，水生靠一只左手撑了十几年。

In the eighth month the river rose again, and Shuisheng was the only one who would take a boat out. The first two crossings he squeezed inside the mouth, once a little above, once a little below, and the people of Weizhai held their breath and said nothing. On the third he was carrying salt. Halfway across, a wind came slanting down the river, and he took that gust for a lean of the current; he answered it with a full stroke, the boat swung broadside, and struck the rock ledge above the mouth. The boat and the salt stayed under the ledge; Shuisheng's right arm caught in the gunwale, and after they carried him home he never raised that shoulder again. That winter Xiuzhi was born. The elders of the two banks sat at the ferry for a day and struck the exception from the old rule: in high water the crossing closes, whatever the errand. Guan moved into the straw hut by the shingle, watched the water for three years, said nothing more of the rule, and died. On calm water Shuisheng kept the ferry going with his left hand for a dozen years and more.

秀枝十四岁上船。头一个月她不撑船，天不亮就坐到鹰嘴石上看水：看上游漂下来的柴禾、断树，一样一样，看它们不走人的手，自己斜到哪里，在哪里靠岸。看完一个月，她在鹰嘴石上重新认了个记号，把舅公那句口诀挪了三指。她爹教不了篙，就用左手在船帮上比划，只教一句话：大溜要给，碎浪不理。她的船从此回回落在桩前三五步，风大就七八步，回回不一样，可回回在口子里。有人问她怎么不落在桩上，她说：「风的那一份，我不挣。」

Xiuzhi took the pole at fourteen. For her first month she did not steer. Before daybreak she sat on Eagle-Beak Rock and watched the river carry down what it carried — driftwood, fallen trunks — watching where, with no hand upon them, they slanted and where they made their landings. When the month was out she chose a new mark on the rock and moved her great-uncle's rule three fingers' width. Her father could not teach the pole, so he taught with his left hand on the gunwale, one sentence only: give to the long lean; pay no mind to the chop. From then on her boat landed within three or five paces of the stake, seven or eight in strong wind, never the same, and never outside the mouth. Someone asked why she did not land on the stake itself. She said: "The wind's share I do not try to earn."

如今带学徒，秀枝头一天不教撑篙，先把人领到石滩上，看那两样印子，看半天，才许摸篙。上船那天她只教一句：「一条船的差，是三份合的：口诀里带来的一份，手跟着水走的一份，还有风自己扣下的一份。口诀那份，想压没它，手就得跟每一朵浪走，手那份就涨上来；手那份想压没它，就一回也不许答水，口诀那份就原样立在滩上。」头两份她只肯往小里压，压不没；风的那一份，谁的船也讨不回来。她撑到第三年，两岸才把老例里那条例外又还给她的船，只还给她一条。她撑渡这些年，离桩最近的一回差了两步——那天没有风。

When she takes an apprentice now, the first day is no day for poles. She leads the boy to the shingle and shows him the two marks, and only after half a day of that may he touch the boat. On the day he first goes out she gives him one saying: "A boat's miss is made of three shares — the share the rule brings with it, the share the hand goes along with the water, and the share the wind keeps for itself. If you would grind the rule's share away, the hand must follow every single wave, and the hand's share swells; if you would grind the hand's share away, you may not answer the water at all, and the rule's share stands on the shingle exactly as it is." The first two she will press small, never out; the wind's share no boat ever bargains back. In her third year the two banks gave the exception back to her boat, and to hers alone. In all her years on the ferry, the closest she has come to the stake is two paces — and that day there was no wind.

注解：这是偏差-方差权衡（the bias-variance tradeoff）：口诀咬死，恒差（偏差）立现；口诀放活，落点跟着每一趟的水漂散（方差）——两份此消彼长，谁也压不到零，之外还有风扣下的那一份（不可约误差），再稳的船也得给它留出三五步。

Note: This is the bias-variance tradeoff: a rule held stiff shows its constant miss (bias), a rule let loose scatters its landings along each crossing's water (variance) — the two rise and fall against each other, and neither can be pressed to zero; beyond them the wind keeps a share of its own (the irreducible error), and even the surest boat must leave it those few paces.
