人类并未发明艺术，事实恰恰相反

J F Martel·Aeon·2026年10月15日·16分钟阅读·原文

1940年，关于通往一座古老庄园的秘密通道的传闻，诱使四个少年钻进了法国西南部蒙蒂尼亚克（Montignac）村附近的一个地洞。他们没有找到满载黄金的隧道，却发现了一连串缀满精美绘画的天然洞室：马与驼鹿、羱羊与公牛，在石灰岩壁上保存得近乎完好。此后数年，专家利用放射性碳测年法考察拉斯科（Lascaux）洞穴，估计这些画作大约绘制于一万九千年前。如今人们认为，这些图像已有约两万年历史。但拉斯科远非人类艺术最古老的例证。自1940年以来，欧洲、澳大利亚、印度尼西亚和非洲又发现了许多洞穴，其中的艺术同样令人惊叹，有些的年代更是拉斯科壁画的两倍以上。

In 1940, rumours of secret passageways to an old manor lured four teenage boys into a hole in the ground near the village of Montignac in southwestern France. Instead of a tunnel laden with gold, they found a series of natural chambers bejewelled with exquisite paintings: horses and elk, ibex and bulls, almost perfectly preserved on the limestone walls. Experts examining the Lascaux caves in subsequent years using radiocarbon dating estimated that the paintings had been made some 19,000 years ago. Today, the images are believed to be around 20,000 years old. But Lascaux is far from the oldest example of human art. Since 1940, many other caves have been found in Europe, Australia, Indonesia and Africa containing art no less astounding. Some of it is more than twice the age of the Lascaux murals.

这些发现使「人类于何时、为何发明艺术」的问题，成为人类科学的中心课题。这类问题固然恰当而深刻，却未触及更深的谜团：艺术究竟是什么？它在人类灵魂中源自何处？关于实在的本性，它又能向我们揭示什么？

These discoveries have turned the questions of when and why humans invented art into central problems of the human sciences. As fair and poignant as such questions may be, they leave unaddressed the deeper mystery of what art is in the first place, where it comes from in the human soul, and what it might reveal to us about the nature of reality.

据流行的传闻，巴勃罗·毕加索（Pablo Picasso）在拉斯科洞穴被发现数年之后前去参观，出来时惊叹道：「我们什么也没有发明。」——艺术从它最早的实例起，便已以成熟的形态出现。通常的解读是：毕加索是在谦逊地把艺术的发明归于史前祖先，而不是归于他自己与他的同代人，或任何现代的人类。但这个真伪难考的故事，或许传达了一个更奇异的真理：根本没有谁真正发明过艺术。恰恰相反，是艺术发明了人类。

Popular legend holds that when Pablo Picasso visited the Lascaux cave a few years after its discovery, he emerged exclaiming: ‘We have invented nothing’ – art had appeared full-grown from its earliest instance. The common interpretation is that Picasso was humbly attributing the invention of art to our prehistoric ancestors rather than claiming it for himself and his contemporaries, or any human of the modern age. But perhaps this apocryphal story conveys a weirder truth, namely that no one really invented art. Instead, it was the other way around: art invented humanity.

1819年，约翰·济慈（John Keats）完成了《希腊古瓮颂》（Ode on a Grecian Urn），这首诗对待古代艺术的精神，与大多数现代考古学家截然不同。济慈与诗中的古瓮交谈，仿佛它是一个活物，而不是死者遗留给我们的物件；而这个活物说的是美的语言。两个世纪之后，他与古瓮的这场对话，仍给我们留下关于艺术在世界中之位置的未解之谜，其中最令人难忘的，是那结束语：
当暮年使这一代人凋零，
你仍将长存，置身于异于
我们的哀愁之中，做人类的朋友，对他说道：
「美即是真，真即是美——这就是
你们在尘世所知的一切，也是你们唯一需要知道的一切。」

In 1819, John Keats completed ‘Ode on a Grecian Urn’, a poem that engages with ancient art in a totally different spirit from that of most modern archaeologists. Keats converses with the eponymous urn as though it were a living being rather than an object bequeathed to us by the dead, and this living being speaks in the language of beauty. Two centuries later, his conversation with the urn still leaves us with unresolved puzzles about the place of art in the world, most memorably with the closing words:
When old age shall this generation waste,
Thou shalt remain, in midst of other woe
Than ours, a friend to man, to whom thou say’st,
‘Beauty is truth, truth beauty, – that is all
Ye know on earth, and all ye need to know.’

在现代心灵看来，「真理与美在某种意义上是同义词」、二者竟能重合的想法，多半显得荒谬。美与真理彼此究竟有什么相干？退一步说，既然美如此频繁地欺骗我们，它难道不是更接近于不真吗？

To modern minds, the idea that truth and beauty are somehow synonymous, that they might coincide at all, is likely to seem absurd. What do beauty and truth have to do with one another? If anything, isn’t beauty closer to untruth, given how frequently it deceives us?

哲学家会回答：这取决于我们如何定义所用概念。例如，德国电影人维尔纳·赫尔佐格（Werner Herzog）曾为他在纪录片中摆拍或操控场景的做法辩护，其方式是区分两种真理。第一种是纯粹事实性的真理：当一个陈述与事态相符时它便成立，比如你说「乌鸦正飞过田野」，而乌鸦恰好正在这样做。赫尔佐格无疑承认这种真理的实用价值，但他对它的评价足够低，以至于称之为「会计师的真理」。第二种他称为「出神的真理」（ecstatic truth）。在他看来，它更有意思，因为它所指明的，正是艺术家、诗人和哲学家正在（或者说应该）寻求的东西。出神的真理不是可以贴在准确陈述上的标签，而是一件不可重复的事件，以启示的全部力量降临。它每一次发生都是新的。其中有某种怪异的东西，某种唤起存在之谜的东西。

A philosopher would answer that it depends on how we define our terms. The German filmmaker Werner Herzog, for instance, once defended his practice of staging or manipulating scenes in his documentaries by distinguishing two kinds of truth. The first is the purely factual kind that obtains when a statement corresponds to a state of affairs, as when you say ‘the crows are flying over the field’ because the crows happen to be doing just that. Herzog no doubt acknowledges the practical value of this kind of truth, but he holds it in low enough regard to call it ‘the truth of accountants’. The second kind is what he calls ‘ecstatic truth’. It is more interesting to him because it denotes what artists, poets and philosophers are (or should be) seeking. Ecstatic truth isn’t a label you slap onto accurate statements but an unrepeatable event that comes with the full force of revelation. It is new each time it occurs. There is something weird in it, something that evokes the mystery of existence.

赫尔佐格的两种真理并非截然对立。它们彼此依存。在一个事实无法被言明的平行宇宙里——一个过于混乱或动荡、容不下连贯陈述的宇宙——出神的真理同样无从表达。然而，若没有出神的真理，任何事实陈述都将不值一提。且容我解释。

Herzog’s two kinds of truth aren’t exactly opposed. Each depends on the other. In a parallel universe where facts are impossible to articulate – a universe too chaotic or unstable to allow for coherent statements – ecstatic truth would likewise be impossible to express. However, without ecstatic truth, no statement of fact would be worth making. Let me explain.

文森特·梵高（Vincent van Gogh）最晚期的作品之一《麦田上的鸦群》（Wheatfield with Crows，1890年），正如标题所提示的，画的似乎不过是黄昏时分飞过田野的乌鸦，全无奇幻之处。但画面里有那样一种动势、那样一种激荡，使我们抗拒那些嗤之以鼻的扫兴者——「不过是几只乌鸦飞过田野罢了——有什么了不起。」如果说这幅画也算是在「作陈述」，那么它陈述的是属于另一种秩序的真理，而「出神」正是描述它的一种方式。

One of Vincent van Gogh’s last paintings, Wheatfield with Crows (1890), appears to depict – as the title suggests – nothing more fantastical than crows flying over a field at dusk. But there is such movement, such turbulence to the image that we resist the spoilsports who would turn up their noses, saying: ‘It’s just a bunch of crows flying over a field – big deal.’ If the painting can be said to be ‘making a statement’ at all, it is a statement pertaining to truth of a different order, and ‘ecstatic’ is one way to describe it.

「出神」一词源自希腊文 ekstasis，意为「站到自身之外」。出神的真理把我们带出自身；它暗示着一种意义的盈余，超出我们原本可能化约为直白事实陈述的那部分。归根结底，这幅画是一个单一事件，超越任何具体的诠释。它有赖于存在一个可理解的世界——在其中乌鸦是乌鸦、田野是田野——但它又抗拒任何向这些事实的还原。这幅画在说：没有任何事物「不过是」任何事物。

The word comes from the Greek ekstasis, ‘to stand outside oneself’. Ecstatic truth takes us out of ourselves; it hints at an excess of meaning, over and above what we might otherwise reduce to plain statements of fact. The image, ultimately, is a singular event that transcends any particular interpretation. It depends on there being an intelligible world in which crows are crows and fields are fields, but it also resists any reduction to those facts. Nothing, the picture says, is ‘just’ anything.

今天，许多人会主张，寻常的「会计师的真理」比出神的真理更根本，因为后者在很大程度上依赖感知、诠释与主观感受。但这种看法恰恰错过了艺术所揭示的实在。

Today, many would argue that the ordinary ‘truth of accountants’ is more fundamental than ecstatic truth, which relies heavily on perception, interpretation and subjective feeling. Yet this misses the reality that art discloses.

想想经验科学。要拥有它，我们必须相信宇宙在原则上是可理解的。不知怎的，我们人类恰好拥有能照世界之所是来思考世界的认知装备。我们必须能够足够地信任自己的感官，才能相信我们从感官经验中提炼出的概念与数学观念，能够告诉我们某种关于感官经验之外的世界是什么样子的东西。于是，科学的基本实在论立足于如下主张：宇宙是这样的——事实可以在其中被平实地陈述，尽管事实本身未必平实。

Think about empirical science. In order to have it, we must believe that the Universe is intelligible in principle. Somehow, we humans have the cognitive equipment to think about the world as it is. We must be able to trust our senses enough to believe that the conceptual and mathematical ideas we derive from sensory experience can tell us something about what the world is like beyond sensory experience. The basic realism of science, then, rests upon the following claim: the Universe is such that facts can be plainly stated within it, even though the facts themselves need not be plain.

现在，从表面上看，「宇宙是这样的：事实可以在其中被平实地陈述」这一陈述，是第一种真理的一个实例。宇宙容许事实陈述，这似乎是一个平实的事实陈述：一种会计师的真理。但在更深的层面上，它同样例示了第二种真理，因为我们看不出任何理由，为什么我们的宇宙必须如此可理解、如此合乎理性的秩序，以致容得下科学家与艺术家的存在（更不必说会计师了）。给出的任何理由都会预设理性，而此处恰恰要解释的正是理性。换言之，这个平实事实的宇宙，其存在本身就是一个谜。不止如此，它是一个根本性的谜，因为它直抵万物的根（源自拉丁文 radix），并将万物尽括其中。仅仅在指出「世界是可理解的」这一事实时，我们便已触及一切事实与理性背后的某个出神的真理。我们可以计数、测量事物，从而把它们贬为可供标记与分类的纯粹对象；但我们之所以能这样做，只是因为那些事物就在那里，可供计数与测量——而这无法解释。

Now, on the surface, this statement – ‘the Universe is such that facts can be plainly stated within it’ – is an instance of the first kind of truth. That the Universe allows for factual statements appears to be a plain statement of fact: a truth of accounting. At a deeper level, however, it also exemplifies the second kind of truth, because there is no discernible reason why our universe must be so intelligible and so rationally ordered as to permit the existence of scientists and artists (let alone accountants). Any reason given would presuppose reason, and it is precisely rationality that is here to be explained. In other words, the very existence of the plain-fact universe is a mystery. More than this, it is a radical mystery, because it goes right to the root (from the Latin radix) of things, and encompasses them all. Merely in pointing out the fact of the world’s intelligibility, we are touching an ecstatic truth behind all fact and reason. We can count and measure things, and thereby reduce them to mere objects to be tagged and classified, but we can do this only because the things are there to be counted and measured, inexplicably.

除了告诉我们许多关于一群群乌鸦、麦田、渐暗的天空与田间小径的事情之外，梵高的画还在向我们展示：活在这颗行星上、活在这个宇宙里意味着什么。毫无疑问，它是借助评论家与史学家在画中发现的那些主观观念做到这一点的，例如死亡与绝望（这幅画作于画家自尽前不久）。但画作透过这些主题所揭示的东西，绝非主观。事实上，它是我们唯一能够客观认识的东西：每一个事件仅凭其发生便予以确认的那个根本之谜。谜不在于乌鸦与麦田意味着什么，而在于它们竟然有所意味；它们之所以有所意味，是因为它们已然如其所是地存在。在这种更深的意义上，艺术家的真理先于并包含会计师的真理。在说任何东西值得计数或值得记账之前，必须先承认它——哪怕只是无意识地，有时或许还是愤愤然地。

In addition to telling us much about murders of crows, fields of wheat, darkling skies, and footpaths, van Gogh’s painting is showing us what it means to be alive on this planet, in this universe. No doubt it does this by means of the subjective ideas that critics and historians have found in the painting, such as mortality and despair (the picture was made shortly before the painter took his own life). But what the painting reveals through these themes is not subjective at all. It is, in fact, the one thing we can know objectively: the radical mystery that every event, by its mere occurrence, affirms. The mystery isn’t what the crows and the field mean, but that they mean, and that they mean because they have all come to be, just as they are. The truth of artists, in this deeper sense, precedes and contains the truth of accountants. It is what must be acknowledged – if only unconsciously, sometimes perhaps resentfully – before anything can be said to be worth counting or accounting for.

我猜想赫尔佐格会同意，出神的真理与美密切相关，而美是一个与艺术联系得更明确的理想。倘若如此，那么我们或许也需要把美分为两种，大致类比于他的两种真理。我们不妨把第一种称作「几何学家的美」，将它与传统上据称会引起审美愉悦的种种量化属性联系起来：对称、和谐、比例、规整等等。毫无疑问，这类属性之所以悦目悦耳、从而在狭义上显得美，有强有力的演化理由。承认这一点，就为另一种美腾出了空间——这另一种美不可测度，因为它不寓于规整与共性，而寓于质性的、单一的、充满事件的、不可重复的、乃至怪异之物：作为陌生与新奇之出神的美。

Herzog, I suspect, would agree that ecstatic truth is closely related to beauty, an ideal more explicitly associated with art. If so, then perhaps we need to also divide beauty into two kinds, roughly analogous to his two kinds of truth. Let us call the first kind ‘the beauty of the geometers’, and associate it with quantitative properties traditionally said to cause aesthetic pleasure: symmetry, harmony, proportion, regularity and so on. No doubt, there are strong evolutionary reasons why such properties are pleasing to the eye and ear, and thus appear beautiful in a narrow sense. Recognising this makes room for another beauty, this one immeasurable because it inheres not in regularity and generality but in the qualitative, the singular, the eventful, the unrepeatable, even the monstrous: beauty as the ecstasy of the strange and the new.

同样，这两种美并不对立，而是相互依存。正是完美形态的稀少——与自然界占主导的不规则形成对照——才使对称与重复在我们的眼中如此醒目。正是一切随时可能坠入彻底混沌这一事实，才使巴赫（Bach）的赋格作为一个异数脱颖而出——像深渊底部一片优雅起伏的白色裹尸布。出于同样的道理，许多让我们觉得美的事件，没有一丝一毫的比例或和谐可言。爱德华·蒙克（Edvard Munch）作于1893年的《自然的呐喊》（The Scream of Nature），震聋我们的不是音乐，而是一种戳穿一切常态、并塑造出尖叫者世界的可怖喧嚣。如果说古典艺术擅长从第一种美中引出第二种美，那么现代艺术也许是一种尝试——有时成功，有时不那么成功——要在出神之美的本有领地上与它相会。在绘画中，例子并不止于蒙克的呐喊；想想威尔汉姆·哈默肖伊（Vilhelm Hammershøi）笔下闹鬼的房间，雷梅迪奥斯·瓦罗（Remedios Varo）结晶而成的梦，弗朗西斯·培根（Francis Bacon）破碎的灵魂。

Again, the two kinds are not opposed but interdependent. It is the rarity of perfect forms – their contrast with the predominant irregularities of the natural world – that makes symmetry and repetition so striking to our eyes. It is the fact that all could bottom out in chaos at any time that makes a Bach fugue stand out as an anomaly – a white shroud undulating gracefully in the depths of an abyss. By the same token, many events strike us as beautiful that contain not a hint of proportion or harmony. Edvard Munch’s The Scream of Nature, painted in 1893, deafens not by its music but by a hideous clamour that belies all normalcy and shapes the screamer’s world. If classical art excels at drawing the second kind of beauty from the first, modern art may be an attempt – sometimes successful, sometimes less so – to meet ecstatic beauty on its own ground. In painting, the examples do not stop at Munch’s scream; think of Vilhelm Hammershøi’s paintings of haunted rooms, Remedios Varo’s crystallised dreams, Francis Bacon’s broken souls.

与第二种真理一样，这种美寓于永不重复的单一事件。它的转瞬即逝中有悲剧，但也有惊奇：竟然有事发生这一惊奇。从这个角度看，济慈从希腊古瓮那里听到的讯息——「美即是真，真即是美」——就完全说得通了。真理与美，在作为一般范畴运用于世间之物时固然不同，但一旦置于根本之谜的背景之前，它们便彼此重合。

Like the second kind of truth, this kind of beauty inheres in singular events, never to be repeated. There is tragedy in its evanescence but also wonder: the wonder that anything happens at all. From this perspective, the message Keats hears from the Grecian urn – ‘Beauty is truth, truth beauty’ – makes perfect sense. Truth and beauty, though different when applied as general categories to things in the world, coincide when placed against the backdrop of radical mystery.

「美的东西总是古怪的，」夏尔·波德莱尔（Charles Baudelaire）在1855年写道（引文由我译），「我的意思是，它总是含有几分奇异，几分单纯的、未经谋划的、无意识的奇异，而正是这几分奇异，赋予了它作为美的独特品质。」在每一次美的经验中，都有怪异的一丝暗示。那一刻可能伴随着一次骤然的屏息，以及一个念头：这怎么可能？在落日面前，在星云面前，在所爱之人面前：这怎么可能？

‘The Beautiful is always bizarre,’ wrote Charles Baudelaire in 1855 (my translation). ‘I mean that it always contains a touch of strangeness, of simple, unpremeditated, and unconscious strangeness, and that it is this touch of strangeness that gives it its particular quality as Beauty.’ In every experience of the beautiful, there is a hint of the weird. The moment may come with a sudden intake of breath, along with the thought: How can this be? Before the sunset, the nebula, the beloved: How can this be?

在美之中，我们遭遇一道鸿沟：一边是我们预期在一个受物理定律支配的物质宇宙中找到的东西，一边是我们实际在那里找到的东西——无论那何其稀少。说美是古怪的，等于说它是一种盈余——但它是实在的盈余，而非幻想的盈余。比「竟然有一个世界存在」更令人惊愕的，是「这个存在的世界竟然如此充满惊奇与奇异」，因为每一例惊奇或奇异，都阻止那精于算计的理智攥紧事物、并说服我们说自己已把它们琢磨透了。

In beauty, we encounter a gap between what we expect to find in a material universe governed by physical laws – and what we actually find there, however rarely. To say that beauty is bizarre is to say that it is a kind of excess – but an excess of reality, not fantasy. More astounding than the fact that a world exists at all is the fact that the existing world should be so full of wonder and strangeness, because each instance of wonder or strangeness prevents the calculating intellect from clamping down on things and persuading us that we have figured them out.

两种真理，两种美。由此推之，必然还有两种艺术——同样彼此不同，却又以某种方式互为补充。第一种我称之为「造作之艺」（artifice）：这类艺术被构想出来，是为了确认、强化并扩展我们寻常生活于其中的那个事实帝国。造作之艺对应于前一种美与前一种真理：几何学家的美；会计师的真理。它可以提升或贬损、鼓舞或压抑、教化或蒙蔽，但它不转化任何东西。它让一切保持它发现时的原样，因为它出自这样一个假定：我们平常思考世界的方式，足以让我们知道世界真正是什么样子。例子信手拈来：宣传、色情与广告；煽情的、道德训诫式的或说教的艺术；以及无数仅仅反映其时代流行意见的作品。社会需要造作之艺、甚至从某些造作之艺中受益，这一点我并不怀疑。我唯一坚持的是：它算不上艺术。它做不到唯有艺术才能做到的事。

Two kinds of truth, two kinds of beauty. It follows that there must also be two kinds of art – similarly different and yet, somehow, complementary. The first is what I call artifice: art conceived so as to confirm, reinforce and extend the empire of facts in which we ordinarily live. Artifice corresponds to the first kinds of beauty and truth: the beauty of geometers; the truth of accountants. It may elevate or degrade, uplift or depress, edify or delude, but it transforms nothing. It leaves everything as it found it, because it proceeds from the assumption that the way we normally think of the world is enough for us to know how it really is. Examples come readily to mind: propaganda, pornography and advertising; sentimental, moralistic or didactic art; the countless works that merely reflect the ambient opinion of their time. That society needs artifice, and even benefits from some of it, I do not doubt. My only insistence is that it is not quite art. It does not do what art alone can do.

那么，唯有艺术才能做到的是什么？前面我说过，一个可理解宇宙的存在并非诸多问题中的一个，而是一个根本之谜。出神的艺术（或者说詹姆斯·乔伊斯（James Joyce）所称的「正当的」艺术）使我们接触到第二秩序的真理与美，只要那谜在其中得到确认。它无法仅凭再现自然做到这一点，因为再现不过是复制，什么也不改变。毋宁说，正当的艺术参与创造之谜本身——参与世界每一刻从自然的子宫中重新诞出的那个过程。

And what is it that art alone can do? Earlier I said that the existence of an intelligible universe isn’t one problem among many but a radical mystery. Ecstatic art (or what James Joyce called ‘proper’ art) puts us in touch with truth and beauty of the second order insofar as that mystery is affirmed therein. It cannot do this by merely representing nature, because to represent is simply to reproduce, changing nothing. Rather, proper art participates in the mystery of creation itself, the process through which the world emerges anew from the womb of nature at each moment.

于是，这两种艺术从截然不同的视角逼近自然。十七世纪的哲学家巴鲁赫·斯宾诺莎（Baruch Spinoza），沿承某些中世纪思想家的思路，在区分「被造化的自然」（natura naturata）与「造化着的自然」（natura naturans）时，清楚地表述了这种差异。前者，即 natura naturata——「已然被造化的自然」——是因果的领域，是诸多界限分明的同一性所构成的钟表宇宙，万物都被整整齐齐地贴上我们借以把握它们的标签。用威廉·詹姆斯（William James）与亨利·柏格森（Henri Bergson）的语言来说，这是「既成之物」的世界，也就是已被心灵切分完毕、收拢在干净概念与范畴之中的固定对象的世界。当人们说落日「只是」落日、树「只是」树时，他们栖居的正是这个宇宙，并把它当作最终定论。与此相对，natura naturans——「造化着的自然」——指的是「生成中之物」的世界，是一切事物在被定格为事实之前，作为事件而生成的活的运动。它并非机械意义上的因果，而是纯粹的、令人无从索解的创造。

The two kinds of art, then, approach nature from radically different perspectives. The 17th-century philosopher Baruch Spinoza, following certain medieval thinkers, articulated this difference when he distinguished between natura naturata and natura naturans. The first, natura naturata or ‘nature natured’, is the realm of cause and effect, the clockwork universe of discrete identities, neatly tagged with the labels by which we apprehend them. It is, in the language of William James and Henri Bergson, the world of ‘things made’, that is, of fixed objects that the mind has already carved up and captured in clean concepts and categories. When people say that a sunset is ‘just’ a sunset or a tree is ‘just’ a tree, they are inhabiting this universe and taking it as final. By contrast, natura naturans – ‘naturing nature’ – refers to the world of ‘things in the making’, the living movement by which all things come to be as events before they can be rendered as facts. It is not causal in the mechanical sense, but purely and bafflingly creative.

当我们走进一座大教堂，或凝视一幅马克·罗斯科（Mark Rothko），令我们惊愕的不只是这些作品的完成形态，而是每一件作品所体现的那种奇异转化。我知道石头重得难以置信，却看见它悬浮着，仿佛比空气还轻；我看见一道道笔触，而色域却因这些笔触反而显得愈发坚实。除此之外，正是这建筑与这画的无端偶然性——它们完全可能从未被制作出来这一无可否认的事实——让我屏住了呼吸。它们的存在系于时间之中的一次创造之举、一个去制作的自由决定；而借着制作，它们扩展了世界，并更改了世界从此可被再现的诸种条件。

What astounds us when we enter a cathedral or contemplate a Mark Rothko is not merely the finished form of these works but the strange transformation each embodies. I know the stone to be impossibly heavy, yet I see it suspended as though lighter than air; I see the strokes of paint, yet the colour field feels all the more solid for them. Aside from this, it is the gratuitous contingency of the building and the painting – the undeniable fact that they might just as well never have been made – that takes my breath away. Their existence depends on an act of creation in time, a free decision to make, and by making, to enlarge the world and alter the terms by which it can henceforth be represented.

从画前转过身，或迈步走上街头，我可能会在惊愕中仿佛初次看见世界。剥去我习惯性地投射其上的那层熟悉感，每一样东西似乎都显露出我方才在艺术品中遭遇的那种偶然性。世界本身突然显得像是一次创造性活动的结果，而这活动即便在我直觉它的此刻仍在继续。如果说这段经验具有转化之力，那是因为它让我接触到我自身所是的那个转化——我作为人的生活所代表的那场持续创造——即我参与的那个奇异进程：直到此刻，世界仍然是一件生成中之物，而非一件既成之物。

Turning from the painting or stepping out onto the street, I may in my astonishment see the world as though for the first time. Stripped of the overlay of familiarity that I habitually project upon it, everything appears to exhibit the very contingency I encountered in the artwork. The world itself suddenly seems the result of a creative act that continues even as I intuit it. If the experience is transformative, it is because it puts me in touch with the transformation that I myself am, the ongoing creation that my life as a human being represents – my participation in the weird process by which the world, even now, is a thing-in-the-making rather than a thing made.

只有人类创作出神的艺术。这或许是唯一能为某种「人类例外论」（human exceptionalism）辩护的东西。然而事实是，恰恰是那个令我们如此例外的东西，本应保护我们免于人类中心主义（anthropocentrism）。回望已知最早的一些艺术——世界各地洞穴中发现的图像——我们会惊讶地注意到：人类形象要与画家们深情绘于石上的动物、怪兽和抽象形态并肩走到画面中心，竟花去了数千年时间。以旧石器时代艺术家赋予动物的解剖学精确性与个性来描绘人体的形象，直到约公元前一万年开始的新石器革命（Neolithic Revolution）才告出现。更早的人类形象是存在的——维尔奥努尔（Vilhonneur）一幅抽象化的面孔，画于约两万七千年前；苏拉威西（Sulawesi）的人兽混合形象，距今四万八千多年——但它们从未获得与拉斯科的马和野牛形象同等的对待。事实上，具有清晰头部、躯干、手臂、腿脚，尤其是面孔的人类形象，是在拉斯科壁画完成约八千年之后才出现的。确实，某些最古老的洞穴艺术以喷印的人手为特色——可是，手不正是人类与非人类相触之处、不正是自我与世界的界面本身吗？

Only humans produce ecstatic art. It is perhaps the one thing that might justify a kind of human exceptionalism. The truth is, however, that the very thing that makes us so exceptional is precisely what ought to safeguard us against anthropocentrism. If we look back to some of the earliest art known – the images found in caves around the world – we notice, remarkably, that it took thousands of years for human figures to take centre stage alongside the animals, monsters and abstract forms that the painters lovingly set upon the stone. Figures rendering the human body with the anatomical precision and individuality that Palaeolithic artists gave animals didn’t appear until the Neolithic Revolution, beginning around 10,000 BCE. Earlier human images exist – an abstracted face at Vilhonneur, painted around 27,000 years ago, and human-animal hybrids at Sulawesi, more than 48,000 years ago – but they’re never given the same treatment as the images of horses and bison at Lascaux. In fact, human figures with detailed heads, torsos, arms, legs and, most importantly, faces, emerged only around 8 millennia after the Lascaux paintings were made. It’s true that some of the very oldest cave art features stencils of human hands – but what is the hand if not the place where the human touches the nonhuman, the very interface of self and world?

如果艺术归根结底真是西格蒙德·弗洛伊德（Sigmund Freud）等人所认定的那种自恋事业，我们本该看到人类形象从最初起就无处不在。那么，在人类最早现身的种种迹象之中，人类形象何以奇怪地缺席？也许，艺术首要关注的并非人，而是实在的壮观景象——在其中，人只扮演一个无穷微小的角色。正是在仰望无月夜空的星辰，或是远眺远处山脊上的原牛之时，艺术的想象力诞生了。

If art were, at bottom, the narcissistic endeavour that Sigmund Freud and others took it to be, we would expect to see human figures everywhere from the start. What explains their curious absence from the earliest signs of their presence? Perhaps art is not primarily concerned with the human so much as with the spectacle of the real, in which the human plays an infinitesimal part. It was in looking up at the stars in the moonless sky, or at the aurochs on the distant ridge, that the artistic imagination was born.

艺术从来不是对人类的礼赞，而始终是对那些边地的探索——在那里，人之所是终止，别的东西开始。即便对于处理看似纯属人类的观念、情感与事物的艺术，也是如此。唯有通过艺术，一座城市才能变成丛林，一栋房屋才能变成异形构造，一种悲伤才能变成一场天气事件。

Art has never been a celebration of humanity, but always an exploration of those borderlands where the human ends and something else begins. This applies even to art dealing with seemingly human ideas, feelings and things. Only through art can a city become a jungle, a house an alien structure, a sadness a kind of weather event.

乍看之下，我们或许会说，史前洞穴壁画为「艺术模仿自然」这一古老哲学观念提供了有力的证据：早期人类被「既成之物」打动，于是选择用再现来纪念它们。但斯宾诺莎对两种自然的区分允许另一种解读——这种解读不仅考虑洞穴中诸形象的身份（一头犀牛、一匹马、一颗星），也考虑这些图像中的表现，那种据说让毕加索说出「拉斯科的画师们已经发明了一切」的艺术性。

At first glance, we might say that prehistoric cave paintings offer strong evidence for the old philosophical idea that ‘art imitates nature’. Early humans, impressed by ‘things made’, chose to commemorate them in representations. But Spinoza’s distinction between two kinds of nature permits a different interpretation, one that considers not only the identity of the figures in the caves (a rhinoceros, a horse, a star) but also the expression in the images, the artfulness that allegedly led Picasso to say that the Lascaux painters had invented everything.

洞穴壁上的那些事物，并不完全是一心致力于测量与量化的科学心智所能辨认的东西。因为它们在那里是以生成中之物的面貌出现的。而如果自然确实是在成为因果的之前先就是创造的、在成为机械的之前先就是有魔力的，那么我们完全有理由说，它从来就已经是艺术，并且从一开始就是。一切事物都是一个变化的过程，一种生成；当我们给它贴上一个固定的身份、一个概念时，我们便打断了它的进程——在心智上打断，因而也是人为地打断。

The things on the cave walls are not quite those that a scientific mind, intent on measurement and quantification, would recognise. For they appear there as things-in-the-making. And if it is true that nature is creative before it becomes causal, magical before it becomes mechanical, then we are within our rights to say that it is always-already art, and was so from the beginning. Everything is a process of change, a becoming whose course we halt – mentally and thus artificially – when we tag it with a fixed identity, a concept.

对动物、植物、岩石与星辰为真的东西，对人类同样为真。等到我们终于在洞穴中完整现身时，我们是作为一出戏剧中的角色现身的——这出戏既是物质的又是想象界的，既是身体的又是心灵的，而且整个地多于人类。因此，「人类发明了艺术」、「艺术『属于』人类」这些观念，只是事后为真。在一种更原初的意义上，是艺术发明了人类：它让人类得以进入它借以诞出一个世界——连同万物——的那个过程。

What is true of animals, plants, rocks and stars is also true of human beings. By the time we fully appear in the caves, we do so as figures in a drama that is at once material and imaginal, physical and psychic, and altogether more than human. The idea, then, that human beings invented art, that art ‘belongs’ to humanity, is true only after the fact. In a more primordial sense, art invented humanity by giving humans access to the process through which it brings forth a world, along with everything else.

我的意思是：艺术并不是用来表达一个已然存在的人性的。毋宁说，它充当了一面我们所需的镜子，让我们得以在自然的流变之中瞥见自己的形象。「人类何时发明了艺术」这一问题，引导我们把早期人类想象成与我们相差无几的样子：某一天决定用符号和形象来表达自己。这个问题里潜藏的假定是：他们早就拥有与我们一样的心灵，只是在冰河时代某个寒冷的日子里，在所有可做的事情中间，选择了拿起赭石去画动物。但在我看来，证据指向另一个方向：正是制作图像这一实践，经过数百代人的反复，促成了那些我们如今视为理所当然的心灵的涌现。当我们把自己理解为感觉与意义的源头时，我们是在本末倒置。实际上，我们参与在一个超出我们的事物之中，而伟大艺术的美学的、诗学的维度，自然而然地让我们与那盈余重新相连。

What I mean here is that art did not serve to express a humanity that already existed. Rather, it acted as the mirror we needed to catch sight of our own image in the flux of nature. The question of when humans invented art leads us to imagine early humans much like ourselves, deciding one day to express themselves through symbols and figures. The assumption hidden in the question is that they already had minds like ours and simply chose, out of all the things they might have done on a frigid day in the Ice Age, to pick up ochre and make animal pictures. But the evidence, it seems to me, points the other way: it was the practice of making images, repeated over hundreds of generations, that occasioned the emergence of those minds we take for granted. We are putting the cart before the horse when we understand ourselves as the source of sense and meaning. In reality, we are participants in something that exceeds us, and the aesthetic, poetic dimension of great art reconnects us with that excess as a matter of course.

但我们又一次被引回两种艺术。造作之艺是模仿自然的艺术：一种按人类的条件来再现世界的尝试，其结果按柏拉图（Plato）的说法是不完善的映像，按亚里士多德（Aristotle）的说法是完善化的抽象。与此相对，真正的艺术实行的是 natura naturans——即创造本身。如果我们在此还能谈论模仿，那也不是机械复制意义上的模仿，而是基督教神秘主义者所说的「效法基督」（imitatio Christi）意义上的模仿：一种去成为而非徒然仿效的模仿，一个超越理智将生成中之物还原为既成之物的一切方式的创造性事件。

But again, we are led back to two kinds of art. Artifice is art that imitates nature: an attempt to represent the world on human terms, resulting either in imperfect reflections (according to Plato) or in perfected abstractions (according to Aristotle). Real art, by contrast, enacts natura naturans – that is, creation as such. If we can speak of imitation here at all, it is not in the sense of mechanical copying but of what the Christian mystics call imitatio Christi: the imitation that becomes instead of merely aping, a creative event transcending all the ways in which the intellect reduces things-in-the-making to things made.

唯有艺术使我们接触到某些特定的真理与美。正是以这种方式，艺术把我们重新交还给实在。然而，它若要成功，就必须听凭它自用其器（或自无其器）。它必须自由地摒弃功利的逻辑，自由地去体现那种被手段与目的思维的重压所窒息的自由。

Art alone puts us in touch with certain kinds of truth and beauty. And in that way, art restores us to reality. For it to succeed, however, it must be left to its own devices (or lack thereof). It must be free to eschew the logic of utility and to embody the freedom we stifle under the pressures of means-and-ends thinking.

「一切艺术，」奥斯卡·王尔德（Oscar Wilde）在《道林·格雷的画像》（The Picture of Dorian Gray，1890年）的序言里打趣道，「都相当无用。」在王尔德看来，艺术在必然且本质的意义上不服务于任何目的——而艺术家们应当更明白事理，不会盼望它不是这样。与此同时，他在别处又以同样的笃信主张：若不是印象派画家的努力，伦敦的雾根本不会存在。他在《谎言的衰朽》（The Decay of Lying，1891年）中声称，正是他们催生了世界的这一面貌——在那之前，它一直无人得见：
若不是从印象派画家那里，我们又从何处得来那些奇妙的棕色雾霭——它们悄然爬过我们的街道，模糊了煤气街灯，把房屋化作狰狞的怪影？……过去十年间伦敦气候发生的惊人变化，完全归功于这一特定的艺术流派……人在看到事物的美之前，什么都看不见。唯有在那时，也只有在那时，它才真正存在……伦敦或许几百年来一直有雾。我敢说确实如此。但没有人看见过它们，因此我们对它们一无所知。直到艺术发明了雾，它们才存在。

‘All art,’ quipped Oscar Wilde in the preface to The Picture of Dorian Gray (1890), ‘is quite useless.’ For Wilde, art necessarily and essentially serves no purpose – and artists ought to know better than to wish it otherwise. At the same time, he elsewhere argued with equal conviction that if it weren’t for the efforts of the Impressionist painters, the London fog would not exist. It was they, he claimed in ‘The Decay of Lying’ (1891), who brought forth this aspect of the world, which had gone unseen until then:
Where, if not from the Impressionists, do we get those wonderful brown fogs that come creeping down our streets, blurring the gas-lamps and changing the houses into monstrous shadows? … The extraordinary change that has taken place in the climate of London during the last 10 years is entirely due to this particular school of Art … One does not see anything until one sees its beauty. Then, and then only, does it come into existence … There may have been fogs for centuries in London. I dare say there were. But no one saw them, and so we do not know anything about them. They did not exist till Art had invented them.

克劳德·莫奈（Claude Monet）和他的同道们并没有再现已然在场的东西；他们让迄今为止缺席的东西在场了。在王尔德看来，正是这种让我们看见的力量，使艺术无价。

Claude Monet and his circle did not represent what was already present; they made present what had hitherto been absent. This power to make us see, for Wilde, is what makes art invaluable.

这里有矛盾吗？艺术怎么可能既无价又「相当无用」？这取决于我们如何定义最后那个词。有人或许会说：在那时之前，没有人留意悄然漫过维多利亚时代伦敦的雾，是因为留意它毫无用处。印象派画家关注的恰恰是没人在意去看的东西，他们从人类世界那看不见的背景之中，拽出了一个从此再也无法忽视的新实体。如果说在王尔德那里艺术是无用的，那只是在这个意义上：它探索的是我们现有的理智尚未找到用处的那些实在区域——至少暂时还没有。套用保罗·克利（Paul Klee）的话说，艺术让原本看不见的东西变得可见，从而扩展了我们对世界的感知。因此，它所履行的功能恰与科学的功能相类似；只不过科学受一种功利的理想驱动，而艺术寻找的是尚未出现在我们雷达上的东西。艺术家让我们警觉于那些我们尚不知道自己不知道的事物。经由他们的作品，新的实在、新的观念与新的情感变得可为我们所用。

Is there a contradiction here? How can art be both invaluable and ‘quite useless’? It depends on how we define that last term. One might argue that if nobody paid attention to the fogs that crept through Victorian London until then, it was because there was no use in doing so. By attending to what no one cared to notice, the Impressionists drew from the invisible background of the human world a new entity that then became impossible to ignore. If art is useless for Wilde, it is only in the sense that it explores those regions of reality for which our present intellect has found no use – at least not yet. By making visible that which we could not see, to paraphrase Paul Klee, art expands our perception of the world. It thus fulfils a function precisely analogous to that of science; but whereas science is driven by an ideal of utility, art seeks what has not yet appeared on our radars. Artists alert us to what we do not yet know we do not know. Through their work, new realities, new ideas and new emotions become available.

的确，科学始于——也只能始于——艺术所造就的世界。归根结底，我们所谓的艺术，是人类想象力之能力的果实：超越直接感知，去梦想这个转角之外、人类世界之外有什么。从这个观点看，科学方法的假设阶段不折不扣就是艺术：一种练习，借由它，我们走出实际之物的平庸，去探索可能本身，检验我们的梦想，并把我们的触角伸进那多于人类的存在。

Indeed, science begins – and can only begin – in the world that art has made. Ultimately, what we call art is the fruit of the human imagination’s capacity to go beyond immediate perception, to dream what lies around this corner, outside the human world. From this point of view, the hypothetical stage of the scientific method is nothing less than art: an exercise through which we step out of the mundanities of the actual to explore the possible as such, testing our dreams and extending our reach into the more-than-human.

正因如此，法国哲学家吉尔·德勒兹（Gilles Deleuze）在1968年把美学描述为一门「可感者的科学」。在他看来，艺术造出科学随后去测量的世界；但它所造出的世界终究超出一切测量之外，因为那是我们在把它切分成长度、大小与数量之前就存在的世界。柏格森——德勒兹思想的一大渊源——曾把这称作「质性的多样性」：它在本性上更接近一支绵延不断的旋律，而不是一系列可以彼此孤立的离散而静止之物。它是先在的，因为它就是实在本身。

This is why the French philosopher Gilles Deleuze in 1968 described aesthetics as a ‘science of the sensible’. Art, for him, makes the world that science later measures, but the world it makes is ultimately beyond all measurement because it is the world that exists before we divide it into lengths, magnitudes and quantities. Bergson, a major influence on Deleuze, had called this a ‘qualitative multiplicity’, closer in nature to a continuous melody than to a series of discrete, static things that can be isolated from one another. It is what comes first, because it is the Real itself.

吊诡的是，唯有被允许保持彻底的无用，艺术才是有用的——也就是说，彻底自由地去它要去的地方。在这里，王尔德在波德莱尔那里找到了同盟：后者在指出美的奇异之后，紧接着对那些欲将艺术之美隶属于功利逻辑的人予以尖锐的痛斥。波德莱尔问道：
这种必然的、不可化约而又无穷多样的奇异……怎么可能被这个星球上某座小小科学殿堂里构想出的乌托邦规则所控制、修订和矫正，而不给艺术本身带来致命的危险？

Paradoxically, art is useful only if it is allowed to remain entirely useless – that is, entirely free to go where it will. Wilde finds an ally here in Baudelaire, who followed his observation of the strangeness of the beautiful with a sharp rebuke to those who would subordinate artistic beauty to a utilitarian logic. Baudelaire asks:
How could this necessary, irreducible, and infinitely varied strangeness … ever be controlled, amended, and corrected by Utopian rules conceived in some little scientific temple or other on this planet, without mortal danger to art itself?

把有用置于无用之上、把劳作置于游戏之上，就是艺术的死亡——也是那个被给予的世界的死亡；我们的功利精神若要有目的与意义，本必须植根于那个世界之中。

The privileging of utility over uselessness, of work over play, is the death of art – and the death of the given world in which our utilitarian spirit must be grounded if it is to have purpose and meaning.

今天，艺术正被种种力量挤压，这些力量想让它隶属于种种太属人的目的。有人说，艺术应当教育并提升那些易受影响的头脑，训练他们遵照一套既存的道德规范行事。另有人声称，艺术应当扮演治疗的角色，或者充当医学的工具，或者充当逃避的手段，让劳动者在下一个工作日到来之前恢复气力。还有人坚持，艺术应当服务于国家或市场的需要，维护一个政体先入为主的种种观念——而这个政体把自己对实在的有限看法错当成了实在的整体。人工智能的兴起，只会把这把艺术拉平为非艺术的势头推得更猛。根据定义，人工智能只能产出造作之艺，因为它栖居于一个由既成之物（数据）而非生成中之物构成的虚拟宇宙。分不清它生成的造作之艺与它模仿的真正的艺术，就是把再现误认作创造，从而让我们自己闭目不见那个使艺术与人类皆成为可能的源头。

Today, art is pressed upon by forces that would subordinate it to all-too-human ends. Some say that it should educate and elevate impressionable minds, training them to behave in accordance with a preexisting moral code. Others claim that it should play a therapeutic role, either as an instrument of medicine or as a means of escape by which workers might regain their strength before the next working day. Still others insist that art ought to serve the needs of the state or the market, upholding the preconceived ideas of a polity that mistakes its limited view of reality for the whole of reality. The rise of artificial intelligence has only intensified this levelling of art into non-art. By definition, artificial intelligence is capable only of artifice, since it inhabits a virtual universe of things made (data) rather than things-in-the-making. To fail to distinguish between the artifice it generates and the true art it imitates is to mistake representation for creation, and so to blind ourselves to the very source of what makes both art and humanity possible.

艺术必须保持无用（或者重新变得无用），而且必须以真理之名、也以美之名这样做。在这篇文章的开头，我区分了这两种理想的各自两种形态：一种仅仅确认我们自以为知道的东西；另一种则提醒我们，在所有局部的知识与不完满的信念之下，还有那个谜。最重要的是，艺术使我们直面那个根本之谜——没有它，我们的解题与功利主义都将失去意义。这个谜不是一种理论，而是一个真理，是那真理的地基本身；柏拉图称这真理的光华为美，赫尔佐格称之为出神，而济慈在对他古瓮的言说中，宣称它与真理本身为一体。它是出神的，因为它把我们带出自身之外，让我们与世界之心燃烧着的那份惊奇相连；而这惊奇，从每一次对答案的寻求之中，召唤出新的问题——以及随之而来的、新的活着的理由。

Art must remain useless (or become it again), and it must do so in the name of truth as well as beauty. At the beginning of this essay, I distinguished two forms of each of these ideals: one that merely confirms what we think we know, and another that reminds us of the mystery that underlies all our partial knowledge and imperfect beliefs. Above all, art confronts us with that radical mystery, without which our problem-solving and utilitarianism would be pointless. This mystery is not a theory but a truth, the very ground of that truth whose splendour Plato called beauty, Herzog called ecstasy, and Keats, speaking to his urn, declared to be one with truth itself. It is ecstatic because it takes us outside ourselves, connecting us with the wonder that burns at the heart of the world, and which, out of every search for answers, calls forth new questions – and, with them, new reasons to live.
