无处安放的爱

Derek van Zoonen·Aeon·2026年10月29日·12分钟阅读·原文

多年来，我一直在哀悼我的母亲，尽管她还活着。有时在入睡之前——在那介于清醒与沉睡之间、自我变得疏松多孔的阈限地带——我会看见那个甜美体贴的女人的形象：她来道晚安，脸颊上敷着厚厚的晚霜。然后，不知从何处起，这形象会翻转为那个暴怒的女人——她深陷于自己的苦难，以至于把满腔的挫败都倾泻到我们身上。年复一年，随着她被卷入我哥哥的毒瘾，第二个女人慢慢吞掉了第一个。很难说她的不幸在哪里结束、我哥哥的痛苦又从哪里开始，但两人一同漂进了一个平行的现实，与各自头脑之外的世界隔绝。这一切没有葬礼。没有慰问卡。没有鲜花。没有电话。没有丧假。我究竟该哀悼什么？

I have been grieving my mother for years, even though she is still alive. Sometimes before falling asleep – in that liminal space between waking and sleeping where the self becomes porous – I’d see the image of the sweet and caring woman who would come to say goodnight, her cheeks covered in thick layers of night cream. Then, out of nowhere, the image would flip into the raging woman who was so entangled in her own suffering that she turned her frustration on us. Over the years, that second woman slowly swallowed the first, as she got pulled into my brother’s addiction. It’s hard to say where her own unhappiness ended and my brother’s suffering began, but together they drifted into a parallel reality, cut off from the world beyond their heads. There was no funeral for this. No condolence cards. No flowers. No phone calls. No leave of absence. What was I supposed to mourn?

然而，我对他们的感受——至今仍有的感受——带着一种无可错认的哀悼的重量。没有死亡，也能有哀伤吗？我渐渐认为，我们哀伤的次数远比我们意识到的多，而死亡只是哀伤最显眼的场合，并非它的本质。如果这与你的经验不符，也许是因为我们很少叫出其他那些失去的名字。我们失去的绝大多数东西，来时并无葬礼相随。这正是人的处境：我们是生来注定依恋的造物，却活在一个无一物长驻的世界。迟早，我们会失去所在意的东西。何况我们又是复杂的存在——有身体、有心智、有社群、有环境，爱着许多不同的事物——可失去的东西也就格外多：从身体健康到自我认同，从关系、故土，到我们与其他物种共享的自然世界。对像我们这样的造物而言，心碎无从绕开：活着，就是失去。哀伤是爱在对象消失之后的所作所为。它是无处安放的爱。

And yet what I felt for them, what I still feel, has the unmistakable gravity of mourning. Can there be grief without death? I have come to think that we grieve much more often than we realise, and that death is grief’s most visible occasion rather than its essence. If that doesn’t match your experience, it may be because we rarely call these other losses by their name. Most of our losses arrive without a funeral. That is the human predicament: we are creatures built to attach, living in a world where nothing stays. Sooner or later, we lose the things we care about. And since we are complex beings with a body, a mind, a community and an environment – beings who love many different things – there is a great deal we can lose, from our physical health to our sense of who we are, from relationships to homelands to the natural world we share with other species. For creatures like us, there is no way around heartbreak: to live is to lose. Grief is what love does when its object is gone. It is love with nowhere to go.

过去几年，我积攒了许多这样的失去。有一种心理上的失去：我所认识、所深爱的那个父亲——一场凶险的中风夺走了他行走与骑车的能力，而骑车，用他自己的话说，是唯一能让「他的思绪变得流动」的事。有旧日友谊的失去：起初，你们只是不再参与彼此的生活，仅剩互通近况；然后某一天，连主动开口都变得尴尬。还有一种学者身份的缓慢消融，那是我用一生中最有生命力的岁月搭建起来的。它是一份真正的志业，一种教我缓慢而审慎地思考的心智生活。但我也渐渐明白，它同样是一种活法：安全地退后一步，与真实生活发生之处保持距离。正从我身上溜走的，是一个未来——在那个未来里，「学术哲学家」本可以完整回答「我是谁」这个问题。

Over the past few years I have accumulated many such losses. There is the psychological loss of my father as I knew and loved him: a brutal stroke rendered him incapable of walking and cycling, and cycling was the only thing that could ‘make his thoughts liquid’, as he used to say. There is the loss of old friendships. First you are no longer part of each other’s lives – you just update one another about your life – and then, one day, it becomes awkward to reach out at all. There is the gradual softening of an academic identity I’d spent the most vital years of my life constructing. It was a genuine vocation, a life of the mind that taught me to think slowly and carefully. But it was also, as I came to realise, a way of living safely at one remove from where actual life takes place. What is slipping away is a future in which being an academic philosopher would be the whole answer to the question of who I am.

还有那个女人，她的沉静、清澈与寡言，集中体现了自搬到挪威（Norway）以来我如此喜爱的北欧人的品质。像一个有魔力的陌生人，她不期而至地走进我的生活；可与此同时，她的到来又那么自然，她的在场那么理所当然，仿佛她从来就在那里。第一次见面持续了将近二十四小时，事后我在日记里写道：Ze is gekomen om te blijven——「她是来留下的。」她确实留下了，度过了九个月浓烈的时光。她试着教我滑雪。我们一起听尼尔斯·弗拉姆（Nils Frahm）的音乐，那音乐让她想起融雪。而她让我看到，最深切的亲密，发生在词语与词语之间的空隙里。

There was the woman whose calmness, clarity and silence embodied the qualities I have come to like so much about the Nordics since moving to Norway. Like a magical stranger, she entered my life unexpectedly, but at the same time her arrival felt so natural, her presence so obvious, that it was as if she had always been there. After our first meeting, which lasted almost 24 hours, I wrote in my journal: Ze is gekomen om te blijven. ‘She had come to stay.’ And she did, for nine intense months. She tried to teach me to ski. We listened to the music of Nils Frahm, which reminded her of melting snow. And she made me see that the deepest form of intimacy happens in the space between the words.

然而，尽管我们的联结如此之深，她还是走了，猝然决绝，仿佛从未出现过。她爱上了另一个男人。我被留在原地，恍惚而失去方向。尽管心已破碎，这种失去却没有一个名字，配得上我所碎裂的程度。它「不过是一次分手」，甚至连这个名字都不配：我们一直在约会，但严格说来，它从未成为一段正式的关系。它还是一段与另一段浪漫关系并行的联结——那段长久的关系，经双方同意、出于清醒的设计而保持开放。这一切在周围大多数人眼里都难以索解。我几乎是独自面对这份哀伤。没有脚本，没有仪式，没有公认的形式，没有正当性，没有共通的语言，没有办法弄明白发生在我身上的究竟是什么。

But despite the depth of our bond, she left, abruptly, as if she had never been there. She had fallen in love with another man. I was left behind, dazed and disoriented, and although my heart was broken, this kind of loss did not have a name that matched how shattered I felt. It was ‘just a breakup’, and it didn’t even deserve that name: we had been dating, but it never technically became a relationship. It was also a romantic connection that ran alongside another one, a long partnership that was open by mutual agreement and conscious design. Much about it wasn’t legible to most people around me. I faced the grief largely by myself. No scripts, no rituals, no recognised form, no legitimacy, no shared language, no way to make sense of what had happened to me.

让这段经历如此令人迷失的部分原因，在于一种对哀伤的狭隘理解。在心理学、哲学以及整个西方文化中，哀伤几乎被排他地视为对所爱之人死亡的反应。这是一种干净齐整的理解。最负盛名的哀伤回忆录——想想琼·狄迪恩（Joan Didion）的《奇想之年》（The Year of Magical Thinking，2005）与C·S·刘易斯（C S Lewis）的《卿卿如晤》（A Grief Observed，1961）——写的都是人生伴侣的失去。而迈克尔·乔尔比（Michael Cholbi）的《哀伤：一部哲学指南》（Grief: A Philosophical Guide，2021）——对该情绪最详尽的哲学论述之一——关注的也主要是我们在所爱之人、或对我们别具意义之人死去时的哀伤。

Part of what made the experience so disorienting was a narrow idea of what grief is. In psychology, in philosophy and in our Western culture at large, grief has been treated almost exclusively as a response to the death of someone we love. It’s a neat, tidy understanding. The most celebrated grief memoirs – think of The Year of Magical Thinking (2005) by Joan Didion and A Grief Observed (1961) by C S Lewis – are concerned with the loss of a life partner. And Michael Cholbi’s Grief: A Philosophical Guide (2021) – one of the most extensive philosophical treatments of the emotion – focuses mostly on the grief we experience at the death of someone we love or who otherwise matters to us.

死亡可见而终局：它有一具躯体、一个日期、一纸医学证明。其余的失去则模糊得多：没有实体对象，没有明确的开始或结束，没有它们确实发生过的有形证据。我们还为死亡准备了一整套基础设施：葬礼、慰问卡、鲜花、送上门的砂锅菜、丧假——一整套社会脚本。也许正是这套基础设施定义了什么算数：若没有社会与文化的脚手架来消化这段经验，那它想必就算不上真正的哀伤。

Death is visible and final: it has a body, a date, a medical certificate. The other losses are blurrier: they have no physical object, no clear beginning or end, no tangible proof they happened. We also have an infrastructure for death: funerals, condolence cards, flowers, casseroles, bereavement leave – a whole social script. Perhaps the infrastructure defines what counts: if there is no social and cultural scaffolding to digest the experience, it must not be real grief.

然而，之所以一心一意聚焦于丧亲，首要原因恐怕只在于：丧亲是范围更广、程度有别的情绪现象中最刺目的实例。但把哀伤限定于丧亲，就好比把疼痛限定于丛集性头痛，把焦虑限定于恐慌发作，把悲伤限定于抑郁症，把快乐限定于极乐狂喜——这是拿某样东西最极端的实例，当作它的本质。

The prime reason for the single-minded focus on death loss, however, must be that bereavement is simply the most striking instance of a broader emotional phenomenon that comes in degrees. But restricting grief to bereavement is like restricting pain to cluster headaches, anxiety to panic attacks, sadness to depression, or pleasure to ecstasy – it is taking the most extreme instantiation of something and making it the essence of that thing.

如果哀伤不只是关于死亡，它究竟关于什么？这些形形色色的哀伤，彼此有什么共通之处？要回答这个问题，一个好的起点是注意到：每一种情绪都有它特属的所关对象。恐惧关乎危险或威胁；愤怒关乎冒犯或不义；幸福与快乐关乎繁盛，关乎自我与世界之间的和谐。那么哀伤关乎什么？如果你回答「死亡」，你就在做一件奇怪的事：你在用触发情绪的那个特定事物来定义情绪，而不是用那个事物对我们的意义来定义它。用哲学的行话说，你犯了把情绪的「质料对象」与「形式对象」混为一谈的错误。怕鲨鱼、怕公开演讲、怕高处，并不是三种不同的情绪——鲨鱼恐惧、演讲恐惧、高处恐惧。三者之所以都算恐惧，是因为它们都关乎危险：它们的目的，都是告诉你有东西正在构成威胁。

If grief isn’t just about death, what is it about? What do all these cases of grief share? A good way to answer this question starts from the observation that every emotion has a characteristic thing it is about. Fear is about danger or threat; anger is about offence or injustice; happiness and joy are about flourishing or harmony between self and world. What is grief about? If you say ‘death’, you are doing something weird: you are defining the emotion by the particular thing that sets it off, rather than by what that thing means to us. In technical philosophical jargon, you are making the mistake of conflating an emotion’s material object with its formal object. Fear of sharks, fear of public speaking and fear of heights aren’t three different emotions – shark-fear, public-speaking-fear and heights-fear. All three count as fear because they are about danger: their aim is to tell you something is threatening.

哀伤显然与失去有关。这让我们向前走了一段，却还不够远。单纯的失去是必要条件，但非充分条件。如果我丢了一支笔或一件旧毛衣，我不会经历任何哀伤；若说有什么情绪反应，也不过是一丝轻微的恼意掠过。也许应当补上一句：要有哀伤，我们失去的必须是所在意或所爱的东西。但这同样必要而非充分。如果我弄丢一本真心喜欢的书，或没能得到梦寐以求的工作，我体会到的是挫败或失望，也许是难过，但不是哀伤。

Grief clearly has something to do with loss. Although this gets us somewhere, it doesn’t get us far enough. Mere loss is necessary but not sufficient. If I lose a pen or an old sweater, I am not experiencing any grief. If I have any emotional reaction at all, it will be a slight ripple of annoyance. Perhaps we should add that for there to be grief we must lose something we care about or love, but, again, this is necessary but not sufficient. If I lose a book I really like or don’t get a dream job I applied for, what I experience is frustration or disappointment, sadness perhaps, but not grief.

让哀伤卓然不同的，是失去侵入生命肌理之深：哀伤始于身份被打断之处。哀伤中所失去的，不只是某个具体的历史对象，而是一簇（面向未来的）可能性，它们被紧紧织入你的身份感、生命结构或经验世界。当它们消逝，你失去的不只是某样东西：你失去的是自己的一个版本，你的生活被整个翻转，而且你在一种实在的意义上，开始栖居于一个不同的世界。这就是为什么死亡是哀伤最极端的实例，而非唯一的实例。当所爱之人死去，一切可能性——不只是我们与他们共同的可能性，还有他们自身的可能性——尽数湮灭。正如约克大学（University of York）的两位哲学学者马修·拉特克利夫（Matthew Ratcliffe）与路易丝·理查森（Louise Richardson）在论文《对非死亡性失去的哀伤：一种现象学视角》（Grief Over Non-Death Losses: A Phenomenological Perspective，2023）中所言：

What sets grief apart is how deeply the loss reaches into the fabric of your life: grief begins where your identity is disrupted. What is lost in the case of grief is not just some concrete, historical object, but a cluster of (future) possibilities that are tightly woven into your sense of identity, life structure or experiential world. When they go, you don’t just lose something: you lose a version of yourself, your life is turned upside down, and you come to inhabit, in a real sense, a different world. This is why death is grief’s most extreme instance, rather than its only one. When someone we love dies, every possibility – not only our own possibilities with them, but their possibilities too – is wiped out. As Matthew Ratcliffe and Louise Richardson, academic philosophers at the University of York, put it in their paper ‘Grief Over Non-Death Losses: A Phenomenological Perspective’ (2023):

这种重组并不仅仅发生在认知层面。它还栖身于许多别处：在身体里（那只仍会伸向手机、想给不再回复你的人发消息的手），在你体验周遭环境的方式里（你们曾一同光顾、如今却显得陌生甚至含有敌意的咖啡馆），或是在你的自传式记忆里（那个只有你们才懂、在别人面前就是落不下来的玩笑）。

This reorganisation is not merely cognitive. It lives in many other places: in the body (the hand that still reaches for the phone to text someone who no longer answers your messages), in how you experience your surroundings (the café you used to visit together that now feels alien or even hostile), or in your autobiographical memories (the shared joke that simply doesn’t land with other people).

我的母亲还活着。没有人死去：并没有一个具体的历史对象被拽离我的掌握。但那一整束曾被织入我生命与世界结构的可能性——全都系于「有一个在场的、有回应的、足够好的母亲」——已经消逝了。我再也无法打电话给她，去探一探她那颗头脑，那颗异常自由、又异常深沉的头脑。

My mother is still alive. No one has died: there is no concrete, historical object that has been pulled beyond my grasp. But a whole set of possibilities that had been woven into the structure of my life and world, all bound up with having a mother who was present, responsive, good enough – those are gone. It has become impossible to call her and poke that mind of hers, so unusually free and unusually deep.

我父亲也是如此。是的，他从那场中风中活了下来。但那个不安分的头脑需要靠骑车才能让思绪变得流动的男人——我与那个男人的未来，在法国（France）南部结伴骑行的旅程，那些需要反复而循环的踩踏才能活过来的对话——那个未来被取消了。同样的结构也适用于那个女人：她的消失，让一个我刚刚寻得的、家一般温暖的经验世界轰然坍塌；也适用于我用将近二十年搭建起来的学者身份。当别的志业开始越来越急切地召唤，那种独一的、吞没一切的在世方式——思考者、过心智生活的人、未来将围绕阅读与写作组织起来的人——渐渐松开了它的掌控。

The same goes for my father. Sure, he survived the stroke. But the man whose restless mind needed to cycle to make his thoughts liquid – the future I had with that man, the shared cycling trips in the south of France, the conversations that needed repetitive, cyclical movement to come alive – that future was negated. The same structure holds for the woman whose disappearance collapsed an experiential world that felt like a home I had only just found, and for the academic identity I had built over almost two decades. As other vocations began to call more insistently, a single, all-consuming way of being in the world – the thinker, the life of the mind, the person whose future would be organised around reading and writing – loosened its hold.

不过，最能证成这种哀伤观的案例，是一种根本不存在具体失去对象的情况。拉特克利夫与理查森的核心例子是非自愿的无子状态——为一个从未存在过的孩子而哀伤：一个曾被想象、被期盼、被深深织入自我感的未来，却从未成为现实。在这里，没有人死去，也没有人离开。没有任何有形之物可供指认：没有坟墓，没有周年忌日，没有登记在册的名字，没有追思会，没有共同的习俗来为这份失去赋予言词与意义。然而，正如研究中一位受访者所说：「没有人能理解背负这种失去是什么滋味。在他们心里，没有孩子，就意味着没有失去。」另一位受访者径直叫出了哀伤的名字：「我在哀悼那些我曾想象、并相信自己会拥有却终究无法拥有的、未来的孩子们。」第三位的说法则令人心寒：「一个未来生命的死亡。」

The case that makes this way of thinking about grief most compelling, though, is one where there is no concrete object of loss at all. Ratcliffe and Richardson’s central example is involuntary childlessness – grief for a child who never was, a future that was imagined, anticipated and deeply woven into one’s sense of self, but that never materialised. Here, no one died and no one left. There is nothing tangible to point to: no grave, no anniversary, no recorded name, no memorial service, no shared practices to give words or meaning to the loss. And yet, as one respondent in their study put it: ‘No one can understand what it’s like to carry this loss. In their minds, no baby meant no loss.’ Another respondent simply called their grief by its name: ‘I am grieving the future children I imagined and believed I would have but am unable to.’ And a third spoke, chillingly, of ‘the death of a future life’.

他们缺乏解释性的资源，去理解并分享自己经验中那些重要的部分

They lack the interpretative resources to understand and share significant aspects of their experience

「没有孩子，就意味着没有失去。」许多形式的非死亡性失去，正是家庭治疗师葆琳·博斯（Pauline Boss）所说的「模糊性失去」（ambiguous loss）。这类失去没有清晰的边界，没有确定的开始或结束——有的情形是人在身体上在场、心理上已然离去；有的则相反，身体已离去，心理上仍驻留。想想一个自我被疾病抹除的阿尔茨海默病患者，或一名作战失踪的士兵。正因为许多非死亡性失去是模糊的，随之而来的哀伤得不到公开承认、社会确认或公共哀悼——这正是哀伤研究学者肯尼斯·多卡（Kenneth Doka）所说的「被剥夺权利的哀伤」（disenfranchised grief）。

‘No baby meant no loss.’ Many forms of non-death loss are what the family therapist Pauline Boss calls ‘ambiguous loss’. These are losses without clear boundaries, without a definite beginning or end – cases where someone is physically present but psychologically gone, or physically gone but psychologically still there. Think of an Alzheimer’s patient whose self has been erased by the disease or of a soldier missing in action. Because many non-death losses are ambiguous, the grief that follows them is not openly acknowledged, socially validated or publicly mourned – what the grief scholar Kenneth Doka has called ‘disenfranchised grief’.

那些因失去本身模糊而被剥夺哀伤权利的人，缺少的不只是脚本、仪式或传统。他们缺乏解释性的资源，去理解并分享自己经验中那些重要的部分。当我们缺少概念来命名「作为我们是怎么回事」、命名「我们正在经历什么」时，我们便在遭受哲学家米兰达·弗里克（Miranda Fricker）所说的「解释学不正义」（hermeneutical injustice）——一种无力使自身经验对自己、对他人变得可理解的处境。这并非因为我们糊涂了，而是因为共有的语言或概念装置——我们借以理解自身的那些框架——还没有跟上。正如拉特克利夫与理查森的两位受访者所说：「我过了好一阵子才明白，我的感受就是哀伤」；「直到遇到处境相似的人，我才意识到这就是哀伤。」

Those whose grief is disenfranchised because their losses are ambiguous don’t just lack scripts or rituals or traditions. They lack the interpretative resources to understand and share significant aspects of their experience. When we lack the concepts to name what it is like to be us, what it is we are going through, we suffer what the philosopher Miranda Fricker calls a ‘hermeneutical injustice’ – an inability to make our own experience intelligible to ourselves and to others. This is not because we are confused, but because the shared language or conceptual apparatus – the frameworks we use to make sense of ourselves – hasn’t caught up. As two of Ratcliffe and Richardson’s respondents put it: ‘It took me a while to understand that what I was feeling was grief’; ‘I never realised this was grief until I found others in a similar situation.’

同样的事也发生在我身上：当那位有魔力的陌生人消失时，我的失去是模糊的——她还存在于世上，我们从未正式在一起，我还有另一位伴侣——于是我的哀伤躲了起来。在一种把关系结束看作「不过是一次分手」、把一夫一妻奉为不成文规则的文化里，我既缺乏消化哀伤的仪式，也缺乏把它概念化、说出口的解释学资源。用哲学家查尔斯·泰勒（Charles Taylor）的话说，对我们这类「自我诠释的动物」而言，结果就是：我们坠入黑暗，成了自己的陌生人。

The same happened to me: when the magical stranger disappeared, my loss was ambiguous – she still existed, we were never officially together, I had another partner – and my grief went into hiding. In a culture where the end of a relationship is ‘just a breakup’ and monogamy is the unspoken rule, I lacked the rituals to digest the grief and the hermeneutical resources to conceptualise and communicate what had happened to me. For ‘self-interpreting animals’ like us, in the words of the philosopher Charles Taylor, the result is that we end up in the dark, as strangers to ourselves.

把哀伤窄化为丧亲之痛，绝不止于一个概念错误。它造成真实的伤害。作家兼哀伤咨询师斯蒂芬·莱文（Stephen Levine）所说的「无人照料的悲伤」不会消散，而是转入地下，藏进阴影，日后以意想不到的症状重新浮出水面。这些我都经历过。胸口一种机械般的发紧，肚子硬邦邦地绷着，像套着一副挽具来抵住生活。撕扯人的梦。试图麻痹那些独自去感受就太过疼痛的东西。而最糟的，是犬儒之心，以及对信任的缓慢侵蚀——不只是对他人的信任，还有对自己感受的信任，对任何一段新联结的深度、可持续性与未来的直觉。当身边没有人认出你的哀伤，你便开始怀疑：当初感受到它，是不是本来就错了。

The narrowing of grief to bereavement is more than a conceptual mistake. It does real damage. What the writer and grief counsellor Stephen Levine called ‘unattended sorrow’ doesn’t dissolve but goes underground, hiding in the shadows, later resurfacing as unexpected symptoms. I have been there. A mechanical tightness in the chest and a hard belly, like wearing a harness to brace against life. Harrowing dreams. Attempts to numb what is too painful to feel alone. And, worst of all, cynicism and a slow erosion of trust. Not just in other people, but in your own feelings, your own intuitions about the depth, sustainability and future of any new connection. When no one around you recognises your grief, you begin to wonder whether you were wrong to feel it at all.

西格蒙德·弗洛伊德（Sigmund Freud）明白这一点，尽管他把它读成了病理。在《哀悼与忧郁》（Mourning and Melancholia，1917）一文中，他主张哀伤是健康且正常的，但前提是它可理解、有边界。他认为，哀悼的任务是把你从失去的对象那里松开，把你的情感能量重新投注到别处。倘若这项任务无法完成——倘若失去本身不清楚、无以名状或含混不明——你就不再是在哀悼，而是患上了名为忧郁症的疾病。在弗洛伊德看来，无法完成其工作的哀伤，会钙化成某种更幽暗、也更难以捉摸的东西。

Sigmund Freud understood this, though he read it as pathology. In the essay ‘Mourning and Melancholia’ (1917), he contended that grief is healthy and normal, but only if it is intelligible and bounded. The job of mourning, he argued, is to detach you from the lost object and reinvest your emotional energy elsewhere. If you can’t complete that job, if the loss is unclear or unnamed or ambiguous, you are no longer mourning. Instead, you are suffering from an illness called melancholia. For Freud, grief that cannot do its work calcifies into something darker, something more elusive.

但伤害比这更深。当我们无法为一场失去哀伤时，我们还会随之失去另一样东西：确认「什么对我们要紧」的机会。哀伤，是我们借着所爱之物的缺席，得以知晓自己爱过什么的方式。或者，用心理治疗师、哀伤工作者弗朗西斯·韦勒（Francis Weller）在《悲伤的野性边缘》（The Wild Edge of Sorrow，2015）中的说法：

But the damage runs deeper than that. When we cannot grieve a loss, we lose something else along with it: the chance to register what mattered to us. Grief is how we come to know what we loved in the presence of its absence. Or as the psychotherapist and grief worker Francis Weller puts it in The Wild Edge of Sorrow (2015):

你的哀伤告诉你：你曾有勇气去爱，你曾容许某个人、某样东西进入「你存在的核心，并在你心里找到一个家」。如果你剥夺一个人哀伤的权利，你就剥夺了他通往所爱与所珍之物的入口，也就是说，你剥夺了他通往自身的入口。如此一来，你从他们身上夺走的就不只是他们的爱，还有他们的自知。

Your grief tells you that you had the courage to love, that you allowed someone or something else to enter ‘the core of [your] being and find a home in [your] heart’. If you deny someone the right to grieve, you deny them access to what they loved and valued, which is to say that you deny them access to themselves. You don’t just rob them, then, of their love; you also rob them of self-knowledge.

我大半生所置身其中的那个传统——追问「人应当如何生活」的西方思想家谱系——是我寻求理解自身遭遇时最先转向的地方。关于哀伤，这些思想家说过很多，但多半带着怀疑与否定。柏拉图（Plato）相信，好人是所有人中最自足的，也最不易被失去动摇。在《斐多篇》（Phaedo）里，哲学尚未开场，哭喊着的克珊提帕（Xanthippe）——苏格拉底（Socrates）的妻子——就被带了出去；而在对话接近尾声处，苏格拉底还斥责哭泣的朋友们，说他们像女人一样。斯多葛派中也能找到相似的情绪。爱比克泰德（Epictetus）在《手册》（Enchiridion）中，把丧妻失子比作一只罐子被打碎。而失去过自己许多孩子的马可·奥勒留（Marcus Aurelius），则在《沉思录》（Meditations）中提醒自己：不要祈祷让孩子留下，而要祈祷让自己不再害怕失去他。

The tradition I spent most of my life in – the lineage of Western thinkers who asked how a human being should live – was the first place I looked to understand what was happening to me. These thinkers had a lot to say about grief, but most of it was suspicious and sceptical. Plato believed a good man is the most self-sufficient of all, and least shaken by loss. In the Phaedo, Socrates’ wife Xanthippe is led away wailing before the philosophy can begin, and towards the end of the dialogue, Socrates rebukes his weeping friends for behaving like women. A similar sentiment can be found among the Stoics. Epictetus, in the Enchiridion, compares the loss of a wife or child to a jug breaking. And Marcus Aurelius, who lost many of his own children, reminds himself in his Meditations not to pray that he might keep his child, but to pray that he might not fear losing him.

在这种思考方式的背后，是一个代价高昂的信念：依恋是一种负债，而一个智慧的人——一个已经想明白如何过上最好生活的人——不需要任何可能被夺走的东西。如果哀伤是无处安放的爱，那么一个把这种爱当作错误的传统，从一开始就不会知道该拿它怎么办。

Behind this way of thinking lies an expensive belief: that attachment is a liability, and that a wise person – someone who has figured out how to live the best possible life – does not need anything that can be taken away from him. If grief is love with nowhere to go, a tradition that treated such love as an error was never going to know what to do with it.

基督教保留了古人已经得出的判决。在《忏悔录》（Confessions，公元397至400年）中，圣奥古斯丁（Saint Augustine）回望挚友死后自己的溃败，将其诊断为一场灵性上的过失：「若不是我把灵魂倾倒在了沙地上，爱一个终有一死的人，仿佛他永不死去，那悲伤又为何能如此轻易、如此深地刺入我？」巴鲁赫·斯宾诺莎（Baruch Spinoza）——以任何常规意义而言都谈不上信教——得出了同样的结论。他在《伦理学》（Ethics，1677）中写道，悲伤是心灵向较小完满的过渡，是行动力量的一次折损。这意味着它永远不可能本身即为善，而它典型的来源，是对易逝之物爱得过多。哀伤的解药——这一点让他彻底回到了古代的理智主义传统——是理解：当我们豁然明白，所失去之物从来就不可能留住，哀伤自会松开。

Christianity kept the verdict the ancients had arrived at. In his Confessions (397-400 CE), Saint Augustine looks back on his devastation after a friend’s death, and diagnoses it as a spiritual mistake: ‘Why had that grief penetrated me so easily and so deeply, if not because I had poured out my soul onto the sand, loving someone who would die as if he never would?’ Baruch Spinoza – not religious in any conventional sense of the word – reached the same conclusion. Sadness, he writes in his Ethics (1677), is the mind’s move to a lesser perfection, a loss of its power to act. This means it can never be a good in itself, and its typical source is too much love for things that are impermanent. The cure for grief – and this puts him squarely back into the ancient intellectualist tradition – is understanding: the moment it dawns on us that what we lost could never have been kept, our grief will relax.

「自己的一部分已然失去」这种感受，如今作为障碍的标记，出现在精神医学最重要的手册里

The sense that part of oneself has been lost now appears in psychiatry’s most important manual as a mark of disorder

阿图尔·叔本华（Arthur Schopenhauer）与他之前的许多人都不同：他并不鄙夷哀伤。在《作为意志和表象的世界》（The World as Will and Representation，1818）中，他看重悲苦，理由只有一个：巨大的个人苦难能折断意志，通向自我否定（Selbstverneinung）。如此说来，哀伤值得一有，是因为它有力量治好我们对这个世界的欲求本身——这意味着，归根结底，他与前人相去不远。对他们所有人而言，善好人生，就是松开我们系于易逝与脆弱之物的依恋。

Arthur Schopenhauer, unlike many before him, did not despise grief. In The World as Will and Representation (1818), he values sorrow for one reason only: great personal suffering breaks the will and leads to self-renunciation (Selbstverneinung). Grief is worth having, then, because it has the power to cure us of wanting the world at all, which means that, in the end, he is not so far from the thinkers who came before him. A good life, for all of them, is a matter of loosening our attachments to what is impermanent and fragile.

发端于哲学家的学说，渗进了更广大的文化。人类学家杰弗里·戈勒（Geoffrey Gorer）在其里程碑式的研究《当代英国的死亡、哀伤与哀悼》（Death, Grief, and Mourning in Contemporary Britain，1965）中发现，哀悼日益被看作「一种软弱、一种自我放纵、一种应受指摘的坏习惯，而非一种心理必需」。1970年代，历史学家菲利普·阿里耶斯（Philippe Ariès）在工业化西方各地追溯出同一场转变：死亡，这位旧日的熟客，变得可耻，成了要藏起来的东西。与此同时，医学也加入了这场论争。1961年，精神科医生乔治·恩格尔（George Engel）半带挑衅地问道：哀伤是一种疾病吗？2000年，哲学家斯蒂芬·威尔金森（Stephen Wilkinson）给出了答案：是。在学术哲学界的一份顶尖期刊上，他论证说，即便是正常、健康的哀伤，也符合精神障碍的标准定义，因而够格算作一种精神障碍：像所有精神障碍一样，它包含显著的痛苦，并中断正常的功能。2013年，《精神障碍诊断与统计手册》（Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders）第五版删除了一项条款，该条款此前一直把新近丧亲者排除在重度抑郁症的诊断之外。随后在2022年，修订版新增了延长哀伤障碍（prolonged grief disorder），其可能症状包括身份紊乱，例如感到自己的一部分已经死去。「自己的一部分已然失去」这种感受，如今作为障碍的标记，出现在精神医学最重要的手册里——不过，很说明问题的是，只有在确实有人死去时才作数。

What began as a doctrine of philosophers leaked into the wider culture. In his landmark study Death, Grief, and Mourning in Contemporary Britain (1965), the anthropologist Geoffrey Gorer found that mourning was increasingly seen as ‘a weakness, a self-indulgence, a reprehensible bad habit instead of as a psychological necessity’. In the 1970s, the historian Philippe Ariès traced the same shift across the industrial West: death, once familiar, had become shameful, something to be hidden. Meanwhile, medicine had entered the debate. In 1961, the psychiatrist George Engel asked, half provocatively, whether grief was a disease. In 2000, the philosopher Stephen Wilkinson answered: yes. In one of academic philosophy’s leading journals, he argued that even normal, healthy grief qualifies as a mental disorder because it meets the standard definition of one. Like all mental disorders, it involves significant distress and interrupts normal functioning. In 2013, the fifth edition of the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders removed the clause that had excluded the recently bereaved from receiving a diagnosis of major depressive disorder. Then, in 2022, the revised edition added prolonged grief disorder, whose possible symptoms include identity disruption, such as the feeling that part of oneself has died. The sense that part of oneself has been lost now appears in psychiatry’s most important manual as a mark of disorder – though, tellingly, only when someone has actually died.

但西方传统把这件事弄反了。压制、根除、否认或将哀伤病理化，守护不了善好人生；它切断我们与所爱之物的联结，恰恰暗中破坏了人的繁盛。更进一步，它切断了我们彼此。被命名、被吐露的哀伤，是可以被迎住的哀伤，是可以放在你与另一个人之间的哀伤，让你们俩一同看着它、一同捧住它。这种共有的脆弱，让一个人得以在共同的人性中认出另一个人。正如韦勒提醒我们的：哀伤从来就不该独自背负；它本该由一整个村庄来捧持、来消化。剥夺它的名字，你就剥夺了人们在失去中被看见的可能，剥夺了他们发现那道自以为独有的伤口，其实也为身边之人所共有。

But the Western tradition had it backwards. Suppressing, eradicating, denying or pathologising grief doesn’t protect the good life; it sabotages human flourishing by cutting us off from the things we love. More than that, it cuts us off from each other. Grief named and disclosed is grief that can be met, grief that can be placed between you and another person, where the two of you can look at it and hold it together. That shared vulnerability lets one person recognise another in their common humanity. Grief, as Weller reminds us, was never meant to be carried alone; it was meant to be held and processed by a whole village. Strip it of its name and you strip people of the possibility of being seen in their loss, of discovering that the wound they thought was theirs alone is in fact shared by those around them.

最近在荷兰（the Netherlands），我和父亲一起参观了克勒勒-米勒博物馆（Kröller-Müller Museum）——就是那位未来被中风取消了的父亲。我从远处看他：这个曾把生活整个建立在骑行之上的男人，骑到过法国、罗马（Rome），骑到过西班牙（Spain）的圣地亚哥-德孔波斯特拉（Santiago de Compostela）——如今佝偻在他的助行器后面。我们一起站在文森特·梵高（Vincent van Gogh）的画作《在永恒之门》（At Eternity's Gate，1890）前：一个老人，脸埋进双拳，分明在受苦。一个悲伤的男人，看着一个悲伤的男人，看着一个悲伤的男人，被另一个悲伤的男人画了下来。在这幅画绘成的许多年前，当梵高第一次为一张石版画描下同一个形象时，他写道：那样一位老人的表情里，可以有一种「难以言喻的动人」，某种「珍贵的东西、高贵的东西」。

Recently, while in the Netherlands, I visited the Kröller-Müller Museum with my father, the same father whose future was negated by a stroke. I looked at him from a distance, the man who once built his life around cycling – to France, to Rome, to Spain’s Santiago de Compostela – now hunched behind his rollator. We stood together in front of Vincent van Gogh’s painting At Eternity’s Gate (1890): an old man, face buried in his fists, clearly hurting. A sorrowful man, looking at a sorrowful man, looking at a sorrowful man painted by a sorrowful man. Years before he painted it, when he first drew the same figure for a lithograph, Van Gogh wrote that there can be an ‘unutterably moving’ quality, ‘something precious, something noble’, in the expression of an old man like that.

我不知道父亲和我一样深爱着梵高。我们在那里站了很久——不知怎么，触到了哀伤的魔力——某种东西在我们之间打开了，它没有名字，却让人觉得重要，纤毫毕现地活着。我们没有谈我的母亲，没有谈这个家如何一寸寸散了架，没有谈中风从他身上拿走了什么。我们不必谈。那幅画，说出了我们谁也说不出的东西。

I didn’t know my father loved Van Gogh as much as I do. We stood there for a long time – somehow in contact with the magic of grief – and something opened up between us that had no name but felt important, exquisitely alive. We didn’t talk about my mother, or how our family had unravelled, or what the stroke had taken from him. We didn’t have to. The painting said what neither of us could.
