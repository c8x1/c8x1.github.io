为什么没有摩擦的世界，就是一个没有进步的世界

Jennifer R Vail·Aeon·2026年10月27日·9分钟阅读·原文

在交谈中，很少有词能比「摩擦」更快抓住我的注意力——尤其是那些要把一切变得「无摩擦」的谈论。从职场到买菜购物，我们的社会似乎正竭力把摩擦从万事万物中清除出去。其动机似乎在于：阻力越少，生活就越轻松顺滑。

In conversations, few words catch my attention faster than ‘friction’ – and especially talk of making things frictionless. From the workplace to grocery shopping, our societies seem to be striving to remove friction from everything. The motivation appears to be that decreasing resistance makes life easier and smoother.

作为一名摩擦学家（tribologist），我的职业生涯都以科学方式研究摩擦、磨损与润滑，对此我有些自己的看法。我承认，在职业生涯的大部分时间里，我也一直在设法降低摩擦——开发新材料、新设计乃至新仪器，来降低喷气发动机中的摩擦，或减少你家汽车的排放。不过，我也曾为去除牙齿上的牙菌斑而琢磨如何增大摩擦。

Having spent a career as a tribologist, studying friction, wear and lubrication scientifically, I have some opinions on this. I’ll confess that, in most of my career, I too have sought to reduce friction, developing new materials, designs or even instrumentation in order to lower friction in a jet engine or reduce emissions from your car. However, I’ve also found myself trying to figure out how to increase friction to remove plaque on teeth.

事实是，摩擦尽管看似处处与人作对，却不可或缺，甚至颇有建设性。自人类第一次从石头上击出火星起，这种阻碍运动的力恰恰一直在推动我们前进。而这一悖论，恰好提供了一副透镜，让我们得以重新解读人类整部技术史。

The reality is that friction, despite seeming so antagonistic, is necessary and even productive. The force that resists motion has, since humans first struck a spark from a stone, been the very thing that propels us forward. And this paradox offers a lens through which to reread humanity’s entire technological history.

要领会对摩擦的理解曾帮助我们取得多少成就，就必须从头说起，讲清楚摩擦究竟是什么。我们把摩擦定义为阻碍运动的力，这意味着摩擦几乎无处不在。只要有东西在动——无论滑动还是滚动——就有摩擦。固体、液体、气体？无所谓。只要有运动，摩擦就在那儿阻碍它。遗憾的是，正因为摩擦的职责就是阻碍，我们把它当成了讨厌的累赘。但它是一种极其重要的力，人类正是凭借不断加深对它的理解才兴旺发达。到了20世纪60年代，在与摩擦相伴演化数千年之后，我们终于承认：摩擦是世界中如此关键的一环，值得为它专设一门科学——摩擦学（tribology）。

In order to appreciate all that an understanding of friction has helped us to accomplish, it’s necessary to start at square one and state what friction actually is. We define friction as the force that resists motion, and that means friction is rather ubiquitous. If things are moving, whether they’re sliding around or rolling, there’s friction. Solids, liquids, gases? Doesn’t matter. If there’s movement, friction is there to resist it. Unfortunately, because friction’s job is to resist, we’ve developed an aversion to it as a nuisance. But it’s a very important force, and we’ve thrived by expanding our understanding of it. By the 1960s, after millennia of evolving with friction, we finally accepted that friction is such a critical part of our world that a scientific field of study needed to be devoted to it: tribology.

像我这样的摩擦学家，研究的正是这种力的种种形态。摩擦学是研究相对运动的相互作用表面的科学，关注材料磨损、摩擦与润滑。说白了，摩擦学就是专门研究物体相互摩擦的学问。你或许会对一门专注于此的科学不以为然，但只要停下来想想我们体内、我们身边乃至整个宇宙之中正在发生多少运动，你就会体会到摩擦学的天地何其广阔。没有人比摩擦学家更清楚摩擦会惹出什么麻烦，但他们同样知道，随着我们对阻力理解的加深，随之而来的是怎样的成就。

Tribologists like me study this force in all its forms. Tribology is the science of interacting surfaces in relative motion, concerned with the wear of materials, friction and lubrication. Basically, tribology is the specialisation of things rubbing against each other. You may raise an eyebrow at a field of science focused on this, but when you pause to consider how much motion is going on inside us, around us and throughout the Universe, you can appreciate that tribology is vast. More than anyone, tribologists know the problems friction can cause. But they also know the achievements that have followed as we’ve come to understand resistance better.

尽管摩擦学成为一门正式学科还是相当晚近的事，摩擦塑造人类技术创新的历史却已十分久远。比如，不妨想想千百年来摩擦在人们从甲地到乙地的旅途中所扮演的关键角色。我敢打赌，每当你需要搬动笨重的家具、或是把那一大袋猫砂运回家时，你的本能就是找辆小推车来帮忙。早期人类也有过类似念头：借助圆木滚子来挪动重物，并由此想出了我们最伟大的发明之一——轮子。滚动之所以让移动物体更省力，是因为它减少了物体与其所压表面之间的摩擦。相比滑动，滚动过程中的表面相互作用要少得多。关于轮子可说的很多，比如直到2026年我们似乎仍无法就它的发明起源达成共识，但毫无疑问，它是应对摩擦的绝妙创新。可轮子同样离不开摩擦：当我们施力让轮子前进时，地面与轮子之间的力——静摩擦——防止轮子原地空转；任其空转，我们就哪儿也去不了。而一旦开始向前运动，阻碍运动的阻力就变成了动摩擦，它同样不可或缺。如果你在冰面上开过车，你就知道摩擦太少有多危险。

Although tribology as a formalised field is relatively modern, friction has long shaped humanity’s technological innovations. For example, consider its pivotal role in people’s journeys from A to B over the centuries. I’m willing to bet that whenever you need to move unwieldy furniture or carry home that giant bag of cat litter, your instinct is to find a cart to help you. Early humans had a similar thought, turning to log rollers to move heavy objects, and coming up with one of our greatest inventions: the wheel. Rolling makes it easier to move objects because it reduces friction between the object and surface you’re moving it against. There are far fewer surface interactions in the process of rolling versus sliding. Plenty can be said about the wheel, including the fact that even in 2026 we can’t seem to agree on the origin of its discovery, but there is zero doubt that it’s a brilliant innovation for dealing with friction. But wheels also need friction: when we exert a force to move a wheel forward, the force between the ground and the wheel – static friction – keeps it from spinning freely, which would result in us going nowhere. Once forward motion begins, the resistance to motion becomes dynamic friction, which is just as important. If you’ve driven on ice, you know the danger of too little friction.

然而，倘若人们没有在这些基本原理之上不断改进，那么即使是轮子，也会因摩擦而显得相当低效。

However, if people hadn’t continued improving on these basic principles, even the wheel would be rather inefficient due to friction.

维京人（Vikings）的长船把形状阻力降到最低，从而得以飞速破水前行

The Vikings’ longships minimised form drag so they could cut through the water quickly

古罗马人在他们的竞技场里发现了这一点。罗马战车竞赛并非全无烦恼，或者更确切地说，是脚上的烦恼。我敢肯定，车夫们多半抱怨过双脚发烫——那是车轮与车轴之间的摩擦把周围部件烤热所致。在抒情诗集《颂歌》（Odes）中，诗人贺拉斯（Horace）就描写过战车轮子在比赛中灼灼发光的情景。今天，科学家知道这种光来自运动能量向热能的转化。罗马设计师最终想出办法缓解这一难题：在车轮与车轴之间涂抹动物油脂（axungia）充当润滑脂。这么做时，他们运用的正是苏美尔人（Sumerians）早已开发利用的摩擦学知识：作为表面间的润滑剂，油脂比水更有效。然而，这又带来了新的难题：润滑脂一旦热到一定程度，就有起火的危险。解决办法？冷却液。具体而言，他们往战车上泼水——相当于古代版的进站维修。

The ancient Romans discovered this in their amphitheatres. Roman chariot races didn’t come without their headaches. Or rather, foot aches. I’m certain the drivers likely complained about their hot feet, caused by friction between the wheels and axle heating up the surrounding parts. In Odes, a collection of lyric poems, the poet Horace described how chariot wheels would glow during racing. Today, scientists know that this glow is from turning the energy of motion into thermal energy. Roman designers eventually figured out that they could temper the problem by putting axungia (animal fat) grease between the wheel and axle. By doing so, they were applying tribological knowledge already exploited by the Sumerians: that fats and oils work more effectively than water as lubrication between surfaces. However, that created a new conundrum: if the grease got hot enough, it risked catching fire. The solution? A coolant. In this case, they splashed water on the chariots, in the ancient equivalent of a pit stop.

条条大路通罗马（Rome），而你可以说，片片海洋通维京。维京人同样巧妙地适应了远洋航行途中遇到的阻力。在流体中运动会产生一种叫做「拖曳阻力」（drag）的摩擦，而拖曳阻力内部还可再细分：表皮阻力（skin drag）来自船体表面与水之间的摩擦，形状阻力（form drag）则源于船身破水而行时的外形。当需要安全运送货物时，维京人使用宽体坚固的船只。更宽的船体会增大形状阻力、拖慢航速，但换来的稳定性更为关键。而他们著名的长船则修长纤细，尽可能压低形状阻力，从而得以飞速破水前行。

While all roads led to Rome, you could say all seas led to the Vikings. They, too, cleverly adapted to the resistance that met them as they journeyed through the oceans. Moving through fluids produces a type of friction known as drag. Even within drag there are subsets. Skin drag is from the friction between the surface of the ship and the water, whereas form drag stems from the shape of the hull as it moves through the water. When they had to transport cargo safely, the Vikings used sturdy ships with wide hulls. This wider hull increased form drag and slowed them down, but the stability provided was more critical. Their famous longships, however, were long and slim, minimising form drag as much as possible so they could cut through the water quickly.

然而，正如一切涉及摩擦的情境一样，维京人也不得不在相互冲突的需求之间求取平衡。长船固然越快越好，但同样要紧的是，船绝不能被海浪打散。他们需要柔韧与坚固兼备，同时让船体尽量轻巧，好在水里浮得更高，以减小阻力。为此，维京人用了他们的「搭接」（clinker）工艺：用铆钉把长条木板钉合在一起。与更光洁平滑的设计相比，铆钉与叠接的木板确实会增加阻力，但正是一块块独立的木板，赋予了船体扭转与弯折的本领，得以扛过风暴。要是我正身处大洋中央的一次航行，我也乐意牺牲一点减阻效果，免得被突如其来的疯狗浪掀翻。

As with all friction scenarios, however, the Vikings found themselves having to balance some competing demands. Faster longships are better, yes, but it was also imperative that the ships not break apart into pieces from the waves. They needed flexibility and strength, while keeping the ships as light as possible to ride higher in the water to reduce drag. To do this, the Vikings used their ‘clinker’ technique of nailing together long planks of wood. The rivets and overlapping planks would increase drag compared with a more polished, smoother design but these individual planks gave the ships the ability to twist and flex to weather storms. If I was on a boat trip in the middle of the ocean, I too would be happy to sacrifice a little bit of drag reduction to avoid capsizing from a rogue wave.

看看今天的舟船舰艇便知，我们显然并没有把维京人的设计奉为航海技术的终极完满。同样，今天也没有人再驾着罗马战车到处跑了。此后，在如何量化摩擦、进而围绕摩擦进行设计方面，我们取得了惊人的飞跃，终于以科学的方式接近摩擦。20世纪20年代，克莱斯勒（Chrysler）的工程负责人卡尔·布里尔（Carl Breer）萌生了按空气动力学来设计汽车的念头：飞机似乎能毫无阻滞地划过空气，汽车为何不能如此？布里尔派工程师威廉·恩肖（William Earnshaw）去请教奥维尔·莱特（Orville Wright）本人，学习如何在风洞中设计汽车。由此诞生的泪滴形克莱斯勒「Airflow」被认定为一款败笔——许多颠覆性的设计在过于惊世骇俗、难以迎合口味时往往如此——但这场失败彻底革新了我们设计汽车的方式。风洞测试至今仍是标准做法，而摩擦的存在，就像空气本身一样理所当然。

Looking at boats and ships today, it’s clear that we didn’t just accept these Viking designs as the culmination of nautical perfection. Likewise, we aren’t driving around in Roman chariots today. We have since made incredible leaps forward in understanding how to quantify friction and thus design for it, finally approaching friction scientifically. In the 1920s, Chrysler’s head of engineering Carl Breer had the idea to design cars with aerodynamics in mind. Planes seemed to slice seamlessly through the air. Shouldn’t cars do the same? Breer tasked the engineer William Earnshaw to consult with none other than Orville Wright to learn how to design a car in a wind tunnel. The resulting teardrop-shaped Chrysler Airflow was considered a flop, as many disruptive designs can be when they’re just too jarring for taste, but that flop revolutionised how we design cars. Wind-tunnel testing remains a standard today, and friction’s presence is as expected as the air itself.

1937年款克莱斯勒Airflow在克莱斯勒沙龙（Chrysler Salon）展出。图片由美国国会图书馆（Library of Congress）提供

A 1937 Chrysler Airflow at the Chrysler Salon. Courtesy the Library of Congress

如今，摩擦学测试已是汽车行业的常规环节；从医疗到食品，各类行业里都散布着摩擦学实验室。走进这些实验室，你会看到各种仪器，它们能在从宏观尺度直至原子层面的范围内测量摩擦。我在佛罗里达大学（University of Florida）攻读博士的实验室里，摩擦仪（tribometer）可测的力程横跨三个数量级。我甚至还待过这样一间实验室：里面有一台专门为测试真实火车车轮与铁轨而建造的摩擦仪。摩擦是个出了名的挑剔角色——它取决于运行条件：物体运动得多快、承受多大的力、身处何种温度与环境。正因如此，摩擦学家在发明测量摩擦的新方法上展现出惊人的巧思。

Tribology testing is routine for the automotive industry today, and there are tribology labs scattered throughout a variety of industries, from medical to food. In these labs, you’ll find instruments designed to measure friction at macro-scales down to the atomic level. My PhD lab at the University of Florida had tribometers measuring friction in force ranges that covered three orders of magnitude. I’ve even worked in a lab that had a tribometer built to test an actual train wheel against a railway. Tribologists are incredibly clever with innovating ways to measure friction, given how persnickety friction is. Friction is dependent on the conditions of the operation – how fast things are moving, how much force they experience, and what temperature and environment they’re in.

温度尤其是个有趣的变量，因为摩擦本身就会引起温度变化；在某些情形下，摩擦生热甚至可能相当极端。就像罗马战车的例子那样，润滑脂在某些温度下会起火，因此必须控制摩擦热，以免不小心越过那一温度。而对于喷气发动机这类本就极热的应用，我们已经研发出能承受高温的低摩擦固体材料。

Temperature in particular is a fun variable to contend with, because friction itself leads to temperature changes; indeed, frictional heating can be extreme in some cases. Like with Roman chariots, greases can ignite at certain temperatures making it imperative to control friction heating in order to avoid accidentally reaching that temperature. And for applications like jet engines, which get very hot anyway, we have engineered solid materials with low friction to withstand the heat.

当摩擦学家碰壁时，我们会开发新仪器，去听懂摩擦想告诉我们什么

When tribologists run into a wall, we develop a new instrument to understand what friction is trying to tell us

你或许觉得摩擦学已经够冷门了，可我们在这门学科内部还有更细的专业分工。我做的就是固体润滑，负责为喷气发动机和汽车开发并测试材料。有些时候，最终服役条件极为严苛，以至于与我测试材料对磨的钢材会产生足以彻底改变其颜色的摩擦热。到了这个地步，你就知道自己已触及摩擦学的极限；但这些极限必须被攻克，而它们显然也的确被攻克了——毕竟，我们有空间站在绕地球运行，也有着陆器驻留在火星上。

You may think tribology is already a niche field, but we have specialisations within it. Mine was solid lubrication, and I worked on developing and testing materials for jet engines and automobiles. In some cases, the conditions of the end use were so aggressive that the steel I was running my material against would experience enough friction heating to completely change the colour of the steel. That’s how you know you’ve reached the extremes in tribology, but those extremes must be met and they obviously are. After all, we have space stations orbiting Earth and landers on Mars.

这就把我们引向关于摩擦在人类进步中所扮角色的另一个关键认识：阻力是进步的产物，阻力的存在本身就是前进的标志。倘若我们没有在技术上进步到足以设计涡轮发动机——而涡轮发动机本身又是我们搞定动力飞行的成果——就不会有如今稳固兴旺的固体润滑剂产业。正是凭借这些成就，我们的进步把我们带到了新的阻力面前。我认为，每当遭遇意想不到的阻力时，记住这一点至关重要；每当我在工作中被卡住，我也是这样提醒自己的：要不是我成功前进到了这个阻力所在的位置，我也不会被这道新阻力难住。

Which brings us to another critical point about friction’s role in human progress: resistance is a consequence of progress, and its existence is a sign of progression itself. We wouldn’t have the now-robust business of solid lubricants if we had not advanced technologically to design turbine engines, which themselves were the result of us figuring out powered flight. By achieving that, our progress led us to new resistance. I think this is critical to remember anytime we find ourselves confronting unexpected resistance, and it’s something I remind myself of whenever I get halted in my work. I wouldn’t be stumped by this new resistance if I hadn’t managed to advance myself to this position where the resistance exists.

摩擦学家碰壁时，常常会着手开发一种新仪器，帮助我们弄清摩擦想传达的信息。人们已经设计出能模拟上述所有变量的摩擦仪——仅此一项就堪称人类工程学的一大壮举——而如今，我们还会把它们与其他技术手段相结合，设法在事情发生的同时看清正在发生什么。从运行中摩擦生热的分布图，到摩擦如何影响细胞运动，这些仪器与技术让我们把摩擦领进了一个驱动我们向前的新纪元：我们不再只是寻找巧妙的办法去克服摩擦，而是能把它驾驭为工具。

Often when tribologists run into a wall, we find ourselves developing a new instrument to help us understand what friction is trying to tell us. Tribometers have been designed to mimic all of the variables mentioned above, and that alone is quite a feat of human engineering, but now we also couple them with other techniques that allow us to somehow see what is happening while it’s happening. From maps of frictional heating during operation to how friction is influencing cellular movement, instruments and techniques have enabled us to usher friction into a new era to drive us forward, doing more than just finding clever ways to overcome friction. We can harness it as a tool.

就摩擦而言，20世纪可以被看作这样一个世纪：人类接受了阻力，并围绕摩擦建立起严谨的工程规范，以此推动技术进步向前。关键在于，那也是我们把「如何克服摩擦以提高效率」加以体系化的时代。不过，作为一名摩擦学家，我更因21世纪带给我们的东西而兴奋。我们不再只是克服摩擦，而是开始充分领会如何驾驭这种阻力，去开启一个进步的新纪元。头号例证又一次出现在我们的汽车上：摩擦在那里被用作可持续发展工具。刹车需要摩擦，于是工程师们发问：我们何不反过来利用摩擦的后果？如今，在电动车与混合动力车的再生制动中，刹车摩擦产生的热被用来发电。面对摩擦，我们必须继续保持这种富有创造性的新鲜视角，而不是一味朝着「无摩擦」猛冲。设想一下，倘若那些工程师死盯着摩擦的坏处不放，没有去驾驭它的力量，会怎样？

In terms of friction, the 20th century can be thought of as the century in which humans accepted the resistance, and established engineering rigour around friction to propel our technological advances forward. Critically, it’s when we formalised how to overcome friction to be more efficient. As a tribologist, however, I’m far more excited about what the 21st century has brought us. Instead of just overcoming friction, we began fully realising how to harness that resistance to bring in a new era of progress. Exhibit A is once again found in our cars, where friction has been deployed as a tool in sustainability. Braking requires friction, so engineers asked: why don’t we use the consequences of that friction to our advantage? The heat from friction in braking is now used to create electricity in regenerative braking in EVs and hybrids. We must continue to use this creative and fresh perspective when dealing with friction, as opposed to always driving towards frictionlessness. Imagine if those engineers had been so fixated on the negatives of friction that they didn’t harness its power.

把摩擦用作工具，这是随着我们对摩擦的深层认识不断演进才达成的——包括它的行为方式，以及它可能潜伏在何处。我们知道它参与动态过程，那就用它来调控这类过程。诚然，借助刹车、减震器之类装置，我们这样做了有一阵子了，但把这一思路拓展到机器之外，则是近几十年才真正落地生根的。如今，我们正在研究摩擦如何成为那台或许是最了不起的机器中的工具：人体。摩擦有可能成为一根杠杆，帮助我们更好地建模和理解生物过程，进而助力疗法的开发。病毒从其蛋白外壳（衣壳，capsid）中喷出的速率，可能决定它是引发活动性感染，还是在细胞中保持潜伏。如果我们想让病毒去定向感染并击败其他病毒或细菌，那么控制病毒的喷出速率就至关重要，而摩擦正是解锁这一可能性的钥匙。2023年，研究人员发现，通过以特定方式包装病毒DNA的螺旋线圈，可以在喷出过程中增大摩擦，从而减缓喷出速率。

Using friction as a tool has come to us only as we’ve evolved to understand the depths of friction, including its behaviours and where it may be lurking. We know it is involved in dynamic processes, so let’s use it to control such processes. Yes, we’ve done so for a while with mechanisms like brakes and dampeners, but expanding this beyond machines has really taken hold in recent decades. Now, we’re looking at how friction may be a tool in perhaps the most impressive machine there is: the human body. It’s possible friction may be a lever to help us better model and understand biological processes and, in turn, help in the development of therapeutics. The rate at which a virus ejects from its protein shell or capsid can dictate whether it results in an active infection or instead remains dormant in the cell. If we want to use viruses to intentionally infect and defeat other viruses or bacteria, then controlling the virus’s ejection rate is crucial. Friction is the key to unlocking this possibility. In 2023, researchers found that, by packing the coils of virus DNA in certain ways, they could increase friction during ejection and slow down its rate of ejection.

我们的每一次进步都会产生新的摩擦。解决一个问题，摩擦又抛出另一个

Every advance we make generates new friction. We solve one problem, and friction presents another

近来，我们的摩擦学知识把我们带到了更令人惊奇的地方，甚至延伸到帮助我们理解暗物质（dark matter）。而「阻力是进步给你的回报」这一理念，最好的例证或许当属天体物理学家苏布拉马尼扬·钱德拉塞卡（Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar）。尽管一度遭到权威科学家的讥嘲，钱德拉塞卡后来成为天体物理学的奠基人之一，我们今天之所以理解恒星的演化与星际动力学，正是拜他所赐。20世纪30年代，他对恒星之间的相互作用如何导致碰撞产生了兴趣。他计算了这类碰撞的时间尺度，却发现——尽管是发表之后才发现——事情对不上账。这让他回到原点重新推敲，而就在那时，他察觉到一种此前无人指认的阻力：动力学摩擦（dynamical friction）。这种摩擦是一种由引力诱发的拖曳阻力，作用于在空间中相互作用、穿行的物体。

Recently, our knowledge of friction has taken us to even more surprising places, extending even to helping us understand dark matter. The concept of ‘resistance is your reward for progress’ may be best illustrated by the astrophysicist Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar. Despite the fact that established scientists mocked his theories, Chandrasekhar would go on to become a founder of astrophysics, and is the reason we understand the evolution of stars and interstellar dynamics. In the 1930s, he was interested in how the interactions between stars could lead to their collisions. He calculated the timescales of such collisions and realised, though only after publishing, that things weren’t adding up quite right. This led him back to the drawing board, where he spotted there was a resistance present that no one before had identified: dynamical friction. This friction is a gravitationally induced drag that acts on objects as they interact and move through space.

快进到21世纪，动力学摩擦已被用作推断暗物质存在的工具。2024年，香港的研究人员发现，他们研究的几颗据信正环绕黑洞运行的恒星，轨道周期怎么也对不上。他们端详钱德拉塞卡的动力学摩擦概念，心想：如果把黑洞周围暗物质所带来的摩擦贡献也纳入计算，结果会怎样？瞧！恒星的预测与计算轨道周期，突然与他们实测的数据吻合了。当人类最初开始摆弄火与轮子时，摩擦在场；如今当我们探索宇宙的前沿时，它依然在场。

Fast-forward to the 21st century, and dynamical friction has been used as a tool to infer the presence of dark matter. In 2024, researchers in Hong Kong realised that orbital times weren’t matching up with a couple of stars they were studying that were believed to be circling around black holes. They looked at Chandrasekhar’s concept of dynamical friction and wondered what would happen if they incorporated the contribution of friction from the dark matter surrounding the black hole. Voila! Suddenly, the predicted and calculated orbital times of the stars matched with what they were measuring. Friction was present when humanity first started experimenting with fire and the wheel. Now it’s there as we probe the frontiers of our Universe.

我们需要摩擦才能移动。没有它的阻力，地球板块将永无休止地漂移。我们将无法行走，无法驾车，甚至无法握住东西。在一个需要这种阻力的世界里，懂得如何实现我们所想所要，一直是人类发展的关键。

We need friction to move. Without its resistance, Earth’s plates would be in constant motion. We could not walk, or drive, or even hold objects. Understanding how to achieve what we want, in a world requiring this resistance, has been essential in our development.

然而，还有一个更深层的悖论在起作用。威廉·詹姆斯（William James）曾一语道破：生命正是构筑于「自我对世界的自主抵抗」之中。我们的每一次前进都会产生新的摩擦。我们解决一个问题，摩擦又抛出另一个。我们向前迈步，新的阻力已在等候。这就是进步的含义：我们已走进一片需要新的理解的疆域。

Yet there is a deeper paradox at work, one that William James captured when he wrote that life is built ‘in the self-governing resistance of the ego to the world’. Every advance we make generates new friction. We solve one problem, and friction presents another. We step forward, and new resistance awaits. This is what progress means: that we have moved into territory that demands new understanding.

罗马工程师在战车轮子起火时领悟了这一点。钱德拉塞卡在计算对不上时发现了这一点。而当我们继续向前推进时，阻力也将继续迎面而来。

Roman engineers learned it when their chariot wheels caught fire. Chandrasekhar discovered this when his calculations didn’t add up. And we will continue to encounter resistance as we push forward.

当人们谈论无摩擦的世界时，他们想象的是轻松顺遂。可是，没有阻力的轻松顺遂，只是一只原地空转的轮子——有运动，而无进步。

When people speak of a frictionless world, they imagine ease. But ease without resistance is a wheel spinning in place, motion without progress.
