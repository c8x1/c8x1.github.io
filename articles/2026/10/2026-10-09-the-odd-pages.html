单数的账页

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年10月9日·4分钟阅读

天亮时，第四十三局收了官。詹生把最后一子按进棋枰，对面的麻掌柜抬手认了输，花厅里没有人说话。阿卯吹熄灯，把账册合上，搁在灵前的供桌上。吊客里有人低声问，这一夜怎么会是屠师傅开的头——他和聂老爹三十年不说话，往年开枰，他从来不肯先坐；又有人问，蒲掌柜那页上的一道朱线是谁画的，他昨夜提着行李立在门口，一屋子人几乎赶着他走。要说清这两件事，得回到两天以前。

At dawn the forty-third game came to its close. Zhan Sheng pressed the last stone onto the board; across from him Shopkeeper Ma lifted a hand and conceded, and no one in the flower hall spoke. A'mao pinched out the lamp, shut the ledger, and laid it on the offering table before the coffin. Among the mourners someone asked, under his breath, how this night could have been opened by Master Tu — he and Old Nie had not spoken in thirty years, and in every year before he had never consented to sit first at the opening. Someone else asked who had drawn the red line through Shopkeeper Pu's page, when the man had stood in the doorway at midnight, luggage in hand, and the whole room had all but driven him off. To make sense of those two things you must go back two days.

两天以前，聂老爹咽了气。花厅的规矩是他亲手写的：会中两人一年只对一局；谁要是先走了，名下欠着的局，要在落葬前一夜打完，枰上不能空——输家起身，赢家守枰，下一局的人坐进空位；这一夜若断在半路，花厅按他的遗嘱归了侄子，卖契都写好了。司账的阿卯连夜理账：聂老爹自己的欠局随他入了棺，全册还剩四十六局，蒲掌柜名下挂着三局。麻掌柜直摇头，说天亮前哪里打得完。阿卯说，局数数不出答案，要数就数账页。他一页一页翻过去，翻到屠师傅那页，停了停，又翻到蒲掌柜那页，又停了停：单数的账页，一共四张。

Two days earlier, Old Nie had breathed his last. The rules of the hall stood written in his own hand: any two members were to meet at the board exactly once a year; if a man went first, the games still owed under his name were to be played out in one night before burial, and the board was never to stand empty — the loser rose, the winner held the board, and the next player sat into the empty seat. If that night broke midway, the hall passed by his will to his nephew, and the deed of sale was already drawn up. A'mao, who kept the ledger, went through it by lamplight: Old Nie's own debts had gone into the coffin with him; forty-six games remained in the book, three of them hanging under Shopkeeper Pu's name. Shopkeeper Ma shook his head — they could never be played out before dawn. A'mao said the count of games held no answer; count pages, he said, and see whose came out odd. He turned them one by one; at Master Tu's page he paused, and at Shopkeeper Pu's he paused again: four odd pages in all.

账念到一半，蒲掌柜到了，一身行装：南边的船天亮前开，家眷已经在码头上。他听见自己页上挂着三局，把行李放下了，说打完这三局再走。满屋子人都松了口气，只有阿卯把账册往回翻了一页，说这三局今夜打不得。蒲掌柜变了脸色：欠下的局，哪有躲的道理。阿卯说，你这三局要是不挪出去，单数的账页就是四张；四张单数页，这一夜无论怎么排，都要断在半路上——枰一空，花厅就没了。挪到明年开春，单数的账页落回两张，这一夜才接得起来。蒲掌柜在门口立了半晌，把行李重新提了起来。

Halfway through the reading Shopkeeper Pu arrived in traveling clothes: the southbound boat sailed before dawn, and his family was already at the wharf. Hearing that three games hung on his page, he set his luggage down and said he would play out the three before he left. The whole room exhaled with relief; only A'mao turned the ledger back a page and said those three games could not enter tonight. Pu's face darkened: who ever dodged a game he owed? A'mao said that if those three stayed on tonight's ledger there would be four odd pages, and with four odd pages no arrangement in the world could keep the night from snapping midway — the board would stand empty, and the hall would be gone. Carried over to next spring, the odd pages would settle back to two, and the night could be joined end to end. Shopkeeper Pu stood in the doorway a long moment, then picked his luggage up again.

蒲掌柜走后，阿卯重理了一遍账：那三局记到明年开春——欠白先生、麻掌柜和詹生的各一局；白先生与麻掌柜的页随即平了，詹生的页立起来，成了单数。单数的账页剩两张：屠师傅，詹生。白先生入会四十年，岁岁都是他开枰，这回也当仁不让。阿卯翻开他的页给他看：您欠的是双数；双数的人坐在夜的中段，开不了这一夜。今夜只能从屠师傅起头，詹生收尾，换谁来开都不成。白先生盯着自己那页，看了半晌，没再言语。屠师傅在门边站着，望着灵柩，很久没有作声。三十年前，他和聂老爹一局没下完就掀了茶碗；往后一年一局虽照下，两人没再说过一个字。他终于走过来，挽起袖子：「那就从我开始。我跟他怄了半辈子气，今夜替他把灯守到底。」

After Pu had gone, A'mao went through the ledger once more: the three games were carried to next spring — one owed to Master Bai, one to Shopkeeper Ma, one to Zhan Sheng — and Master Bai's and Shopkeeper Ma's pages evened out at once, while Zhan Sheng's, even until that hour, stood up odd. Two odd pages remained: Master Tu's, and Zhan Sheng's. Master Bai had been in the hall forty years and had opened the board every one of them; this year too he made to take the first seat. A'mao opened Bai's page and showed it to him: what you owe is even; a man with an even page sits in the middle stretch of the night and cannot open it. Tonight could begin only from Master Tu and end with Zhan Sheng; no one else could take the first seat. Master Bai stared at his own page a long while and said nothing more. Tu stood by the door, looking at the coffin, silent for a long time. Thirty years ago he and Old Nie had overturned the tea bowl over an unfinished game; the yearly game between them had gone on ever since, but the two men had not exchanged one word. At last he came over and rolled up his sleeves. "Then it starts with me. I sulked against him for half a life; tonight I keep his lamp to the very end."

入夜开枰。屠师傅坐下就不曾起身，一口气连对三局。有人低声嘀咕，说一人连坐三局，怕不合规矩。阿卯头也不抬：规矩管的是两个人一年只对一局；人坐几回，规矩从来不管。茶凉了三回，枰前的人换了一茬又一茬，白先生坐在夜的中段，不慌不忙，棺木在厅角安安静静躺着。詹生是聂老爹亲手带出来的徒弟，今夜反倒成了压轴。天将亮时，第四十三局在他手里收官；落最后一子的时候，他的手比谁都稳。

At nightfall the board was opened. Master Tu, once seated, did not rise; he took on three games running. Someone murmured that one man sitting three games in a row might be against the rules. A'mao did not look up: the rule bound each pair to a single game a year; how many times a man sat was never the rule's business. The tea went cold three times over, the players at the board changed one after another, Master Bai sat unhurried through the middle stretch of the night, and the coffin lay quiet in the corner of the hall. Zhan Sheng, the pupil Old Nie had trained with his own hands, found the night falling to him to close. As dawn drew near, the forty-third game ended in his hands; when he set down the last stone, his hand was steadier than anyone's.

落葬那天，侄子拿着卖契来了。阿卯把合上的账册递过去：四十三局，枰没有空过一盘。卖契当着灵前烧了。第二年开春，南边捎来蒲掌柜的信：花厅开枰，他头一个到，那三局一局不落；信尾又添一句，多亏那夜没人留他。屠师傅把信折进袖子里。他和聂老爹那局没下完的棋，册上早已寻不见，随人去了。

On the day of burial the nephew came with the deed of sale. A'mao handed him the shut ledger: forty-three games, and the board had not stood empty once. The deed was burned in front of the coffin. Next spring a letter came up from the south: Shopkeeper Pu wrote that when the hall opened its board he would be the first to arrive, and he would play out those three games to the last; at the end he added one line — that he owed it to a room that had not kept him. Master Tu folded the letter into his sleeve. The unfinished game between him and Old Nie was nowhere to be found in the ledger; it had gone away with the man.

注解：会中两人一年只对一局，又要接成落葬前不能停的一夜，这正是图论里的欧拉路径（Euler paths）：每条线都恰好走一遍；能否走通，先看整本账是否连成一气，再数每个地点连出单数条线的有几处——恰好零处或两处才走得通，是两处时，必须从一处起头、到另一处收尾。账的厚薄与此无关；人也尽可反复入座，要恰好走一遍的是线，不是地点。

Note: A club where each pair plays exactly once a year, strung into a single night that may not rest before the burial, is what graph theory calls an Euler path: every line is to be walked exactly once, and whether that can be done is settled by structure — the whole ledger must hang together, and the places from which an odd number of lines run must number exactly zero or two; when there are two, the night must set out from one and finish at the other. The thickness of the ledger has nothing to do with it; the players may take the seat as often as the pages demand, for it is the lines that must be walked once, not the places.
