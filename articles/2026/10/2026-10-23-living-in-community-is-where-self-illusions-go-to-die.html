共同体生活，正是自我幻象的葬身之地

Elizabeth Oldfield·Aeon·2026年10月23日·14分钟阅读·原文

今年早些时候，我从睡梦中浮起，听到一阵奇怪的声响：一种响亮的、人的歌声，穿透我们卧室的天花板回荡着，随后是一阵低沉的、近乎兽性的呻吟。我和丈夫对视了一眼。「我想哈蒂（Hatty）要生了。」他说。

Earlier this year, I surfaced from sleep to a strange noise, a loud, human singing reverberating through our bedroom ceiling, then a low, almost animal groaning. My husband and I looked at each other. ‘I think Hatty’s in labour,’ he said.

那天余下的时光照常运转：给孩子们做早饭，送他们上学，开会，处理邮件，预约汽车年检（MOT），只是这些寻常事务之间，不时穿插着为助产士开门、给哈蒂沏茶、找毛巾围裹客厅里突然出现的分娩池。临近傍晚，我们的第八位室友随着一股奔涌的液体降生了。两辆救护车满载而来的急救人员站在四周，像一群绿黄相间的伦敦鹦鹉，所幸全无用武之地。一时之间，我们这栋伦敦联排住宅里容纳了九名医疗专业人员、七位室友、新生儿的两位祖辈，外加一只猫。事后我想，那甚至都没显得有多满——当时我正往血迹斑斑的毛巾上倒漂白剂，把一根被遗漏在角落的废弃导尿管扔进垃圾桶。

The rest of the day proceeded in the usual way, getting breakfast for our children, dropping them off at school, meetings, emails, booking in the car for an MOT, but these activities were interspersed with opening the door to midwives, making cups of tea for Hatty, and finding towels to drape around the birth pool that had appeared in our living room. Towards evening, our eighth housemate appeared in a rush of fluids. Two ambulance-loads of paramedics stood around like a flock of green and yellow London parakeets, blessedly unrequired. At one point, there were nine medical professionals, seven housemates, two grandparents of the new arrival and a cat in our terraced London house. It didn’t even feel that full, I thought later, as I poured bleach on bloodstained towels and binned a discarded catheter that had been overlooked in a corner.

我今年42岁，从没料到自己会走到今天这一步。和大多数同龄人一样，我被递来一套值得为之奋斗的家宅图景——里面的人要少得多、少得多——起初，我也照着那个剧本走。作为1980年代的中产孩子，我读书不错，离开家乡郡里的通勤小镇去上大学，从此再没回去过。伦敦、英国广播公司（BBC）的工作、执掌一家智库，相继而来。我和好朋友合住，在彼此冲突的个人日程之间腾挪着与其他人见面。后来，人们开始成双结对、生儿育女。我遇到了一个人，随之效仿，我们拼尽全力，才勉强攀上住房阶梯的最底一级。那是一种可爱的人生。或许有点无休无止，边缘处也有些孤独——日程越来越难严丝合缝地拼合，工作与照料的担子越来越沉。但我们做到了。我们这个带着受抚养人的小小二人组，靠自己站稳了脚跟——尽管脚底下分明有什么东西，晃得人心里发虚。

I am 42, and I did not expect to end up here. Like most of my peers, I was handed a vision of a home worth striving for with far, far fewer people in it and, to begin with, I followed that script. A middle-class 1980s child, I did well at school, moved away from my home county dormitory town for university, and never went back. London, jobs at the BBC, running a think tank all followed. I lived with good friends, and juggled seeing others between our clashing personal schedules. Then people began to pair up and procreate. I met someone, followed suit, and we clawed our way onto the very bottom of the housing ladder. It was a lovely life. A bit relentless, perhaps, a bit lonely around the edges, too, as the schedules got ever harder to tessellate, the work and care burdens heavier. But we had done it. Our little dyad, with dependants, was standing on its own feet, even if something felt decidedly unstable underneath.

更大的空间，更多的隐私，更强的掌控——在我们多数人不知不觉怀揣的剧本里，它们被兜售成天经地义、不容置疑的好东西。对核心家庭而言，这些是成功与成熟的徽章；而舍弃这些「好处」、与你既无床笫之欢亦无养育之责的人同住，则总与地位不高的学生时代联系在一起。正经的成年人不这么过。我和丈夫开始感到这套叙事愈发强劲的拖拽。我有时在脑海里把它看作一股潮水：把年轻成年人从父母家中卷进暂时交织的群聚（多半在城市），再卷出去，涌向郊区乃至更远处，成双的或独自一人，去寻觅一座花园、一间更大的厨房、一处能收纳我们必然积攒起来的如山杂物的储物空间。人人被冲散，彼此越隔越远。

More space, more privacy, more control – all are sold as natural, unquestioned goods to be sought in the unconscious script that most of us carry. For the nuclear family, they are tokens of success and maturity, whereas forgoing these ‘goods’ and living with people you are neither shagging nor parenting has an association with low-status, studenty seasons. Not for proper grownups. My husband and I began to feel the strengthening drag of this story. I sometimes saw it in my mind’s eye as a tide, washing young adults from their family homes into temporarily intertwined clusters (largely in cities), then out again, to the suburbs and beyond, in pairs or alone, in search of a garden, a bigger kitchen, storage space for the mountains of stuff we inevitably accumulate. Swept away from each other, everyone further and further apart.

在智库里，我们研究的每一个政策议题，最终都不容抗拒地指向「失联」。它被冠以各种名目（过度消费、信任磨损、民主侵蚀，孤独、心理健康、照料、气候、住房的种种危机）。但有一条线索把它们串在一起：我们无力彼此担待、无力分享（我们的物品、空间、技能，以及最深的自我），正在切实地把世界变得更糟。我遇到过一些与我直觉相同的思想者：某种大体无形却无所不在的东西，正妨碍我们像更健康的文化还记得的那样共同生活。有人称之为个人主义，或现代性。有人归咎于晚期资本主义那台「机器」。我始终没有选定一个自己喜欢的名字，但它的不安，我感同身受。然而，所有聪明的分析与批判，似乎从来不曾真正引向进步的行动，反而引向一种保守的转向——其社群主义与扎根乡土的在地性有一半令我心动，但它对待女性和「局外人」的方式，我却无法认同。

Every policy issue we researched at the think tank led back inexorably to disconnection. It was labelled different things (overconsumption, fraying trust, the corrosion of democracy, crises of loneliness, mental health, care, climate, housing). But a thread linked them all: our inability to bear each other, to share (our stuff, space, skills, and deepest selves) was actively making the world worse. I came across thinkers who shared my intuition that something largely invisible but all-encompassing was getting in the way of our being together as healthier cultures remembered how to do. Some called it individualism or modernity. Others blamed ‘the machine’ of late-stage capitalism. I never settled on a name I liked, but I felt the disquiet of it. Still, all the intelligent analysis and critique never quite seemed to lead to progressive action, but instead to a conservative turn that half-appealed in its communitarianism and rooted localism, but not in its approach to women and ‘outsiders’.

这套讯息似乎是：我们为自由个人主义的种种自由付出的代价，是孤独。你可以拥有个人自主、隐私和无尽的选择；你可以把大多数需求外包给市场，而不必去经营那些把你系进互惠关系里的、复杂的非交易性人际关系；你可以把周遭环境、外表与经历优化到与自己的偏好分毫不差，但随之捆绑奉送的，是一种存在性的失重感。

The message seems to be that the price we pay for the freedoms of liberal individualism is loneliness. You can have personal autonomy, privacy and endless choice; you can outsource most of your needs to the market rather than navigate complex non-transactional relationships with other people that tie you into reciprocity; you can optimise your surroundings, appearance and experiences to your exact preferences, but what gets bundled in for good measure is a sense of existential weightlessness.

我反叛了。在原子化——哲学家查尔斯·泰勒（Charles Taylor）所谓的「缓冲自我」（buffered self）——与等级森严、威权式的共同体之间，在「大女主」（girl-boss）与「传统主妇」（trad-wife）之间，一定还存在着别的什么。让我最终动身去寻找它的最后一推，是恐惧。第一个孩子出生时，气候危机的现实从一个理论性的政策问题，变成一团在我腹中酸涩翻涌、继而沉进骨头里的东西。我把这个毫无自保之力的生命带进了一个着火的世界，而「努力工作加上个人积累就能护她周全」的想法，从此不再说得通。脚下的大地颠簸翻腾，此时此刻，置身一张稠密的人际之网——网上的人不在彼此生命里扮演过客，而是许下代价高昂的相互照料——这份需要显得刻不容缓。我想要「一个村庄」，如今大家都这么说——不是为了Instagram上那种一群朋友围着上镜餐桌欢笑的金色画面，而是因为，在我最灰暗的时刻，我以为它可能是活下去的必需品。

I rebelled. Surely there was something between atomisation – what the philosopher Charles Taylor calls the ‘buffered self’ – and hierarchical, authoritarian community; between girl-boss and trad-wife. The final push to try and find it, ultimately, was fear. When my first child was born, the reality of the climate crisis moved from a theoretical policy problem to one that bubbled acidly in my belly, settled itself in my bones. I had brought this powerless being into a world on fire, and the idea that hard work and individual accumulation could keep her safe stopped making sense. The need to be part of a thick web of humans who would not play a passing role in each other’s lives but commit to costly mutual care felt urgent as the ground bucked and roiled beneath our feet. I wanted ‘a village’, as we say now, not for the golden Instagram images of laughing friends around a photogenic table, but because, in my bleakest moments, I thought it might be essential for survival.

初为父母的日子孤立无援、疲于奔命，这是老生常谈——可它非得如此吗？我们开始质疑那些被视为当然的规范，不是闲来无事地幻想建立公社，而是迈出认真的步伐。我想与旁人贴身共度人生的这个季节，彼此见证，彼此扶持。而不是事后把修饰一新的细节上传到群聊里，把苦难熨平成梗图，把欢喜憋回去、生怕听来沾沾自喜。这个领悟向外裂开，接通了一个更深的直觉：我希望整个生活都如此。我想看见，也想被看见。不是所有人都这样生活——这件事本身感觉就是错的，仿佛在匆忙积攒财物、以退回自家门后来保卫我们的安全、地位与自主的过程中，某种重要的东西已被丢失。

It is a truism that early parenthood is isolating and relentless, but does it have to be? We started questioning accepted norms, not idly fantasising about building a commune but taking serious steps. I wanted to live this season of our lives up close with others, witnessing each other, supporting each other. Not uploading the tidied-up details into group chats, after the fact, or smoothing the misery into memes, holding back the joys for fear of sounding smug. This realisation cracked outwards, connecting with a deeper intuition: I wanted all of life to be like that. I wanted to see and be seen. It felt wrong that everyone didn’t live this way, like something important had been lost in the rush to accumulate stuff and protect our security, status and autonomy by disappearing behind our own front doors.

无论你把当下称作「多重危机」（polycrisis），还是干脆叫它一团翻滚搅动的巨型烂摊子，我都愈发确信：我们大多数病症的药方，是主动地、反着文化潮流地走向他人。去抵抗那些把我们彼此驱开的强大力量。当然，方法有很多种。但松开「家必须是躲开他人的私人退居处」这个念头，似乎是个不错的起点。我相信，家可以成为我们重新学习互惠、分享与相互照料之习惯的场所——在历史的大部分时间里（在非西方世界的许多地方，至今依然如此），这些习惯都是一种配得上「文化」之名的文化的标志。而这样的文化，我们将迫切需要。

Whether you call the present a polycrisis or just a gigantic roiling clusterfuck, I became more convinced that the medicine for most of our ills is to actively and counterculturally move towards other people. To resist the powerful forces driving us apart. There are lots of ways of doing this, of course. But letting go of the idea that our homes must be private retreats from other people seemed a decent place to start. I believe that our homes can be places to relearn the habits of reciprocity, sharing and mutual care that for most of history (and, in many parts of the non-Western world, still) are the defining characteristics of a culture worthy of the name. A culture we are urgently going to need.

这件事，说起来可比实际上简单、轻松得多得多。

This sounds much, much simpler and easier than it is.

对我们而言，这花去了经年累月有意识的交谈、旁人起初高涨随后偃息的热忱、官僚程序的缠斗，还有大量的调查研究。我们最终找到了同样坚定投入的人（有意思的是，他们原本并不是我们的朋友），一起租房试水。我们学会了应付性爱的声响和浴室的气味（隔音和背景音乐对前者大有帮助）。我们就厨房储物、彼此在开销与清洁标准上的分歧，谈过几场剑拔弩张的话。它还翻搅出我们各自童年的东西，我们不得不诚实面对、逐一清算。整个过程（大部分发生在新冠疫情（COVID-19）封锁期间，我确信封锁加速了进程）充满挑战、令人暴露无遗，有时难受到几乎无法忍受。

For us, it took years of intentional conversations, initial enthusiasm from others that then petered out, bureaucratic wrangling, and research. We eventually found people who were equally committed (who were not, interestingly, friends already), and rented together to test it. We learned to navigate sex noises and bathroom smells (sound-proofing and background music helps a lot with the first). We had tense conversations about kitchen storage, our different expectations around spending and cleanliness. It surfaced stuff from our childhoods, which we then had to be honest about and confront. The process (much of which happened during COVID-19 lockdowns, which I’m sure accelerated it) was challenging and exposing, almost unbearably uncomfortable at times.

我们还练习「破裂与修复」，并意识到：即便看见了彼此最荒唐、最脆弱、最戒备的部分，我们也没有逃跑。汤姆·斯托帕德（Tom Stoppard）在他1982年的剧作《真爱》（The Real Thing）中，把爱恰如其分地定义为「对彼此的认知……对自我的认知，真实的他，真实的她，在最危急的时刻，面具从脸上滑落」。在我们建立信任的过程中，那确实很像爱。不是建立在表面相似或浪漫之上的爱，而是建立在彼此相互承诺之上的爱。那感觉像是长大成人。存在性的空虚、那种摇晃不稳的眩晕感，都缓和了。

We also practised rupture and repair, realising that, even as we saw the most ridiculous, fragile, defensive parts of each other, we were not running away. In his play The Real Thing (1982), Tom Stoppard aptly defined love as ‘Knowledge of each other … knowledge of self, the real him, the real her, in extremis, the mask slipped from the face.’ In the course of our building trust, it did feel a lot like love. Not love based on surface similarities or romance, but on a mutual commitment to each other. It felt like growing up. The existential emptiness, the unsteady sense of vertigo eased.

概念试过之后，我们着手进行一场迷宫般繁复的联合购房。交易在一拖再拖的交割日当天告吹——而那时，我们租约的退租通知期已经走到尽头。一连数月，我们没有固定住址，四散在亲友的沙发与空床上。我最小的孩子开始做噩梦。凌晨4点，我抱着他，他问：「为什么我们不能待在我们原来的公寓里，只有我们自己？」那套剧本散发着诱人的微光。如果与他人同住是答案，那它实在不该这么难。我们稳住了心神。

Having tested the concept, we embarked on a labyrinthically complex joint house-purchase. It fell through on the day of a long-delayed exchange, after we’d reached the end of the notice period on our rental. For months, we had no fixed address and scattered to the sofas and spare beds of friends and family. My youngest child began having nightmares. As I held him at 4 am, he asked: ‘Why couldn’t we stay in our flat, just us?’ The script glowed temptingly. If living with others was the answer, surely it shouldn’t be this difficult. We held our nerve.

迄今为止，我们与没有血缘关系的人贴身同住——近到体液相接的程度——已近六年。在这栋共同按揭的房子里，除了卧室，所有空间都是共享的。我们把大部分钱合在一起用，一起做饭、购物、祈祷，一年一起招待数百位来客。我们也处理着数不清的鸡毛蒜皮的冲突：牛奶、洗碗机、台面上的面包屑——这些冲突往往遮掩着更深的问题：我属于谁？我在哪里能真正安身、真正做自己？与这些人并肩，我正在成为怎样的人？而当一切归结到底，我们能否成为彼此的安全所在？

To date, we have lived up close – bodily fluids close – with people we are not related to for nearly six years. We share all the space in our jointly mortgaged home except the bedrooms. We pool most of our money, cook, shop, pray and host hundreds of guests a year, together. And we manage countless petty conflicts, over the milk or the dishwasher or the crumbs on the side, conflicts that often mask deeper questions: who do I belong to? Where can I be really at home, be really myself? Who am I becoming, alongside these people? And are we able, when it comes down to it, to be each other’s safety?

我们正在做的事，可循的范本寥寥无几。或许最坚实的一个，是修道传统。大多数修女会与修士会都认为，彼此贴身共处对灵性成长至关重要。他们说，恰恰是在近距离被人见证自己最糟糕样态的不适之中，在被要求让渡偏好、去分享、去服务他人之中，我们才真正长大。

There are very few models for what we are doing. Perhaps the most robust is the monastic tradition. Most orders of religious Sisters or Brothers see the proximity of living up close with each other as essential to spiritual growth. It is precisely in the discomfort of being witnessed, up close, at our worst, in being asked to compromise preferences, to share and serve others, they argue, that we really grow up.

我这颗政策呆子的头脑知道，有种种系统在把我们隔开。不只是社会剧本，还有货币政策、按揭条款、「多户合租住宅」（HMO）法规。然而共同生活教会我：让我们难以相守的，也包括我们内心里的东西。我那些关于名副其实的文化——以相互依存与相互照料为标志——的浪漫想象，显然同样需要特定类型的人：拥有情感技能、成熟品格、能够许下持久承诺的人。而这些，恰恰是我所受的个人主义式养成未曾慷慨赠予我的。

My policy-wonk brain knew there were systems keeping us apart. Not just social scripts but monetary policies, mortgage terms, HMO (house in multiple occupation) legislation. Yet living communally has taught me that it is also what is inside us that makes it hard to be together. My romantic ideas about cultures worthy of the name, marked by interdependence and mutual care, clearly also require certain kinds of people – with emotional skills, developed character, an ability to make lasting commitments. None of which my individualistic formation had gifted me an abundance of.

与他人同住，让我看见了自己身上那些我宁愿无视的东西。我的内里还有多少仍是个孩子——凡事要按自己的意思，动不动就嚷「这不公平」。我一边学习这一切，一边看着我们周遭的世界松散解体。这反差令人心绪难宁。如果这真的是世界的终结——至少是我们所认识的那个世界的终结——那么到那时，需要什么样的人？要变得更有勇气、更有韧性与弹性、更快乐、更有创造力也更自由，需要什么？至少，得是一个能耐心等着上厕所、能把自己心爱的马克杯让给别人的人吧。一个能牺牲睡眠去照看别人家的孩子、能就厨房地板没拖这桩事展开一场艰难谈话、而不是避而不谈的人。我想学会这些习惯。我有种预感：未来要求于我的，可能远不止这些。要求于我们所有人的，都是。

Living with others has shown me things in myself that I would prefer to ignore. How much of me is still a child, wanting my way, quick to cry ‘it’s not fair’. I have been learning all this as the world around us unravels. The contrast is disconcerting. If it really is the end of the world, at least as we have known it, then what kind of people will be needed? What would it take to be braver, more resilient and flexible, more joyful, creative and free? A person who could, at bloody least, wait patiently to go in the bathroom, or let someone else use a favourite mug. Someone able to sacrifice sleep to care for another’s child, and have the hard conversation about a failure to mop the kitchen floor, rather than avoiding it. I want to learn these habits. I have a hunch that much more than this might be asked of me. Of all of us.

这项工程，在我们人人时时被耳提面命的自我提升、自我发展、自我优化之中，能找到微弱的回声——心灵方面，心理治疗（或致幻剂，或斯多葛主义）；身体方面，Hyrox混合体能赛（或器械普拉提，或瑜伽）（至于没有在以某种方式打磨自己的人，则属例外）。有意思的是，我们家这种非传统的生活，很少被推荐给寻求自我认知或自我发展的人。标准的建议是向内转，以便对「本真自我」做一场私密的发掘，并辅以坚固的个人边界，在自我与他人之间划出清晰的界线。可渗透性，则往往被描述为对「自我工程」（Project Self）有害之物。诚然，多数修道会都鼓励独处与静默的时段，但日常生活的很大一部分，是在一种令你无处遮掩的近距离共处中度过的——单是这念头，就足以让我们大多数人起一身荨麻疹。

This project finds faint echoes in the self-improvement, self-development, self-optimisation we are all constantly hectored into – therapy (or psychedelics, or stoicism) for the mind and Hyrox (or reformer Pilates, or yoga) for the body (with those who are not working on themselves in some way being the exception). Interestingly, the unconventional way our household lives is rarely recommended to those seeking self-knowledge or self-development. The standard advice is an inward turn in order to facilitate a private excavation of an authentic self, along with strong personal boundaries that draw distinct lines between that self and others. Porousness tends to be framed as deleterious to Project Self. Most monastic orders encourage periods of solitude and silence, to be sure, but much of daily life is spent in an exposing proximity to others, the very idea of which would bring most of us out in hives.

这种艰难的贴近——在其中我们见证他人也被他人见证，身上卸下了防护性的、抛光过的对外人格——是一种强大得惊人的变革力量。你可以在团体治疗、戒瘾康复运动以及一些短期强化的自我发展课程中看到它运转，但这种强有力的「自我技术」（technology of the self）——借用米歇尔·福柯（Michel Foucault）的说法——稀罕到近乎绝迹。这种见证是有效的（例如，在我室友分娩时，我见证了她身上那些原本只有她伴侣才见过的部分），因为改变要求我们对自己目前究竟是谁保持诚实。生活在共同体之中，正是关于自我的种种幻象的葬身之地！

This difficult proximity, where we witness and are witnessed without our protective, polished personas is an immensely powerful change agent. You can see it in action in group therapy, the recovery movement and some short-term intensive self-development programmes, but this powerful ‘technology of the self’, to use Michel Foucault’s phrase, is vanishingly rare. This kind of witnessing (during my housemate’s labour, for example, I witnessed parts of her that only her partner otherwise sees) is effective because change requires us to be honest about who we currently are. Living in community is where illusions about the self go to die!

我们家常用的一句话是：「我们为彼此而造，也由彼此而造。」西尔维娅·费代里奇（Silvia Federici）等马克思主义者称之为「社会再生产」（social reproduction）。从神经学上看，这说得通：从生命最初的时刻起，人与人的互动就在塑造我们的大脑。每一个人降生人世，都在寻找一束注视。

One of the phrases we use a lot in our household is ‘We are made for each other and we are made by each other.’ Marxists such as Silvia Federici call this ‘social reproduction’. Neurologically, this makes sense: human interaction shapes our brains from our very earliest moments. Every human comes into the world in search of a gaze.

婴儿出生那天，他们的母亲还在接受缝合，我在帮急救人员挪垃圾桶，我那上小学的儿子是最早抱起那个小小身躯的人之一。他们把婴儿裹在一只黄色的锡箔保温袋里，他俯身凝望了长长的几分钟，那份专注，仿佛他捧着的当真是一颗巨大的Quality Street太妃糖。这个婴儿已从深度的身体纠缠——藏在我们其中一人的身体里——转入一种不那么可见、却同样深刻的相互依存。到如今，抱过他们的手臂，已多过寻常西方婴儿所得；无论是妈妈、爸爸、我们这些室友、家人、邻居还是教会朋友，我们都摆出和我儿子一样的姿势。脸转向脸。眼睛望进眼睛。婴儿发出声响，我们以声响回应。而在我们凝视与咿呀之间，他们大脑中的菌丝正在生长。

The day the baby was born, while their mother was being stitched up and I was moving bins for the paramedics, my primary-school aged son was one of the first people to hold the tiny form. They’d wrapped the baby in a yellow foil survival bag, and he gazed down for long minutes with as much attention as if he was indeed holding a giant Quality Street toffee. This baby had moved from deep bodily entanglement, hidden in just one of us, to a less visible but no less profound interdependence. They have now been held by more arms than your average Western infant, and whether it’s mum, dad, us housemates, family, neighbours or church friends, we all adopt the same posture as my son. Face turned towards face. Eyes meeting eyes. The baby makes noises, and we make noises back. And as we gaze and gurgle, the mycelia of their brain are forming.

临床精神科医生、人际神经生物学（interpersonal neurobiology）创始人丹·西格尔（Dan Siegel）说，我们最早的关系「塑造了那些创造经验表征、让我们得以连贯地看待世界的神经结构本身：人际经验直接影响我们如何在心理上构建现实」。我们所有人都本能地与婴儿共舞的那种「脸庞之舞」所形成的连接，帮助创造出让婴儿得以体验何为真实的条件。不止如此，这些注视正是他们自我感的来源。西格尔说，在这些最早的连接经验之外，关系在我们一生中持续塑造着「我们如何感受，如何思考，如何记住事情，如何讲述我们是谁的故事，自我感……什么给你一种目标感」。我们之所以成为我们，是因为我们注视着谁，又是谁在回望我们。

Dan Siegel, the clinical psychiatrist and founder of interpersonal neurobiology, says that our earliest relationships ‘shape the very neural structures that create representations of experience and allow a coherent view of the world: Interpersonal experiences directly influence how we mentally construct reality.’ The connections formed by the face-dancing we all instinctively do with babies help create the conditions that enable them to experience what is real. More than this, these gazes are where their sense of self comes from. Siegel says that, beyond these earliest experiences of connection, relationships shape, throughout our lives, ‘how we feel, how we think, how we remember things, how we tell the story of who we are, the sense of self … what gives you a sense of purpose.’ We become who we become because of who we gaze on, and who gazes back at us.

婴儿对照护者的可渗透性，尤其他们对面对面联结的需要，早已广为人知。神经心理学家艾伦·肖尔（Allan Schore）称婴儿是一个「开放的动态系统」（open dynamic system）。我们的家正帮我看见：人类在多大程度上始终是「开放系统」，而心灵、自我与灵魂（皆是「psyche」一词的翻译）又在多大程度上是在我们之内、我们之间生起的。我们并非封闭的自我，相互打着旗语传递信息、作出自由而理性的选择。我们始终在彼此塑造——相互见证这一行为，或它的缺席，像捏湿黏土一样揉塑着我们。

The porousness of babies to their caregivers, and especially their need for face-to-face connection is widely understood. The neuropsychologist Allan Schore calls a baby an ‘open dynamic system’. What our house is helping me see is how much humans remain ‘open systems’, and how much mind/self/soul (all translations of the word ‘psyche’) arises within and between us. We are not closed selves, semaphoring information to each other and making free rational choices. We are always makers of each other – the act of mutual witnessing, or its lack, moulding us like wet clay.

于是，看待我们这种非传统居住安排的又一种方式是：它提供了一条路径，让我们选择自己想被谁塑造，仿佛在有意地策展他人那带着力量的目光。我们押下的赌注是：那些真实的代价——在掌控、便利与舒适上的损失，「面具从脸上滑落」之后被看见、被知晓的暴露——是值得的。我相信，这是一条苛严却有效的路径，通往成为这个世界此刻所需要的那种人。

One way of thinking about our unconventional living situation, then, is that it offers a way of choosing who we want to be formed by, as if consciously curating the powerful gaze of others. Our bet is that the real costs, in control, convenience and comfort, the exposure of being seen and known ‘with masks slipped from faces’, are worth it. I believe that it is a demanding but effective method of becoming the kind of people the world needs now.

我可能是错的。对这类实验的现实，我不戴玫瑰色眼镜：若办得糟糕，它们能把人伤得很重。我们的生活方式未必持续得下去。与修士修女不同，我们只以五年为期作出承诺。尽管有种种挑战，这一季仍是我此生最有意义、最美、最快活的一段时光。它让我更勇敢，也腾出了大片时间与精力——这些本会耗费在一个只有两名成年人的家庭的持家琐务上。眼下它运转良好，真的运转良好——但我们是现实主义者。我们常常谈起退出，谈起我们多半不会永远住在一起这件事。或许我们是既想把蛋糕留着、又想吃掉它：一边体验着扎根的、彼此承诺的相互照料带来的好处，一边仍把一只脚留在自由个人主义的自主之中。我们的实验并不完美。但我的直觉是：这样的实验，我们将需要更多。

I could be wrong. I am not rosy-tinted about the realities of experiments like these, how badly they can hurt people if done poorly. The way we live may not last. We commit, unlike monks and nuns, only in five-year increments. This season has been the most meaningful, the most beautiful and the most fun of my life, despite the challenges. It has made me braver, and freed up swathes of time and energy that I would have otherwise spent on home admin in a household of only two adults. It is working, really working, for now, but we are realists. We talk a lot about exits, about the fact that we will probably not live together forever. Perhaps we’re trying to have our cake and eat it, keeping one foot in the autonomy of liberal individualism even as we experience the benefits of rooted, committed mutual care. Our experiment is imperfect. My hunch, though, is that we are going to need more of them.

作为一个社会，我们面对的生存威胁之巨，我们仍大半处在否认之中——而个人主义无法在这些威胁面前护我们周全。在生态、技术与政治的深度危机时代，我们或许会被迫重新记起，如何成为彼此的安全所在。去一针一线地拆解那个古怪而富足的西方加在我们身上的条件反射，去戳穿那个鬼话：掌控、积攒，以及一种毫无摩擦、精确优化的生活，曾有可能让我们幸福。尽管我知道那是鬼话，我身上仍有一大部分想要它。然而，当我每天都能更清楚地看到个人主义的剧本把我塑造得有多深，我也同样看到，它把我们服侍得有多糟。

As a society, we are still largely in denial about the scale of the existential threats facing us, and against which individualism cannot keep us safe. In times of deep ecological, technological and political crisis, we may be forced to remember how to be each other’s safety. To unpick, laboriously, the conditioning of the weird, prosperous West and call bullshit on the lie that control, accumulation and a frictionless, precision-optimised life was ever going to make us happy. Though I know it’s a lie, a lot of me still wants it. However, as I learn every day how deeply the script of individualism has formed me, I also see how poorly it serves us.

每天在这栋房子里醒来，我都在选择上演一个不同的剧本——在其中，他人的忧患对我而言更加真实。我室友的产痛、这个婴儿的啼哭，是我的事。反过来，我偶发的存在性恐惧、我孩子们在学业上的挣扎，也比原本可能的情况被更广的一群人真切地感知着。我们彼此利害与共。其中有一种炽烈的温柔，常常让我落泪。艾丽丝·默多克（Iris Murdoch）在1959年的《崇高与善》（The Sublime and The Good）一文中描述过与之相近的东西，她写道：
爱，是极其艰难地认识到：自身之外的某样东西是真实的。爱……是对现实的发现……克服自我、驱逐幻想与俗套的操练……确实令人振奋。而倘若我们把它做得妥当——我们几乎从来做不到——它也是疼痛的。

Each day that I wake up in this house, I am choosing to enact a different script, one in which other people’s concerns are more real to me. My housemate’s labour pains, this baby’s cries, are my business. Conversely, my occasional existential terror or my children’s school struggles are tangible to a wider group of people than they would otherwise be. We have a stake in each other. There is a fierce tenderness in it that often brings me to tears. Iris Murdoch describes something like it in ‘The Sublime and The Good’ (1959) when she writes:
Love is the extremely difficult realisation that something other than oneself is real. Love … is the discovery of reality … The exercise of overcoming one’s self, of the expulsion of fantasy and convention … is indeed exhilarating. It is also, if we perform it properly which we hardly ever do, painful.

孤立让我们得以维持关于自己、关于他人的种种幻象。相比之下，与一种共享的现实保持系连，则要求我们真正看见他人——不是在社交媒体那折射变形的哈哈镜里，而是以规律的、身体在场的方式。我们借助他人对世界做三角测量，从一系列视角读取读数，以拼合出事物更准确的图景。在我脑海里，这个过程是这样一幅图像：人类像蝙蝠一样回声定位，而把声波弹回来的物件，正是他人的声音与面孔。勒内·笛卡尔（René Descartes）也许以为，要找到真理，他必须彻底退出社会——但恕我直言，他错了。向内转、抽身退离，只会让我们失去方向。社会理论家萨拉·斯坦·卢布拉诺（Sarah Stein Lubrano）主张，我们独处越久，就越容易陷入妄想。像我们这样的共同体常遭人笑话，被斥为天真而不值一提。但如果我们的心智确实需要一张稠密的强联结之网才能把握现实，那么我们这些怪人，说不定正在学着更清晰地看见这个世界。

Isolation allows us to maintain all kinds of illusions about ourselves and about others. In contrast, remaining tethered to a shared reality requires us to really see others, not in the refracted fun-house mirror of social media, but in regular, embodied ways. We triangulate the world via other people, taking readings from a range of perspectives to build a more accurate picture of things. My image of this process is of humans echolocating like bats, where the objects sounding back are the voices and faces of other people. René Descartes might have thought he needed to withdraw from society completely to find truth but, respectfully, he was wrong. Turning in, pulling away disorientates us. The social theorist Sarah Stein Lubrano argues that the more time we spend alone, the more prone we are to delusion. Communities like ours are often laughed at, written off as naive, but if our minds do indeed require a thick web of strong relational connections in order to grasp reality, then we weirdos are possibly learning to see the world more clearly.

那么，为什么像我们这样的实验如此罕见？为什么我——一个公开的倡导者、共同体生活种种真实益处的领受者——在更起劲地主张它成为常态时，仍感到矛盾？部分原因在于，这样的生活，正如默多克所言，是疼痛的。把我的偏好挪出中心位置，松开那些自我膨胀的幻象，是疼的。这些是成长的阵痛，每一阵都值得——可终究是疼。邀他人进入一件如此严苛、如此昂贵的事，眼下并不时兴。

Why, then, are experiments like ours so rare? Why do I, a public advocate, a recipient of the very real benefits of communal living, still feel conflicted about arguing more strongly for it to become the norm? It is partly that such a life is, as Murdoch says, painful. Decentering my preferences, letting go of my self-aggrandising illusions hurts. These are growing pains, every one worth it – but still. Inviting others into something this rigorous, and costly, is not in fashion.

还有另一个原因。我们生活其中的这个共同体，提供相互的见证、相互的认可、更大的容量与照料，还有规模经济，但当一切都显得难以承受时，单凭这些还不足以托住我们。当有人来我们家，想请教如何建立类似的东西时，我们总是告诉他们：为共同体而共同体，行不通。公社或许可以是高效的自我塑造之所，但那些主要为此目的而设计的公社，多半会垮塌。我的经验是，我们这个共同体（以及其他延续下来的共同体）之所以凝聚不散，靠的是把注意力转向自身之外。能否把这项事业安放进一幅关于善的愿景——政治的、生态的、哲学的或其他——之中，是不可或缺的基石。

There is another reason. The community we live in offers mutual witnessing, mutual recognition, increased capacity and care, and economies of scale, but this alone is not enough to hold us when it all feels too much. When people come to our home wanting advice about setting up something similar, we always tell them: community for the sake of community doesn’t work. Communes might be highly effective sites of self-formation, but the ones designed primarily to do that will likely collapse. My experience is that our community (and other lasting ones) hold together by turning attention beyond ourselves. The ability to locate the project in a vision of the good – political, ecological, philosophical or other – is an essential foundation.

关于我们的家，还有一件事我没多提。你或许捕捉到了那句一笔带过的「一起祈祷」。查尔斯·泰勒等人关于现代性的孤立倾向有一个论点：它与世俗化相关。他认为，前现代人对自身何等可渗透有着强得多的感知——知道彼此如何缠结，与自然缠结，与神性或魔性的临在缠结。他把我们日益划界、日益「缓冲」的自我感与宗教的失落联系起来：我们这些现代人，不再是神（或诸神）所造、为之而造，不再是彼此所造、为彼此而造，而是自己所造、为自己而造。我对这个论点心存几分认同，因为若没有一个坚实的、集体的智慧传统，要不断选择彼此走近，实在艰难。尽管出于政策理由、情感理由、神经生物学理由，甚至该死的生存理由，我都信这种生活方式，我仍不敢说，若没有一个强有力的「为什么」——在我们的情形里，一个宗教的「为什么」——我能够做到这一切。

There is something about our house I have not mentioned much. You may have caught that throwaway line about praying together. One of the arguments made by Charles Taylor and others about the isolating tendencies of modernity is that it is linked to secularity. He argues that pre-moderns had a much stronger sense of how porous they were, how bound up with each other, with nature, and with divine or magical presences. He links our increasingly boundaried or buffered sense of self to the loss of religion: we moderns are not made by and for god(s), by and for each other, but by and for ourselves. I have some sympathy with the argument, because without a robust, collective wisdom tradition, it is challenging to keep choosing to move towards each other. Despite believing in this way of life for policy reasons, emotional reasons, neurobiological reasons, even goddamn survival reasons, I am not sure I would be able to do it without a powerful, and in our case, religious, reason why.

有人请耶稣（Jesus）概括他的教导，他给出了两条相互关联的诫命。其一，爱神圣者（「尽心、尽性、尽意、尽力爱主你的神」）；其二，爱他者（「爱人如己」）。没有道德规条，没有宗教仪文，而是把关系性本身立为「道」。我花了很多时间，反复咀嚼那个「如」字在「爱人如己」里所做的功课。是以你爱自己的同等分量？是与此同时？还是因为他们就是你自己？哪怕他们把胡子茬剃满了洗手池？

When asked to sum up his teaching, Jesus offered two, interlinked commands. Firstly, love of the divine (Love the Lord your God ‘with all your heart, soul, mind, and strength’) then, love of the other (‘Love your neighbour as yourself’). No moral prescriptions or religious ritualism, but relationality as The Way. I have spent a lot of time sitting with the work that the word ‘as’ is doing in ‘love your neighbour as your self’. In the same measure you love yourself? At the same time as? Because they are yourself? Even when they have left beard clippings all over the sink?

好在，关于学会彼此担待、彼此共处，耶稣还讲过许多不那么玄奥的话。《新约》（The New Testament）把一件事当作前提：人是凌乱的、不诚实的、自私的、被误导的，我们会彼此伤害、彼此触怒。我们或许为彼此而造、由彼此而造，但《新约》预设了：总有些日子，我们会巴不得并非如此。这种断开的倾向，这种近乎引力般的彼此远离，让马丁·路德（Martin Luther）在奥古斯丁（Augustine）的基础上称我们为「Homo incurvatus in se」（向自身弯曲的人）：一种朝自己内部蜷缩的人性。我认为这是关于罪的较有用的定义之一。

Helpfully, Jesus had a lot of other less cryptic things to say about learning to bear each other and be together. The New Testament takes it as a given that humans are messy, dishonest, selfish, misguided, that we will hurt and anger each other. We may be made for and by each other, but it assumes there will be days when we wish we were not. This tendency to disconnect, this almost gravitational pulling away from each other led Martin Luther, building on Augustine, to call us ‘Homo incurvatus in se’: humanity turned in on itself. I find it one of the more useful definitions of sin.

《新约》在很大程度上是一组书信——往来于我们今天或可视为社群主义实验的诸群体之间，并不停地与我们向内转的倾向角力。早期信徒在尝试共享生活与财物时那些鸡毛蒜皮的口角，就白纸黑字摊在纸面上，人人可见。困难本身就是要义的一部分，而非回避它的理由。

The New Testament is largely a set of letters between what we might see as communitarian experiments, and reckoning with our inward-turning tendencies. The petty squabbles between early believers trying to share their lives and possessions are there on the page for all to see. The difficulty was part of the point, not a reason to avoid it.

或许正是这种务实，使得本文尽管承认共同体生活实实在在的益处，却不成为一则为之高声叫卖的广告。代价是真实的，所以你需要一个有力的故事来托住你。一套关于好生活模样的、截然不同的剧本。我不认为它非得是宗教的，但它必须有力量，而且必须超越个人利益。把我的生活建立在相互照料与互惠之上，是我此生做过的最好的事，但它与我与生俱来的那套剧本之间的摩擦，有时仍会磨出水泡。在它向我索取得多于我领受的日子里，让我留下的，是那个「为什么」。

This realism might be why, despite its very real benefits, this is not a full-throated advertisement for communal life. The costs are real, so you need a powerful story to hold you. A completely different script of what a good life looks like. I don’t think it necessarily has to be a religious one, but it does need to be powerful, and go beyond individual interest. Setting up my life for mutual care and reciprocity is the best thing I have ever done, but its chafing against the script I was born into still leaves blisters sometimes. On days when it asks more of me than I am receiving, it is the ‘why’ that makes me stay.

我希望，我们能集体（重新）发现一些宏大的「为什么」，希望更多人受启发去试验彼此走近。我认为，气候变化、人工智能（AI）以及它们所有目前无法预见的效应对这个世界的掀翻，很可能会迫使我们如此。这终究是灵魂的功课。我们这个家先行了几步，不过一门先修课而已；而随着旧剧本的残余日渐磨损，事情正变得越来越顺。我想——我希望？——我比从前更有耐心、更少小气；更安稳，也更不抗拒分享。更朝向一种严苛的爱，因而也更能在这个受伤的世界里派上一小点用场。至于这到底是事实，还是又一层自欺，这个问题，你得去问我的室友们。

I hope that, collectively, we can (re)discover some big whys, that more people are inspired to experiment with moving towards each other. I think the upending of the world by climate change, AI and all their currently unforeseen effects will likely force us into it. It is, ultimately, soul work. Our household has had a few years’ head start, no more than a foundation course, yet it is getting easier as the vestiges of the old script become threadbare. I think – I hope? – that I am more patient and less petty; more steady and less resistant to sharing. More oriented towards a rigorous love, and so a tiny bit more of use in this hurting world. Whether that is in fact reality or self-delusion is a question you would have to ask my housemates.
