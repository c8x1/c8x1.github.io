藏在眼皮底下的救命科学知识

Helen Pearson·Aeon·2026年10月9日·12分钟阅读·原文

浏览新闻时，你偶尔也许会被那些反反复复的科学与健康新闻弄得一头雾水：这周说咖啡有益健康，下周又说它有害。红葡萄酒能延年益寿，转眼间又不能了。随着时间推移，新的科学研究似乎彼此矛盾，让我们不知道该相信哪些证据。

When reading the news, you may occasionally be bemused by the flip-flopping science and health stories: one week you’re told coffee is good for you, the next week that it’s bad. Red wine extends your life, then it doesn’t. Over time, new scientific studies seemingly contradict one another, leaving us unsure what evidence to believe.

作为一名科学记者，我逐渐明白，真正的问题往往在于每项研究都被孤立地报道，而没有与我们已知的其他一切相互权衡。有时，这样做的危害风险很高。例如，2025 年 9 月，我报道了美国食品药品监督管理局（US Food and Drug Administration，FDA）宣布将在止痛药对乙酰氨基酚（acetaminophen，即扑热息痛）上加注警示标签，声称孕期服用该药会增加孩子患自闭症的风险。这登上了全球新闻头条。然而，仔细审视相关科学就会发现，FDA 及其上级卫生部门明显是挑拣了少数几项研究，而淡化了许多更为可靠的发现。几个月后，研究人员发表了一项更能代表全部知识积累的重要综述，证实了这一点。该综述的结论是：「现有证据并不能明确地将孕期母亲服用扑热息痛与后代患自闭症或注意缺陷多动障碍（ADHD）联系起来。」当然，到那时为止，无数女性已经被这种被广泛视为孕期最安全药物之一的止痛药无谓地吓到了。

As a science journalist, I’ve learnt that often the actual problem is that each study is reported in isolation, rather than weighed against everything else we know. And sometimes, the risks of harm are high. For example, in September 2025, I reported on the US Food and Drug Administration’s announcement that it would add a warning label to the painkiller acetaminophen (paracetamol), claiming that taking the drug during pregnancy increased a child’s risk of autism. It made global headlines. Yet looking closer at the science, it was clear that the FDA and its parent health department had cherrypicked a few studies, and downplayed other more robust findings. This was confirmed a couple of months later when researchers published a major review that better represented the full body of knowledge. ‘Existing evidence does not clearly link maternal paracetamol use during pregnancy with autism or ADHD in offspring,’ it concluded. By then, of course, countless women had been needlessly scared about a painkiller that is widely considered one of the safest to take during pregnancy.

2025 年，全球学界发表了约 700 万篇学术论文——相当于每天超过 19000 篇。表面上看，这似乎是个值得庆祝的数字，但它也带来了一个问题。随着研究体量急剧膨胀，我们很难辨清这几百万篇论文究竟告诉了我们什么。在这股倾泻而下的信息洪流中，既有严谨的方法和重要的发现，也有令人困惑的矛盾、未经证实的结果和粗劣的科学。此外，一些多元形态的知识——比如亲历经验和原住民的洞见——几乎从未被学术文章和数据库收录。

In 2025, academics worldwide published about 7 million scholarly articles ­– that’s more than 19,000 each day. On the surface, that might seem like a number to celebrate, but it also poses a problem. As the volume of research balloons, it can be hard to discern what these millions of papers actually tell us. Within the firehose, there are rigorous methodologies and important findings, but also baffling contradictions, unconfirmed results and sloppy science. And some diverse forms of knowledge, such as lived experience and Indigenous insights, are rarely captured in scholarly articles and databases at all.

其成因是系统性的。科学家连篇累牍地发表和宣传论文，因为这是他们职业晋升的途径。记者们气喘吁吁地追逐最新、最抢眼的研究，好让自己的标题在网上赢得点击。与此同时，几乎没有人花时间去仔细综合、盘点现有知识，从而弄清这个世界已经知道了什么。伊恩·查默斯（Iain Chalmers）是一位医生，也是总部位于伦敦的证据综合组织科克伦协作网（Cochrane Collaboration）的联合创始人，他曾将此称为「科学未能以科学方式积累证据的丑闻性失败」。

The causes are systemic. Scientists publish and promote one paper after another because that’s how they advance in their careers. Journalists breathlessly chase the latest, flashiest studies so their headlines get clicks online. Meanwhile, hardly any of us spend time trying to make sense of what the world already knows by carefully synthesising and taking stock of existing knowledge. Iain Chalmers, a doctor who co-founded the Cochrane Collaboration, an evidence-synthesis group based in London, once called this the ‘scandalous failure of science to cumulate evidence scientifically’.

正是这种失败，促使我写下了《超越信念：证据如何揭示真正有效的方法》（Beyond Belief: How Evidence Shows What Really Works，2026）一书。在为该书做研究时，我发现了更好地理解世界的方法——但这些方法的知名度远未达到应有的程度。如果它们广为人知，我们在相信基于单一研究的新闻报道之前也许会先停一停，转而认识到真正的工作所在：寻找、筛选和综合证据。这份并不光鲜的劳动，对我们生活的塑造早已超过任何一项单独的研究或任何一条新闻标题。我们或许还会意识到，对于人类面临的许多棘手难题，我们其实早已掌握了所需的大部分知识。所需要的，只是把这些知识整合起来的能力——然后付诸行动。

This failure was a key motivation to write my book Beyond Belief: How Evidence Shows What Really Works (2026). Researching it, I discovered better ways to make sense of the world – but these methods are not as widely known as they should be. If they were, we might pause before believing news stories based on single studies, and instead recognise the real work: finding, sorting and synthesising evidence. This unglamorous labour already shapes our lives far more than any individual study or news headline will. We might also realise that, for many of the wicked problems we face, humans already possess much of the knowledge needed to solve them. All it needs is the ability to assemble it – and then act.

证据综合历史上最重大的事件之一，发生在 1976 年旧金山的一家酒店里。在那里举行的美国教育研究协会年会上，统计学家吉恩·格拉斯（Gene Glass）公布了一项精巧的技术，展示了如何整合大量单项研究的发现。这项技术后来对科学如此重要，以至于它拥有了一部专门的传记。

One of the biggest events in the history of evidence synthesis occurred in a hotel in San Francisco in 1976. There, at the annual meeting of the American Educational Research Association, the statistician Gene Glass revealed an ingenious technique that showed how to integrate findings from a large volume of individual studies. This technique would become so important to science that it has its own biography.

格拉斯的工作源于他自己与心理健康问题的搏斗。大约十年前，他刚读完心理测量学与统计学博士，正饱受焦虑和神经症的折磨。他开始每周接受心理治疗——这让他受益匪浅——并在治疗中坚持了八年。但他的积极体验与学界的主流观点相悖，后者坚持认为心理治疗收效甚微。这在很大程度上源于心理学家汉斯·艾森克（Hans Eysenck）的工作——他那些影响深远的心理治疗研究综述得出的结论是：心理治疗毫无价值——充其量只有安慰剂效应。

Glass’s work had emerged from his own struggles with mental health. A decade earlier, when he had finished his PhD in psychometrics and statistics, he was suffering from anxiety and neurosis. He began weekly psychotherapy sessions – which helped him hugely – and stayed in therapy for eight years. But his positive experience ran counter to the weight of academic opinion, which maintained that psychotherapy had little benefit. This stemmed in large part from the work of the psychologist Hans Eysenck, whose influential reviews of psychotherapy research concluded that it was worthless – or had a placebo effect at best.

格拉斯对此颇为恼火。这不仅意味着他把钱白白扔在了一种无效的治疗上，而且他觉得艾森克做综述的方法本身就有缺陷。

Glass was irritated. Not only did this suggest that he’d flushed away money on ineffective therapy, but he felt Eysenck’s reviewing methods were flawed.

艾森克和当时其他研究者一样，习惯采用所谓「计票法」：数一数有多少研究表明某种疗法有效，又有多少研究没有发现它有用，哪边「票数」多哪边就赢。计票法虽然直观，作为综合研究的方式却具有误导性，部分原因在于它忽略了效应的大小。一项研究可能发现 100 人中有 55 人在接受某种治疗后好转，另一项研究则发现 100 人中有 85 人好转。尽管第二项研究显示疗法的效应要强得多，两项研究却都只贡献一票。格拉斯看出这很有问题——他还惊讶地发现，艾森克仅仅因为数百项有效研究发表在学位论文而非学术期刊上，就武断地将它们排除在外。

Eysenck – like other researchers at the time – tended to do what’s called ‘vote-counting’: counting up the number of studies showing a treatment has benefits, and the number that did not find it helped. The one with the most ‘votes’ wins. Intuitive though vote-counting is, it’s also misleading as a way of synthesising studies, partly because it ignores the size of the effects. One study may find that 55 people out of 100 improved after a particular treatment, and another that 85 out of 100 did. Even though the second study showed the treatment had a much stronger effect, both studies contribute one vote. Glass could see this was problematic ­– and was also astonished that Eysenck arbitrarily excluded hundreds of valid studies just because they were in theses or dissertations rather than published in academic journals.

听众为之震撼。这里终于有了一种办法，能从表面纷乱、各不相同的结果中提炼出意义

The audience was thunderstruck. Here was a way to extract meaning from an apparent mess of different results

格拉斯决心做得更好。他和妻子——心理学家玛丽·李·史密斯——系统地搜罗了他们能找到的每一份将心理治疗的效果与对照组或其他疗法进行比较的研究，最终收集到 370 多项。

Glass set out to do a better job. He and his wife, a psychologist named Mary Lee Smith, systematically hunted down every scrap of research they could find that compared the effect of psychotherapy with a control group or another therapy – ending up with more than 370 studies.

随后，格拉斯和史密斯想出了一个办法，提取并合并每项研究中对心理治疗效应的测量。即使一项研究测量的是该疗法对焦虑的影响，另一项测量的是它对血压的影响，他们也设计出一种方法，把这些结果换算成一个统一的标准量度——「效应量」——然后对所有研究取平均。（这就有点像把各种货币都换算成美元，以便合并到一起。）格拉斯把这种新的统计方法称为元分析——对分析的分析，正如元数据是关于数据的数据。

Then Glass and Smith worked out a way to extract and combine the measurements of psychotherapy’s effect in each study. Even though one study measured the effects of the therapy on anxiety and another its impact on blood pressure, they devised a way to convert these results into one standard measure known as ‘effect size’ and then average them across all the studies. (This is somewhat like converting various currencies into dollars, so they can all be combined.) Glass called this new statistical method a meta-analysis – an analysis of analyses, just as metadata is data about data.

在这项工作上辛苦两年之后，格拉斯和史密斯得出结论：心理治疗确实有益。格拉斯在举办那场教育会议的旧金山酒店里展示了这一方法。关于心理治疗是否有效、何时有效、对谁有效，争论延续至今。（艾森克称这项工作是「一场超级愚蠢的演练」。）但对元分析本身，听众为之震撼。这里终于有了一种办法，能从表面纷乱、各不相同的结果中提炼出意义。

After toiling on this work for two years, Glass and Smith concluded that psychotherapy had a beneficial effect, and Glass presented the method at the San Francisco hotel where the education meeting was taking place. Debate continues to this day about whether psychotherapy works, when and for whom. (Eysenck called the work ‘an exercise in mega-silliness’.) But of the meta-analysis, the audience was thunderstruck. Here was a way to extract meaning from an apparent mess of different results.

元分析并非这一时期出现的唯一综合工具。格拉斯、史密斯以及其他研究者的工作还催生了系统综述——一种结构严谨的证据收集与评估方法。在系统综述中，研究者遍查世界各地的数据库，搜集针对某一问题的所有已发表和未发表的研究；然后从数以千计的研究长名单中筛选出最相关的少数几项，评估其可靠性，提取数据并合并结果。许多系统综述还包含一项元分析，用来汇集入选研究的结果，并估计某种治疗或其他干预措施的总体效应。

The meta-analysis was not the only synthesis tool to emerge around this time. The work of Glass, Smith and other researchers also led to the systematic review, a rigorously structured method for gathering and evaluating evidence. In a systematic review, researchers scour databases worldwide of published and unpublished work for all studies that address a certain question. Then they whittle down a longlist of thousands of studies to the most relevant few, assess their reliability, extract the data, and combine the results. Many systematic reviews include a meta-analysis to pool results from the included studies and estimate the overall effect of a treatment or other intervention.

这些帮助人们理解整套证据的工具，是过去几十年科学界最重要的发展之一。它们有能力识别出一些重要结论，而这些是单独评估每项基础研究所永远无法得出的。这相当于科学版的「见森林，而非只见树木」。

These tools to make sense of a body of evidence are one of the most important developments in science over the past few decades. They have the power to identify important conclusions that would never be possible from assessing each underlying study on its own. It’s the scientific equivalent of seeing the forest, not just the trees.

这些方法悄无声息地改变了现代生活，在健康决策方面尤其如此。在医学领域，这一进程的部分功劳，要归于证据综合的早期倡导者查默斯在 1991 年 5 月的一个清晨、沿着英国牛津的伍尔弗科特磨坊溪（Wolvercote Mill Stream）散步的那段经历。

Without fanfare, these approaches have changed modern life – and particularly so when it comes to health decisions. In medicine, this happened partly thanks to a morning stroll by Iain Chalmers, an early champion of evidence synthesis, along the Wolvercote Mill Stream in Oxford, UK, in May 1991.

查默斯刚刚完成一项开创性的、历时十年的项目：综合妊娠与分娩治疗领域临床试验的全部证据。这项工作涉及数百项系统综述，结果表明，许多标准医疗做法——比如分娩时剃除产妇阴毛、实施外科会阴切开术——几乎没有证据支撑，有些甚至有害。查默斯及其同事的工作帮助改变了其中一些做法。

Chalmers had just finished a pioneering, decade-long project to synthesise all the evidence from clinical trials on treatments in pregnancy and childbirth. This work, which involved doing hundreds of systematic reviews, had shown that many standard medical practices – such as shaving women’s pubic hair during labour, and surgical episiotomies – were based on little evidence and some were harmful. The work from Chalmers and his colleagues helped change some of these practices.

此时，查默斯正在考虑扩大这项工作。他想，要是把医学与医疗保健每个领域的临床试验都综合起来，岂不是大有用处？那样一来，医生和病人就能基于证据，了解治疗糖尿病、癌症、心脏病以及许多其他疾病的有效方法。令人惊讶的是，当时的许多医疗决策依据的是传统惯例或资深医生未经验证的个人意见，而非研究证据。

Now Chalmers was thinking about expanding this work. Wouldn’t it be useful, he thought, to synthesise clinical trials in every area of medicine and healthcare? Then doctors and patients would know, based on evidence, effective ways of treating diabetes, cancer, heart disease and many other conditions. Surprisingly, many medical decisions at that time were based on conventional wisdom or the unsubstantiated opinions of senior doctors rather than on evidence from research.

早在几年前的 1979 年，在威尔士加的夫工作的医生阿奇·科克伦就曾向医学界发起过这样的挑战。他写道：「我们这个职业竟然没有按专科或亚专科组织起一份定期更新的、涵盖所有相关随机对照试验的批判性摘要，这无疑是对我们行业的莫大批评。」查默斯深受科克伦的影响，如今他准备承担这项任务。他和团队开始在学术数据库中检索临床试验，并到图书馆里逐本翻查期刊。任务规模如此庞大，他们招募了志愿者加入搜寻，包括老年人团体，甚至还有出人意料的来源——牛津的海丁顿草地滚球俱乐部。很快，他们就收集到了数以万计的试验。

Some years earlier, in 1979, a doctor called Archie Cochrane working in Cardiff, Wales, had challenged the medical community to do just this. ‘It is surely a great criticism of our profession that we have not organised a critical summary, by speciality or subspecialty, adapted periodically, of all relevant randomised controlled trials,’ Cochrane wrote. Chalmers was greatly influenced by Cochrane, and he was now ready to take on the task. He and his team started searching for clinical trials in online academic databases and scouring journals by hand in the library. The task was so big that they recruited volunteers to join the hunt, including elderly people’s groups and even the unlikely source of the Headington Bowls Club in Oxford. Soon, they had tens of thousands of trials.

大多数看过西医的人，都曾在不知情的情况下受益于系统综述

Most people who have seen a Western doctor have unknowingly benefited from systematic reviews

但查默斯知道，要综合医学有效治疗的全部证据，是一项无比浩大的工程，所需的人力远不止于此。于是，1993 年，他和一群志同道合者创立了科克伦协作网（现简称为 Cochrane），致力于就健康治疗的疗效证据产出高质量的系统综述。

But Chalmers knew that synthesising all the evidence on effective treatments in medicine was such a gargantuan task that it would take more people power than this. So, in 1993, he and a group of like-minded folk started the Cochrane Collaboration (now known as just Cochrane), dedicated to producing high-quality systematic reviews of evidence on the effectiveness of health treatments.

创立后十年之内，科克伦协作网发表了约 2000 篇系统综述，并帮助推广了这类研究。到 2010 年，协作网内外的研究者每天发表 11 篇医疗保健领域的系统综述。

Within 10 years of starting, the Cochrane Collaboration had published around 2,000 systematic reviews and was helping to popularise this type of study. By 2010, researchers inside and outside the collaboration were publishing 11 systematic reviews on healthcare every day.

如今，「科克伦综述」以最严谨、最可靠的科学综合之一著称，而系统综述也被用来制定指导医生决策的临床指南。大多数看过西医的人，都曾在不知情的情况下受益于系统综述。它们已成为医学赖以立足的隐形基石。

Today, the ‘Cochrane review’ is known for being one of the most rigorous and reliable syntheses of science, and systematic reviews are used to develop the clinical guidelines that doctors use to guide decisions. Most people who have seen a Western doctor have unknowingly benefited from systematic reviews. They have become an invisible bedrock of evidence on which medicine is based.

想知道没有它们的世界会是什么样子，不妨看看这条在 20 世纪中叶长期无人质疑的臭名昭著的医学建议。1957 年，儿科医生本杰明·斯波克在其育儿畅销书《育儿常识》（The Common Sense Book of Baby and Childcare，1946 年初版，此后多年多次再版）的第二版中做了一处小小的改动。他说，父母应该让婴儿趴着睡，而不是仰着睡。到了 20 世纪 80 年代和 90 年代，研究已清楚地表明，这是儿童健康史上最致命的毫无根据的建议之一，因为它导致婴儿猝死综合征（SIDS）的病例急剧增加。

To understand what the world would look like without them, consider this notorious piece of medical advice that went unchallenged in the mid-20th century. In 1957, the paediatrician Benjamin Spock made a small change to the second edition of his parenting bestseller The Common Sense Book of Baby and Childcare (1946), reissued several more times over the years. He said that parents should put babies to sleep on their fronts rather than on their backs. By the 1980s and ’90s, studies were clearly showing that this was one of the most lethal pieces of unsubstantiated advice in the history of child health, as it contributed to a steep rise in sudden infant death syndrome (SIDS).

这个故事更令人痛心之处在于：如果研究者当时综合各项研究的证据，而不是一项一项孤立地看，睡姿与婴儿猝死综合征之间的关联本可能更早被发现。当研究者终于在 2005 年发表的一项系统综述和元分析中这样做时，他们发现，早在 1970 年，趴睡与婴儿猝死综合征之间的关联就已清晰可见——这比大多数父母得到风险警告早了大约 20 年。他们估算，如果证据能更早得到综合并付诸行动，欧洲、澳大拉西亚和美国至少 5 万名婴儿的死亡或许本可以避免。

What makes this story even more tragic is that the link between sleeping position and SIDS might have been detected earlier – if researchers had synthesised the evidence from studies rather than looking at them one at a time. When researchers did this with a systematic review and meta-analysis published in 2005, they discovered that the link between front-sleeping and SIDS was clear in 1970, some 20 years before most parents were warned about the risks. They calculated that at least 50,000 infant deaths in Europe, Australasia and the United States might have been prevented if evidence had been synthesised and acted on earlier.

医学并非唯一积累了证据综合宝库的领域。其他学科——从生态保护到教育——都效仿了医学模式。

Medicine is not the only field to have built up a repository of evidence syntheses. Other disciplines – ranging from conservation to education – have mimicked the medical model.

非营利组织国际影响评估倡议（International Initiative for Impact Evaluation）收录了 1700 多篇系统综述，内容关于国际发展领域哪些做法有效，例如改善卫生设施、应对营养不良。大型环境数据库 CEEDER 则收录了 2100 多篇证据综述，探讨哪些方法能有效拯救物种、保护地球。

The nonprofit International Initiative for Impact Evaluation holds more than 1,700 systematic reviews on what works in international development, such as improving sanitation and tackling malnourishment. CEEDER, a major environmental database, hosts more than 2,100 evidence reviews on what works to save species and protect the planet.

教育捐赠基金会是伦敦一家致力于提升弱势儿童学习成果的非营利组织，它利用系统综述——综合了检验各类教育方法的研究——构建了一套广受赞誉的「教与学工具箱」。如今，英格兰近七成的学校领导者在做支出决策时都会用到它；它已被改编用于 30 多个国家，并翻译成九种语言。

The Education Endowment Foundation, a nonprofit organisation in London dedicated to boosting learning outcomes for disadvantaged children, has constructed a widely admired ‘teaching and learning toolkit’ using systematic reviews of studies that have tested education approaches. Nearly 70 per cent of school leaders in England now use it to make spending decisions, and it has been adapted for use in more than 30 countries and translated into nine languages.

政府间气候变化专门委员会（IPCC）的综合报告表明人类正在引发气候变化，从而帮助推动了全球行动

The IPCC’s syntheses have helped to inspire global action by showing that humans are causing climate change

工具箱显示，最有效的策略之一是教学生元认知——即学会如何学习的能力。（例如，一个孩子认识到把解题步骤写下来帮她解出了一道数学题，她就是在练习元认知。）根据这份证据综述，引入元认知平均能让孩子在一年之内额外取得多达八个月的学习进步。相比之下，让学生留级反而会使学习倒退约两个月。

One of the most effective strategies, it shows, is teaching students metacognition – the ability to learn how to learn. (For instance, a child who learns that writing out the steps helped her solve a maths problem is practising metacognition.) Introducing metacognition provides children with an impressive eight months of additional learning progress, on average, over a year, according to the evidence review. Repeating a school year, by contrast, sets back students’ learning by about two months.

最近，气候科学家们也开始从医学那里取经。他们对综合证据并不陌生：自 1988 年以来，政府间气候变化专门委员会已发布六份分量沉重的科学评估报告，它们大概是有史以来规模最大、影响最深远的证据综合——通过表明人类正在引发气候变化，帮助推动了全球行动。但迄今为止，IPCC 的报告对解决方案着墨较少，比如哪些气候政策能带来最大帮助。

More recently, climate scientists have been taking a leaf out of medicine’s book. They are used to synthesising evidence: the six massive scientific assessments that the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) has published since 1988 are probably the biggest and most influential evidence syntheses ever done – helping inspire global action by showing that humans are causing climate change. But the IPCC reports have so far said less about solutions, such as which climate policies could help the most.

一个名为 What Works Climate Solutions 的组织成立于 2024 年，目前正努力建设一个类似科克伦的证据综合库，展示减排或适应全球变暖的最有效途径。这个证据库将为 IPCC 下一份科学评估提供输入——该评估定于 2029 年底前发表——并应能帮助各国政府选择该采纳哪些政策。

A group called What Works Climate Solutions, which started in 2024, is now striving to build a Cochrane-like bank of evidence syntheses that show the most effective ways to cut emissions or adapt to global warming. This evidence bank will feed into the next scientific assessment of the IPCC, due to be published by late 2029, and should help governments choose which policies to adopt.

系统综述的爆炸式流行在学术数据库中清晰可见。仅医学领域的发表量，就从 2000 年的约 1400 篇增长到 2019 年的逾 29000 篇——大约每天 80 篇。一套名为 PRISMA 的规范操作指南，已成为 21 世纪被引用最多的论文之一——据一种统计，被引用次数高达 138000 次。

The soaring popularity of systematic reviews is visible in academic databases. The number published in medicine alone rose from around 1,400 in the year 2000 to more than 29,000 in 2019 – about 80 per day. A set of guidelines for performing them accurately, called PRISMA, has become one of the most cited papers of the 21st century – with as many as 138,000 citations, by one count.

系统综述也有批评者。其一在于，它们按惯例偏重量化研究——即带有数值数据的研究——而忽视了定性研究，例如基于访谈和观察的研究。关于如何最好地整合不同类型的信息流，证据综合专家们正展开一场热烈的争论。

Systematic reviews have their critics. One is that they conventionally emphasise quantitative studies – those with numerical data – and ignore qualitative research, such as that based on interviews and observations. There is a lively debate going on among evidence-synthesis experts about how best to integrate different streams of information.

在生态保护等一些领域，科学家因偏爱西方科学、轻视其他形态的知识而受到诟病，比如当地社群和原住民群体的亲历经验。例如，加拿大的一些原住民对他们所猎捕的洄游鱼类的习性有着广博的知识。这类证据无法装进只会摆弄数字的传统综述里。

In some fields, such as conservation, scientists have been reproached for privileging Western science over other forms of knowledge, such as the lived experience of local communities and Indigenous groups. Some Indigenous peoples in Canada, for example, have extensive knowledge of the behaviours of the migratory fish that they hunt. These types of evidence don’t fit in conventional reviews that just crunch numbers.

但办法是有的。2021 年，加拿大公园管理局与一个名为 Foundations of Success 的组织，为决定是否在贾斯珀国家公园推行一项昂贵而高风险的濒危驯鹿圈养繁殖计划，开展了一次非同寻常的证据分析。他们不仅从研究中收集了多方面的证据，还请原住民群体、动物园以及来自芬兰的驯鹿专家审阅证据，并就是否推进达成一致。（他们确实推进了——2025 年，几只可爱的驯鹿幼崽降生了。）

But there are ways to combine them. In 2021, Parks Canada and a group called Foundations of Success undertook an extraordinary analysis of evidence to decide whether to pursue an expensive and risky captive breeding programme for endangered caribou in Jasper National Park. As well as collecting a range of evidence from research, they asked people from Indigenous groups, zoos, and reindeer experts from Finland, to review the evidence and reach agreement about whether to proceed. (They did – and some adorable caribou calves were born in 2025.)

过去几十年里，研究者开发出了一套方法组合，用于合并不同类型的证据。有些只汇总定性研究，另一些「混合方法」综合则将定性与量化研究结合起来。一些为忙碌的政府决策者提供证据的研究者青睐快速综述——在可用的时间内（有时只到第二天）尽力做出最好的证据综合，来回答他们的问题。他们宁要一份按时交付的优质综述，也不要一份来得太晚的完美综述。

Over the past few decades, researchers have developed a suite of methods for combining evidence of different types. Some pool only qualitative studies, while other ‘mixed-methods’ syntheses combine qualitative and quantitative work. Some researchers who supply evidence to busy government policymakers favour the rapid review, which involves getting together the best evidence synthesis you can, to answer their question in the time available – which could be the next day. They prefer a good synthesis delivered on time rather than a perfect one that arrives too late.

声称对证据提供概览的研究自带权威分量，因此科学家有责任把它们做对

Studies that claim to provide an overview of evidence convey authority, so scientists have a responsibility to get them right

在别处，科学家们还在开发另一项重要创新：动态综述。这类综述随着新研究的发表而频繁、持续地更新，而不是像常见的那样，做成一份很快就会过时的静态系统综述。动态综述可能相当耗费人力，因此最值得用于新研究层出不穷的重要问题。新冠疫情期间，几个团队很快集结起来，开发动态综述来综合快速涌现的新冠疗法和疫苗研究。

Elsewhere, scientists have been working on another important innovation called living reviews. These are updated frequently and continuously as research is published rather than, as often happens, a static systematic review that quickly becomes out of date. Living reviews can be labour intensive and so are most worthwhile for important questions on which new studies are being published quickly. During the COVID-19 pandemic, a handful of groups soon rallied and developed living reviews to synthesise rapidly emerging studies on COVID therapies and vaccines.

过去 20 年间，综合科学的方法变得如此复杂，以至于证据综合本身已成为一门独立的职业。有整本整本专门研究这门手艺的期刊，也有专门的学术中心、讲席教职和奖项。作为一名科学记者，我采访过一些「证据极客」，他们能就系统综述的细枝末节聊上几个小时。

The methods used for synthesising science have become so complex over the past 20 years that evidence synthesis has become a profession in its own right. There are entire journals dedicated to the craft, as well as academic centres, professorships and prizes. As a science journalist, I’ve interviewed evidence geeks who can talk for hours about the finer details of systematic reviews.

有时，关于综合一批研究的最佳方式，紧张气氛也会冒头。纯粹主义者主张，金标准的系统综述是最严谨的做法，其他方法有可能漏掉研究、或纳入劣质工作，从而得出错误结论。实用主义者则反驳说，系统综述太耗时，而且很多时候，一份快速而粗糙的「铝标准」综合就够用了。

And sometimes tensions bubble up about the best way to synthesise a body of research. A purist argues that a gold-standard systematic review is the most rigorous way of doing it and that other methods risk missing studies or including poor-quality work and thus reaching the wrong conclusion. A pragmatist argues that a systematic review takes too long, and that often a quick-and-dirty aluminium-standard synthesis will do.

但说到底，这只是茶杯里的风暴。不同的证据综合方法适用于不同情境。重要的是，对所用的方法及其任何缺陷保持透明。声称对证据提供严谨概览的研究自带权威分量，因此科学家肩负着把它们做对的重任。

But, really, this is a storm in a teacup. Different methods for evidence synthesis suit different situations. What’s important is to be transparent about the methods used and any weaknesses they have. Studies that claim to provide a rigorous overview of evidence convey authority, so scientists have a heavy responsibility to get them right.

在一个新闻报道或社交媒体帖子几小时内就能传遍四方的时代，系统综述面临的最大问题之一就是耗时。一项针对 500 多篇科克伦综述的分析发现，一篇综述通常要花将近三年——有一篇甚至用了冰川般漫长的八年。这是因为做系统综述要完成大约 25 个步骤——小心地查找、筛选和合并研究——其中许多步骤甚至要重复做两遍以避免出错。这一严苛的过程解释了为什么许多研究者一想到要做系统综述就心生畏惧。

In a world where a news story or a social media post can spread in hours, one of the biggest problems in systematic reviewing is the time it takes. An analysis of more than 500 Cochrane reviews found that a review typically took nearly three years ­– and one took a glacial eight. That’s because doing a systematic review involves working through around 25 steps – carefully finding, filtering, and combining studies – of which many are even done in duplicate to avoid errors. This exacting process explains why many researchers view with horror the prospect of doing a systematic review.

研究者长期以来都借助计算机帮忙，而生成式 AI 的飞速进步，正让人们对加速和自动化这项任务燃起新的热情。AI 能否帮助我们读懂浩如烟海的科学文献？

Researchers have long used computers to help, but the rapid advances in generative AI are fuelling new excitement about accelerating and automating the task. Could AI help make sense of the overwhelmingly large scientific literature?

它改变了我作为科学记者以及普通人在日常生活中思考研究的方式

It has changed the way I think about research as a science journalist and in everyday life

初创公司正在竞相开发能够查找、筛选和总结研究论文的 AI 系统，但其中大多数尚未经过充分检验，也未被可靠地证明能产出高质量的系统综述。即便如此，它们终将改进，这一点似乎不可避免；用不了太久，汇整证据所需的大部分苦差事就会常规性地交给机器完成，把人解放出来去核查和解读结果。那可能意味着，任何人几乎只需按下按钮，就能就自己的问题组装出一份可靠的证据综合——一个诱人的目标。

Start-ups are racing to develop AI-powered systems that can find, sort and summarise research publications, but most have not yet been well tested and shown reliably to produce high-quality systematic reviews. Even so, it seems inevitable that they will improve and that before too long most of the grunt work involved in compiling evidence will routinely be done by machines, freeing up humans to check and interpret the results. That could mean that anyone would be able to assemble a reliable evidence synthesis tailored to their question, virtually at the push of a button – an appealing goal.

但这一目标仍有相当距离——关于 AI 与证据综合的担忧也同样层出不穷。其一，AI 正导致大量不准确的科学综述涌现，因为研究者用这些工具飞速赶制劣质之作。其二，人们可能会干脆完全绕过严谨的证据综合：既然动动嘴问问 ChatGPT「证据说了什么」就行，又何必费那个劲？（目前这样做很危险，因为 AI 工具可能产生幻觉、给出不准确的图景，而且大多数工具并不能访问全世界研究文献的完整文库。）

But it’s still some way off – and concerns about AI and evidence synthesis also abound. One is that AI is leading to a flood of inaccurate scientific reviews because researchers are using these tools to race through poor-quality efforts. Another is that people will bypass rigorous evidence syntheses entirely: because why bother, when you can just ask ChatGPT what the evidence says? (This is currently dangerous as AI tools can hallucinate and give an inaccurate picture, and most do not have access to the full corpus of the world’s research.)

这一切带来的一个教训是：要意识到单项研究可能只是离群值——这是我自己在过去几年里学到的。我曾与世界各地数百位研究者交谈，谈论综合证据的重要性；它改变了我作为科学记者以及普通人在日常生活中思考研究的方式。如今，当科学家向我介绍他们的最新研究时，我会尽量追问它与更广泛的研究整体如何契合。在为个人问题寻找证据时——比如一项医学诊断——我倾向于检索系统综述和其他综合（同时也明白并非所有综述都是高质量的）。

One lesson from all this is to be aware that single studies can be outliers – something I’ve learned myself over the past few years. I’ve spoken with hundreds of researchers worldwide about the importance of synthesising evidence, and it has changed the way I think about research as a science journalist and in everyday life. Now, when I speak to scientists about their latest study, I try to ask how it fits with the wider body of research. When seeking evidence on a personal issue, such as a medical diagnosis, I tend to search for systematic reviews and other syntheses (while being aware that not all reviews are good quality).

我们应当抵御新研究的诱惑与光环。新研究确实重要，因为它带来创新，带来保护地球和人类生命的新途径。但如果真想理解世界、让世界变得更好，我们同样需要去做那些不那么光鲜、无人喝彩的工作：整理已有的知识——并弄清这一切究竟意味着什么。

We should resist the allure and glamour of new studies. It’s true that new research is important, because it leads to innovation and fresh ways to protect the planet and human lives. But if we truly want to understand the world and make it a better place, we also need to do the less glamorous, unappreciated work of collating existing knowledge – and figuring out what it all means.
