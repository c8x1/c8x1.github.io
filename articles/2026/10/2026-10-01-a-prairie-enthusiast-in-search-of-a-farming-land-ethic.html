一个草原爱好者，寻索农耕的土地伦理

Craig Maier·Aeon·2026年10月1日·15分钟阅读·原文

四十块铁制的矩形，每一块都方钝如琴键，旋转成一团模糊的狂怒，把秸秆粉碎机（flail chopper）心脏处那根八英尺长的转轴都藏了起来。我高踞于数吨钢铁之上，听我调遣的有八十马力。农活的这一环简单直白——连我这种机械上笨手笨脚的抱树环保迷也能应付：踩下离合器，挂进二挡，再把油门缓缓推向前。拖拉机那高大的深纹轮胎搅动着，转成平顺的旋圈。一切都在旋转：风扇、皮带、交流发电机、传动轴、车轴。我并不完全明白其中的机械原理，可拖拉机、粉碎机和我一道，被推着向前，掠过一片长满粗硬须芒的玉米地——须芒已长得齐膝高。

Forty iron rectangles, each one blunt as a piano key, rotate into a blurring fury that hides the eight-foot-long shaft at the heart of the flail chopper. I am perched atop several tons of iron, with 80 horsepower at my command. This part of farming is straightforward – even a mechanically challenged tree-hugger like me can manage to depress the clutch, shift into second, and ease the throttle forward. The tractor’s tall, deeply treaded tires churn, revolving into a smooth spin. Everything is spinning: fans, belts, alternator, the driveshaft, axles. I don’t understand the mechanism fully, yet the tractor and chopper and I are propelled forward across the knee-high whiskers of a coarsely bearded cornfield.

威斯康星州（Wisconsin）南部正值小阳春。我、妻子和我们年幼的儿子住在她家的农场上。我们俩都在农场之外谋职，但每到周末和许多傍晚，我便做岳父大卫（David）的学徒。今天，我们正抓住一段短暂温暖干燥的天气。冬天将至，我本可以整天守在我们的劈木机旁——那台啜着汽油、凭液压之力干着英雄活的机器，让我们每年都能快速备下几大垛柴火——但这活儿可以再等等，等秋天凉爽晴朗的日子被地球不停歇的自转挤到一边，让位给黑暗、雨夹雪、大雪和冻土再说。

It is Indian summer in southern Wisconsin. My wife and I and our young son live here on her family’s farm. Both of us are employed off the farm, but on weekends and many evenings I am an apprentice to my father-in-law, David. Today, we are taking advantage of a brief period of warm, dry weather. Winter is approaching, and I could be spending the day beside our woodsplitter – the gasoline-sipping, hydraulically heroic mechanism that makes it possible to put up several cords of firewood quickly each year – but that work can wait until the cool, sunny days of fall are shoved aside by the relentless spin of the earth into darkness, sleet, snow and frozen ground.

今夏还立在此处的那堵高高的绿玉米之墙，已被秋天与收获改造殆尽。联合收割机梳穗般扫荡过后，田里几乎没什么还立着。身后那台机械龙卷风轰鸣刺耳：呼啸着撕扯空气，磨掉根部盘结的玉米残茬，吸走枯叶和褪尽籽粒的光穗轴。在我身后，干燥的玉米残株穿过粉碎机，如同被迫卷起白沫的湍流，旋即又落回一片平整安静的表面——平静之下，藏着多少忙碌。

The wall of tall green corn that stood here this summer has been transformed by fall and harvest. Little is standing after the field was stripped by a seed-combing combine. The mechanical tornado flails so loudly behind me: wailing against the air, grinding off the root-buttressed corn-stalk stumps, sucking up spent leaves and stripped cobs. In my wake, dry corn litter passes through the chopper like water compelled into frothy turbulence, falling again into an even, silent surface that hides so much activity below.

时间不容浪费——晴好天气预计还能撑上一天，我得赶在今天下午天光散尽之前把整块地过完。明天，大卫会把这些嚼碎的残渣搂成一条条草垄，然后再回来跑一趟，把它们打成捆。在地里留下一些这样的覆盖草是有益的——等于给土壤盖了屋顶，抵御风雨侵蚀——我们也可以匀出一些拿走，这些还是上好的垫草。凡是牲畜密集圈养之处，它们的排泄物都会积聚出高浓度的氨气和其他气体，对牲畜和人都可能有害。添入稻草能减缓粪尿的分解，减少有害气体的产生，并把宝贵的养分悬浮在半固态的混合物里。这些稻草和粪肥将满载氮、磷和其他养分，重返这片田地。

No time to waste – the weather is expected to hold for another day, and I need to cover the field before the light fails this afternoon. Tomorrow, David will rake this chewed-up detritus into windrows and then come back for another round to bale them up. While it is good to leave some of this thatch on the field – to keep a roof over the soil and guard against erosion by wind and rain – we can afford to borrow some of this, which also makes good straw. Wherever livestock are gathered in close quarters, their waste can produce concentrations of ammonia and other gases that can be harmful to animals and people alike. Adding straw slows the breakdown of manure and urine, reducing the production of noxious gases and suspending valuable nutrients in a semi-solid mixture. The straw and excrement will come back to this field loaded with nitrogen, phosphorous and other nutrients.

这堆厩肥（barn-pack）还饱含有机质，把相当一部分养分长期封存起来，直到被那些在田野平静表面之下干活的生灵分解：足有数吨之重的蚯蚓、甲虫和其他无脊椎动物；绵延许多英里的真菌菌丝；还有不计其数、形形色色的细菌。这一食物圈（foodshed）的居民们忙着分解稻草厚厚的细胞壁，也分解那些牲畜吃干草时穿过牛腹、随粪便排出的半消化的草茎与草叶。

The ‘barn-pack’ is also heavy with organic matter that keeps a significant portion of the nutrients locked up for longer periods of time until it is broken down by the creatures who are at work beneath the calm surface of the field: literally tons of worms and beetles and other invertebrates; many miles of fungal hyphae; and countless diversity of bacteria. The denizens of this foodshed work to decompose the thick cell walls of the straw, as well as the partially digested grass stems and leaves that passed through the cattle and into their manure while the livestock fed on hay.

土壤里的生灵还以肠道微生物的尸体为食——正是这些微生物帮反刍动物消化了干草。这些肠中微生物才是这个系统里真正的食草者：太阳的能量由植物捕捉，变成谷物或干草；农人把它们收割下来，运进谷仓，再由一头大型哺乳动物送入肠道这个栖居地——这头动物的脑、口和肌肉整日操劳，为体内的微生物房客收集食物。然而微生物命短，很快便结束了自己在系统顶端的当班。死去之时，它们跨过后肠的门槛，最终抵达肛门。屎。

The creatures of the soil also feed on the bodies of the gut microbes that helped ruminants digest their hay. The gut-borne microbes are the true herbivores in this system: the power of the sun is harnessed by plants and turned into grain or hay, which is gathered by the farmers and transported to the barn, then delivered to the habitat of the gut by a large mammal whose brain, mouth and muscles spend their days gathering food for its microbial tenants. However, the microbes are short-lived and quickly finish their shift at the top of the system. As they die, they pass through the threshold of hind gut and, finally, the anus. Shit.

把这些都唤作废弃物，公平吗？发臭的厩肥堆里，生命的火苗尚未熄灭，尚未沦入熵的手心。根系向深处掘进，寻找更多的磷、氮和水，好继续那桩忙碌的营生——建造离子转运蛋白、酶、木质素和蛋白质——从根到茎，从茎到叶，从叶到种。春夏秋三季，那些重新回到田地的稻草、植物纤维和死去的微生物，将轮番上岗，参与一场复活。土壤的生命，便是如此。

Is it fair to call any of this waste? The spark of life in the stinking barn pack is not yet exhausted, not yet lost to entropy. Roots burrow deep in search of more phosphorous, nitrogen and water to carry on the busy work of building ion transporters, enzymes, lignin and proteins – from root to stem to leaf to seed. Through spring, summer and fall, the straw, plant fibres and dead microbes that re-enter the field will take shifts in resurrection. Such is the life of the soil.

垄的尽头打断了我的遐想。我把拖拉机掉了个半圆，又轻轻拨动操纵杆，直到那龙卷风恰好再度触地，此时我正好回正车身，咬住下一垄。准备再来一趟横穿田野，顺着垄行而去，方向正西。我的路线与傍晚的太阳只差几度——它正悬在中纬度秋日那柔黄的光线里。

The end of the row breaks my reverie. I turn the tractor in a half-circle and feather the lever again until the tornado touches down just as I straighten out and catch the next row. Ready for another trip across the field, aimed down the rows, now due west. My route takes me just a few degrees north of where the late afternoon sun hangs in the soft yellow light of mid-latitude autumn.

我翻过一道道橡树影斑驳的山脊——冰碛垄（glacial moraines）——它们既见证了近代冰期冰川推进的最远界线，也见证了以火为生计之轴的人们：他们在这片土地上垒起一座座兽形土墩（effigy mounds），后来又开始在用野牛肩胛骨锄草的田里种起玉米和其他作物。再往西，一座锈迹斑斑的窄桥悬过密西西比河（Mississippi River），桥下流淌的水，曾化作雨雪落在北国的森林、三州交界无碛区（Driftless Area）的谷地牧场，以及明尼苏达州（Minnesota）中西部广阔平坦的玉米大豆地上。每年秋天，头顶的天空便挤满白鹈鹕、白头海雕，还有一整个水禽的天堂。循着这幅汇聚与流淌的图景，我愿继续向西，再向西：在艾奥瓦州（Iowa）的海登草原州立保护区（Hayden Prairie State Preserve）里，切碎罗盘草（compass plant）干枯的茎秆和大蓝茎草（big bluestem）高高的须芒，在丝兰（yucca）与垂穗草（side-oats grama）的守望下蹚过大苏河（Big Sioux）。

I crest and ease on over oak-dappled ridges – glacial moraines – that attest to the furthest advance of the recent Ice Age glaciers and the fire-centric economy of the people who covered this land with effigy mounds and who later began to grow corn and other crops in fields they weeded using the shoulder blades of bison. Further west, a narrow, rusty-girdered bridge threads over the Mississippi River, carrying water that fell as rain and snow on north country forests, coulee pastures in the tri-state Driftless Area, and the broad, flat corn-and-soybean lands of central and western Minnesota. Each fall, the skies above fill with white pelicans, bald eagles and a paradise of waterfowl. Against this pattern of gathering and flow, I’d continue west, west: chopping through the dried stalks of compass plant and tall tassels of big bluestem at Hayden Prairie State Preserve in Iowa and fording the Big Sioux beneath the watch of yucca and side oats grama.

到了垄的尽头——又要做决定了。

End of the row – decision time again.

去年九月，我陪大卫去看一块地，就在切茬那块地的正西边，普伦蒂斯溪（Prentice Creek）的北岸。我们是去测量并标记一条草地缓冲带，它将把行栽作物与溪流隔开——这一做法写进了保护地役权（conservation easement），如今已成为这份地产契约的一部分。鉴于过去几年山洪一再扫过这片低洼地，把这块地留在草里，于经济、于生态都说得通。上一次大洪水是二〇〇八年，大卫从这块地上打起了蒙尘的干草——这地若种着玉米，只怕早已彻底绝收。我们沿流而上插更多标旗时，我一次次趟过田边牛蒡、桑树、东部黑莓、林地向日葵和尖叶山蚂蟥纠缠的乱丛，去寻找溪岸被侵蚀的边缘。它不是朝圣者想象中的溪流，而是一条真实的溪流，深深切入从久耕之田侵蚀而下、汇入其变动不居的河床的表土。大卫打算几天后沿着这排标旗作业，同时给旧干草地的其余部分喷上草甘膦（glyphosate），杀掉牧草和苜蓿，为来年春天播种抗除草剂玉米做准备。

Last September, I accompanied David to check out a field that lies due west of the stubble-chopping field, on the north bank of Prentice Creek. We were there to measure and mark a grass buffer that will separate row crops from the creek – a practice encoded in the conservation easement that is now a part of the deed to the property. Given the flash floods that have repeatedly swept across this low ground over the past few years, leaving this land in grass makes good economic and ecological sense. During the last major flood in 2008, David baled dusty hay from this field, while a substantial portion of his corn crop would have been completely lost. As we moved upstream to place more flags, I repeatedly plunged through the field-edge tangle of burdock, mulberry, eastern blackberry, woodland sunflowers and pointed-leaf tick-trefoil to find the eroded edge of the creek bank. It is not a pilgrim’s creek, but a real one, cut deep into topsoil that has eroded off the long-planted fields and into its dynamic basin. David planned to follow this line of flags a few days later, while spraying the rest of the old hayfield with glyphosate to kill the grass and alfalfa, and prepare for next spring’s planting of herbicide-resistant corn.

我们花了一个小时插完缓冲带的标旗，走到地界时，一天的暑气正渐渐消散。回卡车的路上，我们沿着一条长满狗尾草的杂草带走——黄色的穗子在干草起伏的海洋里招摇。我们朝正西行进，因为这排狗尾草恰恰标出了几年前大卫的弟弟推平旧篱笆线时翻动过的土壤。走到离卡车还有一半路的地方，大卫在一段朝西的短坡上停了下来，我有些意外——在那儿，田面在三四十英尺的水平距离里下降了约十英尺。

We spent an hour flagging the buffer, and the heat of the day was lapsing as we reached the property line. To get back to the truck, we followed a strip of weedy foxtail grasses that waved yellow seed heads in a rolling sea of hay. We were moving due west, as the foxtail marked precisely where the soil had been disturbed when David’s brother bulldozed the old fenceline a few years earlier. Halfway back to the truck, I was surprised when David stopped at a short, west-facing slope, where the field dropped about 10 feet over the course of 30-40 feet.

「这个也标上吧。」他说。夏末的苜蓿和牧草长得很密，我看不出下面的土壤怎样，但大卫说，他知道这段坡上的土很轻——一道沙砾带。多半是当年崩塌的冰川渗出融水、涓涓流过的地方。大卫用折叠刀撬起几块石头，我把几块圆滑的卵石在手里翻来覆去。他很快指出了植被里的一个规律：「你可以从雀麦和苜蓿在这儿长得不一样，大致看出土壤的变化。长势更稀。这地种玉米不会有收成，而且要是庄稼扎不住根，土就会被刮走，所以我想把它留在干草里。我会留出够宽的一条，好从缓冲带那头进来打草。邻居们可能会笑话我，但这么做是对的。」

‘Let’s flag this, too,’ he said. Though I couldn’t see much of the soil beneath the late summer growth of alfalfa and grass, David said he knew the soil on this slope was light – a band of sand and gravel. Probably where an outwash stream trickled from the collapsing glacier. David pried up a few stones with his jackknife, and I turned over a few rounded and smooth stones in my hands. He quickly pointed out a pattern in the vegetation: ‘You can sort of see the change in soil in how the brome and alfalfa grows differently here. It’s thinner. This won’t be any good for corn, and it will erode away if the crop doesn’t take hold, so I’m going to leave it in hay. I’ll leave a broad-enough strip so I can come in from the buffer to bale it. The neighbours might laugh at me, but it’s the right thing to do.’

那一刻，我成了一个欢欣鼓舞的农场生态学家——我感到我们对土壤怀有共同的敬重，对守住这片土地怀有共同的责任。他的决定能够遏制乃至逆转土壤侵蚀，长远看还可能攒出更厚的表土、更高的肥力、更好的收成。就算只看眼前，用干草守住这块地，也比买种子、肥料和除草剂去种杂交玉米更划算。这些工具大卫在农场上样样都用，但它们左右不了他全部的耕作决策。他种地带着一种深谋远虑，把账算得更全——既算掏腰包的，也算以土地健康为代价的。

In that moment, I was an elated farm ecologist – I sensed that we shared a respect for the soil and a sense of responsibility to hold this ground. His decision could limit if not reverse soil erosion and perhaps build thicker topsoil, higher fertility and greater yields in the long run. In the short run, too, holding this ground with hay will be more profitable than buying seed, fertiliser and herbicide to plant hybrid corn. David uses all of those tools on his farm, yet they don’t drive all his farming decisions. He farms with a forethought that gives a fuller accounting of costs that come both out-of-pocket and at the expense of the health of the land.

在田野之中，他用交谈的语言，而非研讨会或布道坛上的辞令，表达着他对这个系统的了解和对土地的忠诚。他的知识来之不易。如今他已能把在无数犁沟中的所见，译解为田野里隐微却可辨的规律——在漫不经心的人眼里，那不过是一片均一的绿色海洋。他能做预测、权衡利弊、谋划可持续的方略，走在田里几乎不必停下脚步。这样的知识不该只是被囤积、藏守，然后失传；它可以随一种因地制宜的文化的演进而接力传递——在他把知识就地传授给后代的时候。

Out in the field, he expressed his knowledge of the system and loyalty to the land through the language of conversation rather than the rhetoric of seminar or sermon. His knowledge was hard-won. He was now able to translate what he had observed in many a plough furrow to a subtle but discernible pattern in the field, which would otherwise appear as a uniform sea of green to the inattentive mind. He could make predictions, weigh costs and benefits, and chart a sustainable strategy without hardly pausing in his walk across the field. That kind of knowledge is not simply to be stored up, harboured and lost; it can be relayed along in the evolution of a locally adapted culture as he conveys, in situ, the knowledge to following generations.

又过了一年，多了一层阅历，我终于承认了那个下午自己几乎无法察觉的东西。岳父不是在以农民对生态学家的身份同我说话，而是以长者对学徒的身份。我们作为专业人士彼此投合，但比两种训练有素的视角之间的契合重要得多的，是我们正在缔结的关系。当我们选择安守一地，便开始感受到约束、责任与不确定性的摩擦——那是过去与未来加在我们身上的——与此同时，这一过程也给了我们机会，去缔结更牢固的人间联结。这其中有一种深深的喜悦、满足与希望，是我们收拾行装、驶上坦荡公路的途中所不易得到的——或许根本无从领会。这些年在农场上学艺劳作的时光，让我更加确信：土地伦理不能从头脑里凭空生出来，它必须活在家人与邻里之间展开的劳作与交谈之中，在其中更新、演进。

With another year to add to my perspective, I have finally acknowledged what I could barely sense on that afternoon. My father-in-law was not talking to me as farmer to ecologist but as elder to apprentice. We agreed as professionals, but what was far more important than the congruence of our disciplined perspectives was the relationship we were building. When we choose to stay in place, we begin to feel the rub of constraints, responsibilities and uncertainties now placed on us by the past and future, while simultaneously the process provides opportunities for stronger human connections. There is within this a deep joy, satisfaction and hopefulness that is not readily available – perhaps not comprehensible – when we are in the process of pulling up stakes and heading out on the open road. The time I’ve spent learning to work on this farm has reinforced the idea that a land ethic cannot spring from the mind but must live, renew and evolve in the work and conversation that unfolds among families and communities.

我们的先人从拉丁语 conversari 中衍生出「交谈」（conversation）一词。这个词根也与 convertere 相通，意为「回转」。在这一层面上，交谈体现的不过是轮流倾听与言说这一简单举动。然而 conversari 本义是「与之共同生活、彼此相伴」。在这种更古老的意义上，交谈不只是言语的往来——它是一种共同生活的表达，是在相互依存的关系中比邻而居。

Our antecedents derived the word ‘conversation’ from the Latin conversari. The root is also related to convertere, ‘to turn around’. On this level, conversation reflects the simple act of taking turns listening and speaking. Yet conversari itself meant ‘to live with or associate with’. Conversation, in this older sense, was more than just an exchange of words – it was an expression of shared life, of dwelling alongside one another in relationships of mutual dependence.

这一古老的真理至今依然成立，纵使我们彼此回旋的规模早已远远超出家庭、村庄与流域，借着经济与文化的往来，借着碳与氮、尘埃与污染的共同循环，延展到国家与全球的共同体。无论相隔多远，城里人与乡下人的生命仍然绕着彼此回旋。植根于这一真理的伦理，必须以彼此的关切与挂怀表达出来。

This ancient truth remains valid, even as the scale of our mutual turnings stretches far beyond family and village and watershed to encompass national and global communities through economic and cultural exchanges and the shared cycles of carbon and nitrogen, dust and pollution. No matter the distance that separates us, the lives of urban and rural people still turn around one another. An ethic grounded in this truth must be expressed in mutual interest in and concern for one another.

一九四七年夏，环保主义者奥尔多·利奥波德（Aldo Leopold）致力于整合自己关于土地使用的思考，把土地、荒野与伦理编织进一篇文章——《土地伦理》（The Land Ethic），它后来成为《沙乡年鉴》（A Sand County Almanac，1949）一书的压卷之篇：

In the summer of 1947, the conservationist Aldo Leopold worked to integrate his thoughts on land use, weaving lands, wildness and ethics into a single essay, ‘The Land Ethic’, which became the capstone to his collection A Sand County Almanac (1949):

土地伦理至今仍有生命力，部分原因在于利奥波德把自己的努力看作一种综合——对当时关于人、土地及其间联系的最佳理解的清晰表达——而不是一锤定音的最后判词。

The land ethic remains relevant in part because Leopold recognised his effort as a synthesis, as an articulation of the best of current understanding of people, land and the connections between them, rather than as the final word.

要确立一种足够坚强的土地伦理，足以节制我们对地球资源的胃口、把我们的工作引向恢复土地健康，我们仍有很长的路要走。土地伦理、良好的土地利用，以及一个公平而有礼的社会，源自无数的行动与交谈——它们随着国民的日常生活，不断展开。

We still have far to go in the attempt to encode a land ethic sufficiently strong to temper our appetite for Earth’s resources and focus our work towards restoring land health. A land ethic, good land use, and an equitable and civil society arise from countless actions and conversations, constantly unfolding, as the nation’s citizens go about their daily lives.

农业是一项嵌在生态之中的营生，我们不应容忍那些损耗自然资本、损害生态系统的多样性与功能、伤及环绕并支撑我们的大气、土壤与水域之健康的做法。与此同时，我们必须认识到，农业也嵌在社会之中——一种与社会目标背道而驰的农作方式是难以维系的。我赞同：一座农场可以、也应该被它的主人看重为一处安身之所，一种快乐之源——好好耕种、好好吃饭、与家养的和野生的邻居们打交道。而社会仍在用谷物、肉食、生物质和其他商品的产量来衡量农场、评判农民。农民被卷入一个高度竞争的市场体系，进账与出账之间通常只剩薄薄一线。与此同时，农业公共政策的目标，依旧被对廉价食物的渴望牵着走。

Agriculture is an ecologically embedded enterprise, and we should not tolerate actions that degrade our natural capital, the diversity and function of our ecosystems, or the health of the atmosphere, soils and waters that surround and support us. At the same time, we must recognise that agriculture is embedded in society – a farm practice that runs counter to society’s goals is hard to maintain. I agree that a farm can and should be valued by its owners as a place to live, and as a source of pleasure in good farming, good eating and engagement with both domestic and wild neighbours. Society continues to measure farms, and judge farmers, on their production of grain, meat, biomass and other commodities. Farmers are caught up in a highly competitive market system that typically allows only a slim margin between money coming in and money going out. Meanwhile, the goal of public policy for agriculture continues to be guided by the desire for cheap food.

虽然现代农业的困境看上去严重到我们注定会自我调适、组织起一个更可持续的体系，但澳大利亚莫道克大学（Murdoch University）的生态学家在分析了那片大陆西南部半干旱地区农民面临的问题之后，得出了不同的结论。农民和政策制定者之所以继续投资于不合土地脾性的生产体系，是因为他们已与现代农企和食品生产的格局紧紧捆绑。金融资本流向传统农民，远比流向另类农民容易；而社会资本——农艺研究、技术援助（用于解决动物健康、土壤肥力、水土流失与水污染等问题），尤其是加工、配送与零售——都在支撑着这个高耗能源与自然资本的体系。论文作者们称之为「锁定陷阱」（lock-in trap）：一个产业沉淀的成本（sunk-costs）高到这般地步，以至于它可能继续损耗它所依赖的资源，直到这些资本被彻底抽走。要把这个体系推移到一个更可欲的状态——一个在生态与社会两方面都有韧性的状态——就需要我们在探求更可持续的土地利用方式时，锻造出新的社会联结。

While the predicament of modern agriculture may appear so dire that we will inevitably adapt and organise a more sustainable system, ecologists at Murdoch University in Australia came to a different conclusion after analysing the problems facing farmers in the semi-arid southwestern part of that continent. Farmers and policy-makers continue to invest in production systems that don’t fit the land because they are now tightly bound up in patterns of modern agribusiness and food production. Financial capital goes to conventional farmers more easily than to alternative farmers, and social capital – in the forms of agronomic research, technical assistance (for addressing issues with animal health, soil fertility, soil erosion and water pollution), and especially processing, distribution and retail – supports the energy- and natural-capital intensive system. The authors describe this as a ‘lock-in trap’, ‘an industry that has so much “sunk-costs” that it may continue to degrade the resource it relies upon until the capital is totally removed.’ Shifting the system to a more desirable state – one that is ecologically as well as socially resilient – requires us to forge new social connections in the quest to establish more sustainable patterns of land use.

一个由彼此联结、单个看来却都微小的行动者组成的共同体，能指望把农业从锁定陷阱里解救出来吗？乐观的余地不大，但前景并非全然无望。正如大卫·奥尔（David Orr）提醒我们的：「希望是一个挽起袖子干活的动词。」奥尔在这样的时代鼓励我们怀抱希望时，或许是在延续一场与温德尔·贝里（Wendell Berry）的文学对话——贝里是肯塔基丘陵地带的农民兼作家。贝里说过，对于人们重新与「乡土的财富、健康、知识与欢愉」联结起来的前景，他没有半点乐观的理由，但他仍然断言：「希望是我们的义务之一……自然的健康是希望的首要根基——只要我们能找到那份谦卑与智慧，接受自然为师。」

Can a community of well-connected but individually small actors hope to break agriculture out of its lock-in trap? There is little room for optimism, but our prospects are not hopeless. As David Orr reminds us: ‘Hope is a verb with its sleeves rolled up.’ When Orr encouraged us to be hopeful in times like these, perhaps he was continuing a literary conversation with Wendell Berry, a Kentucky hill country farmer and writer. Berry stated that he had no reason to be optimistic about the prospects of people becoming reconnected with the ‘wealth, health, knowledge, and pleasure of their country’, but still he concluded that ‘hope is one of our duties … The health of nature is the primary ground of hope – if we can find the humility and wisdom to accept nature as our teacher.’

自然的经济在草原上如何运转，我们或许大有可学。在堪萨斯州（Kansas）的弗林特丘陵（Flint Hills），草原确实由高草当家——大蓝茎草、小蓝茎草（small bluestem）、柳枝稷（switchgrass）和印度草（Indiangrass）约占这片风中绿海的八成。这些草之所以占尽上风，是因为它们效率惊人。与许多其他草类不同，这些物种在叶片里造出了通道，让光合作用的效率胜过竞争者。它们进行光合作用所需的酶相对更少，因而所需的氮也更少——氮正是酶的构件。这是一大优势，因为土壤中的含氮量往往正是植物生长的限制因子——所以我们才被人劝着往园子和庄稼里施加人工氮肥。多亏这项创新，高草流失的水分也更少，这使它们在蒸发率高的干燥气候中占得先机。

We may have much to learn from how nature’s economy operates in the prairie. In the Flint Hills of Kansas, the prairies are truly dominated by tall grasses – big and small bluestem, switchgrass and Indiangrass account for around 80 per cent of the prairie’s windswept greenery. These grasses are so dominant because they are remarkably efficient. Unlike many other grasses, these species build channels in their leaves to make the process of photosynthesis more efficient than it is for their competitors. The grasses need comparatively fewer enzymes to carry out photosynthesis and thus require less nitrogen – a building block of enzymes. This is a major advantage, as the amount of nitrogen in the soil is often the limiting factor for plant growth – hence the artificial nitrogen we’re counselled to add to our gardens and crops as fertiliser. The tall grasses also lose less water thanks to their innovation, which helps put them ahead in dry climates with high evaporation rates.

用微观经济学的语言来说，高草的建造与运营成本最低。于是，这些草原之海中的船只得以在表面之下撒开根系的大网，捕获水与氮，又张起绿叶的巨帆迎向太阳，驱动光合作用前行。这些光子与分子是有限的资源，植物无法共享，只能竞争。效率高超的高草舰队本该统治草原。吊诡的是，高草草原却得天独厚，拥有大量更小、效率更低的野花与豆科植物，从加拿大亚寒带的马尼托巴（Manitoba）一路繁盛到路易斯安那（Louisiana）与德克萨斯（Texas）的亚热带海岸平原。正如土壤中遍布的腐朽与新生之谜，微生物就栖居在这个悖论的中心。

In the language of microeconomics, the tall grasses have the lowest costs for construction and operations. As a result, these ships in the prairie sea can cast vast nets of roots below the surface to capture water and nitrogen, and set huge sails of green leaves to catch the sun to propel photosynthesis. These photons and molecules are finite resources that plants cannot share and for which they must compete. The highly efficient tallgrass fleets should rule the prairies. Paradoxically, the tallgrass prairies are blessed with an abundance of smaller, less efficient wildflowers and legumes, prospering from sub-boreal Manitoba in Canada to the subtropical coastal plains of Louisiana and Texas. As with the mystery of decay and renewal that abounds in the soil, microbes dwell at the heart of this paradox.

我们所有哺乳动物的肠道里都有微生物，协助并调节着消化。大型食草动物更把这一点推向极致——庞大的体腔容纳着一套精密的消化系统，收留、供养并收割着一个微生物群落。嘴、牙、舌、消化道和脑，全都为收割和处理草而组织起来，借助微生物的本事，把粗纤维的草吃下去、把易于消化的糖排出来。

All of us mammals have microbes in our guts, facilitating and regulating digestion. Large-bodied herbivores take this to an extreme – huge body cavities accommodate an intricate digestive system that shelters, feeds and harvests a community of microbes. Mouth, teeth, tongue, digestive tract and brain are all organised to harvest and process grass, leveraging the ability of microbes to consume the fibrous grasses and excrete easily digestible sugars.

在高草草原上，美洲野牛选择去哪儿吃草，一定程度上取决于哪里能让它们最大限度地摄取自己最适应的植物资源。一种生物的生态位（ecological niche），或许最贴切的译法是它的宫殿——诗人、翻译家兼生态行动者加里·斯奈德（Gary Snyder）在哲学家道元（Dōgen）的著作中重新发现了这一点，并为我们作了阐发。在演化的轮回中，高草的效率与优势建成了一座绿色宫殿。野牛演化得栖居于这座宫殿。在高草之间，它们安处如归。

In the tallgrass prairie, American bison select where to graze based, to some extent, on where they can maximise their intake of the plant resource to which they are best adapted. The ecological niche of an organism is perhaps most aptly translated as its palace, as the poet, translator and ecological activist Gary Snyder has re-discovered, and interpreted for us, in the work of the philosopher Dōgen. In the cycles of evolution, the efficiency and dominance of the tall grasses built a palace of greenery. The bison evolved to dwell in this palace. Among the tall grasses, they are fully at home.

正如堪萨斯州立大学（Kansas State University）的生态学家艾伦·纳普（Alan Knapp）和同事们从他们在弗林特丘陵的研究中「消化」出的结论：有微生物助力的野牛，在把自己的家园变成他人的宫殿这件事上也扮演着关键角色。野牛大快朵颐、以满足所需之时，也拆掉了高草繁茂的叶之上层建筑。草原上的其他成员则红着脸、开着花，以示感激。

As the ecologist Alan Knapp and his colleagues at Kansas State University digested from their research in the Flint Hills, the microbially facilitated bison play a key role in making their home a palace for others too. As they fulfil their needs by feasting on leaves, the bison demolish the tall grasses’ leafy superstructures. The rest of the prairie blushes and blooms in gratitude.

大蓝茎草的叶片本可能把矮小的野生矮牵牛（wild petunia）幼苗罩进阴影。正因为微生物与野牛结成的超有机体天生善用丰盛的牧草，这株年幼的野花如今大半个夏天都沐浴在光里，吸收二氧化碳，释放氧气，靠光合作用长叶生根。再者，大蓝茎草叶片里的氮本会一直锁到生长季结束——除非恰好路过一队蚱蜢，被白昼渐短之前交配产卵的本能驱使着，落下饱餐一顿，把氮带走。而野牛边吃边排泄，原本锁在头顶叶片里的氮和其他养分，便以其他群落成员用得上的形态归还大地。一头母牛停下来，在罗盘草上蹭了蹭肚皮，随后撒下一股富含氮的尿液——野花也许正要抽出花茎、直指天空，恰好将它吸个干净。

The leaves of big bluestem might otherwise have overshadowed a short-statured wild petunia seedling. Because the microbe/bison superorganism is adapted to make use of the abundant grass, the young wildflower now spends much of the summer bathed in light, taking in carbon dioxide, releasing oxygen, and building leaves and roots through photosynthesis. Furthermore, nitrogen in the big bluestem leaves would have been locked up through the rest of the growing season, unless a passing band of grasshoppers landed for a meal and carried it off, driven by the instinct to mate and lay eggs before the days become any shorter. As the bison eat and excrete, the nitrogen and other nutrients once held in the leaves overhead are returned in forms accessible to other community members. After pausing to scratch her belly on a compass plant, a bison cow returns a cascade of nitrogen-rich urine that the wildflower may absorb just as it begins to send its flowering stalk skyward.

反过来，罗盘草也能多开一朵花——倘若那些氮一直锁在大蓝茎草里，这朵花本开不出来。它碟盘大小的花朵招来并滋养许多本土传粉者，它营养丰富的种子则是食种鸟类的主食。花与种子都造价高昂，因为它们浓缩了大量的氮。罗盘草和野生矮牵牛，不过是数百种可能被优势高草挤垮的野花与草类中的两种。当野牛盯上一片高草，大蓝茎草、小蓝茎草、印度草和柳枝稷的高效统治便可能被压制，草原的资产负债表在「每英亩生物量吨数」这一单一指标上或许要吃点亏——但正如罗宾逊·杰弗斯（Robinson Jeffers）在《比克斯比码头》（Bixby's Landing，1928）一诗中所言：

The compass plant, in turn, could produce one more flower than it might have, had the nitrogen remained locked up in big bluestem. Its saucer-sized flowers attract and nourish many native pollinators, while its nutritious seeds are a staple for seed-eating birds. Both flowers and seeds are costly to produce because they concentrate large amounts of nitrogen. The compass plant and wild petunia are only two of hundreds of wildflower and grass species that can be outcompeted by the dominant tall grasses. When the bison home in on a patch of tall grasses, the dominance of the highly efficient big and small bluestem, Indiangrass and switchgrass may be depressed such that the prairie’s balance sheet might take a hit on the single measure of tons of biomass per acre – but, as Robinson Jeffers puts it in his poem ‘Bixby’s Landing’ (1928):

在这场重组的过程中，以其他生态货币进行的交换成倍增加，因为数不清的生物栖居在野花的宫殿里。大多数食草动物和杂食动物比野牛个头小，也缺少那种让它们得以消受草料的微生物共生。它们转而依赖野花和开花灌木提供的更易消化的叶子、花粉、花蜜、甜美的果实、富含淀粉的块茎与种子，以及高蛋白的叶芽。獾、兔子、老鼠和田鼠、草原榛鸡、松鸡、龟和水禽都能受益。效率较低的植物供养着许多种昆虫幼虫，幼虫又喂养捕食性昆虫、鸣禽雏鸟和食物网上的其他成员。联结之网就这样铺展开去，一路滚过草原。

In the process of reorganisation, exchange in other ecological currencies multiplies, for countless organisms dwell in the palace of wildflowers. Most herbivores and omnivores are smaller than a bison and lack the microbial symbiosis that provides access to grass. Instead, they depend upon the more digestible leaves, pollen, nectar, sugary fruit, starchy tubers and seeds, and protein-rich leaf buds that wildflowers and flowering shrubs produce. Badgers, rabbits, mice and voles, prairie chickens, grouse, turtles and waterfowl can all benefit. The less efficient plants host many species of insect larvae, which in turn feed predatory insects, songbird hatchlings and others on the food web. And so the web of connections rolls out and across the prairies in this fashion.

倘若我们要改造农业、求得更好的结局，就需要一场类似的铺展：让彼此联结、形形色色的人与组织，共同充当一个关键物种（keystone species）。但我们不能干等着联邦政策改革这头野牛踏进工业化农业的堡垒横冲直撞。我们必须行动，免得可持续农业这头野牛失足跌倒，让工业化农业中最无情的力量继续畅行无阻——土壤退化、野生动植物灭绝、社区空心化。要让农业重归一个更多样、更有韧性的共同体，我们每个人都必须在食物市场里表达自己那份土地伦理，在公共领域为我们的价值观发出声音。可持续农业与自然保护之间有着深层的契合，先驱者过去几十年一点一滴赢得的关键进展，我们再也赔不起。

If we are to transform agriculture for better outcomes, we need a similar roll-out of networked and diverse people and organisations who collectively act as a keystone species. But we cannot simply wait for the bison of federal policy reform to come trampling through the industrial agriculture complex. We must act lest the bison of more sustainable agriculture stumble, allowing the most ruthless forces in industrial agriculture to continue unchecked – to the degradation of soil, extinction of wild plants and animals, and hollowing-out of communities. To restore agriculture to a more diverse and resilient community, each of us must express our individual land ethic through the food markets and by giving voice to our values in the public sphere. There are deep congruencies between sustainable agriculture and conservation, and we cannot afford to lose the incremental but crucial gains that pioneers have made over the past few decades.

投资于本地食物与能源生产，会催生对更多类型农业的需求，让那些久已因工业效率之名被简化的景观，重新焕发生态与社会的多样性。这样的景观可以包括：小片精耕的菜田与果园，为新鲜农产品和本地加工者供货；适应当地风土、搭架而生的啤酒花，供养地区的精酿啤酒厂；多年生草地出产健康的畜产品——牛、羊、猪、禽；小片的玉米、小麦和其他谷物的田地，与牲畜饲养相结合，或供应本地人拥有的磨坊；还有疏伐后提供柴火与木材的橡树林地。如今被草芦（reed canarygrass）和灌木壅塞的湿地，可以重新供养野生动物，同时出产干草、垫料和可再生生物质，足以在隆冬封冻时节为住家、商户和学校供暖。把我们更多的钱从大型农企转向这些小规模、本地整合的体系，我们将得到回报：更健康的土壤，更洁净也更充沛的地下水，暴雨之后更少更轻的洪水，拥有管理生物学上复杂的农系之技能的更强大的社区，以及一幅更丰富、更安稳的半自然至荒野栖境的织锦。乡野景观里许多半驯化的角落，对那些点亮我们生活、见证我们善待土地的野生生灵至关重要。这些半野半人的景观的试金石，是小块牧场与漫展的草原、散布的林地与幽深的森林、荫凉的山谷与砂岩封顶的断崖、洁净的源头泉眼，以及水禽喧腾的湿地。

Investing in local food and energy production creates demand for more types of agriculture, renewing ecological and social diversity across landscapes long simplified in the name of industrial efficiency. Such landscapes might include small, intensively cultivated vegetable fields and orchards supplying fresh produce and local processors; trellised hops adapted to local conditions and supporting regional breweries; perennial grasslands producing healthy animal products from cattle, sheep, swine and poultry; small fields of corn, wheat and other grains integrated with livestock or supplying locally owned mills; and oak woodlots thinned to provide firewood and lumber. Wetlands now choked with reed canarygrass and shrubs could once again support wildlife while providing hay, bedding and renewable biomass capable of heating homes, businesses and schools during winter’s deep freeze. By turning more of our dollars away from large agribusiness and toward these small-scale, locally integrated systems, we will be rewarded with healthier soil, cleaner and more abundant groundwater, fewer and less severe floods following intense rains, stronger communities with the skills to manage biologically complex farming systems, and a richer, more secure tapestry of semi-natural-to-wild habitats. Many semi-domestic parts of the rural landscape are important for the wild creatures that enrich our lives and testify to the care we take of the land. The touchstones of these partly wild/partly human landscapes are small pastures and sweeping grasslands, scattered woodlots and deep forests, shady valleys and sandstone-capped bluffs, clean headwater springs, and wetlands alive with waterfowl.

一片多样的乡野景观，若以复杂性与韧性为塑造的目标，而非最大产量与最简核算，在未来岁月里将对我们的社区弥足珍贵。由多样农作体系构成的景观，因其在能源与肥力上对化石燃料的依赖远轻得多，也就更有可能在种种冲击之下保持产出——暴雨、洪涝、干旱、燃料与肥料涨价、土壤结构与肥力衰退、信贷与金融资本难求。而由于气候变化、人口持续增长、资源需求攀升以及经济与社会的不平等，这些冲击预计将愈来愈频繁、愈来愈猛烈。

A diverse rural landscape, shaped by goals of complexity and resilience, rather than maximum production and minimal accounting, will be invaluable to our communities in the coming years. The landscape of diverse farming systems that rely much less on fossil fuels for energy and fertility is much more likely to remain productive despite shocks such as intense rains, flooding, drought, rising fuel and fertiliser costs, declining soil structure and fertility, and limited access to credit and financial capital. Such shocks are predicted to become more frequent and more severe because of climate change, continued population growth, increasing demand for resources, and economic and social inequality.

一家大型农企、食品加工企业或不择手段的零售巨头，还能更怕什么呢？怕的就是我们开始用行动表明：我们并不相信他们的销售额和季度盈利，与我们的安全与幸福有多大的一致。贝里把这样一种社会转变，径直称为一场脱离（secession）。

What could a large agribusiness, food-processing or cut-throat retail corporation fear more than for us to begin acting like we don’t believe there is much congruence between their sales and quarterly earnings, and our security and happiness? Berry has described such a social shift as nothing less than secession.

为基础设施与共同体承担起个人投资的责任，其中一笔代价是：回报大多要到长远才见分晓，而非在下一个季度或未来几年。要树立长远眼光、维系集体承诺，就需要修炼种种本领：在个人与派系的分歧中掌舵，直面社区发展另类模式的不确定性，熬过经济时艰，还要对那股从无名经济流向其受害者的密集宣传，抱持一种既务实又快活的怀疑。这需要远见、诚恳，以及睦邻与参与共同体的艺术。

Among the trade-offs that come with taking personal responsibility for investment in infrastructure and community is that much of the pay-off will come in the long term rather than in the next quarter or next few years. Building a long-term outlook and sustaining collective commitment will require cultivating the skills needed to navigate disagreements between individuals and factions, to face the uncertainty of alternative models of community development, to weather economic hard times, and to engage in both practical and joyful scepticism of the intense propaganda that flows from the anonymous economy to its victims. This demands vision, sincerity and the arts of neighbourliness and community engagement.

在种种可持续性的框架里，我从未遇见过把对邻人与土地的爱奉为指导原则——然而爱正是自然保护、良好农作与强健共同体的心脏。我们不知道未来会带来什么，但抵御未来冲击的韧性，无法仅靠更好的技术、更高的效率或更完善的通信基础设施来保障。这些进步我们都需要，但自然保护对我们的要求更高。利奥波德在概括他的生态教育目标时，他的话正应和着这一召唤：

Nowhere in the frameworks of sustainability have I come across love for our neighbours and the land as a guiding principle, yet love is at the heart of conservation, good farming and strong communities. We do not know what the future will bring, but resilience to coming shocks cannot be secured through better technology, greater efficiency or improved communications infrastructure alone. We need all of these advances, but conservation asks even more of us. Leopold’s words resonated with this call when he summarised his goals for an ecological education:

我们每个人都是个体，但生态学与生态经济学提醒我们：我们唯有在与土地和邻人的关系之中才得以存在。把对土地与邻人的爱付诸行动，我们就担起了巨大的风险。我们很可能要面对消费方式、劳作与交换之性质的种种改变，它们会让人觉得像惨重的牺牲。然而，当我们选择以共同体成员的身份行事，超越效率、向着人与人之间、人与土地之间的和谐奋进，许多丰饶的收获确有可能。许多本会白白浪费的东西，被带回更新的循环。当我们滋养共同体的生命，我们照料的是一片田野——我们的许多希望与欢愉，都可以在其中绽放。

Each of us is an individual, but ecology and ecological economics remind us that we exist only in relation to the land and our neighbours. By enacting our love for the land and our neighbours, we assume great risk. We will likely face changes in consumption patterns and the nature of work and exchange that will feel like severe sacrifices. However, many rich harvests are indeed possible when we choose to act as members of our community, to transcend efficiency and strive toward harmony among people and between people and land. Much that might otherwise go to waste is brought back into cycles of renewal. As we nurture the life of community, we tend a field in which many of our hopes and pleasures can bloom.

到了垄的尽头。又要做决定了。

End of row. Decision time again.
