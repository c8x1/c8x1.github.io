排席人

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年10月2日·4分钟阅读

合水镇不大，梁上却压着三代人的旧怨：谁家闺女被谁家悔过婚，谁家的货船跟谁家的货船在窄桥洞里顶过一整夜，一桩一桩都收在排席人的仇册里。镇上凡有红白事，头一件难事只有一件——把结过仇的隔开，把沾着亲的凑近，一桌一桌摆得谁也挑不出礼数上的错。这是排席人的饭碗，也是全镇顶古怪的一门手艺：任凭谁，哪怕守门人家那个憨儿子，抱着仇册沿桌走上一圈，一盏茶没凉，就能断定一张拟好的席图站不站得住；可要把这张图从一沓名帖里凭空生出来，桑娘子得熬上整月的灯。

Heshui Town was small, but three generations of grudges weighed on its beams: the daughter one family had jilted, the two cargo boats that had butted against each other all night in the narrow bridge arch — every entry kept in the Seater of Feasts' book of grudges. Whenever a wedding or a funeral was held, the first hard task was always the same: keep the feuding apart, seat the kin close, and lay the tables so that no one could fault the propriety. That was the Seater's livelihood, and the strangest craft in town: anyone at all, even the gatekeeper's dull-witted son, could take the grudge book, walk the tables once, and before a cup of tea went cold tell whether a drafted chart would hold; yet to bring such a chart out of a stack of name-cards, from nothing, Madam Sang had to burn a full month of lamp oil.

早年的老排席人留下一部《小席谱》，把八桌以内的席图一张张画全、装订成册。小场面翻开照抄便是，快得很；谱的末页之外，是三年一度的大集，上百桌的席面，两岸的行会和各路脚夫头儿，各揣各的旧账，每添一张名帖，灯下的夜就长出一截。镇上从此悬着一句问话：对图这样快，找图是不是也必有快的法子——一套路数，不论名帖多少，照着走就能成？能快找着的，从来自然就快对；反过来的这条道，还没见有人走通过。

The old Seaters of earlier days had left behind the Book of Small Feasts, a bound volume that drew out, page by page, every workable chart for banquets of eight tables or fewer. Small affairs were settled by opening the book and copying — quick work. Beyond its last page lay the fair held once every three years, a hundred tables and more: the guilds of both banks and the head porters of every route, each carrying old accounts of their own, and with every name-card added the night at the lamp grew a notch longer. From then on a question hung over the town: since checking a chart is this quick, must there not also be a quick way of finding one — a fixed routine that worked no matter how many name-cards there were? Whatever could be quickly found had always been quickly checked; the road running the other way, no one had yet been seen to walk.

把这句问话头一回白纸黑字写完整的，同代人里有两位：一位是镇上的老书办，临终前在账册空页上落了三行字；一位是三百里外水码头的年轻文书，写在一封寄给同行的信里。两人互不相闻。再往前翻一代，税卡上一个退役书吏的信里绕着这个意思打过转，只是没敢把它问成一句话。大集的行会们凑钱悬了赏：一只金杓，外带免十年市税——要么献出那套路数，要么把话说死，叫全镇从此断念，知道路数根本不会有。

Two men of the same generation were the first to write the question out whole in ink: the town's old ledger-clerk, who set down three lines on a blank page of his register before he died, and a young scrivener at a river port three hundred li away, in a letter to a colleague. Neither ever heard of the other. A generation earlier, a retired customs scribe's letter had circled the same idea without daring to ask it outright. The guilds of the fair pooled a prize: a golden ladle, and ten years free of market dues — for whoever either brought the routine itself, or said the thing dead once and for all, letting the whole town give up hoping and know that no such routine would ever exist.

金杓在行会堂里挂了十几年，捧着路数上门的人倒真不少。有的路数在小桌上灵得出奇，一过百桌就散了架；桑娘子不必与谁争辩，挑一串名帖递过去，来人自己对上一圈，脸就红了——拆穿一张路数，原来和对一张席图一样快。可大集的宴席从没因此黄过一回：开席前总有那么几个通宵，桑娘子带着徒弟靠手艺，偶尔也靠撞大运，把图硬排了出来。难找是一回事，找不着，是另一回事。

The golden ladle hung in the guild hall for a dozen years and more, and men came to its door clutching routines often enough. Some routines worked wonders on small tables and came apart past a hundred; Madam Sang needed no quarrel with such men — she handed over a string of name-cards, the caller walked the tables once himself, and his face went red. Breaking a routine was as quick as checking a chart. Yet the fair's banquet never once failed on that account: before every opening day there were always a few nights of sitting up, Sang and her apprentices working the chart out by craft, now and then by sheer luck. Hard to find is one thing; never to be found is another.

也是从那些年起，镇上人渐渐认出一桩怪事：好些熬人的活计，原是宴席换了衣裳。药铺的伙计发愁药架，性子相冲的药材不能挨着放；盐商的管事发愁货舱，受潮的盐包不能压在茶箱上头；巡夜的老总发愁班次，结过梁子的更夫不能同守一道栅门。把这些名目送到排席人手上，竟都能改写成一行行的名字、一桌桌的座次。打那以后，谁家夜里睡不着，都知道该把愁事往哪儿送，也都明白那层意思：这些活计原是彼此的变装，哪一件要是先寻着了快法，其余的连夜就全破了。

It was in those same years that the town came to recognize a strange thing: many of the chores that kept people awake were the banquet in changed clothes. The pharmacy boy fretted over his shelves, for herbs of clashing temper could not be set side by side; the salt merchant's steward fretted over his hold, for damp salt sacks could not be stowed above the tea chests; the old watch captain fretted over his rotations, for two watchmen with a grudge could never share the same gate. Brought to the Seater, every one of these could be rewritten as rows of names and an order of tables. From then on, anyone lying awake at night knew where to bring the worry, and understood what it meant: these chores were disguises of one another, and should a quick way be found for any single one of them, the rest would all fall that very night.

桑娘子的女儿阿棠在《小席谱》边上长大，十七岁那年当着满堂徒弟开了口：「对图一盏茶，找图凭什么就该熬一个月？我不信世上会这样吝啬。」桑娘子拿得出来作答的，只有一双熬坏的手。她信路数不会有，她见过太多路数被长长的名帖撑断；可这话她拿不出凭据，一点也拿不出。母女俩争到灯残。阿棠认定，路数一到手，憨儿子也能一夜排出她母亲一辈子才排得出的图，手艺的光环就得摘下来——她认了，还盼着；桑娘子认定，那点光环底下压着的是几十年的死功夫，若叫一夜拆了，还有些别的东西会跟着塌。天亮时，阿棠把自己的积蓄包进包袱，上路寻路数去了。

Sang's daughter Atang grew up beside the Book of Small Feasts, and in her seventeenth year she spoke out before a hall full of apprentices: "If a chart takes one cup of tea to check, by what right should it take a month to make? I refuse to believe the world is that miserly." All Sang had to answer with was a pair of hands worn out by lamps. She believed no routine would ever come; she had watched too many routines be stretched apart by long strings of name-cards. But of that belief she had no proof, none at all. Mother and daughter argued the lamp down to its stub. Atang held that once the routine arrived, even the dull-witted boy could seat in a single night what had taken her mother a lifetime, and the halo would come off the craft — she accepted that, she even hoped for it. Sang held that beneath that halo lay decades of patient labor, and if it were torn off in a night, something else would come down with it. At dawn Atang wrapped her savings into a bundle and took the road to look for the routine.

阿棠回来那年，桑娘子已经下葬三年。她鬓角灰了，包袱里只有一路收来的破路数：没有一件撑得过百桌，也没有一件能叫人断定自己永远撑不过。镇上答那句老问话的人一年比一年齐——路数不会有；真熬过通宵的，几乎全这么答。阿棠把仇册和《小席谱》接了过来。堂前那两扇雕花门是不是同一个木匠的手笔，镇上至今没人说得清：它不像谱里的活计那样好对付，又始终没人能把它改写成一行行的名字；它卡在正中间，哪一边也不归。开席前一天，她沿桌走一圈，一盏茶没凉；夜里她伏在灯下，替下一回大集的百桌排名字。金杓还挂在行会堂里，每回开集前有人掸一回灰，始终无人领走。

The year Atang came back, Sang had already been three years in her grave. Her temples were gray, and her bundle held nothing but the broken routines she had gathered along the way: not one had stood up to a hundred tables, and not one could be shown never to stand up to them. Year by year the townsfolk answered the old question more with one voice — the routine will never come; among those who had truly sat up through the nights, nearly all said so. Atang took over the grudge book and the Book of Small Feasts. Whether the two carved doors at the hall's front came from the same joiner's hands, no one in town could say to this day: they were not so easily handled as the work in the book, yet no one had ever managed to rewrite them as rows of names; they sat stuck in the middle, belonging to neither side. The day before a banquet she could walk the tables once, before the tea went cold; at night she bent over the lamp, setting names in order for the next fair's hundred tables. The golden ladle still hung in the guild hall, dusted once before every fair, claimed by no one.

注解：这说的是P 对 NP 问题（the P vs NP problem）：一张席图，对出来一盏茶，找出来一个月——凡是答案能被快速核对的难题，是否也能被快速解开，至今没有答案。寓言也留了一道口子：镇上的大宴回回照样摆成，正如那些出了名难缠的活计，多数具体场合其实排得开；悬而未决的，只是那套一劳永逸的快法究竟有没有。

Note: This is the P vs NP problem: a chart takes one cup of tea to check and a month to make — whether every hard question whose answer can be quickly checked can also be quickly solved remains unanswered to this day. The fable also leaves an honest gap: the town's great banquets were seated every time all the same, just as many famously intractable chores do yield in most particular cases; what hangs unresolved is only whether a once-and-for-all quick method exists.
