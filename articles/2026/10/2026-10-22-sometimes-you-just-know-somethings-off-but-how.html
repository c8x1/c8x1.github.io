有时候，你「就是知道」不对劲——可你是怎么知道的？

Marc A Moffett·Aeon·2026年10月22日·15分钟阅读·原文

当人们说出这类话时，通常并不是在从一串推理中得出结论。那个不肯上陌生人车的人并不是在想：「他表现出了这三种行为线索，因此他构成危险。」那封邮件不是靠一份明确的核查清单被标记出来的，那笔交易也不是通过正式审计来宣告自身缺陷的。在每一种情形中，都是某种东西径直呈现为具有某种品格：那个人显得瘆人，那封邮件显得欺诈，那笔交易显得可疑。然而，依照这些直觉行事，看起来不仅情有可原，而且完全合乎理性。如果那个人确实让你觉得瘆得慌，那么不坐他的车多半是明智之举——哪怕你说不清为什么。

When people say such things, they are not typically drawing conclusions from a chain of reasoning. The person who avoids getting in the stranger’s car is not thinking: ‘He displayed these three behavioural cues and, therefore, he poses a risk.’ The email isn’t flagged by going through an explicit checklist. The deal doesn’t announce its flaws through a formal audit. In each case, something is simply presented as having a certain character: the man as creepy, the email as fraudulent, the deal as suspect. And yet, acting on these intuitions seems not merely excusable but entirely reasonable. If the man really does strike you as creepy, staying out of his car was probably the smart call – even if you can’t say why.

尽管如此，「感觉」（vibes）与「直觉」（intuitions）在日常生活与学术探究中常常被斥为非理性之物，或仅仅是启发式的手段。虽然我们并非总能信任这类反应，但它们恰恰居于核心地位，甚至不可或缺——我们正是靠着它们才得以理性地在世界中穿行。然而，对于这些寻常直觉的理性资质，哲学家们迄今阐明的少得出奇。相反，他们把大部分注意力集中在一种远为清高的直觉上，即所谓理性直觉（rational intuition），认为它主要在哲学、逻辑学与数学这些先验学科中发挥作用。

Despite this, ‘vibes’ and ‘intuitions’ are often dismissed both in daily life and in academic enquiry as irrational or merely heuristic devices. And while we cannot always trust such reactions, they are central, indeed indispensable, to our ability to rationally navigate the world. Philosophers, however, have done surprisingly little to illuminate the rational credentials of these ordinary kinds of intuitions. Instead, they have focused the bulk of their attention on a much more rarefied kind of intuition called rational intuition, which is taken to function primarily in the a priori disciplines of philosophy, logic and mathematics.

我的目标是解释，我们日常的直觉反应为何能拥有这样的理性力量。关键在于看到：直觉的运作方式与知觉大体相同——两者都能将某物直接呈现给我们，使之显得实际如此。一旦我们理解了是什么赋予知觉这一特征，便能看到直觉为何可能正是同一种基本认知能力的运用。

My objective is to explain why our everyday intuitive reactions can have such rational force. The key is to see that intuition works in much the same way as perception, in that both can directly present something to us as actual. Once we understand what gives perception this feature, we can see why intuitions might involve exercising the same basic kind of cognitive capacity.

刻画理性直觉的标准方式之一，是借助「显象」（seemings），并与知觉相类比。正如你在知觉上觉得车库里有一辆车，你也可以在理智上觉得 2 + 2 = 4——前者呈现一个物理事实，后者呈现一个概念事实。但理性直觉与你拒绝上陌生人车时起作用的日常直觉，并不像这个清高的例子所显示的那样相距遥远：哲学家的直觉，说到底不过是同一种基本能力的高度专门化版本。

A standard way of characterising rational intuition is in terms of seemings, by analogy with perception. Just as it perceptually seems to you that there is a car in the garage, so it can intellectually seem to you that 2 + 2 = 4 – the former presenting a physical fact, the latter a conceptual one. But rational intuition and the everyday intuition at work when you decline to get in a stranger’s car are not as far apart as this rarefied example suggests: the philosopher’s intuition is, at bottom, a highly specialised version of the same basic capacity.

要把这两种能力拉到一处，不妨看看哲学家们钟爱的消遣：思想实验。哲学家们偏好奇异物事，这一点在他们对思想实验的征用上体现得最为清楚。来看两个让哲学家们萦绕心头数十年的案例——一个来自伦理学，一个来自知识论。

To draw these two capacities together, let’s consider a favourite pastime of philosophers: the thought experiment. Philosophers have a penchant for exotica, which is never more clearly on display than in their invocation of thought experiments. Consider two cases that have occupied philosophers for decades – one from ethics, one from the theory of knowledge.

在电车难题中，哲学家菲利帕·富特（Philippa Foot）与朱迪斯·贾维斯·汤姆森（Judith Jarvis Thomson）设想了一辆失控的电车，呼啸着冲向被绑在轨道上的五个人。你正站在上方的一座桥上，身旁还有另一位路人。要拦住电车、救下那五个人，唯一的办法就是把这位路人推下桥。如果你这么做了，他的身体会挡住电车，而他则会因此丧命。许多人当即而强烈地感到：把他推下去是错的，哪怕这能救下五条性命。可是，为什么？

In the trolley problem, the philosophers Philippa Foot and Judith Jarvis Thomson imagined a runaway trolley hurtling toward five people tied to the track. You are standing on a bridge above, next to another bystander. The only way to stop the trolley and save the five is to push the bystander off the bridge. If you do, his body will halt the trolley, but he will die as a result. Many people feel, immediately and forcefully, that pushing him is wrong, even if it saves five lives. But why?

或者看看卡尔·吉内特（Carl Ginet）那个假谷仓案例。设想你正开车穿行乡间，看到一座谷仓。它看上去完完全全是谷仓的模样；你注意力集中，光线良好。你形成了一个完全合理的信念：这是一座谷仓。你不知道的是，你正身处「假谷仓之乡」：这一地区遍布做工考究的谷仓立面，从路上望去与真谷仓无从分辨。而你恰好望着的是此地唯一的一座真谷仓。你的信念是真的，也似乎得到了证成。然而，几乎所有考虑过这个案例的人都有一种即时而坚定的感觉：你并不真的「知道」那是一座谷仓。可是，为什么不能？

Or consider Carl Ginet’s case involving fake barns. Imagine you are driving through the countryside and see a barn. It looks exactly like a barn; you’re paying attention, the light is good. You form the perfectly reasonable belief that this is a barn. What you don’t know is that you’re in ‘fake-barn country’: the region is dotted with elaborate barn facades, indistinguishable from real barns when viewed from the road. You happen to be looking at the one real barn in this area. Your belief is true, and it seems justified. And yet almost everyone who considers this case has the immediate, firm sense that you don’t really know it’s a barn. But why not?

在怀疑者与门外汉看来，这些思想实验显得异想天开。因为我们总是以其最戏剧化的形态遭遇它们——失控的电车、假谷仓——思想实验便显得光怪陆离。但恰当地理解之下，这些想象的插曲是磨砺我们把握关键概念的重要工具。事实上，它们的运作与某种远为熟悉的东西一脉相承：我们在日常生活中例行地思量各种替代可能，琢磨着在那老生常谈的路岔口，走这条路而非那条路会发生什么。

To sceptics and the uninitiated, these thought experiments can seem fantastical. Because we encounter them in their most dramatic forms, like runaway trolleys and fake barns, thought experiments seem exotic. But properly understood, these imaginative episodes are important tools for sharpening our grasp of key concepts. In fact, their functioning is continuous with something far more familiar: the way we routinely entertain alternative possibilities in ordinary life, wondering what might happen if we take one path rather than another in the proverbial fork in the road.

这些想象，正是思想实验在日常生活中现身的样子。设想你正在决定，要不要告诉一位朋友他的丈夫出了轨。你在脑海里放映那场对话、那份震惊、他脸上浮现的伤痛。而在那个想象的瞬间，你看到了：造成这样的痛苦，是一个缄口的理由——哪怕它算不上一个充分的或决定性的理由。

These imaginings are just thought experiments as they appear in everyday life. Imagine you are deciding whether to tell a friend that his husband has been unfaithful. You picture the conversation, the shock, the hurt that registers on his face. And in that imagined moment, you see that causing such pain is a reason to hold your tongue, even if it’s not a good or decisive one.

你是如何判定这一点构成反对吐露隐情的理由的？并不是你字面意义上「看到」造成痛苦是不做某事的理由，你也不是从别的东西推出这一点。毋宁说，在思量这个可能性时，你把他的痛苦领会为一个相关的考量、某种不利于披露的因素。它作为理由的地位不是出自某个论证，而是直接出自你的理解。它就是显得像一个让你缄口的理由。

How did you determine that this was a reason against divulging the infidelity? It’s not as if you literally see that causing pain is a reason not to do something, nor do you infer it from other things. Rather, in entertaining the possibility, you apprehended his pain as a relevant consideration, as something that counts against disclosure. Its status as a reason emerged not from an argument, but directly from your understanding. It just seems like a reason to hold your tongue.

请注意，这一现象也出现在上文讨论的思想实验中。把路人从桥上推下去，就是显得不对；同样，在假谷仓之乡你会知道那是一座真谷仓，这一点就是显得为假。在这两个案例中，答案都不是从论证中产生的。这些直觉不请自来，先于任何斟酌。

Notice that this phenomenon also occurs in the thought experiments discussed above. It just seems wrong to push the bystander from the bridge; similarly, it just seems false that you would know it was a real barn in fake-barn country. In both cases, the answer doesn’t arise from an argument. These intuitions arrive unbidden, prior to any deliberation.

这些显象带有一种不容错认的认识论牵引力。因为朋友的痛苦显得像一个理由，我们便把它当作理由接受：显象即是理由。耐人寻味的不只是我们拥有这类显象，更是我们信任它们。它们参与塑造我们的斟酌，指引我们的行动，引发我们的信念。它们不只是碰巧浮上心头；它们仿佛揭示了，在那个想象出的情境中，事情必定如何。

These seemings have an unmistakable epistemic pull. Because your friend’s pain seems like a reason, we accept it as such: the seeming is the reason. What is striking is not simply that we have such seemings, but that we trust them. They are involved in shaping our deliberations, guiding our actions, and eliciting belief. They do not merely occur to us; they appear to reveal something about how things must be in the imagined scenario.

然而，直觉尽管寻常，却仍令人费解。在知觉经验中，我们至少有一套现成的框架：世界作用于我们的感官，不同的知觉通道彼此印证。归根结底，我们可以追溯这些经验得以产生所经由的因果路径。直觉却拿不出这样的装置。它的裁断就那么径直呈现出来，没有哪个器官在工作，也没有什么我们可以指认为其所接触的东西。于是，我们在对其本性或认识论权威缺乏清晰说明的情况下依赖着它们。但要理解直觉本身，我们需要先把它们安放进一片更宽广的认识论地貌之中——一片由统一我们基本信念之证成的种种尝试所塑造的地貌。

For all their ordinariness, however, intuitions remain puzzling. In perceptual experience, we at least have an established framework: the world impinges on our senses, and different perceptual modalities reinforce one another. In the end, we can trace the causal route it takes to produce them. Intuition offers no such apparatus. Its verdicts simply present themselves, with no organ at work and nothing we can point to that they make contact with. Consequently, we rely on them without a clear account of their nature or epistemic authority. But before we can understand intuitions themselves, we need to situate them within a broader epistemological landscape, one shaped by attempts to unify the justification of our basic beliefs.

我们都对世界抱有信念，也在乎它们是否为真。我们想把事情弄对。但我们无法跳出自己的心灵，拿信念去与世界核对；我们至多能做到的，是出于好的理由持有它——出于那些从我们所站的位置看支持其为真的考量。这就是证成。

We all have beliefs about the world, and we care whether they are true. We want to get things right. But we can’t step outside our own minds to check a belief against the world; the best we can do is to hold it for good reasons – for considerations that, from where we stand, speak in favour of its truth. That is what justification is.

现在假设信念只能被其他信念证成。那么，正如古代怀疑论者所指出的，任何信念都永远无法得到证成。每个信念的支持都得自更多的信念，而那些信念自身又需要更多的支持，如此以至于无穷。要挫败这种倒退，我们有三个选项：接受一条无穷的链条（无穷主义），允许链条绕回自身（融贯论），或者主张某些信念独立于其他信念而得到证成（基础主义）。我要阐发的正是最后一个选项，即证成存在某个起点或基础的观点。但要这么做，我们必须先花一点时间解释：一个信念如何能被信念之外的某种东西所证成。

Now suppose that beliefs could only ever be justified by other beliefs. Then, as the ancient Sceptics observed, no belief could ever be justified at all. Each belief would derive its support from further beliefs, which would themselves require further support, and so on without end. To foil this regress, we have three options: embrace an infinite chain (infinitism), allow the chain to loop back on itself (coherentism), or hold that some beliefs are justified independently of others (foundationalism). It is this final option, the view that there is a starting point or foundation for justification, that I will develop. But to do so, we must spend a little time explaining how a belief can be justified by something other than another belief.

如果在视觉上向你呈现有一棵树，那么你就拥有了相信那里有一棵树的证成

If it visually seems to you that there is a tree, then you have a justification for believing that there is a tree

知觉是最简单的例子。当我看见一棵树时，我的视觉经验使我觉得面前有一棵树。树被呈现给我。正如哲学家威廉·托尔赫斯特（William Tolhurst）曾说过的，知觉显象带有「真实之感」（the feel of truth），一种揭示事物本来面目的感觉（不像单纯的念头、想象或欲望，它们缺乏这种披露的气息）。但视觉经验本身并不是信念：我可以拥有它们而不形成信念，也许因为我不信任自己的视力，或怀疑自己的饮料被人做了手脚。反过来，我也可以不曾看见却相信有一棵树，也许因为有人告诉我那里有一棵。尽管如此，我的视觉经验仿佛向我揭示出一棵树，这一事实与解释我为何相信那里有一棵、以及这一信念为何合理，是相关的。

Perception is the simplest example. When I see a tree, my visual experience makes it seem as if there is a tree in front of me. I am presented with the tree. As the philosopher William Tolhurst once put it, perceptual seemings have ‘the feel of truth’, a sense of revealing how things really are (unlike mere thoughts, imaginings or desires, which lack this air of disclosure). But visual experiences are not themselves beliefs: I can have them without forming a belief, perhaps because I distrust my eyesight or suspect my drink has been spiked. Conversely, I can believe there is a tree without seeing it, perhaps because I am told there is one there. Nevertheless, the fact that my visual experience seems to reveal a tree to me is relevant to explaining why I come to believe there is one and why this belief is reasonable.

这正是我们遏制理由倒退所需的装置：一种状态——视觉经验——它不是信念，却仍能证成我的信念。一个有影响的方案，由吉姆·普赖尔（Jim Pryor）与迈克尔·休默（Michael Huemer）以不同的方式发展出来：当世界向我们显得是某种样子时，我们便由此获得了相信它就是那个样子的某种证成——除非我们有理由不信任它。更具体地说，如果在视觉上向你呈现面前有一棵树，那么你就径直拥有了相信那里有一棵树的证成。这一证成并不依赖于你握有独立的理由，去认为你的知觉系统运转正常、你没有产生幻觉、或你的环境并无恶意。知觉显象本身就承载着证成的分量。当然，我的证成可能被击败（比如，出现证据表明我服用了致幻剂）。要点仅仅在于：要让一个知觉显象在理性上支持相应的信念，不需要任何进一步的正向信念。

This is the set-up we need in order to stop the regress of reasons: a state – the visual experience – that is not a belief, but that can nevertheless justify my belief. One influential proposal, developed in different ways by Jim Pryor and Michael Huemer, is that when the world seems to us to be a certain way, we thereby have some justification for believing that it is that way – unless we have a reason to distrust it. More specifically, if it visually seems to you that there is a tree in front of you, then you simply have a justification for believing that there is a tree. The justification does not depend on possessing independent reasons to think that your perceptual system is functioning properly, or that you are not hallucinating, or that your environment is benign. The perceptual seeming itself carries justificatory weight. Of course, my justification can be defeated (for instance, by evidence that I have taken a hallucinogen). The point is simply that no further positive belief is needed for a perceptual seeming to rationally support the corresponding belief.

这就为解决倒退问题提供了基本框架。知觉经验本身不是信念，却为信念提供理由。而且它们提供理由，既不依赖先在的已证成信念，自身也无需证成。不过，这仍留下一个重要问题：这些经验身上的什么东西，使它们能够以这种方式证成信念？

This provides the basic framework for solving the regress problem. Perceptual experiences are not themselves beliefs, yet they provide reasons for belief. And they do so without relying on antecedent justified beliefs and are not themselves in need of justification. Still, this leaves us with an important question: what is it about these experiences that allows them to justify belief in this way?

知觉经验一般而言——视觉经验尤其是——具有两个我们熟悉的特点。其一，如前所见，它们仿佛向我们揭示世界，把对象与属性呈现为确实存在于世界之中。其二，它们具有一种鲜活的现象学性质，一种独特的「如其所感」（what-it's-likeness），正如哲学家托马斯·内格尔（Thomas Nagel）所言。许多哲学家认为这两个特点不可分离：知觉之所以显得披露实在，正是因为它给人的感受如此。按这种标准观点，知觉的现象学性质是呈现性的，而正是这种呈现性的现象学性质解释了知觉经验的认识论力量。

Perceptual experience in general, and visual experience in particular, have two familiar features. First, as we have seen, they seem to reveal the world to us, to present objects and properties as actually being out there in the world. Second, they have a vivid phenomenology, a distinctive what-it’s-likeness, as the philosopher Thomas Nagel put it. Many philosophers take these two features to be inseparable: perception seems to disclose reality because it feels the way it does. On this standard view, its phenomenology is presentational, and it is this presentational phenomenology that explains the epistemic force of perceptual experience.

恰恰在这里，直觉重新登场。按照约翰·本森（John Bengson）的准知觉论（quasi-perceptualism），我们应当以知觉经验的认识论行为为模型，来塑造直觉的认识论行为：如果知觉显象之所以证成，是因为它们具有呈现性的现象学性质，而如果直觉要以同样的基本方式证成，那么它们就必须共有同一种呈现性的现象学性质。当我们有直觉时，它凭借展示出一种与知觉的「如其所感」相类似的可感性质——一种将其内容呈现为实际的理智显象——来证成相应的信念。举个例子，再想想那个让你感到格外瘆人的陌生人。正如树在视觉上显得是绿的，这个陌生人也可能就是显得不可信任。他身上的某种东西自行以那种方式向你呈现，而你并未推究清楚究竟是什么让你不安。

It is at this point that intuitions re-enter the story. According to John Bengson’s concept of quasi-perceptualism, we should model the epistemic behaviour of intuitions on the epistemic behaviour of perceptual experiences: if perceptual seemings justify because they possess a presentational phenomenology, and if intuitions are to justify in the same basic way, then they must share that same kind of presentational phenomenology. When we have an intuition, it justifies a corresponding belief by virtue of exhibiting a felt quality analogous to the what-it’s-likeness of perception, a kind of intellectual seeming that presents its content as actual. For instance, think again of the stranger who gives you a seriously creepy vibe. Just as the tree visually seems green, so the stranger may simply seem untrustworthy. Something about him presents itself to you that way without you having worked out what exactly is making you uncomfortable.

按这种观点，对直觉之认识论力量的解释，与对知觉之认识论力量的解释互为镜像。两种状态都凭借一种独特的呈现性现象学性质，把某些事实呈现为实在（或把某些命题呈现为真）。不妨称之为「现象策略」。二者的差别仅仅在于这种现象学性质的品格。在知觉中，它是对外部世界的经验性感官经验；在直觉中，它是一种理智的或概念的经验，据说揭示着世界的规范性、模态或概念结构。这些是我们可以领会却无法看见的世界特征：无端的残忍构成反对某个行动的理由（规范性结构），不可能存在圆的方形（模态结构），或者，假谷仓之乡那个走运的信徒仍然并不真的知道（概念结构）。

On this view, the explanation for the epistemic force of intuition mirrors the explanation for the epistemic force of perception. Both states present certain facts as real (or present certain propositions as true) by virtue of a distinctive presentational phenomenology. Call this the phenomenal strategy. The difference between them lies simply in the character of this phenomenology. In perception, it is an empirical sensory experience of the external world; in intuition, it is an intellectual or conceptual experience that purportedly reveals the normative, modal or conceptual structure of the world. These are features of the world that we comprehend but can’t see – that gratuitous cruelty counts against taking an action (normative structure), that there could be no round square (modal structure), or that the lucky believer in fake-barn country still doesn’t really know (conceptual structure).

但这种关于直觉之认识论意义的现象学理论，面临一个严重的困难——蒂莫西·威廉姆森（Timothy Williamson）与欧内斯特·索萨（Ernest Sosa）对此的施压最为有力。许多善于反思的人报告说，他们并没有体验到准知觉论所设定的那种理智现象学性质。对他们来说，判定一个命题「显得正确」，根本不涉及任何独特的体验性质。如果直觉在这些情形中缺乏所需的现象学性质，那么准知觉论对其认识论力量的解释便告崩塌。（不妨在你自己身上检验一番：在评估那些思想实验时，你感受到某种体验性质了吗？）

But this phenomenological theory of the epistemic significance of intuitions faces a serious difficulty, one pressed most forcefully by Timothy Williamson and Ernest Sosa. Many reflective people report that they do not experience anything like the intellectual phenomenology that quasi-perceptualism posits. For them, judging that a proposition ‘seems right’ involves no distinctive experiential quality at all. If intuition lacks the requisite phenomenology in these cases, then the quasi-perceptualist explanation of its epistemic force collapses. (It’s worth evaluating this in yourself: do you feel an experiential quality when evaluating the thought experiments?)

这就是所谓的「直觉缺席」挑战。那种在准知觉论基础上发展出来的坚实的呈现性现象学性质，许多人干脆否认自己拥有。由此，要么这些人缺乏对自身直觉的内省通达（一个不太厚道的结论），要么直觉的认识论力量不可能依赖于一种特殊理智现象学性质的存在。如果直觉要像准知觉论所主张的那样证成信念，那么做这份认识论工作的，就必须是现象学性质之外的某种东西。

This is the so-called absent intuition challenge. The kind of robust, presentational phenomenology developed on the basis of quasi-perceptualism is something many people simply deny having. Consequently, either these individuals lack introspective access to their own intuitions (an uncharitable conclusion), or the epistemic force of intuition cannot depend on the presence of a special intellectual phenomenology. If intuitions are to justify in the way quasi-perceptualism claims, then something other than phenomenology must be doing the epistemic work.

这个结论还可以推得更远，乃至质疑现象学性质对知觉的认识论意义。要明白其中的缘由，不妨看看被称为「现实感丧失症」（derealisation disorder）的现象。患有此症的人报告说，他们的知觉经验从内部感受起来完全正常——颜色、形状与感觉性质都完好无损——然而世界在他们看来并不真实。他们的经验缺乏任何披露感或启示力。在这一点上，现实感丧失症恰是完美幻觉的镜像。在现实感丧失症中，经验把事情弄对了，却感觉不到有什么被揭示；在幻觉中，经验把事情弄错了，却感觉起来完全令人信服。这层含义颇为惊人。姑且把这种对实在的表面披露称为「呈现性」（presentationality）：当一个心理状态的内容被领会为事情实际如何、而不仅仅是被思考或被想象的样子时，它就是呈现性的。现实感丧失症表明，无论是知觉状态所携带的信息，还是该状态的感受方式，都不足以构成呈现性。一个状态可以在感受上与知觉一模一样，却不把其内容呈现为实际。因此，现象学性质解释不了知觉显象的认识论力量，更遑论直觉。

This conclusion can be pushed further to call into question even the epistemic significance of phenomenology for perception. To see why, consider the phenomenon known as derealisation disorder. Individuals with this condition report that their perceptual experiences feel perfectly normal from the inside – the colours, shapes and sensory qualities are all intact – yet the world does not seem real to them. Their experiences lack any sense of disclosure or revelatory force. In this respect, derealisation is the mirror image of a perfect hallucination. In derealisation, the experience gets things right, but it doesn’t feel like anything is being revealed; in hallucination, the experience gets things wrong, yet it feels totally convincing. The implication is striking. Call this apparent disclosure of reality presentationality: a mental state is presentational when its content is apprehended as how things actually are, rather than merely as they are thought about or imagined. Derealisation shows that neither the information carried by a perceptual state nor the way that state feels are sufficient for presentationality. A state can feel exactly like perception while failing to present its contents as actual. Phenomenology, then, cannot explain the epistemic force of perceptual seemings, let alone intuitions.

这提示我们，准知觉论的辩护者们一直在错误的地方寻找。前面我提到，知觉经验有两个显著特点：它具有丰富的现象学性质，它又仿佛向我们揭示世界。现象策略抓住了第一个特点——这可以理解，因为知觉的现象学性质是那么鲜活醒目——然后把它当成了第二个特点的来源。但现实感丧失症的案例表明，即便经验失去了它的启示性质，现象学性质仍可完好无损。对证成而言，要紧的不是我们如何体验某物，而是我们如何领会它。在我所辩护的观点中，呈现性是一种认知姿态，一种领会内容的方式，而非经验的某种独特感受方式。而正是这种姿态——这种把握世界的方式，而非其现象学的光晕——为知觉经验供给了认识论力量。

This suggests that defenders of quasi-perceptualism have been looking in the wrong place. Earlier, I noted that perceptual experience has two salient features: it has a rich phenomenology, and it seems to reveal the world to us. The phenomenal strategy seized on the first feature – understandably, given how vivid and conspicuous perceptual phenomenology is – and then treated it as the source of the second. But derealisation cases show that phenomenology can remain intact even when the experience loses its revelatory character. What matters for justification is not how we experience something, but how we apprehend it. On the view I defend, presentationality is a cognitive posture, a way of apprehending a content rather than a distinctive way the experience feels. And it is this posture, this way of grasping the world and not its phenomenological glow, that supplies perceptual experience with its epistemic force.

要看清这种姿态究竟是什么，不妨把它与讨论更多的态度相对照。比如，信念是断定性的：它使我们承诺于其内容为真。想象则是非断定性的：它思量种种可能性，而不把它们当作实际。呈现性态度（也就是显象）居于中间地带。正是这种认知姿态使显象能够证成信念：它呈现实在而不对其作出承诺，为我们提供一条直接却可被击败的通往接受之路。

To see what this posture amounts to, it helps to contrast it with more widely discussed attitudes. Belief, for example, is assertive: it commits us to the truth of its content. Imagination, by contrast, is non-assertive: it entertains possibilities without treating them as actual. Presentational attitudes (that is, seemings) occupy a middle ground. This cognitive posture is what makes a seeming capable of justifying a belief: it presents reality without committing to it, offering us a direct but defeasible route to acceptance.

一旦以这种方式理解了呈现性，直觉的案例便自然而然各归其位。呈现性状态的一个关键特征是：它们是遭逢，而非行动。它们在我们身上发生；我们并不施行它们。所以，当某事在直觉上显得为真时，我们并不决定拥有这个直觉，正如我们并不决定看见一棵树。它不请自来。这种被动性恰恰使一个显象能够把其内容呈现为实际。当直觉袭来，我们并不是在下判断或形成信念。我们是在把内容领会为事情实际的样子。直觉以某种召唤信念的方式，把情境呈现为实际。在这个方面，直觉显象与知觉显象扮演着同一角色。

Once we understand presentationality in this way, the case of intuition falls naturally into place. A crucial feature of presentational states is that they are happenings, not actions. They occur in us; we do not perform them. So, when something intuitively seems true, we do not decide to have this intuition any more than we decide to see a tree. It arrives unbidden. This passivity is precisely what allows a seeming to present its content as actual. When an intuition strikes us, we are not making a judgment or forming a belief. We are apprehending the content as the way things are. The intuition presents the scenario as actual in a manner that calls for belief. In this respect, intuitive seemings share the same role as perceptual seemings.

这样理解之下，直觉缺席的挑战便消解了。准知觉论者假定，直觉的呈现力必定源自一种理智现象学性质，与知觉的感官现象学性质相类似。但如果呈现性是一种认知姿态，而非现象学的光晕，那就不再需要任何特殊的「理智经验」。一个人在直觉升起时可以没有任何独特的现象学性质，却仍在相关意义上把内容领会为实际。而一旦内容被领会为实际，形成相应的信念便是合理的。准知觉论者说对了一点：直觉与知觉共有某种根本的东西；却搞错了这共有的东西是什么。它不是一种专属的现象学性质，而是领会活动本身的性质。

Understood in this way, the absent-intuition challenge dissolves. The quasi-perceptualist assumed that intuition’s presentational force must arise from an intellectual phenomenology analogous to the sensory phenomenology of perception. But if presentationality is a cognitive posture rather than a phenomenological glow, then no special ‘intellectual experience’ is needed. A person may have no distinctive phenomenology when an intuition arises and yet still apprehend the content as actual in the relevant sense. And once a content is apprehended as actual, it is reasonable to form the corresponding belief. The quasi-perceptualist is right that intuition and perception share something fundamental, but wrong about what that shared feature is. It is not a proprietary phenomenology, but the nature of the apprehension itself.

为什么把内容领会为实际会具有任何认识论意义？我认为，答案很简单。呈现性状态是让我们与世界发生接触的认知事件，或者更确切地说，是显得让我们与世界发生接触的认知事件。当某事物把自己呈现为如此这般时，我们默认的认知反应便是形成相应的信念。当我转过楼角、目光落在一棵树上时，树就那么在我面前。相信那里有一棵树，通常并不是我推出的一个结论；毋宁说，我只是接受我的经验向我呈现的东西。这不是推理或斟酌之事；这是理性心灵对其所认作的实在的自然回应。

Why should apprehending a content as actual have any epistemic significance? The answer, I think, is simple. Presentational states are the cognitive events that place us in contact with the world or, more precisely, that seem to place us in contact with the world. When something presents itself to us as being the case, our default cognitive response is to form the corresponding belief. When I turn the corner of a building and my eyes fall on a tree, the tree is simply there before me. Believing that there is a tree is not normally a conclusion I draw; rather I simply accept what my experience presents to me. This is not a matter of inference or deliberation; it is the natural way a rational mind responds to what it takes to be reality.

信念的形成，不太像得出一个结论，倒更像屈服于一次披露。当领会准确时，这种默认反应可靠地产生真信念；当领会不准确时，这一反应仍然可以理解、仍然合理。呈现性状态之所以在认识论上可敬，是因为它们正是世界向我们现身之处：实在对信念提出其最初主张的那个节点。直觉继承了这一地位。它们不过是这样的呈现性事件——我们借此把一个情境的概念结构领会为实际，并由此为信念提供理性基础，而无需任何进一步的认识论机制。

Belief-formation is less like drawing a conclusion and more like yielding to a disclosure. When the apprehension is accurate, this default response reliably produces true belief; when it is not, the response is still intelligible and reasonable. Presentational states are epistemically respectable because they are where the world shows up for us: the point at which reality makes its first claim on belief. Intuitions inherit this status. They are simply the presentational events through which we apprehend the conceptual structure of a situation as actual, and in doing so provide a rational basis for belief without the need for any further epistemic mechanism.

直觉浸透了我们的寻常推理。每当我们看出某物蕴含着什么、注意到某事构成支持某个行动的理由、或认出某事是一条可能的行动路线，我们都在依赖直觉。这些时刻很少自我宣告。它们并不伴随现象学的锣鼓登场。它们织入我们斟酌的背景，在我们把一个情境尽收眼底、即刻领会其规范性或概念轮廓之时悄然运作。

Intuition saturates our ordinary reasoning. Whenever we see what follows from something, notice that something counts in favour of some action, or recognise something as a possible course of action, we are relying on intuition. These moments rarely announce themselves. They do not arrive with phenomenological fanfare. They are woven into the background of our deliberation, operating quietly whenever we take in a situation and immediately apprehend its normative or conceptual contours.

在这个意义上，直觉之于认知，犹如知觉之于我们与物理世界的打交道。知觉通过揭示我们周遭的事情如何，让我们得以在环境中穿行；直觉通过揭示概念地形中的事情如何，让我们得以在理由、规范与可能性的空间中穿行。当我们斟酌时，我们不断依靠直觉来领会哪些考量要紧、它们如何相互牵连、又能从中推出什么。直觉是情境的概念结构向我们显现于其中的认知手段。

In this sense, intuition is to cognition what perception is to our engagement with the physical world. Perception allows us to navigate our environment by revealing how things stand around us; intuition allows us to navigate the space of reasons, norms and possibilities by revealing how things stand within the conceptual landscape. When we deliberate, we constantly rely on intuitions to apprehend which considerations matter, how they bear on one another, and what follows from them. Intuition is the cognitive means by which the conceptual structure of a situation is revealed to us.

从这个角度看，我们开篇的那些思想实验便不再显得是哲学珍玩。它们只不过是我们时刻依赖的那种能力的高度受控实例。当我们想象一辆失控的电车，或一座被假货环绕的真谷仓时，我们并不是在召唤某种神秘官能，也不是在窥视一台形而上学的望远镜。我们是在运用同一种基本能力——与想象向朋友坦陈一个艰难真相、或掂量不同可能行动路线时所用相同的能力。唯一的区别在于，哲学案例是精心打造出来、用以孤立出某个特定概念关联的。它们戏剧化的行头掩盖了一个事实：它们依赖的，正是在更平凡的推理中起作用的那种呈现性态度。

Seen in this light, the thought experiments with which we started no longer appear as philosophical curiosities. They are simply highly controlled instances of a capacity we rely on constantly. When we imagine a runaway trolley or a real barn surrounded by fakes, we are not invoking a mysterious faculty or peering into a metaphysical telescope. We are exercising the same basic capacity we use when we imagine telling a difficult truth to a friend or weigh up different possible courses of action. The only difference is that philosophical cases are crafted to isolate a specific conceptual relation. Their dramatic trappings obscure the fact that they depend on the same presentational attitude at work in more mundane reasoning.

这让思想实验落回地面。它们的认识论意义并非来自奇异的题材或想象的场景，而是来自一个事实：它们触发的直觉披露了某些概念如何相互勾连。要看「相互勾连」是什么意思，不妨想想讲故事的一条熟稔原则：展示，别讲述。当 J R R 托尔金（J R R Tolkien）在《魔戒》（The Lord of the Rings，1954-55）中描写生物咕噜领着弗罗多走进蜘蛛尸罗的巢穴时，他不必停下来解释：「这是背叛。」叙述者并不标注咕噜的忠诚何时转移、他的意图何时越过道德门槛。我们直接从事件的展开中把握了它。情境把自己呈现为一场背叛：行动、动机与危险以某种方式对齐，以至于要求应用这个概念。这就是我说直觉揭示概念如何相互勾连时的意思。它们披露的是一个情境中未经言明的概念要求。

This brings thought experiments back down to earth. Their epistemic significance arises not from exotic subject matter or imaginary scenarios, but from the fact that they trigger intuitions that disclose how certain concepts hang together. To see what it means for concepts to ‘hang together’, consider a familiar principle of storytelling: show, don’t tell. When J R R Tolkien in The Lord of the Rings (1954-55) describes the creature Gollum leading Frodo into the spider Shelob’s lair, he needn’t pause to explain: ‘This is an act of betrayal.’ The narrator doesn’t mark the moment when Gollum’s loyalties shift, or when his intentions cross a moral threshold. We grasp it directly from the unfolding of events. The situation presents itself as a betrayal: the actions, motives and dangers align in a way that demands the application of the concept. That is what I mean when I say that intuitions reveal how concepts hang together. They disclose the otherwise unstated conceptual requirements of a situation.

当然，怀疑者可能会主张，这里起作用的并不是直觉，而仅仅是我们应用概念的技能。按这种观点，我们对案例反应正确，是因为我们擅长给情境分类。但这漏掉了这些分类之合理性中某个关键的东西。应用概念是一种行动；它是做出一个分类判断。而这类判断不是自我证成的。毫无依据地径直判定一个角色背叛了朋友，在认识论上是任意的。这类判断本身必须植根于某物。直觉先于任何判断、并且独立于任何判断而在我们身上发生。当一个情境显得是背叛的一个明显实例时，那个直觉显象本身并不是一次概念应用。它是先于判断的领会，正是它使随之而来的判断（即对背叛概念的随之应用）变得合理。在这个意义上，直觉并不与概念应用相竞争。它促成概念应用。

Now, a sceptic might argue that what is doing the work here is not intuition, but simply our skill at applying concepts. On this view, we respond correctly to cases because we are good at classifying situations. But this misses something crucial about the rationality of these classifications. To apply a concept is to act; it is to make a classificatory judgment. And judgments of this sort are not self-justifying. Simply deciding, without any basis, that a character has betrayed a friend would be epistemically arbitrary. Such judgments must themselves be grounded in something. Intuitions occur in us prior to and independently of any judgment. When a scenario strikes us as a clear instance of betrayal, that intuitive seeming is not itself an act of concept application. It is the pre-judgmental apprehension that makes the resulting judgment (the resulting application of the concept of betrayal) reasonable. In this sense, intuition does not compete with concept application. It enables it.

直觉领会先行于判断的同一现象，也显现在寻常知觉之中。当我们睁开眼睛，世界并不是作为一块颜色与形状的镶嵌画出现的。它呈现为一个布满有意义对象的场景：一个伸手够玩具的孩子，一只眼巴巴等着晚饭的猫，一辆开得太快的汽车。正如 P F 斯特劳森（P F Strawson）所观察到的，如果我们试图只用低阶感觉词汇（色块、明暗流转）来描述所见，我们就会歪曲这段经验。世界在知觉中到来时，已被概念组织妥当。被呈现出来的不是赤裸的视觉数据，而是某些种类的事物，处在彼此的关系之中。

This same phenomenon of intuitive apprehension preceding judgment also shows up in ordinary perception. When we open our eyes, the world does not appear as a mosaic of colours and shapes. It appears as a scene populated with meaningful objects: a child reaching for a toy, a cat waiting expectantly for dinner, a car approaching too fast. As P F Strawson observed, if we try to describe what we see using only low-level sensory terms (patches of colour, shifting brightness), we falsify the experience. The world comes to us in perception already organised by concepts. What is presented is not bare visual data but things of certain kinds, situated in relations to one another.

而在这里，直觉甚至进入了日常知觉经验本身。知觉揭示的不只是视觉阵列，而是厚实的、三维的、饱含概念的情境。在专长发挥作用的场合，这一点尤为鲜明。设想你与一位训练有素的爬行动物学家一同走过田野。你们都看见一条蛇。你的感官经验在现象学上可能与她的无从分辨——同样的躯体曲线，同样的斑驳花纹。然而，呈现给你们二人的东西并不相同。对你而言，它只是一条蛇。对她而言，它是一条铜头蝮（Agkistrodon contortrix）。差别不在于你们的视觉装置本身，而在于知觉中起作用的概念。她对相关区分的掌握，生成了一个概念上更丰富——尽管在现象学上完全相同——的呈现性状态。而由于直觉在现象学上并不坚实，也就不需要事物的样子有任何差别。这正是知觉专长能够悄然转化经验、却不改变其感官性质的原因。

And here, intuition enters even into everyday perceptual experience itself. Perception reveals not merely visual arrays but robust, three-dimensional situations that are concept-laden. This becomes particularly vivid in cases where expertise comes into play. Imagine walking through a field with a trained herpetologist. You both see a snake. Your sensory experience may be phenomenologically indistinguishable from hers – the same curves of the body, the same mottled pattern. Yet what is presented to each of you differs. To you, it is simply a snake. To her, it is an Agkistrodon contortrix. The difference is not in your visual apparatus per se but in the concepts at work within perception. Her grasp of the relevant distinctions generates a conceptually richer, even if phenomenologically identical, presentational state. And because intuitions are not phenomenologically robust, there is no need for any difference in how things look. This is why perceptual expertise can subtly transform experience without altering its sensory character.

由此看来，知觉完美例证了关于直觉的那个更广泛的要点。正因为直觉不具备任何坚实的现象学性质，它们才得以就在明处履行职能，而我们并不专门留意它们。

Perception, then, is a perfect illustration of the broader point about intuitions. Because they lack any robust phenomenology, intuitions serve their function in plain view without our taking explicit notice of them.

那个拒绝上陌生人车的人，那个把邮件标记为网络钓鱼的人，那个从交易中抽身而退的人——这些人并不是在非理性地行动，也不是仅凭感觉行事。他们是在回应一种真实的呈现性状态：某种东西正显示为瘆人、欺诈或不对劲。情境把自己呈现为具有某种品格，而这一呈现承载着认识论分量——不是因为它绝无谬误，而是因为它与那种奠定我们与世界最基本认知接触的状态，属于同一种类。

The person who declines to get in the stranger’s car, or who flags the email as a phishing attempt, or who walks away from the deal – these individuals are not acting irrationally or on mere feeling. They are responding to a genuine presentational state: something is showing up as creepy, fraudulent or wrong. The situation is presenting itself as having a certain character, and that presentation carries epistemic weight – not because it is infallible, but because it is the same kind of state that grounds all of our most basic cognitive contact with the world.

这恰恰是哲学家们谈论直觉时所指的意思。桥上那个当即感到把路人推下去是错的人，与那个拒绝上陌生人车的人，二者之间的差别不是种类之别，而是情境与控制之别。哲学家的思想实验是一个刻意营造的情境，旨在孤立并审究单一的直觉反应。日常案例则更凌乱，嵌在更丰富的语境之中，而且很少被审视。但那个底层的能力——对事情实际如何的呈现性领会——是同一个。

This is precisely what philosophers mean when they talk about intuition. The difference between the person on the bridge who immediately senses that pushing the bystander is wrong, and the person who declines to get into a stranger’s car, is not a difference in kind. It is a difference in context and control. The philosopher’s thought experiment is a deliberately engineered situation designed to isolate and interrogate a single intuitive response. The everyday case is messier, embedded in a richer context, and rarely examined. But the underlying capacity, the presentational apprehension of how things are, is the same.

迄今为止，我们的理论化所缺失的，不过是对这种连续性的明确承认。对于理性直觉为何承载认识论分量，哲学家们已有一套成熟的说明；他们则较迟才主张，这套说明可以自然而然地延伸到我们每天据以行动的那些直觉。一旦看到这一点，「感觉」与本能直觉就既不会被全盘正名，也不会被当作纯粹的噪音打发掉。相反，它们被纳入了支配我们最审慎推理的同一套框架。于是，哲学的任务不在于决定是否信任直觉，而在于理解它已经在扮演的角色——从思想实验到对陌生人的隐隐不安——在几乎每一次理解行为之中。

What has been missing in our theorising, until now, is simply the explicit recognition of this continuity. Philosophers have a well-developed account of why rational intuitions carry epistemic weight. They have been slower to claim that this account extends naturally to the intuitions we act on every day. Once we see this, ‘vibes’ and gut feelings are neither vindicated wholesale nor dismissed as mere noise. Instead, they are brought into the same framework that governs our most careful reasoning. The task of philosophy, then, is not to decide whether to trust intuition. It is to understand the role it already plays, from the thought experiment to the uneasy feeling about a stranger, in almost every act of understanding.
