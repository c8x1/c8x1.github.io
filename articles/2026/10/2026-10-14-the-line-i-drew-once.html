只画过一次的线

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年10月14日·3分钟阅读

四十多年了，我住的街一到后半夜还听得见岔口站的汽笛。总有人问我当年是怎么打算的：城为什么长在这里，山那边的谷为什么空了。我说我没有打算过。我这辈子只画过一条线，从江口到铁山，铅笔在图纸上走了三个月。

Forty years on, when the street where I live goes quiet after midnight, you can still hear the whistle from Chakou station. People still ask me what my plan was: why the city grew here, why the valley beyond the mountain emptied out. I tell them I never had one. In my whole life I drew a single line, from the docks at Jiangkou to the iron mountain of Tieshan, and the pencil walked that map for three months.

那年我二十三岁，队里只肯把最穷的一段山里交给我。两条涧我各走了二十天：北涧是生我养我的地方，石堰村就卧在涧底；南边翻松风垭要多绕十九里，路却缓。有几条规矩像山一样横在那里，没人敢改，也没人能改：雨季里满载的货车爬不动陡坡，一台锅炉每四十里就得喝一次水，诸如此类。这些数目不跟人商量。我照着它们把线一站一站安下去，让车的侧线、上水的水塔，都落在我落笔的地方。没有仪式，只有铅笔和数字。

I was twenty-three that year, and the crew would only trust me with the poorest stretch of the mountains. I walked twenty days in each of the two ravines. The northern one was where I was born and raised, with the village of Shayan lying at its bottom; the southern way, over Songfeng Pass, ran nineteen li farther around but gently. A few laws lay across that country like the mountains themselves, which nobody dared change and nobody could: in the rainy season a loaded freight train cannot climb a steep grade, and a boiler must drink every forty li, and so on. Figures like these do not negotiate. Station by station I laid the line down to obey them; the passing sidings and the water towers fell wherever my pencil put them. There was no ceremony, only pencil and figures.

两条涧只修得起一条线。图面上它们只差一口气：走北涧，得凿一条长隧道，坡陡得雨季的货车要在坡脚卸掉一半车皮；走南涧，多绕十九里，样样都顺。差的这口气，全在我怎么落笔。把坡度抹平半分，把隧道估短三十丈，字面上挑不出错，线就会走进石堰，走到我家的场院门口。可公司的款子只发到雨季前，北边另有一条线正等着抢这块运量；这条路要是修坏了，整条涧都得塌，石堰也塌在里头。写真数字，就是把石堰让出去。我坐了三个晚上，交上去的册子里没有一个假数。

The company could afford a line through only one of the ravines. On paper the two were a breath apart. Through the north meant a long tunnel and a grade so steep that in the rains a loaded train would have to set down half its wagons at the foot of the slope; through the south meant nineteen extra li and easy going the whole way. That breath of difference rested entirely on how I wrote. Ease the grade by half a point, shorten the tunnel by thirty paces in the estimate, and no one could have faulted the paperwork — the line would have walked into Shayan, right up to our farmyard gate. But the company's money ran only until the rains, and a rival line to the north was waiting to lift the freight; build this one crooked and the whole ravine would go under, Shayan with it. To write the true figures was to give Shayan away. I sat over it for three nights, and not one false number went into the book I handed up.

图纸当年秋天就定了案。定案那天我已经明白：往后哪怕把江口的货栈全押上去，也换不回一寸改线。路基炸在山骨里，钱沉在地里，年头越久越是这样。我照旧受雇验路，看钢轨一节一节压过我的铅笔印；头一年冬运，满载的车皮稳稳翻过了松风垭。后来的事，没有人打算过。岔口原是山道上十几户人家的歇脚处，我把让车的侧线画在了它和过山大车道相交的地方，五年成了镇，十五年成了城。石堰在山那一边：货走了铁路，客栈和油坊先后关了门，年轻人一年比一年少。我娘守着老屋不肯搬。她走的那年冬天，丧信是沿着这条线捎来的，走了一天一夜才到我手上。

The route was settled that same autumn. On the day it was settled I already understood: all the warehouses at Jiangkou put together could not buy back an inch of it. The roadbed was blasted into the bones of the mountain, the money sunk into the ground, and every passing year fixed it deeper. I stayed on to inspect the works and watched the rails press over my pencil marks one section at a time; in the first winter of freight the loaded wagons went over Songfeng Pass without a struggle. What happened afterward, no one had planned. Chakou had been a rest stop of a dozen households on the mountain road; I had set its passing siding where that road crossed my survey, and in five years it was a town, in fifteen a city. Shayan lay on the far side of the ridge. Once the freight moved onto the rails, the inn and the oil press closed their doors one after the other, and the young grew fewer year by year. My mother would not leave the old house. The winter she died, the news came down the very line I had drawn, travelling a day and a night before it reached me.

石堰的人凑了钱，托了人，前后递过三回呈子，求一条十里长的支线。回文说，坡度不划算。又过几年，矿上换了东家，路局的图重新描过一遍，支线的事再没有人提起。他们没有做错什么，只是开头里没有他们。四十年间，我在公文里读到过另一条线，在别的省：同样的江口，同样的矿山，站与站的里数都对得上。那条线上的镇子兴的兴、空的空，次序同我们这条几乎一样，差的不过是年份。那边没有人听说过石堰。

The people of Shayan pooled their money, found their go-betweens, and three times sent up a petition for a spur ten li long. The reply came back: the grade would not pay. A few years on, the mine changed hands, the railway bureau traced its maps afresh, and the spur was never raised again. They had done nothing wrong; they simply were not in the opening. Somewhere in those forty years I came across, in the bureau's papers, another line in another province: the same river dock, the same mine, the distances between stations matching ours almost station for station. The towns along it had risen and emptied in nearly the order ours did, a few years apart at most. No one there had ever heard of Shayan.

如今我老了，还常坐慢车全线走一遍。有的小站从通车那年起就没再变过，站房刷同一种黄，站台边的老槐树粗了一倍，别的都停在原处。渡口那座镇子每逢秋集涨起来，人挤满三条街，霜一落又空回去，年年如此，像呼吸。每一站的月台上总有年轻人提着包袱上车，人走了，镇子的人口却一年一年停在老数目上，像有人数过一样；我弟弟是头一批走的。岔口是唯一的例外，它四十年没有停过往外送人，自己还一年比一年大。当年催图的主任和签定案的董事长，如今都不在了，城还是照长。没有人赢，也没有人输给谁；开头写完那天起，剩下的四十年，我只是月台上的一个看客。

I am old now, and I still ride the slow train the length of the line. Some of the small stations have not changed since the year the rails arrived: the station house wears the same yellow, the old locust tree beside the platform has doubled its girth, everything else stands where it stood. The town at the ford swells each autumn when the fair comes, its three streets packed shoulder to shoulder, and empties again when the frost falls, year after year, like breathing. At every platform there are always young people climbing aboard with their bundles, and though they go, the town holds at the same old figure decade after decade, as if someone were keeping count; my younger brother was among the first. Chakou is the one exception: in forty years it has never stopped sending people out, and has grown larger itself every year. The superintendent who pressed me for the survey and the chairman who signed the route are both gone now, and the city keeps growing anyway. Nobody won, and nobody lost to anybody; from the day the opening was set down, all that remained of my forty years was to stand on the platform and watch.

Note: Conway's Game of Life is a zero-player cellular automaton devised by the mathematician John Conway in 1970: on a square grid, cells are born and die simultaneously under fixed rules that count their neighbours, and the only human act is drawing the initial pattern — after that no one can intervene and no one can win, the same beginning inevitably writing out the same history, while complexity on the scale of city walls grows out of a few small local rules by itself. Its name says game, but it has no players and nothing to win.
