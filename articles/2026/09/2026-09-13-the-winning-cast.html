中奖班底

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年9月13日·2分钟阅读

那年剧院经费紧张，院长决定赌一把：把三百个角色全部随机派发——报名者谁演什么、站哪个位子、穿哪套戏服，全由抽签决定。我作为导演，面对的便是一盘毫无章法的庞大阵容。我不相信运气能造出好戏，但院长说，先让全班底演满一整个演出季，看看效果。

That year the theater was broke, and the director-in-chief decided to gamble: all three hundred roles were handed out at random — who played what, stood where, wore which costume, all determined by drawing lots. As the director, I was handed one enormous, senseless cast. I did not believe luck could make good theater, but the chief said: let the full company play out an entire season first, then judge.

三百人的阵容，两两之间能拆出多少种小班底？我算过：那是阵容所有搭配的幂集，随人数膨胀到一个天文数字。这么多组合里，哪怕「碰巧凑成一台好戏」的概率低到荒谬，在这般浩瀚的候选中，几乎必然存在至少一组——纯粹靠偶然——能撑起整场演出。但我无从事先知道是哪一组。

A cast of three hundred — how many small troupes can you carve out of it? I did the math: it is the power set of every pairing, swelling with each additional person to an astronomical figure. With that many candidates, even if the odds of accidentally assembling a great show are absurdly low, among so vast a field there is almost bound to be at least one group — purely by chance — that could carry an entire performance. But I had no way of knowing which one in advance.

演出季跑完了。全班底的卖座率是七分。然后我开始裁减：把阵容一截截削小，每次留下一小队人马，让他们单独排练同样多的周数，再送进剧场评分。绝大多数小队塌了台——缺了关键角色，丢了配合的节奏。但有一支十二人的小班底，单独排练同样长的时间后，竟也拿到了七分，和全班底一样。

The season ran. The full company scored a seven out of ten at the box office. Then I began cutting: trimming the cast down section by section, each time leaving a small squad to rehearse alone for the same number of weeks, then sending them into the theater for a score. Most squads collapsed — missing a key role, losing the rhythm of their coordination. But one twelve-person troupe, rehearsing alone for the same length of time, also scored a seven — identical to the full company.

我以为我抓住了可以复用的配方：十二个位子，十二段走位。于是我把这十二个位子留给新人，重新抽签填入。结果那支新班底只拿到三分。我又试了一次，三分。位子还是那些位子，换上不同的人，全盘崩溃。使一支班底「中奖」的，是它的人与那些人当时恰好落定的关系两者缺一不可——同一套空位换一拨陌生面孔，就什么都不是了。

I thought I had a recipe I could reuse: twelve positions, twelve bits of staging. So I kept those twelve slots and filled them afresh by drawing lots with new actors. The new troupe scored a three. I tried again — three again. Same positions, different people, total collapse. What made a troupe "winning" was the people themselves together with the exact relationships they happened to fall into — neither could be separated. Plug a fresh batch of strangers into those same empty slots, and you had nothing.

院长的两难摆在面前：经费只够养一支班底。留下安全的全班底？还是押上剧院的存亡，只留那十二人？我做了件不可能在演出季开始前做的事——我是在让全班底演完之后才裁减出这十二人的。早一步动刀，没演过、没磨合过，我根本无从辨认谁是中奖的那一批。这份好运是随机派角的副产物，不是谁刻意寻得的目标。

The chief's dilemma was plain: we could afford to keep only one company. Stick with the safe full cast? Or stake the theater's survival on those twelve alone? What I had done was impossible before the season even began — I could only find those twelve by cutting down after the full company had already played through. Cut earlier, with no performances run and no chemistry formed, I had no way of identifying which batch was the winning one. This good fortune was a byproduct of random casting, not a target anyone had aimed for.

我把赌注押在了那十二人身上。那天解散其余二百八十八人时，整个后台空了大半，我站在只剩十二张椅子的化妆间里，手心全是汗。我不知道这份偶然的好运能否在下一季复现，但我确信一件事：中奖的班底不是被造出来的，是在浩瀚的随机中被碰见的——而要碰见它，你得先让那盘庞大的乱局跑完它的一整轮。

I placed the bet on those twelve. The day I released the other two hundred and eighty-eight, the backstage emptied by more than half. I stood in a dressing room with only twelve chairs left, palms slick with sweat. I did not know whether this accidental fortune could recur next season. But I was certain of one thing: the winning cast is not manufactured — it is encountered within a vast field of randomness. And to encounter it, you first have to let that enormous, chaotic ensemble run its full course.

注解：彩票假设（the lottery ticket hypothesis）指，一个随机初始化的稠密网络中存在某个子网络，其特定的连接结构与精确初始权重共同决定了它在单独训练后能达到与整个网络相当的测试精度；这种「中奖」是随机初始化的结构性副产物，须先训练原网络再剪枝才能发现，换用同结构的新随机初始化则性能骤降。

Note: The lottery ticket hypothesis states that within a randomly initialized dense network there exists a subnetwork whose specific connectivity and exact initial weights together determine that, when trained in isolation, it can match the test accuracy of the full network; this "winning" is a structural byproduct of random initialization, discoverable only after the full network is trained and then pruned, and it collapses if the same structure is re-initialized with fresh random weights.
