优秀的怀疑论者：多于怀疑，少于确定

Massimo Pigliucci·Aeon·2026年9月24日·10分钟阅读·原文

你的信念应该握得多紧？这个问题你每周都要面对好几次，自己却毫无察觉。握得太松，你会变得毫无立场；握得太紧，你会变成任何论证都无法触及的人。我们大多数人都在这两极之间摇摆，却没有一套词汇来描述中间的地带。一位罗马律师曾在远比我们可能面对的更为严酷的压力之下，把这个问题想了个明白——而他的答案至今仍是可得的最好答案。

How tightly should you hold your beliefs? You face the question several times a week without noticing. Hold them too loosely and you don’t stand for anything, hold them too tightly and you become the sort of person no argument can reach. Most of us swing between the two and have no vocabulary for the ground in between. A Roman lawyer worked one out, under pressure far worse than anything we are likely to face, and it is still the best answer available.

时间是公元前45年初，地点是意大利南部那不勒斯（Naples）西北、卢克里努斯湖（Lucrine Lake）畔的一座别墅。马尔库斯·图利乌斯·西塞罗（Marcus Tullius Cicero）——整个西方历史上最迷人也最具争议的人物之一——正在写完一部日后以《学园篇》(Academica)闻名的书。在这部书中，他阐明了怀疑论哲学（sceptical philosophy）的本质，以及它为何不仅对人类知识的进步至关重要，对过一种良善的生活同样如此。

The time is early 45 BCE, the place a villa near Lucrine Lake, northwest of Naples, in southern Italy. One of the most fascinating and controversial figures in the entirety of Western history, Marcus Tullius Cicero, is finishing writing a book that will be known as the Academica, where he explains the nature of sceptical philosophy and why it is so crucial not just to the advancement of human knowledge, but to living a good life.

在这样的时刻退隐别墅写作，实在有些反常。西塞罗深爱的罗马共和国（Roman Republic）即将毁于觊觎独裁大权的野心家之手：尤利乌斯·凯撒（Julius Caesar）——他多年的朋友、如今的政治对手——已自封终身独裁官，而这一步很快将导致他在公元前44年三月望日（Ides of March,即3月15日）遇刺身亡。在个人生活方面，西塞罗刚刚与结发三十年的妻子特伦提娅（Terentia）离婚，几个月前，他挚爱的女儿图利娅（Tullia）又死于产后并发症。你大概会以为，一个私人世界与公共世界同时剧烈崩塌的人，几乎不可能有心思写哲学。然而对西塞罗而言，这种写作本身就是一种治疗，而他写下的这些论著日后将影响中世纪的基督徒、文艺复兴时期的作家，以及美利坚共和国的缔造者们。

It is a strange moment to retire into one’s villa to write. The Roman Republic, so beloved by Cicero, is about to be destroyed by aspiring autocrats: Julius Caesar, a longtime friend and now a political opponent, has declared himself dictator for life, a move that will soon lead to his assassination on the Ides of March 44 BCE. On the personal front, Cicero has recently divorced Terentia, his wife of 30 years, and a few months ago his beloved daughter Tullia died from complications following childbirth. You would think a man whose private and public worlds are collapsing so dramatically would hardly have the focus to write philosophy. Yet for Cicero such writing is a form of therapy, and the treatises he produces will influence medieval Christians, Renaissance authors, and the Founders of the American Republic.

西塞罗之所以能撑过这一切，要归功于他所皈依的哲学——「学园怀疑论」(Academic Skepticism)。之所以如此命名，是因为从公元前266年到公元前90年，这种思想正是柏拉图（Plato）所创学园的主导特征。过去，人们常把他仅仅视为一个普及者：一个才华横溢的罗马人，转述着并不真正属于他的希腊思想。但几十年前学者们就已抛弃了这种刻薄的论断，因为人们清楚地看到，西塞罗是在各种思想来源之间进行论辩与取舍，而非简单地照本誊录。他还从零开始构建了一套拉丁语哲学术语，欧洲思想中的许多抽象词汇都源自他的创造。在他自己的学派最看重的问题上——怀疑论者能否持有见解，以及在何种条件下可以——他的立场自成一派，与他的老师们并不相同。属实的是：他在公开场合做哲学，并在法庭上、在元老院里、最终在死刑判决面前检验自己的结论。这不是通俗化，这是在街头层面上实打实做的哲学。

Cicero managed all this because of his adopted philosophy, ‘Academic Skepticism’, so-called because it characterised, for the period from 266 to 90 BCE, the Academy originally founded by Plato. He used to be treated as a mere populariser, a gifted Roman relaying Greek ideas he did not really own, but scholars abandoned that uncharitable verdict decades ago as it became clear that Cicero argued and decided among his sources rather than simply transcribing them. He also built a Latin philosophical vocabulary from scratch, and much of the abstract terminology of European thought descends from his coinages. On the question that mattered most to his own school – whether a sceptic may hold opinions, and on what terms – his position was his own and differed from those of his teachers. What is true is that he philosophised in public, and tested his conclusions in the courts, in the Senate, and eventually against a death sentence. That is not popularisation: it’s philosophy done at street level.

这也正是他今天值得你花时间了解的原因。接下来的内容，一部分是一个人的故事，但更多的是一套可用的方法：一种持有信念的方式，它既不是职业怀疑者表演出来的谦卑，也不是那种自称「做过自己的研究」之人披坚执锐般的确信。西塞罗为它起了个名字——probabilia(可然之物)——我相信，每当你需要评估某个观点是否站得住脚、并就此作出决定时，你都会发现它极为有用。

Which is why he is worth your time now. What follows is partly the story of a man, but mostly a usable method: a way of holding beliefs that is neither the performed humility of the professional doubter nor the armoured certainty of the person who has ‘done their own research’. Cicero had a name for it – probabilia – and I think you will find it very useful any time you have to assess the validity of a notion and make a decision about it.

真正的怀疑论者，面对有力的新证据会更新观点——而唱反调的人不会

A genuine sceptic, confronted with compelling new evidence, updates – whereas the contrarian does not

「怀疑论者」(sceptic)一词源自希腊语 skeptikos,意为「探究者」，在哲学中有着悠久而严肃的谱系。然而自1990年代以来，这个词被各种反科学运动劫持，催生出「气候怀疑论者」「疫苗怀疑论者」「进化论怀疑论者」这类自相矛盾的组合词。

‘Sceptic’ comes from the Greek skeptikos, meaning enquirer, and has a long and serious pedigree in philosophy. Yet, since the 1990s, the term has been hijacked by science-denial movements, giving us oxymorons like ‘climate sceptic’, ‘vaccine sceptic’, and ‘evolution sceptic’.

这些运动明白，「怀疑论者」一词自带文化声望：它听起来严谨、有独立思想、反建制，于是他们把它据为己有。这一手高明而具腐蚀性，因为它把否认重新包装成了探究。两者的区别在实践中一目了然：真正的怀疑论者面对有力的新证据会更新观点，而唱反调的人不会——他们挪动球门柱、不断抛出新的反对理由，或者干脆无视自己无法解释的东西，同时选择性地引用研究，搬出那寥寥几位对证据挑肥拣瘦的异议专家。他们的怀疑并不是开启探究的、富有建设性的苏格拉底（Socrates）式怀疑，而是封死探究的修辞盾牌。这些自我标榜的怀疑论者其实是教条主义者：结论早已预定，此后所做的不过是合理化辩护而已。

These movements understood that ‘sceptic’ has cultural prestige: it sounds rigorous, independent-minded, and anti-establishment, so they colonised it. The move was brilliant and corrosive, as it reframed denial as enquiry. The difference shows in practice: a genuine sceptic, confronted with compelling new evidence, updates – whereas the contrarian does not: they shift goalposts, raise new objections, or ignore what they cannot explain, all while citing studies selectively and appealing to the handful of dissenting experts cherrypicking the evidence. Their doubt is not the productive Socratic doubt that opens enquiry but a rhetorical shield that forecloses it. These self-styled sceptics are in fact dogmatists, having reached their conclusions in advance and thereafter simply engaging in rationalisations.

而在另一端，某种唯科学主义（scientism）则矫枉过正，把确定性当作任何值得持有的信念的入场券，把「相信科学」(follow the science)用作终止对话的令牌，而非探究的邀请。这同样不是真科学运转的方式。

On the other side, a certain strand of scientism has overcorrected by demanding certainty as the price of admission for any belief worth holding, deploying ‘follow the science’ as a conversation-stopper rather than an invitation to enquiry. That is not how real science works either.

这两种病态犯的是同一个错误：把怀疑与确定当作仅有的两个选项。两者都没有给我们真正需要的东西留出空间——经过校准的信心，即说出「这是目前最好的解释，我据此行动，同时我保持自己可能出错的心态」的能力。因此，我们所缺的是一种「暂定却坚定」的信念哲学——而西塞罗早在两千年前就用拉丁文把它构筑完毕了。

Both pathologies make the same mistake: they treat doubt and certainty as the only two options. Neither leaves room for what we actually need: calibrated confidence, the ability to say This is the best available account, I will act on it, and I remain open to being wrong. What is missing, then, is a philosophy of provisional but committed belief, a philosophy Cicero already worked out, in Latin, 2,000 years ago.

故事始于公元前87年：柏拉图学园的时任园长、拉里萨的菲洛（Philo of Larissa）因雅典政局动荡出逃，来到罗马——颇具讽刺意味的是，那场动荡的结局正是罗马人洗劫雅典。西塞罗时年十九岁，已是雄辩术与法律方面的神童。他去听菲洛讲学，为之倾倒。这位访客讲授的不是柏拉图抽象的形而上学，而是更为实用的东西：一套立足于苏格拉底式探究传统的方法，以及一个激进的理念——智慧始于承认自身知识的边界。

It began with a visit to Rome, in 87 BCE, by the then-head of the Platonic Academy, Philo of Larissa, having fled Athens amid political upheaval that eventuated, ironically, in the Roman sack of the city. Cicero, 19 years old and already a prodigy in oratory and law, went to hear Philo and was transfixed. What the visitor taught was not the abstract metaphysics of Plato but something more practical: a method based on the Socratic tradition of enquiry, the radical idea that wisdom begins in recognising the limits of one’s own knowledge.

学园怀疑论者把自己的学派称为「新学园」(New Academy),以区别于柏拉图创立的旧学园。阿凯西劳斯（Arcesilaus）与卡尔内亚德斯（Carneades）——公元前3世纪与公元前2世纪两位伟大的怀疑论者——把柏拉图和苏格拉底的对话式方法推向了一个激进的结论：我们不太可能达致确定，因为人类知识来自感官与理性，而这两者都难免出错。

The Academic Skeptics called their school the New Academy, to distinguish it from the Old one started by Plato. Arcesilaus and Carneades – the great sceptics of the 3rd and 2nd centuries BCE – had pushed Plato’s and Socrates’ dialogical method toward a radical conclusion: we are unlikely to achieve certainty, because human knowledge comes from our senses and from reason, both of which are fallible.

关键在于，他们并没有由此得出「我们因此就什么都不能信」的结论。卡尔内亚德斯提出了 pithanon(一个希腊语词)的概念——有说服力的、看似可信的东西——作为行动的恰当标准。西塞罗将其译为拉丁文 probabile(复数 probabilia,英语的 probable 一词即源于此),并在此基础上建立起他的整个认识论。

Crucially, they did not conclude that we should therefore believe nothing whatsoever. Carneades introduced the concept of the pithanon (a Greek word) – the persuasive, the plausible – as the proper standard for action. Cicero translated the word into Latin as probabile (plural probabilia, from which we get the English ‘probable’) and built his whole epistemology on it.

西塞罗运用的不是泛泛的开明心态，而是一套分级、可检验的程序

What Cicero deployed was not a generic mood of open-mindedness but a graded, checkable procedure

西塞罗本人的表述很平实。在《学园篇》中，他用一句话将自己的学派与教条主义者区分开来：别人宣称某些事情已经确定、已经固定，而「我们认为许多事物是 probabilia(可然之物),我们可以轻易依从，却几乎无法断言」。「轻易依从，鲜少断言。」整个方法就浓缩在这一不对称之中。

Cicero’s own formulation is plain. In the Academica, he separates his school from the dogmatists in a single sentence: others declare that certain things are settled and fixed, whereas ‘we hold many things to be probabilia, which we can easily follow but can scarcely affirm.’ Follow easily, affirm rarely. The entire method is encapsulated by that asymmetry.

probabilia 一词并非现代定量统计学意义上的「或然」，而更接近「值得认可」：一个经得起推敲、与现有证据相吻合、理性的人可以表示同意同时又保留修正余地的主张。它足以作为行动的依据。

The term probabilia does not mean ‘probable’ in the modern, quantitative statistical sense, but something closer to ‘worthy of approval’: a claim that has withstood scrutiny, coheres with the available evidence, and a reasonable person would assent to while remaining open to revision. It is enough to act on.

而且 probabilia 并不是一个单一、无差别的范畴——这正是通常被略去的关键部分。卡尔内亚德斯按照「表象」(impressions,即初步判断)经受住推敲的程度为其分级：第一级，一个表象只是让你觉得其为真的可能略大于为假；第二级，它上升为具有说服力；第三级，它是「不被带偏的」(undiverted),意思是你相信的其他东西没有与之相冲突；第四级也是最高一级，它还经过了彻底的考察，基本上就是科学调查的产物。琐碎小事只需第一级或第二级；医学决策或刑事判决则要求第三级或第四级。因此，西塞罗继承并运用的，不是一种泛泛的开明心态，而是一套分级、可检验的程序。

Nor is ‘probabilia’ a single, undifferentiated category, and this is the important part usually left out. Carneades graded impressions (ie, preliminary judgments) by how much scrutiny they had survived. At the first level, an impression simply strikes you as a bit more likely to be true than not. At the second level, it rises to being persuasive. At the third, it is ‘undiverted’, meaning that nothing else you believe conflicts with it. At the fourth and highest level, it is also thoroughly examined, essentially the result of scientific investigation. Trivial matters need only the first or second grade; a medical decision or a criminal verdict demands the third or fourth. What Cicero inherited and deployed, then, was not a generic mood of open-mindedness but a graded, checkable procedure.

正是这种分级，让学园派与古代另一大怀疑论学派——皮浪主义（Pyrrhonism）——分道扬镳。皮浪主义主张在大多数问题上悬置判断，其余时候则按照事物显现的样子行事，并遵循当地习俗。两种进路有三点区别。第一，学园派会表示同意——以弱化但真实的意义：他们认可某个主张、为它论证，并在它被证明错误时承担责任；皮浪主义者则不予认可，仅仅是跟着走。第二，学园派进行排序：表象按经受住检验的程度被归类，有些确实比另一些更可靠；而在皮浪主义者那里，表象到来时不带任何比较权重——这一个与那一个同样好（或同样坏）。第三，两个学派想要的东西不同：皮浪主义者追求 ataraxia,即心神安宁，悬置判断是他达成它的手段；学园派追求的仍然是真理，他们只是怀疑自己能否确证已经抵达了真理。一个是对焦虑的治疗，另一个是一种研究纲领——由一种难免出错、却仍必须在世间行动的生物来执行。

That grading is where the Academic parts company with the other great sceptical school of antiquity, Pyrrhonism, which counselled suspending judgment on most issues and otherwise acting according to how things appear to be while following local customs. There are three differences between the two approaches: first, the Academic assents, in a weakened but real sense: they approve the claim, argue for it, and carry the responsibility if it proves wrong, whereas the Pyrrhonist withholds approval and merely goes along. Second, the Academic ranks: appearances are sorted by how much testing they have survived, and some genuinely turn out to be better than others, while to the Pyrrhonist appearances arrive with no comparative weight attached – one is just as good (or bad) as another. Third, the two schools want different things: the Pyrrhonist pursues ataraxia, meaning tranquility, and suspension of judgment is his means to it. The Academic is still after truth; they simply doubt they will ever be able to certify that they have reached it. One is a therapy for anxiety, the other is a research programme conducted by a fallible animal that nevertheless needs to act in the world.

西塞罗在《学园篇》中说得明白：智者依最佳可得之见行动，不是因为他确信其为真，而是因为悬置一切判断将使生活无法继续。学园派说：「我们不是那种认为万事皆非真的人；我们是说，有些虚假之物混杂在真实之物之中，且与后者如此相似，以至于我们没有可靠的判准加以甄别，所以我们只能暂且评估。」

Cicero is explicit in the Academica: the wise person acts on the best available view, not because they are certain it is true, but because suspending all judgment would make life impossible. We are not the kind of people who think nothing is true, say the Academics; we are the people who say that some falsehoods are mixed in with truths, so similar to them that we have no sure criterion for judging, so we can assess only provisionally.

对西塞罗而言，这一切绝非纸上谈兵。它贯穿了他一生的每一个重大决定，包括其中争议最大的那一个。

None of this was merely theoretical for Cicero. It informed every major decision of his life, including the most contested one.

公元前63年，身为共和国罗马最高官职执政官（Consul）的他，遭遇了一场推翻政府的阴谋，领头者是心怀不满的元老卢基乌斯·塞尔吉乌斯·喀提林（Lucius Sergius Catilina），此人已陈兵于外，以一支军队威胁着罗马。西塞罗尽其所能收集情报，并依据 probabilia 行动。他把证据摆到元老院面前，元老院随即通过了「元老院终极决议」(senatus consultum ultimum)——一项授权执政官采取国家安全所需之一切措施的紧急法令。随后，西塞罗把落网阴谋分子的命运交由元老院全体辩论，辩论中凯撒主张监禁，小加图（Cato the Younger）主张处死。按照西塞罗与元老院多数派所持的宪制理解，接下来的处决是合法的。这种理解在当时和此后都备受争议：公民享有上诉权，而几年之后，西塞罗正是被一部（追溯既往适用的）法律驱逐流亡，该法律的立意恰恰是认定他侵犯了这一权利。这里没有任何东西能够裁定他究竟是对是错，我也不打算主张他是对的。真正有教益的是他得出结论的方式：他依据一个明知可能出错的判断采取行动，没有声称任何自己并不拥有的确定性，然后用余生捍卫这个决定，而不是与它撇清干系。这就是战火之下的学园怀疑论——不是瘫痪失措，也不是莽撞的确信，而是对最佳可得之判断连同其代价的、承担后果的坚定承诺。

As Consul – the highest office in Republican Rome – in 63 BCE, he faced a conspiracy to overthrow the government led by a disgruntled senator, Lucius Sergius Catilina, who was in the field threatening Rome with an army. Cicero gathered what intelligence he could and acted on probabilia. He laid the evidence before the Senate, which passed the senatus consultum ultimum, an emergency decree authorising the consuls to take whatever measures the safety of the state required. Cicero then put the fate of the captured conspirators to a full senatorial debate, in which Caesar argued for imprisonment and Cato the Younger for death. On the constitutional understanding that Cicero and the Senate majority held, the executions that followed were lawful. That understanding was contested, then and since: citizens had a right of appeal, and several years later Cicero was driven into exile by a (retroactively applied) law framed precisely to assert that he had violated it. Nothing here settles whether he was right, and I am not arguing that he was. What is instructive is his method of arriving at a conclusion: he acted on a judgment he knew might be mistaken, claimed no certainty he did not have, and then spent the rest of his life defending the decision rather than disowning it. That is Academic Skepticism under fire – not paralysis, nor reckless certainty, but consequential commitment to the best available judgment, including its costs.

西塞罗知道自己可能是在签下死刑令，但可然的善压过了可然的代价

Cicero knew he might be signing his death warrant, but the probable good outweighed the probable cost

被凯撒的独裁排除在公共生活之外后，西塞罗退居庄园，以惊人的速度写起哲学来：《论神的本性》(De natura deorum)、《论占卜》(De divinatione)、《学园篇》、《论善恶的界限》(De finibus bonorum et malorum)、《图斯库路姆论辩集》(Tusculan Disputations),以及其他多种。《论占卜》把学园怀疑论应用于迷信与神秘主义，如今被认为是史上第一部探讨科学与伪科学之别的论著。他在其中使用的方法，任何一线科学家都会觉得眼熟。对那些解读脏器、观察鸟飞以断吉凶的占卜官（augurs）与肠卜僧（haruspices），他并未一概斥为无稽。他逐一检验：动物肝脏预测战役胜负的机制是什么？是否存在真实可考的成功预测模式，还是只有事后被记住的、经过挑选的灵验案例？逐项检验下来，证据都无法令人不得不表示同意。关键在于，他为诸神借预兆传达信息的可能性留了门，只是得出结论：这些证据达不到他在任何其他领域都会要求的标准。早卡尔·萨根（Carl Sagan）那句「非同寻常的主张需要非同寻常的证据」的名言两千年，西塞罗就在践行同一种纪律，并把它施加于罗马人珍视的制度之上。这是智识上的勇气，而不是学术上的含混托词。

Once removed from public life by Caesar’s dictatorship, Cicero retreated to his estates and wrote philosophy at extraordinary speed: De natura deorum, De divinatione, Academica, De finibus bonorum et malorum and the Tusculan Disputations, among others. De divinatione applies Academic Skepticism to superstition and mysticism, and is now considered the first treatise ever written on the difference between science and pseudoscience. His method there is recognisable to any working scientist. He does not dismiss out of hand the augurs and haruspices who read entrails and interpret the flight of birds. He examines them: what is the mechanism by which an animal’s liver predicts a battle’s outcome? Is there a genuine pattern of successful prediction, or only cherrypicked successes remembered after the fact? On each count, the evidence fails to compel assent. Crucially, he leaves open the possibility that the gods communicate through omens, concluding only that the evidence falls short of the standard he would demand anywhere else. Two thousand years before Carl Sagan’s dictum that extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence, Cicero was practising the same discipline and applying it to cherished Roman institutions. That is intellectual courage, not academic hedging.

他死前几个月与好友阿提库斯（Atticus）的往来书信，是西塞罗留下的最具教益的文献。明知马克·安东尼（Mark Antony）要他性命，他仍在反复权衡：要不要逃，要不要回罗马，要不要信任年轻的屋大维（Octavian,即后来的皇帝奥古斯都）——在不确定中推理，权衡各种或然性，然后无论如何都要作出决定。当他返回罗马，在后世称为《反腓力辞》(Philippics)的系列演说中重新向安东尼开火时，他知道自己可能是在签下自己的死刑令，但可然的善压过了可然的代价。公元前43年12月7日，他被杀害。他对奉命前来行刑的军团士兵说的最后遗言是：「我就待在这里。过来吧，士兵。你正在做的事没有一件是对的，但至少，把我的头砍利落些。」

The letters he exchanged with his friend Atticus in the months before his death are the most instructive documents Cicero left. Knowing that Mark Antony wanted him dead, he debated whether to flee, whether to return to Rome, whether to trust the young Octavian (the future emperor Augustus): reasoning under uncertainty, weighing probabilities, deciding anyway. When he returned and resumed attacking Antony in the discourses known as the Philippics, he knew he might be signing his death warrant, but the probable good outweighed the probable cost. He was murdered on 7 December 43 BCE. His last words, to the legionnaire sent to kill him, were: ‘I am stopping here. Come over, soldier. There is nothing right about what you are doing, but at least make sure you cut off my head properly.’

学园怀疑论的进路，今天是否值得重新审视、加以应用？非常值得，至少在三个领域是如此。

Is the Academic Skeptic approach worth revisiting and applying today? Very much so, at least in three areas.

第一，科学——与你可能听到的说法相反——并不靠确定性运转。科学知识不是一堆板上钉钉的事实，而是经过最充分检验的理论所达成的暂定共识，其中每一条都保留被修订的可能。托马斯·库恩（Thomas Kuhn）表明科学范式会随历史而更替；伊姆雷·拉卡托斯（Imre Lakatos）则论证，科学家面对无法解释的反常仍坚持其研究纲领是正当的：先修订外围假设，最后才放弃理论内核。科学所实践的，在结构上与西塞罗的 probabilia 别无二致：以相称的信心持有当前最佳的观点，并对修订保持开放。现代贝叶斯概率论（Bayesian probability theory,对托马斯·贝叶斯 Thomas Bayes 于18世纪提出的定理的阐发）把同样的洞见形式化了：先验随证据更新，永无确定，始终相称。数学是新的，智慧不是。

First, science – contrary to what you may have heard – does not run on certainty. Scientific knowledge is not a collection of established facts, it is a provisional consensus of the best-tested theories, each held subject to revision. Thomas Kuhn showed that scientific paradigms shift over history, and Imre Lakatos argued that scientists rightly persist with research programmes despite anomalies they cannot explain, revising peripheral hypotheses before abandoning a theory’s core. What science practises is structurally identical to Cicero’s probabilia: the best current views, held with proportionate confidence, open to revision. Modern Bayesian probability theory (elaborating the theorem Thomas Bayes set out in the 18th century) formalises the same insight: priors updated by evidence, never certainty, always proportionality. The mathematics is new, but the wisdom is not.

这不是相对主义——即认为理论纯属社会建构的观点。恰恰相反：现代科学哲学所描绘的，是科学逐步收敛于对现实越来越好的解释的过程；它不是从一个确定行军到另一个确定，而是通过淘汰较差的假设、打磨较好的假设来前行。科学家与学园怀疑论者一样，从不宣称终极真理，但这并不意味着怎么都行。有趣的是，社会学家罗伯特·K·默顿（Robert K Merton）在1942年把科学描述为一种「有组织的怀疑」(organised scepticism)。

This is not relativism, the view that theories are mere social constructs. Quite the opposite: modern philosophy of science describes a process by which science converges on progressively better accounts of reality, not by marching from certainty to certainty but by eliminating worse hypotheses and refining better ones. The scientist, like the Academic Skeptic, never claims final truth, but it doesn’t follow that anything goes. Interestingly, the sociologist Robert K Merton in 1942 described science as a kind of ‘organised scepticism’.

第二，民主要求人们保有被说服的意愿。只有当公民把政治观点当作 probabilia 而非教条来持有时，协商审议才可能，而当前的政治危机在某种程度上正是一场认识论危机。当然，理性公民的黄金时代从来就不存在。但各项测量对我们行进的方向毫不含糊：对美国国会（US Congress）唱名表决的分析显示，两党在意识形态上的重叠自1970年代起不断收窄，到2000年代已实际消失；皮尤（Pew）等机构的调查则记录了自1990年代以来，持一贯意识形态立场、视对方阵营为国家威胁的党徒人数急剧攀升。而且，这种变化更多是情感性的而非纲领性的：各党支持者在具体政策上的分歧，远小于彼此厌恶程度的加深——而这恰恰是证据无法触动的那种执着。社交媒体与假新闻经济则加速了这一切。

Second, democracy requires the willingness to be persuaded. Deliberation is possible only if citizens hold their political views as probabilia rather than as dogma, and the current political crisis is partly an epistemological one. Of course, there was never a golden age of reasonable citizens. But the measurements are unambiguous about the direction in which we have been moving: roll-call analyses of the US Congress show ideological overlap between the parties shrinking from the 1970s and effectively gone by the 2000s, while survey work by Pew and others documents a sharp rise since the 1990s in partisans holding consistently ideological positions and viewing the other side as a threat to the nation. Much of the change, moreover, is affective rather than programmatic: partisans have diverged on actual policy far less than they have come to dislike one another, which is exactly the sort of commitment that evidence cannot touch, and social media and the fake-news economy have accelerated it.

把自我凝成一块永不更新的结晶，并不是操守：那是披着品格外衣的僵化

Developing a crystallised self that never updates is not integrity: it is rigidity dressed up as character

一个真正奉行或然论（probabilism）的公民会是什么样子？他会持有坚定的政治承诺，因为 probabilia 并非含糊骑墙之物，完全可以带着信念去践行。但他会把价值观与关于世界如何运转的经验信念区分开来。假设某人支持全民基本收入（universal basic income）。其底层的价值——在一个富裕社会里，任何人都不应赤贫度日——他可以坚定持有。但「基本收入是实现这一价值的最佳工具」却是一个经验性的赌注，目前若干国家正在开展的试验也许会证明这个赌注站不住脚。这时，或然论公民能够毫不羞愧地说：「我原以为这行得通；现在看来并不行；所以我要更新我的看法。」这不是「立场反复」，而恰恰是让民主说服成为可能的那种认知姿态。它其实是一种始终如一：证据指向哪里，就跟随到哪里。

What would a genuinely probabilist citizen look like? They would hold strong political commitments, since probabilia are not wishy-washy and can be acted on with conviction. But they would distinguish their values from their empirical beliefs about how the world works. Suppose someone supports a universal basic income. The value underneath it – that no one should live in destitution in a wealthy society – is theirs to hold firmly. That a basic income is the best instrument for delivering it, however, is an empirical bet, and the trials now running in several countries might prove that bet ill-founded. The probabilist citizen would then be capable of saying, without shame: ‘I thought this would work; it does not appear to; therefore I am updating my view.’ That is not ‘flip-flopping’, it is exactly the cognitive posture that makes democratic persuasion possible. It is in fact a form of consistency: following the evidence wherever it may lead.

第三，在个人层面上，西塞罗的认识论是对固化身份之诱惑的一剂解药。那个已经「找到了自己的真理」并停止追问的人并不智慧；他已经臣服于西蒙娜·德·波伏娃（Simone de Beauvoir）在《岁月的力量》(The Prime of Life,1960)中所说的「那种被称作『哲学体系』的有意识的疯狂之举」——其信奉者由此生出一种执迷的态度，把自己试探性的图景赋予了普世的洞见与普适性。经过充分省察的人生，是一种不断校准、不断修订的人生：承诺坚定到足以让人正直地行动，又松弛到足以在证据改变时随之更新。

Third, on a personal scale, Cicero’s epistemology is an argument against the seductions of fixed identity. The person who has ‘found their truth’ and stopped questioning is not wise; they have surrendered to what Simone de Beauvoir, in The Prime of Life (1960), called ‘that conscious venture into lunacy known as a “philosophical system”,’ from which its adherents derive an obsessional attitude endowing their tentative patterns with universal insight and applicability. The well-examined life is one of continuous calibration and revision: strong-enough commitments to act with integrity, loose enough to update when the evidence changes.

在一个把「本真」理解为拥有固定自我、并敦促我们认领自身身份的时代，这种主张是逆流而动的。西塞罗大概会觉得那种想法不过是自我嘉许。他曾在对朋友、同盟与战术判断的看法上改变立场，并为此忧虑——他的书信对此毫不讳言。但是，把自我培养成一块永不更新的结晶并不是操守：那是披着品格外衣的僵化。西塞罗式的人，可辨识之处在于：他愿意为早先的确信感到难为情，并且不把这种难为情当作失败，而是当作探究正在奏效的证据。

This is countercultural in an age that prizes authenticity in the sense of having a fixed self, and urges us to own our identity. Cicero would have found that to be self-congratulatory. He changed his mind about friends, alliances and tactical judgments, and worried about it, as his letters make plain. But developing a crystallised self that never updates is not integrity: it is rigidity dressed up as character. The Ciceronian person is recognisable by a willingness to be embarrassed by earlier certainties, and by treating that embarrassment not as failure but as evidence that enquiry is working.

他对共和国的判断错了——它终究还是倾覆了。但他继续行动，仿佛它仍可挽救，因为在他自己的分析中，那仍是最佳可得之见，值得以行动相托。而他的遗产，就存留在那些写于安东尼复仇渴望阴影之下的书里，流传了下来。

He was wrong about the Republic – it fell anyway. But he went on acting as though it could be saved, because that was the best available view on his analysis and worth acting on, and his legacy survived in the books he wrote in the shadow of Antony’s thirst for revenge.

真正的怀疑论者，不是那个怀疑一切的人，也不是那个在相信任何东西之前都索要证明的人。他是那个学会了以恰到好处的握力持有自己最佳观点的人：紧到足以据此行动，松到足以在证据转向时松手。这就是 probabilia 的含义。它比教条主义更难，也比虚无式的怀疑更难，但西塞罗认为，它是一个有限的人所能采取的唯一诚实的立场。他多半是对的。

The genuine sceptic is not the person who doubts everything, nor the person who demands proof before believing anything. It is the person who has learned to hold their best views with exactly the right strength of grip: firmly enough to act on them, loosely enough to let go when the evidence turns. That is what probabilia means. It is harder than dogmatism and harder than nihilistic doubt, but Cicero thought it the only honest stance available to a finite human being. He was probably right.
