一件合身的衣裳

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年9月29日·3分钟阅读

婚礼那天一早，十一个人挤进裁缝铺换装，十一件上衣挂满了一排衣架。先穿上身的两位立刻出了事：一位的左肩在布料底下塌了下去，另一位一条胳膊卡在袖子里，抬不起来正领子。每件衣裳各有各的错法，错的方向却是同一个。只有最边上一件——给老爷子自己裁的新外套——穿在老人身上，像浇上去一样合身。雅各布站在门边，手里还捏着皮尺，听见自己说：「来不及改了。时辰到了。」

On the wedding morning, eleven men crowded into the tailor's shop to dress, and eleven coats hung along the rack. The first two into their sleeves met trouble at once: under one man's cloth the left shoulder sank away, and the other had an arm stuck in its sleeve and could not lift it to straighten his collar. Each coat misbehaved in its own way, and every one of them misbehaved in the same direction. Only the coat at the far end — the new one cut for the old man himself — fit him as though it had been poured on. Jakob stood by the door with the measuring tape still in his hand and heard himself say: "No time to alter anything. The hour has come."

三个星期前，老维尔纳拄着杖进铺子，替独子的整场婚礼订衣：十一件，婚期不挪。维尔纳家的账，本是这间铺子的房梁。雅各布给十一个人一一量了身量，莉泽执笔记录；可到了裁案前，那些数目他只看一遍便搁下了——案上躺着那张纸样。城里的裁缝都知道，雅各布裁衣，先问纸样，后问人。落剪之前，他把每一副新身量往纸样上校过：肩膀平的，压低半指；背板直的，放宽一掌；右边的表袋，裁得比任何表袋该有的都深。在他眼里，平直的肩背是待修的毛病。

Three weeks earlier, old Werner had come in leaning on his cane to order clothes for his only son's whole wedding party: eleven coats, the date not to be moved. The Werner account was the beam that held the shop up. Jakob measured all eleven men, with Liese writing the figures down; but at the cutting table he read those figures once and set them aside — on the table lay the pattern. Every tailor in town knew that Jakob asked the pattern first and the man second. Before each cut he checked the new figures against it: level shoulders he pressed lower by half a finger, a straight back he widened by a palm, the right watch pocket he cut deeper than any pocket needs to be. In his eyes a level and upright frame was a fault waiting to be corrected.

那张纸样不是一天长成那样的。三十年里，维尔纳每年春秋两回进铺子添衣，每回试穿之后，雅各布都往纸样上别一张新纸条：肩又沉了半分，别一条；背又弓了一指，再别一条。纸条上写日子，一张也没取下来过；写不下了，就往边上补贴新纸。到后来，纸样厚得像一件百衲衣，抖开来能听见纸响。而试衣镜三十年里照过的，始终是同一副身子：穿在维尔纳身上，处处伏贴，满城都说雅各布的手艺最准。

The pattern had not grown into that shape in a day. Through thirty years Werner came twice a year, spring and autumn, to order new clothes, and after every fitting Jakob pinned a fresh strip of paper onto the pattern: the shoulder had sunk another half-part, one strip; the back had curved another finger's worth, one more. Each strip carried its date, and not one was ever taken off; when there was no room left, a fresh piece of paper was pasted on at the edge. By the end the pattern was as thick as a coat of patches, and when it was shaken open it rustled. And in thirty years the fitting-room mirror had held one body only: put a coat on Werner and every seam lay quiet, and the whole town said Jakob was the most exact hand in the city.

婚期前夜，莉泽抱着一件试身衣上楼来。她背着父亲，照自己记下的身量、用一张干净的新纸样裁的，上头一张纸条也没别。试身衣穿在弟弟身上过得去，穿在邻家少年身上也过得去。「爹，」她说，「拿我这张做底子吧。你那张，太满了。」雅各布把新纸样举到灯下，看了很久；又看一眼案上那张百衲似的旧纸样，每一张纸条都别着三十年的日子。接过来，就等于把那三十年折进抽屉。他把新纸样还给女儿，点起两盏灯，一直裁到天亮。

On the night before the wedding, Liese came upstairs carrying a trial coat. Behind her father's back she had cut it by the figures she had written down, on a clean new sheet with not one strip pinned to it. It sat passably on her younger brother, and passably on the boy from next door. "Father," she said, "cut from mine. Yours is too full." Jakob held the plain sheet to the lamp and looked at it for a long time; then he looked at the old pattern on the table, where every strip carried thirty years of dates. To take his daughter's sheet would be to fold those thirty years into a drawer. He gave it back to her, lit both lamps, and cut until morning.

婚礼照常办完了。宾客们用手攥着衣襟行礼；新郎抬不起手臂，交换戒指时，是新娘伸手替他戴上的。散席后，老维尔纳把雅各布叫到廊下。老人肩上那件新外套处处伏贴；他伸手按了按儿子的前襟，按出一道斜斜的长褶子。「雅各布，」他说，「你从来不是在裁衣裳——你是在裁我。婚钱照付。账，挪到你女儿名下。」开春，莉泽在对街支起自己的裁案，头一件活，就是给那十一个人重裁。她的纸样上只画身量，画完便擦掉，一张纸条也没别过。

The wedding went ahead. The guests kept their coats closed with their fists while they bowed, and the groom could not lift his arms, so it was the bride who reached over and put the ring on his finger. After the feast, old Werner called Jakob out to the gallery. The new coat on the old man's shoulders lay quiet at every seam; he laid a hand on his son's front and pressed out a long diagonal wrinkle. "Jakob," he said, "you were never cutting clothes — you were cutting me. The bill will be paid in full. The account moves to your daughter." In spring, Liese set up her cutting table across the street, and her first work was to cut anew for those eleven men. On her sheet she drew figures only and rubbed them out when she was done; she never pinned on a strip.

秋深的时候，雅各布把纸样收进抽屉；收之前，头一回动了取针的念头。纸条一张一张揭下来，一张比一张旧，揭到最底下那张：纸已黄透，针眼锈成褐色，字是他年轻时的笔迹——左肩低；客人说，是年轻时从灰母马上摔下来的。春，头一件。再没有别的字。他捏着那张纸条在灯下坐了很久，然后把它原样别回最底下，再一张一张，把其余的都别回它上头，合上抽屉。那张纸样，从此没再上过裁案。

When autumn deepened, Jakob put the pattern away in a drawer, and before he did, he took up the unpinning for the first time. Strip by strip he lifted them off, each older than the last, down to the bottom one, gone yellow through, its pinholes rusted brown, the writing in his own young hand: left shoulder low; the client says a fall off the grey mare when he was young. Spring, the first coat. There was nothing else written on it. He sat under the lamp with that strip for a long time; then he pinned it back at the bottom, and strip by strip pinned all the rest back above it, and shut the drawer. The pattern never went onto the cutting table again.

注解：过拟合（overfitting）：在见过的材料上处处精确，到了没见过的情形便失手，因为个例的偶然被当作普遍规律收进了手艺，规矩越攒越多，超出了经验所能担保的范围。故事里这张失效的脸是固执的，而真正的过拟合并非谁的执意，也不是多练几年能挽回的——偶然是在无人察觉时混进来的。

Note: Overfitting means being exact on everything you have seen and then failing on what you have not seen, because the accidents of particular cases get taken in as if they were general laws, and the rules pile up beyond what experience can vouch for. In the fable the fault wears a stubborn face, but real overfitting is nobody's deliberate choice, and years of further practice will not cure it — the accidents slip in unnoticed.
