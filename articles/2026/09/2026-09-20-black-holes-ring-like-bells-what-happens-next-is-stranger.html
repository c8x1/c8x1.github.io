黑洞如钟鸣响，接下来发生的事更加离奇

Richard Dyer·Aeon·2026年9月20日·11分钟阅读·原文

七月一个温暖的周日，剑桥（Cambridge）上午九点，我正走向市中心的一家咖啡馆。大圣玛丽教堂（Great St Mary's）的钟奏响了熟悉的剑桥钟乐（Cambridge Quarters）。这段旋律如今常让人联想到伦敦（London）大本钟（Big Ben）的报时钟声，但它其实最早于1793年在此谱成。这种钟乐因每个一刻钟演奏的旋律而得名，是时间流逝最易辨认的提示。

It is 9 am on a warm July Sunday in Cambridge, as I head towards a café in the city centre. The bells of Great St Mary’s church play the familiar melody of the Cambridge Quarters. The sound, now often associated with the chimes of Big Ben in London, was first composed here in 1793. The quarters, named for the melody at each quarter-hour, are an immediately recognisable reminder of the passage of time.

每口钟发出一个音符：一种带有特定频率的波，穿过空气，抵达我们的耳朵。钟锤敲击钟体，钟开始振荡，来回推挤空气分子。这些推搡的分子到达我们耳中，我们便听到了乐音。随后钟平静下来，交出它的能量，重归寂静。

Each bell produces a note: a wave with a characteristic frequency that travels through the air and reaches our ears. A hammer strikes the bell, and it oscillates, pushing air molecules back and forth. Those jostling molecules reach our ears and we hear a musical note. Then the bell settles, gives up its energy, and returns to silence.

我在剑桥大学（University of Cambridge）的研究常常把我带回钟声这个意象。我不研究声学，但我专注于一个惊人相似的过程，它遍布整个宇宙。我感兴趣的，是另一个同样会鸣响的对象：黑洞（black hole）。

My research here at the University of Cambridge often brings me back to the ringing of a bell. I don’t study the physics of sound, but I do focus on a remarkably similar process, taking place throughout the Universe. I am interested in another object that also rings: a black hole.

我端着咖啡坐下，打开笔记本电脑，屏幕上是一段波浪般的信号，正渐渐衰减成一条平线。两个黑洞刚刚完成并合——一场狂乱的合体过程——留下一道来自残骸黑洞的极其短暂的波动。

As I sit down with a coffee, I open my laptop to a wavy signal that gradually fades to a flat line. Two black holes have finished merging – a frenzied process of coalescence – leaving an extremely brief wiggle originating from the remnant black hole.

就像被敲响的钟一样，这个残存的黑洞正在鸣响，这一过程叫做「铃宕」（ringdown），而且在我研究的每一次并合中它都如出一辙。铃宕让物理学家得以检验他们的引力理论：阿尔伯特·爱因斯坦（Albert Einstein）真的说出了最后的话吗？正如钟声走调可能暗示着设计缺陷，鸣响黑洞频率上的偏差，也可能成为我们对引力的理解中出现的第一道裂缝。

Much like the struck bell, this leftover black hole is ringing in a process called ringdown, and it’s consistent across every merger I investigate. Ringdown allows physicists to test their theories of gravity: did Albert Einstein really have the last word? Just as an off-sounding note from a bell might indicate a design flaw, a discrepancy in the frequencies of a ringing black hole could be the first crack in our understanding of gravity.

这是研究铃宕的科学理由，但同样令我着迷的是接下来发生的事。黑洞鸣响过后，便归于寂静。在那一刻——只持续几毫秒——并合的能量与剧烈程度都消失了。从外部看，这段历史的几乎全部内容突然变得无法触及，剩下的东西毫无特征。黑洞安顿成一个由纯粹时空构成的简单、安宁的物体，没有一丝刚刚那场暴力的痕迹。在某些方面，黑洞遗忘了。

That’s the scientific rationale for studying ringdown, but what fascinates me equally is what happens next. After the black hole rings, it settles into silence. In that moment, lasting only a few milliseconds, the power and intensity of the merger are lost. From the exterior, almost all of that history suddenly becomes inaccessible, and what is left is featureless. The black hole settles into a simple, peaceful object of pure spacetime, with no trace of the violence that just happened. In some respects, the black holes forget.

如此混乱地诞生之物竟能变得如此简单，这似乎有违直觉。为什么黑洞似乎会遗忘自己暴力的历史？这些散布在宇宙中的奇特对象与我们已知的任何东西都不同，但它们的健忘并非它们独有。正如它们的鸣响一样，其背后的物理学同样支配着我们的世界。同样的观念把黑洞与我们自身在时间中的旅程连在一起，冲刷掉我们的历史，带来损失。这种物理学叫做熵（entropy）。它的核心教益是：当我们把镜头拉远，遗忘的不只是黑洞，还有我们周围的世界。

It seems counterintuitive that something so chaotically created can become so simple. Why do black holes seem to forget their violent histories? These strange objects, scattered throughout the Universe, are unlike anything else we know, yet their forgetfulness does not belong to them alone. Just like their ringing, the underlying physics also governs our world. The same ideas connect black holes and our own journey through time, washing away our histories and resulting in loss. The physics is called entropy. And its central lesson is that, when we zoom out, it is not just black holes that forget, but the world around us too.

如今围绕黑洞的种种兴奋，大多关于它们令人费解的量子行为。不过，我想给你讲的是一种更古老的、经典意义上的黑洞理解，它本身已经足够奇特。它要追溯到第一次世界大战，当时卡尔·史瓦西（Karl Schwarzschild）——一位在俄国前线服役的德国天文学家——找到了爱因斯坦引力方程的一个解。他的解描述了可能存在的最简单的一类黑洞。其标志性特征是一个球形的单向表面，叫做事件视界（event horizon）。物体可以越过它进入黑洞，却再也无法离开，无论它们跑得多快、用多大力气挣扎。这意味着我们永远无法看到这一点之外发生了什么。

Much of today’s excitement around black holes concerns their perplexing quantum behaviour. However, I want to tell you about an older, classical understanding of black holes that is strange enough. It traces back to the First World War, when Karl Schwarzschild – a German astronomer serving on the Russian front – uncovered a solution to Einstein’s equations for gravity. His solution described the simplest type of black hole that can exist. Its defining feature is a spherical one-way surface called the event horizon. Things can pass beyond it into the black hole, but they can never leave again, no matter how fast they move or how forcefully they try. That means we can never see what happens beyond this point.

不过，有时逼近这一边界的物体足以扰动黑洞，让我们探测到它。1957年，图利奥·雷格（Tullio Regge）和约翰·惠勒（John Wheeler）想知道，如果史瓦西的完美黑洞受到扰动——像钟一样被某个落进来的东西敲响——会发生什么。那是在惠勒让「黑洞」一词流行开来之前的10年，也是我们听到真实黑洞鸣响之前的近60年。

However, sometimes objects approaching this boundary can perturb the black hole enough for us to detect it. In 1957, Tullio Regge and John Wheeler wondered what would happen if Schwarzschild’s perfect black hole was disturbed: struck, like a bell, by something falling in. This was 10 years before Wheeler even popularised the term black hole, and almost 60 years before we heard a real one ring.

雷格和惠勒想知道，这样一个对象能否在一个充满物质的宇宙中存续。

Regge and Wheeler wanted to know whether such an object could survive in a Universe full of stuff.

当然，我们现在知道它可以。雷格和惠勒也得出了同样的结论：黑洞面对小扰动是稳定的。但就像钟一样，这些扰动会让它们以可预测的频率短暂振荡。这就是铃宕。以这种方式，黑洞遵循着与我们那个似乎更平凡的世界——有着当当教堂钟声的世界——相同的规则。

Of course, we now know it can. Regge and Wheeler reached the same conclusion: black holes are stable against small disturbances. But, like a bell, these disturbances set them oscillating for a brief time at predictable frequencies. This is the ringdown. In this way, black holes follow the same rules that govern our seemingly more ordinary world, with its clanging church bells.

然而，钟是在空气中送出声波，黑洞则是在时空本身之中送出涟漪，称为引力波（gravitational waves）。其中一些波落入黑洞，被困在事件视界之内。另一些逃向远方，以光速穿越空间。最终，其中一些抵达地球，被我们探测到。

Yet, whereas a bell sends sound waves through the air, a black hole sends ripples through spacetime itself, called gravitational waves. Some of these waves fall into the black hole, becoming trapped inside the event horizon. Others escape far away, travelling through space at the speed of light. Eventually, some reach Earth, and we detect them.

我们捕获的第一批引力波于2015年9月抵达地球，由两台LIGO（激光干涉引力波天文台）探测器探测到，一台位于华盛顿州汉福德（Hanford, Washington），另一台位于路易斯安那州利文斯顿（Livingston, Louisiana）。它们源自两个黑洞的碰撞与并合——一场发生在13亿光年之外的灾难性事件。两个黑洞被彼此的引力俘获，锁定在旋近（inspiral）过程中，最终合而为一。残骸黑洞鸣响了短短几毫秒，随后归于寂静。

The first gravitational waves we caught arrived at our planet in September 2015, and were detected by the two LIGO instruments, one at Hanford, Washington, and the other at Livingston, Louisiana. Their origin was two black holes colliding and merging, a cataclysmic event 1.3 billion light years away. The black holes had become locked in an inspiral, captured by each other’s gravity, ultimately coalescing into one. For a period of a few milliseconds, the remnant rang, before settling into silence.

这样一场碰撞的产物是一个虽被压扁、却完美光滑的椭球体。它继承了形成它的那两个并合黑洞的旋转，这使它在赤道处向外鼓起。从这个意义上说，它比雷格和惠勒最初研究的史瓦西黑洞要略复杂一些。它被称为克尔黑洞（Kerr black hole），以找到描述它的解的罗伊·克尔（Roy Kerr）命名。

The result of such a collision is a perfectly smooth, though squashed, spheroid. It carries some rotation from the merging black holes that formed it, causing it to bulge out at the equator. In that sense, it is slightly more complicated than Schwarzschild’s black hole that Regge and Wheeler had originally studied. It is called a Kerr black hole after Roy Kerr who found the solution that describes it.

尽管如此，这类黑洞仍然简单得出奇。从外部看，我们关于它们能知道的内在属性只有两样：它有多重，以及它转得多快。（还有第三个量：电荷。不过，大自然往往会让它刚一出现就被迅速中和，所以我们可以放心地忽略它。）

Nevertheless, such black holes are still remarkably simple. From the outside, we can only ever know two intrinsic things about them: how heavy they are, and how fast they spin. (There is also a third quantity, electric charge. However, nature tends to neutralise this as quickly as it appears, so we can safely ignore it.)

这一特性——所有黑洞无论历史如何，都完全可以由质量、自旋、电荷来描述，再无其他——叫做无毛定理（no-hair theorem），这个说法出自惠勒。为什么这么说？因为从外部看，黑洞没有任何其他能让它们彼此区分开来的特征，或者说「毛发」。

This feature – that all black holes, no matter their history, can be completely described by mass, spin, charge and nothing more – is called the no-hair theorem, a phrase attributed to Wheeler. Why? From the outside, black holes lack any other distinctive features or ‘hair’ that make them different from each other.

这与我们平日的经验大相径庭。当剑桥的钟声敲响时，我看着自己的咖啡，能说出很多。我可以告诉你它盛在什么样的杯子里、温度如何、有多浓、加的是哪种牛奶，等等。这只杯子透露着大量关于它的历史与现状的信息。如果我磕坏了杯柄，或者把咖啡兑稀了，这些信息并不会去别处。杯子记录着发生在它身上的一切。

That is a departure from our usual experience of things. When I look at my coffee as the bells chime in Cambridge, there is a lot I can say. I can tell you about the mug it is in, its temperature, how strong it is, what kind of milk was used, and so on. The mug gives away a lot of information about its history and its current condition. If I chip the mug’s handle, or dilute the coffee, that information doesn’t go anywhere. The mug keeps a record of things that happen to it.

黑洞拥有一段同样复杂、甚至远为复杂的历史。然而它几乎不显露分毫。是什么样的恒星形成了它、它是否曾拥有行星或生命、它经历过一次、两次还是一百次并合，这些都无关紧要。一整颗像地球这样的行星也许曾经落入其中，而黑洞不会留下一丝痕迹。

A black hole has an equally, if not far more, complicated history. Yet it reveals almost none of this. It doesn’t matter what kind of star formed it, whether it had planets, or life, or whether it merged once, twice or a hundred times. An entire planet like Earth might once have fallen in, and the black hole would show no trace.

为什么会是这样？钟的类比在描述铃宕时虽然有力，也贴近底层物理，但它只能带我们走到这里。归根结底，无毛定理以及随之而来的更深的丢失，只能由黑洞自身的独特性质来解释。

Why should this be true? While the analogy of the ringing bell is powerful when describing the ringdown, and close to the underlying physics, it takes us only so far. Ultimately, the no-hair theorem, and the deeper loss that accompanies it, can be explained only by the unique properties of a black hole itself.

要明白这一点，让我们回到黑洞并合后的那一刻。虽然在这样的碰撞中有一些质量以引力波的形式损失掉了，但生成的黑洞总是更大。更确切地说，事件视界的表面积只能保持不变或增大。这件事的意义比看上去更大。

To see this, let’s return to the moment just after a black hole merger. While some mass is lost as gravitational waves during such a collision, the resulting black hole is always larger. More specifically, the event horizon’s surface area can only ever remain constant or increase. That matters more than it seems.

这一事实早在我们探测到真实的并合之前就已确立，如今它成了一个可以用引力波检验的预言。铃宕使我们能够算出新黑洞的大小，因为它与黑洞的质量和自旋相关。这些观测与预言相符：新生的黑洞确实更大。

This fact was established long before we had detected a real-life merger, though it is now a prediction that can be tested with gravitational waves. The ringdown allows us to work out the size of the resulting black hole because it is related to its mass and spin. These observations are consistent with the prediction that the newborn entity is indeed larger.

早在20世纪70年代，黑洞的这一特性就引起了物理学家们的注意。它为黑洞演化引入了一种不可否认的不可逆性。黑洞的面积总是增大，从不缩小。黑洞在暗示着什么：我们的世界与它们的世界之间的又一条纽带。这条纽带就是一个叫做熵的物理量。

As early as the 1970s, this characteristic of black holes attracted the attention of physicists. It introduced an undeniable irreversibility into black hole evolution. The areas of black holes are always increasing, never getting smaller. Black holes were hinting at something: another link between our world and theirs. This link is a quantity called entropy.

要看到这层联系，让我们暂时放下黑洞，回到地球。我们需要理解熵是什么，以及它如何与不可逆性相连。这条纽带乍看可能很遥远。但我想向你展示，一个简单的思想实验如何把我们从微观世界带回黑洞的大小，再引向无毛定理，最终抵达「黑洞遗忘了什么」这个问题。

To see this connection, let’s forget about black holes for a moment and return to Earth. We need to understand what entropy is and how it connects to irreversibility. This link may seem distant at first. But I want to show you how a simple thought experiment takes us from the microscopic world, back to the sizes of black holes, then to the no-hair theorem, and eventually to what black holes forget.

想象一个空房间。当然，它并非彻底的空。房间里有数十亿个空气分子在推挤、碰撞、四处移动，要想完整地量化它们，需要不可思议的耐心与时间。

Imagine an empty room. Of course, it is not completely empty. Inside are billions of air molecules jostling around, crashing into each other, moving from place to place, in a way that would require an impossible amount of patience, and time, to properly quantify.

所幸，我们从不去想单个空气分子的位置和速度。我们思考的是它们行为的总和。这类量包括房间的温度、压强等。例如，我们知道，往深了说到微观层面，温度不过是空气分子撞击我们皮肤的运动。在这个层面上，并不存在通常意义上的温度。那只是一群四处游动的分子，把能量传递给我们身体里的分子。只有当我们把镜头拉远，才会把分子的集体行为当作单一的量来对待。

Thankfully, we don’t ever think about the positions and speeds of individual air molecules. Instead, we think about the sum total of their behaviour. These are quantities like the temperature of the room, or the pressure. For example, we know that temperature is, deep down at the microscopic level, just the motion of air molecules crashing into our skin. At this level, there is no such thing as temperature in the usual sense. It is simply a collection of molecules moving around, imparting energy on the molecules in our body. It is only when we zoom out that we treat the collective behaviour of the molecules as a single quantity.

假设现在我们在房间入口处喷洒香水。起初，对身处其中的人来说，香水的位置一清二楚：它集中在门口附近。但随着时间推移，香水小滴与空气分子混合，香气最终成为遍布整个房间的均匀存在。在喷洒之后的未来某个时刻，门口的香水将不再比房间里任何别处更多。

Let’s say we now spray perfume by the entrance to the room. At first, to anyone occupying the space, the location of the perfume is clear. It is localised around the doorway. But over time, the perfume droplets mix with the air molecules until the scent becomes a uniform presence across the room. There will be some point in the future, after the perfume has been sprayed, when there is no more perfume by the door than there is anywhere else in the room.

香水这样扩散似乎理所当然。但深究下去，它其实很奇怪。它告诉我们时间的流逝。香水总是扩散到整个房间。它似乎从不回到某个角落，也不会无限期地徘徊在门口。这里有一个清晰的方向；一种把较早的时刻与较晚的时刻区分开来的特征。

It might seem obvious that perfume should diffuse in this way. But when we dig deeper, it is strange. It tells us about the passage of time. Perfume always diffuses across the room. It never seems to return to a single corner, or linger by the doorway indefinitely. There is a clear direction; a characteristic that differentiates an earlier time from a later one.

原因在于，香水小滴和空气分子一样，可以去往任何地方。在任一瞬间，它们可能呈现的排布方式都是一个天文数字。但在这些排布中——这里一个分子、那里一滴液滴——压倒性的多数都对应着香水均匀散布于整个房间。只有少到可以忽略的几种对应着它留在门口附近。所以，虽然没有任何东西禁止香水聚拢回瓶子旁，但发生这种事的概率微乎其微。如果你愿意等上极其漫长的时间（远远、远远超过宇宙的年龄），你也许恰好能目睹香水回到瓶中。但我不建议这么做。

The reason is that perfume droplets, like the air molecules, can go anywhere. At any instant, there is a huge number of arrangements they might take. But the overwhelming majority of those arrangements – a molecule here, a droplet there – correspond to perfume spread evenly throughout the room. Only a vanishing few represent it in remaining close to the door. So, while nothing forbids the perfume from gathering back by the bottle, the odds against it are vast. If you were willing to wait an extraordinarily long time (far, far longer than the age of the Universe) you might just witness the perfume returning to the bottle. But I would advise against it.

我们看不见粒子，只能感知宏观图景，比如房间的温度，或钟的声音。我们观察到的是香水的气味，不可逆地从一个位置扩展到整个房间。这就是熵增的一个例子。

We do not see particles, we merely sense the bigger picture, like the temperature of the room, or the sound of the bell. What we observe is the smell of the perfume, irreversibly moving from a single location to the entirety of the room. This is an example of increasing entropy.

我们常把熵与无序联系在一起。我有时把它理解为我们所知之少。当香水还在门口时，我们可以指着那个角落说：「香水是在这里喷的。」过一会儿，这就不再可能了。这些认知消失在数万亿个推挤的空气分子的运动之中。我们看到的只是房间的宏观状态。从这个角度看，房间遗忘了。

We often associate entropy with disorder. I sometimes think about it as how little we know. When the perfume starts by the doorway, we can point to that corner and say ‘The perfume was sprayed here.’ Later, that is no longer possible. The knowledge is lost among the motions of trillions of jostling air molecules. We see only the macroscopic state of the room. From this perspective, the room forgets.

这就是熵的增加，许多物理学家认为正是它设定了时间中的方向。尽管微观物理定律在时间上前向与后向的运作方式完全相同，概率却决定了孤立系统总是从低熵走向高熵。在这里成立的，也在处处成立：在有香水的房间里如此，在整个宇宙中亦然。

This is entropy increasing, and many physicists think this sets a direction through time. While the microscopic laws of physics work the same way backward or forward in time, probability dictates that isolated systems always move from lower to higher entropy. What is true here is also true everywhere: in a room with perfume, or the Universe as a whole.

这把我们带回黑洞。惠勒的学生雅各布·贝肯斯坦（Jacob Bekenstein）知道熵应当永远增加，但他发现了一个疑题。如果你取一团充满熵的热气体云——处于无序运动中的分子，就像那瓶香水——把它丢进黑洞，它的熵看起来就凭空消失了。曾经混乱的东西变成了简单的东西。这似乎违反了一条基本定律：宇宙的熵在减少。然而，贝肯斯坦意识到，黑洞的面积永远在增大，正如熵一样。他由此断定，熵与黑洞的大小之间必定存在某种关系。

This brings us back to black holes. Wheeler’s student Jacob Bekenstein knew that entropy should always increase, but identified a puzzle. If you take a hot gas cloud full of entropy – molecules in disordered motion, like the perfume – and drop it into a black hole, its entropy appears to simply vanish. What was chaotic becomes simple. That seemed to be a violation of a fundamental law: the entropy of the Universe decreasing. However, Bekenstein realised that a black hole’s area always increases, just like entropy. He concluded that there must be a relationship between entropy and black hole size.

所以，黑洞也许终究没那么简单。如果黑洞与熵相关，那它们就必然与我刚才描述的那个房间相关。而如果贝肯斯坦的图景是对的，它就意味着：在事件视界之外必定存在一个我们看不见的世界，那里充满了抽象的状态，就像房间里空气分子的位置。这些状态的具体性质——它们如何排布、位于何处、究竟是什么——仍有争论。它们的微观本质需要一个量子理论。然而，无论它们的终极本性如何，它们都让我们处于无知之中。

So, perhaps black holes are not so simple after all. If black holes are related to entropy, then they must be related to the room I just described. And if Bekenstein’s picture is true, it suggests there must be a world we cannot see, beyond the event horizon, that is filled with abstract states, like the positions of air molecules in the room. The particular nature of these states – how they are arranged, where they are located, and what they are – is still debated. Their microscopic nature demands a quantum theory. Yet, whatever their ultimate nature, they leave us ignorant.

黑洞无数可能的历史就此失落。恒星的坍缩、物质的坠入、一场灾难性的并合——这些细节都无法存活于黑洞最终的外部描述之中。这就是贝肯斯坦对无毛定理的理解：黑洞之所以变得简单，是因为熵带走了我们曾经知道的东西。对那个房间而言，我们失去的是香水的来处。对黑洞而言，我们失去的是除质量和自旋之外的一切。从我们的视角看，黑洞遗忘了。

The countless possible histories of the black hole become lost. The collapse of a star, the infall of matter, a cataclysmic merger; these details do not survive the final exterior description of the black hole. This was Bekenstein’s understanding of the no-hair theorem: black holes become simple because entropy takes away something we once knew. For our room, we lost the origins of the perfume. For the black hole, we lose everything except the mass and spin. From our vantage point, the black hole forgets.

这也许会让你不安。信息是真的丢失了，还是仅仅因为实际局限而无法触及？这种区分也许毫无意义。毕竟，一个你无法逾越的局限，与一条基本定律又有什么区别？但答案颇为微妙，而正是在这里，黑洞与我们的房间分道扬镳。

This might trouble you. Is the information really lost, or is it merely inaccessible due to practical limitations? Perhaps the distinction means nothing. After all, what difference is a limitation you cannot overcome to a fundamental rule? But the answer is subtle, and it is here that the black hole and our room part company.

假设我们暂时获得一种上帝视角来看这个房间。我们让自己完全知晓每个粒子的位置与速度。法国博学家皮埃尔-西蒙·拉普拉斯（Pierre-Simon Laplace）在《概率的哲学随笔》（A Philosophical Essay on Probabilities，1814）中有过类似的想法，他谈到一种「智能，它能理解自然界赖以运行的全部作用力，以及组成自然的万物各自的处境」。换句话说，就是一个知晓物理世界一切可知之事的存在。

Let’s say, for a moment, that we grant ourselves a God’s eye view of the room. We give ourselves full knowledge of the positions and speeds of the particles. The French polymath Pierre-Simon Laplace had a similar idea in his A Philosophical Essay on Probabilities (1814), where he talks about an ‘intelligence which could comprehend all the forces by which nature is animated and the respective situation of the beings who compose it.’ In other words, someone who knows everything that can possibly be known about the physical world.

回到房间，我们逐一清点每一个空气分子和香水分子。我们以完美的精度知晓它们的轨迹。凭借这些信息，我们让时间倒流，用物理定律追溯每一滴香水的轨迹，回到它最初出发的地方。就像按下录像带的倒带键，我们看着空气分子从一处移到另一处。我们这样做时，物理定律照常运转。我们很容易说服自己：这些空气分子正在时间中向前移动。然而小液滴戳穿了这个幻觉。它们曾散布于整个房间，如今却逐渐汇聚到一个地点：门口那只它们来自的瓶子。

Returning to the room, we take stock of every air and perfume molecule. We know their trajectories with perfect precision. With this information we rewind the clock, using the laws of physics to trace the trajectory of each droplet of perfume back to where it originally started. Like pressing rewind on a video, we watch as the air molecules move from place to place. When we do this, the laws of physics run just the same. It would be easy to convince ourselves that the air molecules are moving forwards through time. Yet the droplets betray this illusion. While once spread throughout the room, they gradually converge on a single location: the bottle in the doorway from which they came.

在这个层面上，宇宙朝两个方向运行得同样顺畅。当然，在实践中我们无法实现这种逆转。大自然不会让它轻易实现。但也没有任何定律禁止它。我们几乎还能握住一段记忆。事物随时间变得模糊，却从不彻底消失。

At this level, the Universe runs equally well in both directions. We could not accomplish this kind of reversal in practice, of course. Nature does not make it easy. But no law forbids it. We can almost hold on to a memory. Things become hazier over time, but they are never wholly gone.

对黑洞来说，故事有所不同。事件视界把黑洞与宇宙中的其余一切隔开：无论在比喻意义上还是在字面意义上。事件视界意味着黑洞是一趟单程旅行。如果你带着全知的眼睛冒险进入黑洞，也许你能让时间倒流，看到它的过去发生过什么。（也许不能；谁也不知道。）但无论哪种情况，你都无法告诉任何人，而就大多数目的而言，这与没发现并无二致。你和你的讯息都会被拖入黑洞更深处，搅进它漫长的历史之中。

For black holes, the story is different. The event horizon separates a black hole from everything else in the Universe: both figuratively and literally. The event horizon means a black hole is a one-way trip. If you were to venture into the black hole with all-knowing eyes, perhaps you would be able to rewind the clock and see what happened in its past. (Though perhaps not; nobody knows.) Yet in either case, you wouldn’t be able to tell anyone, which for most purposes is the same thing as not finding out. You and your messages would get dragged deeper into the black hole and become scrambled into its long history.

另一方面，从事件视界之外，我们无法触及黑洞的那些状态。我们永远没有机会像对待房间那样放大细看、追溯它的历史。我们看到的全部只是宏观图景，细节都隐没在事件视界之后——那是无数微观状态聚拢而成的寥寥几个数字，就像房间的温度，或香水的气味。只不过，对黑洞来说，这几个数字是它的质量和自旋。

On the other hand, from outside the event horizon, we are unable to access the states of the black hole. We never have the opportunity to zoom in and retrace its history like we did with the room. Instead, all we see is the bigger picture, with the details shrouded behind the event horizon. The result of countless microscopic states clustered together to give a few numbers, like the temperature of the room, or the smell of perfume. Except, for the black hole, these are its mass and spin.

这是无毛定理的双重体现。第一重与房间相同：把镜头拉远，细节便汇成寥寥几个数字，历史被埋葬在大量不确定的可能性之中。但黑洞又添了新的一重。宏观图景是我们唯一被允许拥有的视角。细节消失在事件视界之后。因此，我们对黑洞的诞生始终一无所知。过去变得无法触及。黑洞遗忘了。

This is the no-hair theorem twice over. First in the same sense as the room: zoom out, and the fine details wash into a handful of numbers, the history buried among a mass of indeterminate possibilities. But the black hole adds something new. The big picture is the only view we are ever allowed to have. The details disappear behind the event horizon. We therefore remain unremittingly ignorant of the black hole’s creation. The past becomes inaccessible. The black hole forgets.

不过，「遗忘」这个说法也许过于简单了。在我们的房间里，过去被冲刷而去。它未必是永久的，但热力学让恢复它的代价变得高昂。而我们并不生活在这个粒子的世界里。我们永远不可能是拥有上帝视角的拉普拉斯智能（可我们为什么要想成为它呢？）。我们听到钟乐，闻到香水，感到房间的温暖，而不是数量多得无法理解的空气分子的碰撞。我们被赐予这些转瞬即逝的体验，与此同时，宇宙缓缓漂向一片浩瀚而无特征的苍茫。这是自然与我们达成的折中：一些美好的事物，终将失去。

Though, perhaps forgetting is too simple. In our room, the past gets washed away. It may not be permanent, but thermodynamics makes it expensive to recover. And we do not live in this world of particles. We are never Laplace’s intelligence with a God’s-eye view (and why would we want to be?) Instead, we hear chimes, we notice the smell of perfume, we feel the warmth of a room, not the collisions of incomprehensibly many air molecules. We are given these transient experiences as the Universe drifts towards a vast, featureless expanse. This is the compromise nature offers us: something beautiful that must eventually be lost.

黑洞并无不同。它们把历史唱尽，从混乱归于沉寂。它们遗忘几乎一切，正如万物终将遗忘一样。只是它们永远无法再透露更多。在视界之内，它们或许仍保有自己生平的某些东西，只是以一种难以想象的艰难形式存留。你可以称之为记忆。也许触及它的唯一方式，就是亲自坠落进去，成为那段历史的一部分。再说一次，我不建议这么做。

Black holes are no different. They sing away their history, moving from chaos to silence. They forget nearly everything in the same way that everything eventually does. It is just that they can never reveal anything more. Through the horizon they may still hold something of their lives, albeit in a form impossibly hard to recover. You could call it memory. Perhaps the only way to reach it is to fall in and become part of the history yourself. Again, I’d advise against it.

剑桥的咖啡馆里，时钟走过上午十点，钟声再度响起，然后归于终结。声音很快消散，只剩下楼下街道隐约的低鸣。我坐在那里，端着早已凉掉的咖啡，意识到自己到来之后已过了一个小时。这就是我们短暂经历的世界：时间向前，熵在增加。要存在，就必须有失去。在某个遥远的所在，一个黑洞也正鸣响着走向寂静。空间、时间和我周围的空气都在遗忘，我也将如此。尽管如此，我很庆幸自己能在场听见这一切。

As the clock passes 10 am in the Cambridge café, the bells ring again, before coming to an end. The sound is quickly gone, with only the faint hum of the street below remaining. I sit with my now-cold coffee as I become aware of the hour that has passed since I arrived. This is the world we briefly experience: time marches forward and entropy increases. You must have loss to have existence. Somewhere out there, a black hole rings into silence, too. Space, time and the air around me forget, as will I. Still, I am glad that I am there to hear it.
