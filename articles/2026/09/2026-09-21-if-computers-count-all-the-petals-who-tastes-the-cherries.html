如果计算机数尽所有花瓣，谁来品尝樱桃？

Liam Spradlin·Aeon·2026年9月21日·10分钟阅读·原文

2015年夏天一个晴朗的日子，我发现自己正站在硅谷（Silicon Valley）一栋办公楼的地下室里。房间里塞满了各种电器，仿佛一间商用厨房被炸开了一般。几摞嵌入式烤箱挤放在手推车上。一团团电线从宽阔的不锈钢操作台后面钻出来。抹布和海绵在巨大的水槽边缘慢慢晾干，水槽上方，一支预冲喷头垂下来，像一根柳枝。旁边，一台电脑显示器上开着Python开发者工具，旁边是一张缀满红绿单元格的电子表格。房间中央是一座临时拼凑的备餐台，台上摆着一台古怪的小机器，看起来像一台专为加热单个纸杯蛋糕设计的迷你微波炉。后来我才知道，那是一台水分分析仪：研究人员用它来检测羊肉、剑鱼、鸡肉、猪肉和牛肉的样本里还剩多少汁水。我把看到的一切都记录下来：上午10点45分。厨房里满是烟。

On a sunny day in the summer of 2015, I found myself standing in the basement of an office building in Silicon Valley. The room was packed full of appliances, as if a commercial kitchen had been blown apart. Several stacks of wall ovens huddled together on dollies. Tangles of electrical cords popped up behind a broad stainless-steel counter. Rags and sponges dried slowly on the edge of a giant sink, over which a pre-rinse nozzle drooped like a willow branch. Nearby, a computer monitor showed Python developer tools next to a spreadsheet dotted with red and green cells. In the middle of the room was an improvised prep station on which there was an odd little machine. It looked like a tiny microwave designed to warm up a single cupcake. I later learned it’s a moisture analyser: the researchers use it to see how much juice is left in their samples of lamb, swordfish, chicken, pork and beef. I’m writing down everything I see: 10:45 am. The kitchen is full of smoke.

那间地下室是一个测试厨房，两位厨师和几名研究人员正忙着在一组原型烤箱里烹制堪比整座农场产量的肉。公司的愿景是发明一种烹饪机器，只需按下按钮就能端出米其林级别的菜肴。团队一边品尝食物，一边填写「感官清单」表格，记录颜色、气味、温度、质地和味道等品质。

That basement room was a test kitchen, where two chefs and several researchers had been busy cooking a whole farm’s worth of meat in a set of prototype ovens. The company’s vision was to invent a cooking machine that could whip up Michelin-grade dishes at the press of a button, and the crew was tasting the food and filling out ‘sensory inventory’ forms that recorded qualities like colour, smell, temperature, texture and taste.

作为烤箱触摸屏界面的设计负责人，我到那里向厨师们取经，以便打造出既服务于他们、也服务于「家庭厨师」（即最终用户）的东西。他们的烹饪直觉与烤箱逻辑之间的张力立刻显露无遗：厨房里烟雾弥漫，是因为一位厨师试图把三架羊排直接塞进上火烧烤器底下烤，好获得那种「用丙烷喷枪燎一下」才有的色泽。这种本能无法翻译成烤箱听得懂的语言。我记下「燎一下？」，继续观察。另一位厨师在另一台烤箱的屏幕上敲敲打打，嘴里嘟囔着：「这些东西一无是处。我得能用上我的厨师蜘蛛感应。」

As the design lead for the oven’s touchscreen interface, I was there to learn from the chefs in order to build something that worked for them and for the ‘home chef’ or end user. The tension between their culinary intuition and the ovens’ logic was immediately apparent: the kitchen was full of smoke because one chef had attempted to cook three racks of lamb directly under a broiler to get the colour you’d get by ‘smacking it with a propane torch’. This instinct didn’t translate to a language the oven understood. I wrote down Smacking? and kept watching. The other chef, tapping away on another of the ovens’ screens, muttered: ‘These things are worthless. I need to be able to use my chef spidey senses.’

厨师们受雇帮烤箱编程，让它遵循自己的食谱。但他们还得假装是给家人做晚饭的普通人，以检验烤箱能否复刻专家技法。一方面，厨师们需要一个控件足够多的界面来做手动调整，为烤箱的训练语料库添砖加瓦；另一方面，最终用户需要的则是简单，以及一种「它很聪明」的印象。

The chefs were hired to help program the ovens to follow their recipes. But they also had to pretend they were normal people cooking dinner for their families in order to see if the ovens could replicate expert techniques. On one hand, the chefs needed an interface with enough controls to make manual adjustments, adding to the oven’s corpus of training data. On the other hand, end users would need simplicity and the impression of smarts.

制造烤箱的公司把人对食谱的干预视为一种负资产。选项太多会诱使用户摆弄食谱，这既有损烤箱的智能，又可能招致糟糕的结果。产品团队反复念叨，用户若过多手动调整食谱，就会养成「坏习惯」。「改变仪式」成了办公室里的口头禅，写满了白板、便利贴和任务追踪器。

The company building the ovens viewed human intervention in the recipes as a liability. Having too many options would invite users to fiddle with the recipes, which would cut against the ovens’ smarts and risk producing a bad result. The product team talked a lot about users forming ‘bad habits’ if they manually adjusted recipes too much. ‘Change the ritual’ became a mantra written on whiteboards, sticky notes and task trackers around the office.

尽管如此，我当时是认真对待这个愿景的。那是我科技职业生涯早期的一个项目，正值2010年代技术乐观主义热潮的顶峰。我相信，只要有正确的信息，烹饪的种种问题都能靠巧妙的设计来解决。但在随后十年里——从城市基础设施到全球设计系统，我在大规模构建界面的经历中——逐渐把那间测试厨房理解为技术行业一场根本斗争的缩影：一边是人的主体性，一边是对万物皆可计算的信仰；一边是人体的感官——味觉，一边是「数据才是更有效的知识形式」的观念。

Still, I took the vision seriously. It was an early project in my tech career, at the height of the 2010s techno-optimist boom. I believed that, with the right information, the problems of cooking could be solved through clever design. But over the next decade of experience building interfaces at scale – from urban infrastructure to global design systems – I came to understand the test kitchen as a microcosm of a fundamental struggle in tech between human subjectivity and a belief in the computability of everything; between the human body’s senses – taste – and the view that data is a more valid form of knowledge.

味觉，无论作为生理过程还是审美偏好，都是主观的。它依情境而定，属于个人。在智能厨房里，正如在技术的诸多追求里一样，这种本质上私人的体验被（而且必须被）弄得标准化、可重复。换言之，味觉需要变成数据——被制成哲学家阿尔伯特·博格曼（Albert Borgmann）所说的「装置」：手段被抽象掉，几乎或完全无需人的投入便能交付结果——于是人的努力（连同家庭厨师的能动性）被一并抽象而去。

Taste, both as a physiological process and an aesthetic preference, is subjective. It’s contextual and belongs to the individual. In the smart kitchen, as in so many of tech’s pursuits, this kind of inherently personal experience is (and must be) made uniform and repeatable. In other words, taste needs to become data – made into what the philosopher Albert Borgmann called a ‘device’ that abstracts the means and delivers the ends with little or no human input – so that human effort (and the home chef’s agency along with it) is abstracted away.

但烤箱不会品尝。它全然不知用丙烷喷枪「燎」一下东西是什么意思。正如设计师尼赞·赫尔蒙（Nitzan Hermon）在他的Substack上所写，追问一个算法能否理解某种主观事物，是一个范畴错误，就像问一块石头喜欢哪几部电影。光靠数一棵树上的花瓣，不可能体验到樱桃的滋味。无论收集多少数据点，仅凭数据无法复现——甚至无法准确表征——具身的经验。然而，烤箱所依赖的假设恰恰是：食物的品质与人的味觉皆可量化。

But an oven can’t taste. It has no idea what ‘smacking’ something with a propane torch means. As the designer Nitzan Hermon writes in his Substack, asking if an algorithm can understand something subjective is a category error, like asking a stone which movies it likes. It’s impossible to experience the taste of a cherry just by counting the petals on a tree. No matter how many data points are collected, data alone cannot reproduce – or even accurately represent – embodied experiences. Yet the oven relied on an assumption that the qualities of food and human taste are quantifiable.

智能厨房是我与一种意识形态的初遇，我称之为「计算主义」（computism）。它主张宇宙由离散、可测量的数据比特构成——哲学家称之为「数字本体论」（digital ontology）——并把这一观念延伸成一套经济与政治体系。依照计算主义，世界，包括人类，皆可被计算：构成万物的比特可作为数据被表征与操纵。宇宙，本质上，可由代码统治。

The smart kitchen was my introduction to an ideology I call computism, which holds that the Universe is made of discrete, measurable bits of data – what philosophers call a ‘digital ontology’ – and extends that view into an economic and political system. According to computism, the world, including humans, can be computed: the bits that everything is made of can be represented and manipulated as data. The Universe can be governed, essentially, by code.

科技领袖们就计算如何操纵世界放出豪言。埃隆·马斯克（Elon Musk）说人工智能将「重写整个人类知识总库」；人工智能公司Anthropic的首席执行官达里奥·阿莫迪（Dario Amodei）则写道，图形处理器（GPU）很快就会把我们变成「数据中心里的天才之国」。还有人主张宇宙可能是一场旨在催生超级智能的模拟，或者声称未来的AI将名副其实地成为神。这一切信念的言外之意是：在一个可以由计算预测的世界里，最有权势的人就是运行计算机的人。我们日常经验的数据化、预测模型日益显著的存在，最好都透过计算主义的透镜来理解。当一切皆可计算，一切皆可预测、可优化、可操纵。

Tech leaders have made enormous claims about how computation can manipulate the world. Elon Musk says that AI will ‘rewrite the entire corpus of human knowledge’ and Dario Amodei, CEO of the AI company Anthropic, writes that graphics processing units will soon turn us into a ‘country of geniuses in a datacenter’. Others suggest that the Universe may be a simulation meant to bring about superintelligence, or that a future AI will be, literally, a god. Implicit in all these beliefs is that, in a world that can be predicted through computation, the most powerful person is whoever runs the computer. The datafication of our daily experiences and the increasing presence of predictive models are best understood through the lens of computism. When everything is computable, everything is predictable, optimisable and manipulable.

这套叙事并非硅谷的发明。古希腊哲学家德谟克利特（Democritus）提出，宇宙及万物由不可再分的微粒构成，他称之为atomos（「不可切」「不可分」）。这种早期的数字本体论在启蒙运动中获得新生：勒内·笛卡尔（René Descartes）接过这一原子模型，把一切物质之物尽数纳入其中，包括人的身体。在笛卡尔的模型里，身体被看作一台复杂的机器，机器里住着一位内置的使用者——灵魂——那是唯一不由比特构成的东西。后来的发明家们则着手证明德谟克利特与笛卡尔是对的。雅克·德·沃康松（Jacques de Vaucanson）18世纪的机械鸭——日后机械织机的垫脚石——模拟了进食与消化；正是这条造物谱系，最终把我引到加州那间地下室，让我试图在一件没那么异想天开的机械物里模拟味觉。

This narrative is not an invention of Silicon Valley. The ancient Greek philosopher Democritus proposed that the universe and everything in it was made of indivisible bits called atomos (‘uncut’, ‘indivisible’). This early digital ontology found new life in the Enlightenment when René Descartes took up this atomic model, incorporating everything physical into it, including the human body. In Descartes’s model, the body was thought of as a complicated machine imbued with an internal user – the soul – which was the only thing not made of bits. Later inventors would set out to prove Democritus and Descartes right. Jacques de Vaucanson’s 18th-century mechanical duck, a stepping stone to later mechanical looms, simulated eating and digestion – a creation that eventually led me to that basement room in California, attempting to simulate taste in a less fanciful mechanical object.

自打在厨房的那些日子起，我眼看着计算主义缓缓扎根，也不得不扪心自问：我自己的工作——界面设计——如何体现着它。设计项目常常以优化用户在界面中的动线为核心。参与度、转化率、效率、生产力——一场与「摩擦」永不停歇的战斗。设计师专注于测量用户的行为，因为在研究环境之外，我们无从触及他们各自的思想。于是对计算机而言，行为便构成了这个人——这意味着，人作为超出行为集合之外的存在，从设计师的视野中消失了。多年来，界面设计从一门主要关涉人类主体性的手艺（正如一切设计本然如此），转向了一门追求机械客观性的行当，力图摆脱人类解释或判断的「污染性」影响。

Since my time in the kitchen, I’ve observed computism slowly taking hold, and have had to grapple with how my own work – interface design – manifests it. Often, design projects centre on optimising how users move through the interface. Engagement, conversions, efficiency and productivity – a persistent fight against friction. Designers focus on measuring the actions of the user because outside of a research environment we don’t have access to their individual thoughts. To the computer, then, actions constitute the person – which means that the person, as anything more than a set of actions, disappears from the designer’s view. Over the years, interface design has shifted from a craft engaged primarily with human subjectivity (as all design is) to one seeking mechanical objectivity, free from the contaminating influence of human interpretation or judgment.

我刚进入科技行业时，许多客户都是设计咨询公司IDEO「设计思维」（design thinking）的信徒：一套可重复的、通过设计解决问题的步骤。这张「配方」被套用到各种事务上，从新的应用到公共治理，催生了用户体验（UX）设计的正式化，为厌恶风险的决策者奉上以数据为凭的客观性表象。UX设计作为驱动技术繁荣的代码与数据的有形体现，正当其时。而我就在一线，欣然拥抱UX设计一夜之间与权力的贴近。「设计是一个可复制的过程」——这一日益高涨的信念既帮我们兜售设计的重要性，也让我们相信：设计与技术携手，能让世界变得更好。

When I entered tech, many of my clients were adherents to the design consultancy IDEO’s ‘design thinking’: a repeatable series of steps for solving problems through design. This recipe, applied to all sorts of things from new apps to public governance, catalysed the formalisation of user experience (UX) design, offering the appearance of objectivity through data to risk-averse decision-makers. UX design, as the tangible manifestation of the code and data that powered the tech boom, was having a moment. And I was there on the ground, embracing UX design’s newfound proximity to power. The growing belief that design was a replicable process helped us sell its importance and helped us believe that design and technology together could change the world for the better.

快进十年，设计成功在商业的桌面上赢得了一席之地。但为了取得这地位，软件设计不得不改变。一旦设计被接纳为一种定量增长机制，它的造物（界面）就被拆散，以便测量。界面的元素——按钮、卡片、列表——被分解为形状、颜色、字体的种种实例，每一个都带有离散的取值。这些视觉品质的星丛被编纂进设计系统。经由软件，设计从一种复杂而依情境而定的、相互关联的结构编排，转向了一个零件可替换的机械物。

Fast-forward 10 years, and design has succeeded in getting its seat at the table of business. But, to attain its status, software design had to change. Once design was accepted as a mechanism for quantitative growth, its artefacts (the interface) were broken apart so they could be measured. The elements of the interface – buttons, cards, lists – were broken down into various instantiations of shapes, colours and typography, each with discrete values. These constellations of visual qualities were codified in design systems. Design, through software, moved from a complex and contextual arrangement of interrelated structures toward a mechanical object with swappable parts.

随着基础设施的改善，诸如A/B测试之类的技术——向不同用户投放略有差异的界面版本，看哪个表现更好——成了数字界面的标准做法。这项技术改编自随机对照实验的一般概念与行为经济学的原理。随着用户基数爆炸式增长，开展实验、把界面调校到期望结果的能力达到了前所未有的高度。久而久之，界面变成了一台单向的数据抽取机器。用评论家西尔维奥·洛鲁索（Silvio Lorusso）的话说：「这个曾经植根于解决问题的、前景可期的领域，[成了]问题本身。」

Techniques like A/B testing – in which slightly different versions of an interface are served to different users to see which performs better – became standard in the digital interface as infrastructure improved. It was a technique adapted from the general concept of randomised controlled experiments and principles from behavioural economics. With exploding user bases, the ability to run experiments and tune the interface toward desired outcomes reached unprecedented heights. Over time, the interface became a one-way mechanism of extracting data. In the words of the critic Silvio Lorusso, ‘what was once a promising field rooted in problem-solving [became] a problem in itself.’

这方面的一个例证，是关于谷歌（Google）蓝色搜索结果链接的一则传奇轶事。谷歌的搜索团队测试了41种蓝色，看哪一种能让用户点开更多链接。设计师道格拉斯·鲍曼（Douglas Bowman）把它引为技术行业数据驱动文化的例证，并因此辞职：

One illustration of this is a legendary anecdote about Google’s blue search result links. The Search team at Google tested 41 shades of blue to see which one got users to click on more links. The designer Douglas Bowman cited it as an example of the data-driven culture of tech that led to his resignation:

（一位设计师甚至告诉我，有一次一个拼写错误让一种倒胃口的棕色混进了候选之列。领导层的回应是？「如果它比那些蓝色表现更好，我们就留下它。」）

(One designer even told me that a typo led to an off-putting brown colour being included in the mix. The response from leadership? ‘If it performs better than the blues, we’ll keep it.’)

这类经由设计交付给用户的机械优化，把这门手艺中人的要素——主观品味所扮演的角色——抽象掉了：它只盯着量化的结果，一路丢失意义。随着从复杂结构到孤立元素的转变把界面进一步推向绝对量化，界面变成了（博格曼意义上的）「装置」。借助软件完成的行为——沟通、规划、学习——被化约为可计算的结果，用户也在这一过程中被异化。

This kind of mechanical optimisation, delivered to users through design, abstracts the craft’s human element – the role of subjective taste – by focusing on quantified outcomes, losing meaning along the way. The interface becomes a ‘device’ (in Borgmann’s sense) as the shift from complex structures to isolated elements moves it further toward absolute quantification. The actions performed through software – communication, planning, learning – are reduced to computable outcomes, alienating users in the process.

数据的支配力渗透进软件，直至一个个用户界面（UI）组件：一套五星评分系统、一串文本、一个下拉菜单、一个话题标签。经验被坍缩成比特。结束一段关系、跟父母谈话这样的行为，如今都因为你可能用什么软件去做而背负上社会潜台词。软件观察、建模并回应用户的能力，被转向创造或增强，而非促成用户的意图。

The dominance of data permeates software down to individual user interface (UI) components: a five-star rating system, a text string, a dropdown, a hashtag. Experiences are collapsed into bits. Actions like ending a relationship or talking to your parents are now charged with social subtext based on what software you might use to perform them. Software’s ability to observe, model and respond to the user is turned toward creating or augmenting rather than facilitating the user’s intent.

计算主义为了给全面数据化的追求正名，推崇一种实在模型：它要挣脱人类身体与心智的局限，去看世界必然的真貌——数据。这一跃需要权力运作方式的转变，哲学家韩炳哲（Byung-Chul Han）在《精神政治学》（Psychopolitics, 2014）中对此作了阐明。随着新自由主义从工厂车间里的肉体强制转向屏幕上的心理优化，用户被一步步引向对自身的剥削。

Computism, in order to justify its pursuit of total datafication, promotes a model of reality that escapes the limitations of the human body and mind to see the world as it must really be: data. This leap requires a shift in how power operates, which the philosopher Byung-Chul Han explains in his book Psychopolitics (2014). As neoliberalism moves from physical coercion on a factory floor toward psychological optimisation on screens, users are driven toward their own exploitation.

我们可以在界面的运作机制里直接看到这一点。一个看似简单的无限信息流，配上老虎机式的下拉刷新交互，并不靠把用户困在平台上强迫其劳动。相反，它让用户自己想要生成数据。这一区别是理解界面设计阴险一面的关键——它利用心智的生理机制，把人对机器的依赖推至极致。

We can see this directly in the mechanics of the interface. Something as simple as an endless feed with a slot-machine-like pull-to-refresh interaction doesn’t force the user to work by staying on the platform. Instead, the user is made to want to generate data. This distinction is key to understanding the insidious side of interface design, which exploits the physiology of the mind to maximise its reliance on the machine.

哲学家安德烈亚斯·施帕恩（Andreas Spahn）认为，这正是数字技术颠覆传统笛卡尔式主客模型的方式。他论证道，数字技术已不再是我们施加动作于其上的被动工具或惰性客体。相反，它们正日益自主。当用户把能动性外包给界面——比如外出办事时让自家厨房替自己做饭——界面便开始沾染主体的性质，而用户则愈发趋近一个生成数据的客体。

This, according to the philosopher Andreas Spahn, is how digital technology upends the traditional Cartesian subject-object model. Digital technologies, he argues, are no longer passive tools or inert objects upon which we act. Instead, they are becoming autonomous. When users outsource agency to the interface – allowing their kitchen, for example, to cook for them while they run errands – the interface begins to take on qualities of a subject while the user moves closer to a data-generating object.

社会学家毛里齐奥·拉扎拉托（Maurizio Lazzarato）的著述加深了对计算主义的批判。他主张，资本主义不满足于把我们化约为生成数据的客体，还利用这些数据从内到外地制造主体性。在《符号与机器》（Signs and Machines, 2014）一书中，他论证说，技术绕开我们的意识行为，把我们拆解成前个体的组件（比如算法侦测出的「兴趣」），再制成可测量的输入。如此一来，技术所具备的能力就不只是观察我们、回应我们，还包括构造我们对它的回应。这与设计本身被拆解成原子化元素、可拼装重组以生成新界面的方式如出一辙。仿佛是要为拉扎拉托的观点作注，许多设计师和工程师日益倾向于把他们的设计系统喂给大语言模型（LLM），以产出设计界面的拟像。在别处，合成数据则被用来——比方说——制造合成用户画像：用拉扎拉托所说的碎片化、前个体的信息拼出的虚构之人，拿来检验他们的想法。活生生的人可以被彻底从这个流程中抽象掉，只留下一场模拟。毕竟，在计算主义模型里，人既然可以被理解为数据，数据便可以顶替人。

The sociologist Maurizio Lazzarato’s writing deepens the critique of computism. He argues that, beyond reducing us to data-generating objects, capitalism uses that data to manufacture subjectivity from the inside out. In his book Signs and Machines (2014), he argues that technology bypasses our conscious behaviour to break us down into pre-individual components (like algorithmically detected ‘interests’) that can be made into measurable inputs. In this way, technology has the capacity not just to observe and respond to us, but to construct our responses to it. It’s similar to the way design itself has been broken down into atomic elements that can be snapped together and recombined to produce new interfaces. As if to illustrate Lazzarato’s point, many designers and engineers have moved increasingly toward feeding their design systems to large language models (LLMs) to produce simulacra of designed interfaces. Elsewhere, synthetic data is used to, for example, create synthetic user personas – imaginary people made of Lazzarato’s fragmentary, pre-individual information against which to test their ideas. The actual human can be abstracted out of the process entirely, leaving just a simulation. After all, in the computist model, humans can be understood as data, so data can be substituted in for humans.

这就是把身体与心智当作装置来对待意味着什么。具身的主观经验被弃置，让位于测量。2024年，企业家彼得·迪亚曼迪斯（Peter Diamandis）对一群长寿诊所发表演讲时说：「这要么是硬件问题，要么是软件问题，而我们终将能把它修好！」如果身体是一台复杂的硬件，心智便是它那段漏洞百出、已然退化的软件，等着被优化，然后派去做更有产出的工作。

This is what it means to treat the body and the mind as a device. Embodied subjective experience is abandoned in favour of measurement. Speaking to a consortium of longevity clinics in 2024, the entrepreneur Peter Diamandis said: ‘It’s either a hardware problem or a software problem, and we’re going to be able to fix that!’ If the body is complicated hardware, the mind is its buggy, degraded software waiting to be optimised and put to more productive work.

这便把我们带到计算主义形而上学的逻辑终点：生成式AI将超越人类，成为一个后人类主体。在计算主义者看来，人类的知识与文化被人性污染太深，无从客观。对「超级智能」的追求，对「数据中心里的天才之国」的追求，对某个终将「修复气候」的新生AI实体（萨姆·奥尔特曼〔Sam Altman〕语）的追求，归根结底都是一项试图调试人类主体性的工程。它关乎逃离人性，把我们的责任、我们的错误与我们的恐惧统统移交给计算机——为「放任人类在如此混乱的状态下延续这么久」这个令人难堪的错误，献上一份道歉。灵性信仰体系之所以常常历久不衰，是因为它们为做人、受苦、死去这些看似无谓之事赋予了一种清晰感。我以同样的眼光看待计算主义，只不过，它不是把人类苦难的根源安放在巨匠造物主（demiurge）、魔鬼或欲望之上，而是安放在「身为人类」本身之上。

And that brings us to the logical conclusion of computism’s metaphysics: the idea that generative AI will transcend humanity to become a post-human subject. To the computist, human knowledge and culture are too contaminated by humanity to be objective. The pursuit of a ‘superintelligence’, a ‘country of geniuses in a datacenter’ or an emergent AI entity that will ‘fix the climate’ (as Sam Altman says) is fundamentally a project that seeks to debug human subjectivity. It’s about escaping humanity, handing the computer our responsibilities, our mistakes and our fears – an apology for the embarrassing mistake of letting humanity carry on in such a messy state for so long. Spiritual belief systems often endure because they bring a sense of clarity to the apparent senselessness of being human, suffering and dying. I see computism in the same way, except, instead of locating the cause of human suffering in a demiurge, a devil or a desire, computism locates it in simply being human.

主体性向数据的坍缩正在实时上演。智能家庭厨房的梦想，已经变形成Wonder之类的付费服务：它用算法综合你的健身目标、偏好与饥饿程度，替你选定下一餐，每天为你分配三餐。烹饪，不必了。甚至连「想吃什么」都不必想——应用自会料理。而这并不限于我们的身体。过去充当拼写检查器的工具，如今以真实人类作者的AI近似体自居，对你指手画脚。写作——一种本质上对话性的意义建构行为（一如设计）——被抽象掉了。工具依据它所能触及的全部人类写作，教人类如何听起来像另一个人类。2026年6月，一家初创公司宣布，连「品味」也是个数据问题，只要追加训练，生成式模型就能创造出伟大的品味之作。

The collapse of subjectivity into data is happening in real time. The dream of a smart home kitchen has morphed into paid services like Wonder, which assigns users three meals a day by algorithmically synthesising their fitness goals, preferences and hunger to choose what they’ll eat next. Forget cooking. Forget even thinking about what you want to eat – the app will handle it. And it isn’t limited to our bodies. Tools that used to act as spell-checkers now offer critiques from AI approximations of real human authors. Writing, an inherently dialogic act of sense-making (like design), is abstracted away. The tool tells humans how to sound like another human based on all the human writing it could access. A startup announced in June 2026 that even ‘taste’ is a data problem and, with extra training, generative models can create great works of taste.

计算主义工程靠收编公共领域的人类知识来运转。技术专家们想要重写的那座「语料库」，是数十亿人几千年间的共同文化产出。计算主义把这集体产出看作一团盘根错节的数据——一种无主的自然资源，有待提炼，涤除矛盾与主观性，直到那些知识——用科学史学家洛琳·达斯顿（Lorraine Daston）与彼得·L·加里森（Peter L Galison）的话说——「最终摆脱了认识者」。计算主义者相信，这种向原始信息的缴械就是解决方案，它将通向新真理的发现——一个乌托邦式的未来：计算机不再需要操作员，而人类不再需要做人，也不必再解决人的问题。一次又一次，当我们想不出如何调和主体与客体，我们就干脆移除主体。等到有一天只剩计算机，我们可以让它设计自己、优化自己，清除那些定义着我们生命的摩擦、错误、无常与挣扎。可是然后呢？

The computist project works by co-opting human knowledge from the commons. The ‘corpus’ that technologists want to rewrite is the shared cultural output of billions of people over thousands of years. Computism sees this collective output as a complicated mass of data – an unclaimed natural resource to be refined, freed from contradictions and subjectivity, until the knowledge, in the words of the historians of science Lorraine Daston and Peter L Galison, is finally free from the knower. Computists believe this capitulation to raw information is the solution, that it will lead to the discovery of new truths – a utopian future in which the computer no longer needs an operator, and humans no longer need to be human or solve human problems. Over and over again – when we can’t figure out how to reconcile the subject and the object – we simply remove the subject. When, one day, only the computer remains, we can let it design itself, optimise itself and eliminate the friction, error, unpredictability and struggle that define our lives. But what then?

计算主义真正的悲剧，不只在于计算机可能接管某些人类活动，或在智力上超越我们。而在于其信奉者可能建造成这样一个世界：每一片花瓣都被数过了，人们到头来才发现，我们把自己品尝樱桃的能力抽象掉了。

The real tragedy of computism is not just that computers could take over some human activities or exceed our intelligence. It’s that adherents could build a world in which every petal is counted, only to eventually realise that we abstracted away our ability to taste the cherries.
