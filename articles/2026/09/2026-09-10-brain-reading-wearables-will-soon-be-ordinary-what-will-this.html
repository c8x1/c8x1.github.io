读取大脑的可穿戴设备很快将变得稀松平常。这对新一代「神经技术原住民」意味着什么？

José M. Muñoz·The Conversation·2026年9月10日·4分钟阅读·原文

虽然听起来像科幻小说，但能读取大脑活动的耳塞已经上市销售。可穿戴技术公司 Neurable 声称，其配备脑电图（electroencephalography，简称 EEG）传感器的耳机可以追踪你的疲劳程度并减少职业倦怠。夜晚，NextSense 的 Smartbuds 捕捉大脑的电信号来记录你的睡眠；白天，同样的 EEG 传感器则帮助把高强度的任务安排在你最清醒的时段。

While it may sound like science fiction, earbuds that read brain activity are already on sale. Wearable technology company Neurable claims that its headphones, equipped with electroencephalography (EEG) sensors, can track your fatigue and reduce burnout. At night, NextSense’s Smartbuds pick up the electrical signals of your brain to chart your sleep. By day, the same EEG sensors help schedule demanding tasks for the hours when you are sharpest.

兴趣并不局限于专业公司。2023 年，苹果公司（Apple）为其带有电极、可测量大脑活动的 AirPods 提交了专利申请。鉴于已有数亿副耳塞在流通，苹果进军 EEG 技术将为真正广泛的、主流的普及铺平道路。

Interest is not just confined to specialist companies. In 2023, Apple filed a patent application for AirPods with electrodes that would measure brain activity. With hundreds of millions of earbuds already in circulation, Apple’s venture into EEG technology would pave the way for truly widespread, mainstream adoption.

这项技术虽然听起来充满未来感，外表却毫不起眼。没有发光的头戴设备，没有显眼的装置，也没有什么庄严宣告说大脑本身已成为一种接口。相反，这一转变如此安静、如此渐进，以至于人们很容易将其完全忽略。

While this technology may sound futuristic, it certainly doesn’t look it. There are no glowing headsets, no visible apparatus, and no solemn declaration that the brain itself has now become an interface. Instead, the shift is so quiet and gradual that it would be easy to miss altogether.

在不久的将来，一个十岁孩子拿到能感应大脑的耳塞时，不会把它们当作神经技术——它们只是用来睡觉、学习或专注的耳塞。有朝一日，这个孩子很可能成为某个工作场所的员工，而在那里，大脑监测已是一件稀松平常的事。

In the near future, a ten year old given brain-sensing earbuds won’t experience them as neurotechnology – they’ll just be earbuds they wear to sleep, study, or focus. One day, that child could well become an employee at a workplace where brain monitoring is unremarkable.

他们将属于在我所说的「神经技术转向」（neurotechnology shift）中成长起来的第一代人。

They will be part of the first generation to grow up within what I’ve called the neurotechnology shift.

神经技术正在离开它诞生的实验室和医院，逐步进入我们的办公室、街道和卧室。这一迁徙代表了一种新的技术境况——在这种境况下，大脑成了我们的生物自我与周围数字系统之间一个常规的接触点。

Neurotechnology is leaving the labs and hospitals where it was born, and settling into our offices, streets and bedrooms. This migration represents a new technological condition – one where the brain becomes a routine point of contact between our biological selves and the digital systems around us.

我的研究将此称为「大脑即接口」（brain-as-interface）时代。这并非某种推测性的预言，而是就在你阅读本文时正在现实中成形的事实。

My research has dubbed this the brain-as-interface era. This is not some speculative forecast, but a reality that is taking shape in the world as you read this.

有两项进展解释了为何这一转向正在此刻到来。第一项是人工智能，它使科学家和开发者能够从非侵入式记录中提取有用的大脑活动模式。这包括由廉价可穿戴传感器产生的信号——而过去这项任务曾需要专门的临床设备。

Two developments explain why this shift is arriving now. The first is artificial intelligence, which is allowing scientists and developers to extract useful patterns of mental activity from noninvasive recordings. This includes signals produced by cheap wearable sensors – a task that once demanded specialised clinical equipment.

第二项是大型科技公司对神经接口所下的赌注。例如，Meta 于 2025 年推出了一款腕带，它能读取肌肉活动的神经电信号以控制其新款眼镜，并有发表于《自然》（Nature）杂志的研究作为支撑。

The second is the bet that large technology firms are placing on neural interfaces. Meta, for instance, launched a wristband in 2025 that reads the neuroelectrical signals of muscle activity to control its new glasses, backed up by research it published in Nature.

神经感应正在被集成进数百万人已在使用的消费设备中。然而，神经技术转向与大脑即接口境况叠加在一起，引出了一个紧迫的问题：一个在这种环境中成长起来的心智会变成什么样？

Neural sensing is being integrated into the consumer devices that millions already use. But in combination, the ongoing neurotechnology shift and the brain-as-interface condition raise an urgent question: what happens to a mind that grows up inside all this?

Your brain for sale? The new frontier of neural data

当今的许多年轻人将是第一批在神经技术已成为日常生活一部分的世界中成长起来的人。我们可以把他们视为「神经技术原住民」（neurotechnological natives）。这一称呼呼应了「数字原住民」的概念，但它所标示的断裂，远比这种类比所暗示的更为深刻。

Many of today’s young people will be the first to grow up in a world where neurotechnology is simply part of daily life. We can think of them as “neurotechnological natives”. The term echoes that of digital natives, but it marks a sharper break than the parallel suggests.

迄今为止，数字技术始终是从外部塑造我们的心智。屏幕、平台和互联网改变了我们关注什么、如何记忆，以及我们彼此相处的方式，但它们始终位于我们与世界之间，中介着我们对世界的体验。神经技术则作用于这条边界的内侧。它测量、解码并调制大脑活动本身。这是质的差别，而非程度的差别。

Up to now, digital technologies have shaped our minds from the outside. Screens, platforms, and the internet changed what we pay attention to, how we remember, and the way we relate to one another, but they have always sat between us and the world, mediating our experience of it. Neurotechnology works on the inner side of that boundary. It measures, decodes, and modulates brain activity itself. That’s a difference in kind rather than degree.

「神经技术原住民」这一假说——我在一篇新近发表的文章中提出并加以发展——并非一种预言，也不主张某一代际将整齐划一地到来，而是一个分析工具，旨在帮助我们提出正确的研究问题。

The neurotechnological natives hypothesis – which I have proposed and developed in a newly published article – is not a forecast or a claim that a uniform generation is on its way, but an analytical tool for asking the right research questions.

「数字原住民」这一标签的意义常常被夸大，我对此有清醒认识，并在此基础上对该概念加以延伸。然而，一个未被命名的人群，研究和监管都将难以看见，更遑论应对。

The significance of the “digital native” label was often overstated, and my extension of the idea is presented in full awareness of that. However, a population nobody names is one that research and regulation will struggle to see, let alone address.

我所提出的研究议程，与神经技术转向一样建立在同样三个维度之上，每个维度都引出三个研究者几乎尚未着手探讨的问题。

The research agenda I propose rests on the same three dimensions as the neurotechnology shift, each of which raises three questions that researchers have barely begun to address.

第一个维度是技术科学维度。我们如何将接口可靠地从实验室和医院移入日常生活？随着人工智能与神经技术日益融合，会发生什么？当神经数据成为流经我们已在使用的生物识别和数字系统的又一条信息流时，它又会怎样？

The first dimension is technoscientific. How do we move interfaces reliably out of the labs and hospitals and into daily life? What happens as artificial intelligence and neurotechnology become more integrated? And what happens to neurodata once it becomes just another stream of information flowing through the biometric and digital systems we already use?

第二个、也是影响深远的维度是认知维度。与这些设备的持续互动，对发展中自我意识和个人身份认同会造成什么影响？当被监测——甚至被监控——的是你的大脑状态而非你的行为时，自我调节和冲动控制会发生怎样的变化？还有一个不那么显眼却最引人注目的问题：内心言语通常是通过将与他人之间的口头交流内化而发展起来的，那么在伴随语音解码和无声交流工具长大的孩子身上，内心言语会如何成形？

The second, highly consequential dimension is cognitive. What does sustained interaction with these devices do to a developing sense of self and personal identity? How do self-regulation and impulse control change when the thing being monitored – or even surveilled – is your brain state rather than your behaviour? And less obvious but most striking: how does inner speech – which normally develops by internalising spoken exchange with others – take shape in children raised with speech-decoding and silent communication tools?

第三个维度是社会文化维度。应由什么样的规范和制度来治理这一切？谁能获得使用机会，谁又被留在神经技术鸿沟的另一侧？当把大脑视为接口变得稀松平常之后，集体想象和叙事框架又会如何描绘大脑？

The third is sociocultural. What norms and institutions should govern all this? Who gains access, and who is left on the far side of a neurotechnology divide? And how do collective imaginaries and narrative frames picture the brain, once treating it as an interface becomes ordinary?

这三个研究维度并非孤立存在——它们相互交叉、相互影响。技术能力使认知层面的利害关系变得真实，并为制度提供了可供采纳的东西。我们对发展中心智的了解，会重塑工程师所建造之物和监管者所忧虑之事。社会辩论和监管压力则决定了哪些设备会被制造出来，又会触及谁的大脑。

These three research dimensions are not isolated – they intersect with and influence one another. Technical capacity makes the cognitive stakes real and gives institutions something to adopt. What we learn about the developing mind reshapes what engineers build and what regulators fear. Social debate and regulatory pressure decide which devices get made and whose brains they touch.

然而，法规与治理不能等到证据出现后再行动，因为产生证据的基础设施已在启动之中。在某些工作场所，例如采矿业，员工已经佩戴 EEG 设备来追踪自身的疲劳水平。

But regulations and governance cannot wait for the evidence to arrive, as the infrastructures that produce it are already being set in motion. In some workplaces, such as the mining industry, employees already wear EEG devices that track their fatigue levels.

任何不记得互联网出现之前那段时光的人，都可以被称为数字原住民。不久之后，同样的话也将适用于伴随神经技术长大的人。在这一新人群尚未完全存在之前就为其命名，意义重大——它使其对研究和监管变得可见，而关于这项新技术的关键问题，只有我们足够早地提出，才有可能得到回答。

Anyone who does not remember a time before the internet can be labelled a digital native. Before long, the same will be said for people who grow up using neurotech. Naming this new population before it fully exists is important – it makes it visible to research and regulation, and the vital questions about this new technology can only be answered if we ask them early enough.

半个世纪前，哲学家托马斯·内格尔（Thomas Nagel）通过追问「做一只蝙蝠是什么感觉？」来探索单一心智想象他者能力的局限。如今，这一问题正转向内在，转向那些在「大脑即接口」时代中成长起来的孩子。对于我们这些在没有它的时代中长大的人来说，我们需要提出的问题是这样的：做一个神经技术原住民，是什么感觉？

Half a century ago, the philosopher Thomas Nagel probed the limits of one mind’s ability to imagine another by asking “what is it like to be a bat?”. Today, this question is turning inward, to the children growing up in the brain-as-interface era. For those of us who grew up without it, the question we need to be asking is this: what is it like to be a neurotechnological native?
