每扇门上的脸

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年9月29日·4分钟阅读

九月末的一个晚上，刷纸巷只剩一家亮灯。石宽站在门口，肩上还带着路上的霜气，说明来意：他从下游的铁砂镇来，家里开着的铺子，管着全镇三百多户的年画。今年开春，一个跑船的客商在他铺子门口笑：你们全镇门上是一张脸，齐整得像座兵营。他把铺子一冬攒下的三十六块银元全带在身上，要买一张不一样的脸。「我来请门神，」他说，「已经贴满我们全镇的那张，不要。」华婶往灶里添了块柴：「坐下说。这条巷子如今只有一张脸可卖。」

Late in the ninth month, only one shop in Print Lane still showed a light. Shi Kuan stood in its doorway with the frost of the road still on his shoulders and explained himself. He had come from Tiesha, the town downriver; his family kept the shop that supplied all three hundred-odd households there with their New Year prints. That spring a boat-trading merchant had stood outside it and laughed, saying the whole town wore one face on its doors, neat as a barracks. So he had brought every coin the shop had saved through the winter, thirty-six silver dollars, to buy a different face. "I came to invite Door Gods," he said. "The kind already on every door of our town, I don't want." Aunt Hua fed a stick of wood to the stove. "Sit down, then. This lane has only one face left to sell."

「五十年前，这条巷子有八家作坊、八张脸，」华婶说。她那年五岁，记得火光是隔着窗纸红的。火是庙会前夜起的，烧过之后，八家的版还剩三家：德茂号的团脸版在对河的仓里——他家大，另有一进院子；荀家的立目版先一步上了庙会的车；骆家的歪眉版那年恰好送去邻县修裂。「烧掉的是房子和一屋子纸，」她说，「火不挑手艺。」

"Fifty years ago this lane had eight workshops and eight faces," Aunt Hua said. She had been five that year, and remembered the fire as red through the window paper. It started the night before the temple fair, and when it was out, three workshops still had their blocks: Demao's round-cheeked face was in the storeroom across the river — the firm was big enough to keep a second yard; the Xun family's wide-eyed face had gone ahead on the fair cart; the Luo family's slanted-brow face happened to be off in the next county for crack repair. "What burned was houses and a houseful of paper," she said. "Fire doesn't judge craftsmanship."

「后来呢？」石宽问。「后来三张脸分全县的门。年与年也不齐：谁家断了纸、谁家湿了版，谁家的脸那年就薄一层；多一年，少一年，没个准头。有一年骆家进了受潮的红纸，歪眉那年少了将近一半，隔了两年才缓回来。二十年前，骆家两个儿子都上了铁路，铺子改卖皇历，歪眉就断了根，老主顾大半挪去了德茂号——门上原来是什么脸，年下还买什么脸。又过了八年，我爹的立目版裂了。他重刻两回，眼力不济，两回都废在梨木上，开春人就走了。我卖了裂版当柴火，换回一张团脸版，印到今天。如今满县的门，年年是同一张脸。省城的刷纸街上有百十家作坊，塌一家，满城的门照旧五花八门；这条巷子剩到三家的时候，一家改行，全县三分之一的门一夜换主。巷子越小，风越掀得动。」

"And after that?" Shi Kuan asked. "After that, three faces split the whole county's doors. No two years matched: if a shop's paper ran out or its blocks got damp, its face wore thinner that year; fat one year, thin the next, no telling. One year the Luos took in a batch of damp red paper, and the slanted brow lost nearly half its doors; it took two more years to climb back. Twenty years ago the Luos' two sons went off to the railway, the shop turned to selling almanacs, and the slanted brow was finished. Most of its old customers moved over to Demao — whatever face a door already wore was what it bought again at New Year. Eight years after that, my father's wide-eyed block cracked. He tried to recut it twice; his eyes weren't up to it, and both attempts were wasted on pearwood. By spring he was gone. I sold the cracked block for firewood, bought a round-cheeked block in its place, and I've printed it ever since. Now every door in this county wears the same face, year after year. In the provincial capital, they say, the print street holds a hundred-odd workshops; one of them collapses and the city's doors stay as mixed as ever. When this lane was down to three, a single shop quitting moved a third of the county's doors overnight. The smaller the lane, the harder any wind can tip it."

石宽不信：「团脸留到最后，总有它的道理吧？德茂号的刀……」「德茂号那张版，眉眼四角是糊的，」华婶说，「我爹生前说，那是块赶工的活。歪眉才是这巷子里最见功夫的刀。一个改了行，一个裂了版，跟刻得好不好不相干。」石宽沉默了一阵，说起祖父：石老大，五十年前德茂号柜上的学徒，大火后第二年，揣着一张团脸版去了铁砂镇。镇上从前不贴门神，祖父的版一开印，就再没有第二张脸进过那个镇子。如今三百多户门上的团脸，全是那块版的徒子徒孙，印了快五十年，眉骨都磨平了，镇上人管那张脸叫「老爷爷的脸」。「你爷爷带走的不是他挑的脸，」华婶说，「是他跟的那家铺子。」

Shi Kuan wouldn't have it. "There must be some sense in the round face lasting to the end. Demao's carving — " "The corners of Demao's eyes and brows run muddy," Aunt Hua said. "My father used to say it was rush work. The slanted brow was the finest knife this lane ever had. One quit the trade, one cracked its block — neither had anything to do with how well they were cut." Shi Kuan was quiet for a while, then talked about his grandfather: Shi Laoda, an apprentice at Demao's counter fifty years ago, who the year after the fire packed a round-cheeked block and left for Tiesha. The town had never pasted up Door Gods before; once his grandfather's block began printing, no second face ever entered that town. The round faces on three hundred-odd doors there now were all descendants of that one block, printed for almost fifty years, brow ridges worn flat; the townspeople called it "grandfather's face." "The face your grandfather carried off wasn't one he picked," Aunt Hua said. "It was the shop he happened to follow."

「立目还有救吗？」石宽问，「多少钱，你开个数。」「钱买不回来。版没了，脸还在我手里——我七岁起给我爹打格子，这张脸的每一刀我都过过手。可没有母版，全凭手上的记忆起稿，要三个月，腊月才上得了纸。我的眼力，够刻这一块的，再往后没有了。」她看着他，「回你的铁砂镇去吧。买回去一张脸，挂你自家门上；三百多户门上的脸，明年还是一张。」石宽站起来：「全镇换不换，随他们。纸墨我自己张罗，门我自己挨家跑。」华婶看了他半晌，进里屋拖出一方梨木——荀家留下的最后一块老料，她搁了十二年没敢动。「工钱不收，」她说，「这张脸我揣了十二年，再揣下去，就跟我一块儿进土了。」

"Is there any saving the wide-eyed face?" Shi Kuan asked. "Name a price." "Money won't bring it back. The block is gone, but the face is still in my hands — I laid out the grids for my father from the age of seven, and every cut of that face has passed through them. With no master block to copy, working from the hands' memory alone, it takes three months; the paper wouldn't see it before the twelfth month. As for my eyes, they are good for exactly one more block, and after it, for nothing." She looked at him. "Go home to Tiesha. You would buy a face and hang it on your own door; the faces on three hundred-odd doors would still be one face next year." Shi Kuan stood up. "Whether the whole town changes is not mine to command. I'll see to the paper and ink myself, and I'll walk the doors myself, house by house." Aunt Hua studied him for a long moment, then went inside and dragged out a slab of pearwood — the last of the Xun family's old stock, kept untouched for twelve years. "No charge for the work," she said. "I've carried this face for twelve years. Carry it much longer, and it goes into the ground with me."

腊月里，版成了。立目的眼睛在梨木上睁开来，华婶把刻刀收进了布套——那是她这辈子最后一块版。纸还印得动，看大不看小了。石宽把三十六块银元全数换成纸和朱砂，除夕前在铁砂镇挨家跑。二月里他回到刷纸巷，带来头一张印成的立目：「三百多户，换下来四十三户。有的说看看明年再讲，有的嫌它瞪人。」华婶把那张纸举到门口的天光里，看了一会儿。「我爹从前讲：一张脸活在多少扇门上，就有多大的命。落得多的难断根，落得少的一阵风就没。」她把纸还给他，「四十三户，比零多。」

In the twelfth month the block was finished. The wide eyes opened out of the pearwood, and Aunt Hua slipped her knife into its cloth sheath — the last block she would ever cut. She could still pull prints; she could see the large, no longer the small. Shi Kuan changed all thirty-six silver dollars into paper and cinnabar, and before New Year's Eve he walked the doors of Tiesha house by house. In the second month he came back to Print Lane carrying the first finished print. "Three hundred-odd doors; forty-three households took it. Some said they'd see about next year; some complained that it glares." Aunt Hua held the sheet up to the daylight at the door for a while. "My father used to say a face has as much life as the doors it lives on. The widely placed are hard to root out; the thinly placed go with one gust of wind." She handed the sheet back to him. "Forty-three doors. More than none."

注解：遗传漂变（genetic drift）说的是小地方的东西靠运气传手：一张脸落在多少门上，来年的门就往哪边偏，谁也说不准；落得多的难断根，落得少的一阵风就没，年深日久，满街只剩一张脸，剩下的那张未必最好，多半只是当时落在门上最多的一张。它只往少里走——故事末尾重刻的立目更像一次重新引入，往后是传开还是断根，仍交给同一场运气。

Note: Genetic drift is how things pass from hand to hand in a small place by luck alone: whichever face sits on more doors pulls the next year's doors its way, and no one can say which way; the widely placed become hard to root out, the thinly placed go with one gust, until at last a single face is all the street has, and the face left standing is seldom the finest — most often it is simply the one that already sat on the most doors. Drift only ever subtracts, never adds; the wide-eyed face recut at the end is closer to a reintroduction, and whether it spreads or is rooted out is once more in the hands of the same luck.
