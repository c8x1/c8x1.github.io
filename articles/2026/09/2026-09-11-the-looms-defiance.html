织机的叛逆

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年9月11日·2分钟阅读

我叫不出声。女儿烧了三天，药铺掌柜说三枚银角子，再晚就不必来了。可我是织工，织机归公会管，每匹绢上的纹样凡载入《通谱》（Codex）的，须缴纹银。纹银加药钱，我凑不齐。

I could not make a sound. My daughter had burned with fever for three days; the apothecary said three silver pieces, and if I came later, not to bother. But I was a weaver, and my loom stood in the guild's courtyard. Every pattern woven into silk that was recorded in the Codex required a pattern tax. The tax plus the medicine was more than I could gather.

验纹师纶伯（Lún Bo）坐在堂中，面前摊着那部厚得像门板的《通谱》。他说，天下凡是能上织机的纹样，每根经线从第一到末尾的颜色，都逐一载在册里。我报了七种配色，他翻了七次，每次都指着某页某行说：看，第几根经线什么色，全对上了。每一个我能想到的纹样，都早被人织过、登记、归了税。

The pattern examiner Lún Bo sat in the hall with the Codex open before him, thick as a door panel. He said every pattern that could go on a loom — every warp thread's color, from first to last — was recorded in its pages. I proposed seven color arrangements; he looked up seven times, each time pointing to a page and a line: look, this thread, this color, all matched. Every pattern I could think of had already been woven, registered, and taxed.

旁边一直坐着个不相识的女人，背上包袱，像是从远道来的。她开口说：给我笔。她翻开《通谱》第一页，看第一号纹样的第一根经线，写下一个不同的颜色。翻到第二号纹样，看第二根经线，又写下一个不同的颜色。第三号，第三根。第四号，第四根。一行一行下去，她写出的这条配色，是一副完完整整的纹样——每一根线的颜色都是公会认可的染料里有的。可它绝不可能在《通谱》里：它和第一号纹样在第一根线上不同，和第二号在第二根上不同，和第三号在第三根上不同……它和册里每一号纹样，至少对不上同一根线。

A woman had been sitting to one side, a stranger, a bundled pack on her back as if from a long road. She said: give me a brush. She opened the Codex to the first page, looked at the first thread of pattern number one, and wrote down a different color. She turned to pattern number two, looked at its second thread, and wrote down a different color. Pattern three, thread three. Pattern four, thread four. Down and down she went, and the color arrangement she wrote was a complete pattern — every thread's color was one the guild recognized, one from an existing dye. Yet it could not possibly be in the Codex: it differed from pattern one at thread one, from pattern two at thread two, from pattern three at thread three... It differed from every pattern in the book at no fewer than one thread.

纶伯的脸涨红了。他抢过那条配色，一页一页翻找。翻完了，从头再翻。我说不上自己心跳了几拍。他抬起头的时候，嘴唇是白的：「这纹样不在册里。」堂里安静了一瞬。纶伯说：那它一定不能织。我说：每一根线的颜色都是你认可的染料。他说：不在册里，就不能织。我说：可你说过册里什么都有。

Lún Bo's face went red. He snatched the color arrangement and searched page by page. When he reached the end, he started again from the beginning. I lost count of my own heartbeat. When he looked up, his lips were pale. "This pattern is not in the Codex." The hall fell silent for a moment. Lún Bo said: then it cannot be woven. I said: every thread's color comes from dyes you yourselves recognize. He said: if it is not in the Codex, it cannot be woven. I said: but you said the Codex contained everything.

那个女人把纸递给我。她说：这不只是这一部册子的事。哪怕他再写一部、再加一千页、再把天下所有的纹样从头登记一遍——你照样可以翻开新册，看第一号的第一根线，换一个颜色；第二号的第二根，换一个颜色……你永远能造出一条不在册里的纹样。她背上包袱走了，像来时一样没有多余的话。我攥着那张纸。织这匹绢，公会会砸了我的织机，逐我出门。不织，女儿的烧等不到天亮。

The woman handed me the paper. She said: this is not only about this one book. Even if he wrote another, added a thousand pages, registered every pattern in the world all over again — you could still open the new book, look at thread one of pattern one, change the color; thread two of pattern two, change the color... You would always be able to produce a pattern not in the book. She shouldered her pack and left, as spare of words as she had come. I gripped the paper. If I wove this silk, the guild would smash my loom and cast me out. If I did not, my daughter's fever would outlast the night.

半夜，我在自家织机前坐着。经线已经穿好了，每一根的颜色都照那张纸上的来。我把梭子掷过经面的时候，忽然明白了一件事：纶伯没有败在我手里，也没有败在那个女人手里。他败在一个事实手里——任何声称登记了一切纹样的册子，都逃不过那条逐行翻转的线。这不是我的力气，不是她的聪明，是那本册子本身的破绽。绢一寸一寸长出来，颜色是我从没见过的排法，可每一根都在规矩之内。我把绢卷好，夹在臂下，走进夜里的巷子，往药铺去。

At midnight I sat before my own loom. The warp threads were dressed, each one the color the paper specified. As I threw the shuttle across the warp, I understood something: Lún Bo had not been beaten by me, nor by that woman. He had been beaten by a fact — any book that claimed to register every pattern would fall to that thread of row-by-row reversals. It was not my strength, not her cleverness, but a flaw in the book itself. The silk grew inch by inch, colors in an arrangement I had never seen, yet every thread within the rules. I rolled the cloth, tucked it under my arm, and walked out into the night lanes, toward the apothecary.

注解：这是康托尔对角线论证（Cantor's diagonal argument）：对任何声称已穷举某集合的列表，逐行取第 n 项的第 n 位翻转，就能造出一个属于该集合却不在表中的元素；因此实数集不可数，存在比自然数更大的无穷。同一技巧也支撑着哥德尔不完备定理、图灵停机问题与罗素悖论。

Note: This is Cantor's diagonal argument: for any list claiming to enumerate a set completely, take the nth position of the nth entry and flip it, and you produce an element that belongs to the set yet is not on the list; therefore the real numbers are uncountable — there exist infinities larger than the natural numbers. The same technique underpins Gödel's incompleteness theorems, Turing's halting problem, and Russell's paradox.
