哈贝马斯的真理观意味着他终会改变主意

Emilie Prattico·Aeon·2026年9月29日·10分钟阅读·原文

尤尔根·哈贝马斯（Jürgen Habermas）于2026年3月14日在德国小镇施塔恩贝格（Starnberg）去世，享年96岁。从1990年代中期到2000年代后期，每年秋天他都会来伊利诺伊州埃文斯顿（Evanston）的西北大学（Northwestern University）授课演讲，一群忠实的学生每个秋季学期都盼着这次到访。留在我记忆里的是2004年，那一年他讲授的是他在《Between Facts and Norms》（1992）中阐发的内容——那是把他的理论应用于民主政治的代表作。法学、哲学、历史学、政治学：不同学科的学生每周聚到一起，来看这位著名的德国学者——他穿着Wrangler牛仔裤和粗花呢夹克——用他特有的鼻音、浓重的德国口音和无懈可击的英语，阐发他的商议政治理论。马克斯·韦伯（Max Weber）和法兰克福学派（the Frankfurt School）于他而言是至为亲近的东西——1950年代他曾是特奥多·阿多诺（Theodor Adorno）的研究助理——这两者在讲述中活了起来，随着他的理论推演，又化作黑板上粉笔画出的图式与图形。

Jürgen Habermas died on 14 March 2026, in the German town of Starnberg, aged 96. Every fall, from the mid-1990s into the late-2000s, he would come to Northwestern University in Evanston, Illinois to teach and give talks, a visit that a dedicated group of students looked forward to every fall term. The year that has stayed with me is 2004, when he taught what he’d spelled out in Between Facts and Norms (1992), his opus applying his theory to democratic politics. Law, philosophy, history, political science: students spanned many disciplines to come watch the famous German scholar every week, dressed in Wrangler jeans and a tweed jacket, expose his theory of deliberative politics in his unmistakable nasal voice, thick German accent, and impeccable English. Max Weber and the Frankfurt School, which were intimate to him, since he’d been Theodor Adorno’s research assistant in the 1950s, came to life in his speech and then in schemas and figures in chalk as he theorised about them.

起初我几乎什么也没听懂：我读过的文字在教室里活了起来，由它们的作者亲口说出，却仍然汇聚不成意义。几次课之后，轮廓清晰起来：这不只是《Between Facts and Norms》在公开场合的重构，而是数十年的工作汇聚到同一个地方。自《The Theory of Communicative Action》（1981）以来，哈贝马斯一直主张，理性在现代生活中只能作为人们共同去做的事情而存续——也就是说，靠彼此给出理由，并保持被理由打动的开放，而不是靠强力、金钱或传统。《Between Facts and Norms》正是把这一思想直接推向法律与民主：一个国家，只有当其法律原则上能够通过恰恰那种公开的交流、向所有受其约束的人证成自身时，才具有合法性。我看着这个论证在数十年的思想史中扎下根来。思考是一种实践，它意味着我们彼此亏欠着解释、提问与回答。这不是一个快的过程，而把它公开地、大声地、当着一屋子人的面做出来，恰恰是它值得做的地方。

I understood almost none of it at first: words I’d read were coming to life in the room, spoken by their author, and still not resolving into sense. After several lectures, the shape came into focus: this wasn’t just Between Facts and Norms being rebuilt in public, but decades of work arriving at a single place. Since The Theory of Communicative Action (1981), Habermas had been arguing that reason survives in modern life only as something people do together, that is, by giving each other reasons and staying open to being moved by them, rather than by force, money or tradition. Between Facts and Norms was that idea turned toward law and democracy directly: a state counts as legitimate only when its laws could in principle be justified to everyone bound by them, through exactly that kind of open exchange. I’d watched that argument take root in a decades-long intellectual history. Thinking is a practice, it involves owing each other explanations, questions, answers. It is not a fast process, and doing it in public, out loud, in front of a room, is what makes it worthwhile.

在西北大学的那些年，我怀着极大的兴趣、坦白说还有几分敬畏，参加了哈贝马斯所有的研讨课。有一天，每周研讨课的主题是包容，我带着一个青涩研究生怯生生的笃定，主张商议性公共领域应当为包容而设计，而进入这些公共领域，要求成员自己接受商议的各项条件。「嗯，看得出来，普拉蒂科小姐（Ms Prattico）确实是个严格普世论意义上的天主教徒」——这不是我料想的回应；而让我意外的，不是他拿我的宗教开了玩笑，而是他把我看成了一个有来历、有一套未经检视的信念的人——这一点，他只有通过教我才能注意到。

I attended all of Habermas’s seminars during my time at Northwestern with great interest and, to be honest, a sense of awe. One day, the topic of the weekly seminar was inclusion, and I had argued, with the timid conviction of a green graduate student, that deliberative publics ought to be designed for inclusion, and that entry into these publics required that members accept the terms of deliberation themselves. ‘Well, we can tell that Ms Prattico really is a Catholic in the strict, universalist sense,’ was not the answer I expected – and what took me by surprise wasn’t that he’d made a joke of my religion, but that he’d seen me as a person with a history and a set of unexamined beliefs, something he’d noticed only by teaching me.

研讨课之后，全班有时会一起出去喝一杯。正是在一次这样的场合，我明白了那个玩笑的来路。我问他，1967年秋天第一次以访问教授身份抵达美国、进入社会研究新学院（the New School for Social Research）是什么情形——几十年前，那所学校曾是学者们逃离德国的避难所，而他们逃离的，正是他成长的那个德国。那是他横渡大西洋这一漫长习惯的起点：四年后是普林斯顿，他在高斯讲座（the Gauss Lectures）上首次尝试了那场朝向语言的探索，它日后成了他的交往行为理论；再往后几十年，便是西北大学——我因此得以坐在那间教室里。

After the seminars, the class would sometimes go out for drinks. It was on one of these occasions that I understood where the joke had come from. I’d asked him what it had been like arriving in the US as a visiting professor for the first time, at the New School for Social Research in the autumn of 1967, which decades earlier had served as a refuge for scholars fleeing the same Germany in which he’d grown up. It was the start of a long habit of crossing the Atlantic: Princeton four years later, for the Gauss Lectures where he first tried out the turn toward language that would eventually become his theory of communicative action; then, decades on, Northwestern, where I got to sit in the room.

哈贝马斯回答我的问题时，追溯得比这一切都更远。他在路德宗的家庭里长大。1960和1970年代访美期间，他接触到了贵格会（Quakers），他们那种小型聚会的某种东西留在了他心里，尤其是他们与在场所有人寻求合一的方式。当他后来着手刻画人们作为平等者共同推理的条件时，他说自己听到了那些聚会的回声：这些条件，只有在每一个可能受某项规范影响的人不只是被纳入、而且拥有发言权、可以提出异议、并且真诚地发言时，才成立。在这些远超单纯包容的条件下，话语剩下的唯一约束，就是他在《Justification and Application》（1993）里所说的「更好论证之无强制的强制力」。这不像他会在讲台上说出的话。

Habermas answered my question by going back further than any of that. He’d been raised Lutheran. Visiting the US in the 1960s and ’70s, he’d been introduced to the Quakers, and something in their small meetings had stayed with him, specifically the way they sought unity with everyone present. When he came to describe the conditions under which people could reason together as equals, he said he heard an echo of those meetings: conditions that hold only when everyone who could be affected by a norm isn’t just included but has a voice, can object, and speaks with sincerity. In those conditions, which go far beyond mere inclusion, the only constraint left on discourse is ‘the unforced force of the better argument’, as he put it in Justification and Application (1993). It didn’t feel like something he’d have said at the podium.

《The Structural Transformation of the Public Sphere》（1962）是他最初以德文出版的奠基之作，它论证的是一件具体的事：18世纪的咖啡馆、沙龙和期刊——在那里，私人公民开始把国家事务当作自己有权公开讨论和评判的东西，而非仅仅服从的对象。哈贝马斯认为，正是这种习惯，让现代民主的合法性第一次扎下根。这本书也指出了会削弱它的种种倾向。他认为公共领域那时已经开始衰败：被广告和大众媒介折回到景观之中，公众从参与者沦为观众。英译本花了将近30年才问世。到1989年它终于出版时，哈贝马斯已经让这本书在原文里发挥出了巨大的效用；事实上，它早已使他在德国之外有了名声。当克雷格·卡尔霍恩（Craig Calhoun）最终把英语世界的回应辑为一册《Habermas and the Public Sphere》（1992），汇集起挑战这一论证的历史学家与理论家，并以哈贝马斯本人的一篇长文回应收尾时，他本可以轻轻松松把这个场合用来为一本已被证明站得住的书辩护。可他没有，他非常公开地与这些批评周旋交锋。

The Structural Transformation of the Public Sphere (1962), his seminal work originally published in German, made an argument about something concrete: 18th-century coffeehouses, salons and journals, where private citizens began treating the affairs of state as something they had standing to publicly discuss and judge, rather than simply obey. That habit, Habermas argued, was where modern democratic legitimacy first took root. The book also identified tendencies that would weaken it, too. He thought that the public sphere had already started to decay, folded back into spectacle by advertising and mass media, the public reduced from participants to an audience. It took almost 30 years for an English translation to appear. By the time it did, in 1989, Habermas had gotten a great deal of mileage out of the book in its original language; indeed, it had already made him a name outside Germany. When Craig Calhoun finally gathered the English-language response into a single volume, Habermas and the Public Sphere (1992), bringing together historians and theorists who challenged the argument, closing with a long response from Habermas himself, he could have easily spent the occasion defending a book that had already proved itself. Instead, he very openly contended with these critiques.

有两位批评者格外突出。南希·弗雷泽（Nancy Fraser）主张，公共领域其实从未对所有人开放过，因为它从一开始就把女性和其他群体排除在外。她提议用许多个公共领域取代单一公共领域，它们彼此争论，其中包括由恰恰被排斥在第一个公共领域之外的群体所形成的公共领域。杰夫·埃利（Geoff Eley）从历史中提出了一个相关的论点：工人阶级的和平民的公共领域，其实一直与资产阶级公共领域并行存在，既不在它之前，也不在它之后。两人都是在延伸奥斯卡·内格特（Oskar Negt）和亚历山大·克鲁格（Alexander Kluge）早在1972年提出的论点：存在着一整个无产阶级的公共领域，而哈贝马斯的书从未把它计入。

Two critics stand out. Nancy Fraser argued that the public sphere was never actually open to everyone since it had excluded women and other groups from the start. She proposed replacing one public with many, arguing with each other, including publics formed by the very groups shut out of the first one. Geoff Eley made a related point from history: working-class and plebeian public spheres had existed alongside the bourgeois one all along, not before or after it. Both were extending an argument that Oskar Negt and Alexander Kluge had made back in 1972, that a whole proletarian public sphere existed, which Habermas’s book never accounted for.

哈贝马斯在一篇题为〈再思公共领域〉（Further Reflections on the Public Sphere）的文章里直接回应了这些批评，该文作为收尾一章印在卡尔霍恩编的书中。哈贝马斯承认，对女性的排斥不是偶然的，而是从一开始就内嵌于资产阶级公共领域的运作方式之中。与对底层男性的排斥——那是一个阶级问题，原则上可以纠正——不同，他此时写道，对女性的排斥「对于政治公共领域一直是构成性的：不仅在于后者由男性支配是出于偶然，还在于它的结构及其与私人领域的关系，是以性别特定的方式被规定的」。哈贝马斯承认，在他当作基石的那个公共领域旁边，还存在着别的公共领域。他也软化了那记警告，但并未放弃它。他后来认为，自己对这段叙事中的一个部分——消费社会的早期萌动——过于怀疑了。但警告本身从未消失。六十年后，他仍在诊断一个被商业力量和结构性力量扭曲的公共领域，只不过如今罪魁祸首是平台和算法，而非广告与大众发行。

Habermas answered these criticisms directly, in an essay called ‘Further Reflections on the Public Sphere’, printed as the closing chapter of Calhoun’s book. Habermas conceded that the exclusion of women was not incidental but built into how the bourgeois public sphere had worked from the start. Unlike the exclusion of underprivileged men, which was a problem of class, and in principle correctable, the exclusion of women, he now wrote, ‘has been constitutive for the political public sphere not merely in that the latter has been dominated by men as a matter of contingency but also in that its structure and relation to the private sphere has been determined in a gender-specific fashion.’ Habermas conceded that other public spheres had existed alongside the one he had taken as foundational. And he softened the warning, without abandoning it. He came to think he’d been too sceptical about one piece of that story, the early stirrings of consumer society. But the warning itself never went away. Sixty years on, he was still diagnosing a public sphere being distorted by commercial and structural forces, only now the culprit was platforms and algorithms rather than advertising and mass circulation.

他并没有放弃一切。他仍然想把公共领域保留为衡量民主的一把尺子，哪怕它背后的历史比他最初呈现的要混乱。批评与他对批评的回答同在一本书里，前后相随地印在一起。哈贝马斯的观点任人挑战，也向我们所有人敞开，让我们得以亲见他的思想处于进行时。

He did not give up everything. He still wanted to keep the public sphere as something to measure democracies against, even if the history behind it was messier than he’d first made it look. The criticism and his answer to it sit in the same book, printed one after the other. Habermas’s views were open to being challenged, and open for us all to witness his thought in process.

博士论文工作早期，我想借助哈贝马斯的商议政治理论探讨专家在民主中的角色，却还不知道该怎么做——无论按什么标准衡量，我都卡住了。我的导师克里斯蒂娜·拉丰（Cristina Lafont）是他最信任的学生之一，她还是安排了一次会面，就在他到访埃文斯顿西北大学校园期间下榻的酒店。我到场时没有一个成型的论证，他似乎并不介意。在当时的家园酒店（the Homestead Hotel）里，将近两个小时，他让我把问题半成品式地摊开，然后自己开始出声地梳理，一路用自己的理论加以检验。他讲解了刻画商议的三种话语类型——道德的、伦理的、实用的——并且明确说：在道德问题上没有专家，只有共同琢磨在特定情境中何为正当的个体。

Early in my dissertation work, I wanted to explore the role of experts in democracies through Habermas’s theory of deliberative politics, though I didn’t yet know how – I was, by any measure, stuck. My advisor, Cristina Lafont, one of his most trusted students, arranged a meeting anyway, at the hotel where he stayed during his visits to Northwestern’s campus in Evanston. He didn’t seem to mind that I arrived without a fully formed argument. Over almost two hours at what was then the Homestead Hotel, he had me lay out the problem as it stood, half-formed, and began working through it himself, out loud, testing it against his own theory as he went. He explained the three types of discourse that characterise deliberation – moral, ethical, and pragmatic – and was clear that when it comes to the moral, there are no experts: only individuals working out, together, what’s right in a given context.

照此推到尽头，专业知识在商议政治中似乎毫无立足之地。但他接着转向了实用话语，在那里专业知识自有实实在在的用场：为已经商定的目标找出经验上最可靠的实现路径。专家对何为正当、何为公平、何为可贵没有权威——只对事实有权威。这其实是他自早期关于科学与政治的研究以来一直在做的论证的缩影：把负载价值的政治问题当作纯技术问题来处理，会腐蚀民主。数十年后，在《Justification and Application》中，他把那天早晨以三分法道出的同一层忧虑，锻造出了更锋利的哲学形状。

Followed to its conclusion, that seemed to leave no room for expertise anywhere in deliberative politics. But he then turned to pragmatic discourse, where expertise does real work: finding the most empirically sound way to reach a goal already agreed on. Experts have no authority over what’s right, fair or worth valuing – only over facts. This was, in miniature, an argument he’d been making since his early work on science and politics: that treating value-laden political questions as though they were merely technical ones corrodes democracy. Decades later, in Justification and Application, he’d given the same worry he’d laid out in the trichotomy that morning sharper philosophical shape.

后来在他办公室的会面——比第一次例行得多——我把这个区分推得更远：它对气候变化意味着什么？用他的术语说，这个问题几乎总被当作实用话语的事务，全是图表、目标和碳预算，几乎从不被当作道德问题。谁来承担代价？我们亏欠承担代价的人什么？酿成损害的资源该如何使用？他没有现成的答案。他一个问题接一个问题地问，把这个案例对着他自己的框架反复检验，仿佛这个情形真的从未在他脑子里出现过。

In later meetings in his office, more routine than that first one, I pushed the distinction further: what did it mean for climate change – a problem almost always treated, in his terms, as a matter of pragmatic discourse, all graphs and targets and carbon budgets, and almost never as a moral one. Who pays? What do we owe the people who pay? How should the resources that caused the damage be used? He didn’t have a ready answer. He asked question after question, testing the case against his own framework as though it genuinely hadn’t occurred to him before.

我永远无法确知，他是在宽厚待人，还是我真的让他看到了自己在运用他那个区分时尚未想到的东西。留在我心里的不是答案，而是作答的姿态：一位年逾七旬的哲学家，在一个定型已久的框架里耕耘数十年，仍然愿意当着一个学生的面，实时地、出声地，用一个他没有考虑过的案例去检验那个框架。

I’ll never know for sure if he was being generous or if I had shown him something he had not yet thought of in applying his own distinction. What stayed with me wasn’t the answer but the manner of it: a philosopher in his 70s, decades into a settled framework, still willing to test that framework against a case he hadn’t considered, in real time, out loud, in front of a student.

到1999年，哈贝马斯放弃了自1973年起持有的立场——那一年他主张，真理不过是人们在足够公平的条件下把问题辩个水落石出之后所会同意的东西。到世纪末，他认定这是个错误：它把真理与证成混为一谈。一个断言可以通过我们现有的一切检验，挺过任何人提出的任何反驳，达到一个断言所能企及的证成程度，结果却仍可能为假。这是因为证成只对我们此刻恰好持有的标准负责，而这些标准日后可能被证明是错的。我们的标准移动时，真理并不随之移动。而道德断言是另一回事，他仍然这么认为：正当的，永远只能是公平的对话会认定下来的东西，正当性之外再无可交代的对象。但关于世界的事实，向世界本身交代，而不只向我们眼下关于它的最佳共识交代。

By 1999, Habermas gave up a position he’d held since 1973, the year he’d argued that truth is simply whatever people would agree on if they argued it out under fair-enough conditions. By the end of the century, he’d decided this was a mistake: it confused truth with justification. A claim can meet every test we have for it, survive every objection anyone raises, be about as justified as a claim can be, and still turn out false. That’s because justification answers only to the standards we happen to hold right now, and those standards can later be shown wrong. Truth doesn’t move when our standards do. Moral claims were different, he still thought: what’s right can only ever be what a fair conversation would settle on. There’s nothing else for rightness to answer to. But facts about the world answer to the world itself, not just to our best current agreement about it.

有几个人一直在把他推向这个转变。美国哲学家理查德·罗蒂（Richard Rorty）从哈贝马斯此刻正要退离的那一侧推：在罗蒂看来，真理无须向听众能被说服同意的东西之外的一切负责，这一点他已向哈贝马斯主张多年。我的导师拉丰——安排那次酒店会面的人——从另一侧推：她援引美国哲学家希拉里·普特南（Hilary Putnam），而普特南大半职业生涯所反驳的，恰恰是那种让真理完全系于人类共识的观点。拉丰自1994年起就论证：哈贝马斯自己的哲学传统，在其语言转向中已经漂移到一个地步，以至于说不出「无论我们怎样谈论世界，世界都岿然不动」。哈贝马斯改变主意时，走向的是拉丰的立场，而不是罗蒂的。

Several people had been pushing him toward this change. The US philosopher Richard Rorty pushed from the side from which Habermas was now backing away: for Rorty, there was no need for truth to answer to anything beyond what an audience could be talked into agreeing on, and he’d been making that case to Habermas for years. Lafont, my advisor, who’d arranged the hotel meeting, pushed from the other side, drawing on the US philosopher Hilary Putnam, who had spent much of his career arguing against exactly the kind of view that made truth depend entirely on human agreement. Lafont had argued since 1994 that Habermas’s own philosophical tradition, in its turn toward language, had drifted into a position where it couldn’t say the world holds firm no matter how we talk about it. When Habermas changed his mind, he moved toward Lafont’s position, not Rorty’s.

哈贝马斯从未说清的是：一旦人们完全无法达成一致，这对道德断言与政治断言意味着什么。在他修正后的看法里，即便所有人都停止交谈，真理仍有一个可去的地方：世界不在乎有没有人在听。而他的正当性哲学从来不曾享有这个选项。于是，当那些本该就某事达成一致的人分裂成彼此不再交谈的群体时，一个事实断言无论如何仍可真可假。而一个道德断言，一旦房间里不再剩下去同意它的人，就再没有任何东西托着它了。

What Habermas never spelled out is what this does to moral and political claims once people stop being able to agree at all. Truth, on his revised view, still has somewhere to go even if everyone stops talking: the world doesn’t care whether anyone’s listening. His philosophy of Rightness never had that option. So, when the people who’d need to agree on something splinter into groups that no longer talk to each other, a factual claim can still be true or false regardless. A moral claim has nothing left holding it up, once nobody’s left in the room to agree on it.

哈贝马斯放弃了坚持近30年的立场，因为身边的人让他看到它站不住。他花了几十年论证：断言之所以得到兑现，靠的是有人能迫使你作答；然后他自己被迫使着作答，并且答了——以白纸黑字，在70岁。那些批评并不是意在致命的攻击：它们恰恰是他的理论所召唤的东西，归根结底，也是这理论赖以奠基的东西。

Habermas gave up a position he’d held for almost 30 years because the people around him showed him it didn’t hold. He’d spent decades arguing that claims get redeemed when someone can compel you to answer, and then was compelled himself, and answered, in print, aged 70. Those critiques were not meant to be damning objections: they were precisely what his theory called for and was built on, ultimately.

哈贝马斯在〈对政治公共领域进一步结构转型的反思与假说〉（Reflections and Hypotheses on a Further Structural Transformation of the Political Public Sphere，2022）中回到了这个主题。他的出发点，是信念与现实之间的一种特定关系。宪政民主的运转，靠的是公民对自己的分歧抱有某些预设：分歧是真实的，分歧关乎某种东西，更好的理由原则上能够了结它们。只有当这些预设与政治的实际运作方式保持相当接近时，这个体系才维系得住。他的论证是：一个被社交媒体贯穿的公共领域，正在威胁这层关系，并把那道缝隙越拉越大。

Habermas returned to the subject in ‘Reflections and Hypotheses on a Further Structural Transformation of the Political Public Sphere’ (2022). His starting point is a particular relationship between beliefs and reality. Constitutional democracy runs on citizens holding certain assumptions about their own disagreements, namely that the disagreements are real, that they’re about something, that better reasons could in principle settle them. The system holds together only as long as those assumptions stay reasonably close to how politics actually works. His argument is that a public sphere, run through social media, threatens that relationship and widens that gap.

在哈贝马斯看来，有两股力量在这道缝隙的扩大中起作用。平台撤掉了报纸和广播电视机构曾经执行的编辑过滤，又把每个用户都变成发布者。对他来说，更深的问题不是由此而来的喧嚣，而是对平台的重度使用制造出比公众更小的东西：半公共领域，自我封闭的孤岛，其成员再也无法把更广阔的公共领域感知为包容的——用他的话说，越过自身视野的普世性主张变得「有伪善之嫌」。在他的叙述里，这种感知本身就是损害，其分量不亚于大公共领域中实际发生的任何事。文末他坚称：「维持一种确保公共领域之包容性、确保公共意见与政治意志形成之商议性的媒介结构，不是一个政治偏好问题，而是一项宪制命令。」

For Habermas, there are two forces at play in this widening. Platforms drop the editorial filtering that newspapers and broadcasters used to perform, and they turn every user into someone who publishes. For Habermas the deeper problem isn’t the resulting noise; it’s that heavy platform use produces something smaller than a public: semi-public spheres, self-enclosed, silos whose members can no longer perceive the wider public sphere as inclusive at all – claims to universality beyond their own horizon become, in his words, ‘suspect of hypocrisy’. That perception is itself the damage, on his account, as much as anything actually happening out in the general public sphere. He ends by insisting that ‘maintaining a media structure that ensures the inclusive character of the public sphere and the deliberative character of the formation of public opinion and political will is not a matter of political preference but a constitutional imperative.’

一个事实断言仍须把世界弄对，无论旁边有没有人核查。道德断言从来无须如此：它们只向话语交代。而话语，恰恰就是他刚刚描述为正在瓦解的东西。他没有亲自为道德断言和政治断言引出这个结论，但把他两本书并排放在一起，结论便自动浮现：一个关于我们彼此亏欠什么的断言所必须交代的那个东西，正是他说正在消失的东西——在一个由社交媒体上的往来交流所主导的时代。

A factual claim still has to get the world right, whether or not anyone’s around to check it. Moral claims never did: they answer only to discourse. And discourse is exactly what he’s just described coming apart. He doesn’t draw the conclusion for moral and political claims himself, but it follows from putting his two books next to each other: the very thing a claim about what we owe each other had to answer to is the thing he says is disappearing, in an age dominated by exchanges on social media.

大约在那前后，我找导师帮忙，请她代为向哈贝马斯邀一篇序言，给我正在编纂的一部哈贝马斯与民主文集。他以严密看守自己的时间著称——极少有人握有他的电子邮箱，而每年数百封此类请求里，他只应允寥寥数封。几个月后，一个我不认识的地址来信了：他答应了，并亲自写信告知。在交代事务性细节之余，他附上一个条件：「你得至少把书的一个实质性部分寄给我，我才能、也才会坐下来敲定一些想法。」92岁的他，要在承诺任何事之前，先把材料攥在手里。

Around that time, I reached out to my advisor for help asking Habermas for a foreword to a collection I was editing on Habermas and democracy. He was known for guarding his time closely – very few people had his email address, and he accepted only a handful of the hundreds of such requests he received each year. A couple of months later, an email came from an address I didn’t recognise: he had accepted, and written to say so himself. Amid the logistics, he added one condition: ‘You should send me at least a substantive part of the book before I can and would sit down and settle a few ideas.’ At 92, he wanted the material in hand before he’d commit to anything.

回来的东西，并不是对构成这本书的各篇访谈的逐篇评点。他自己在开篇几句里就说了：直接评论这些对话不是他的任务。他反而借这个场合，回应了针对其商议民主观的一贯质疑，并以数字化转型收尾——这个主题恰好贯穿了书中几乎每一篇访谈，是他自己察觉、并点名为整部文集核心的。有一节谈专家、常识，以及把专门知识输送进公共推理的那条翻译链条：正是他当年在酒店早餐桌上初次勾画的那个区分，如今在他工作生涯的尽头，被远为细致地铺展开来。

What came back was not a review of the interviews that make up the book one by one. He says so himself in the opening lines: it isn’t his task to comment on the conversations directly. Instead, he used the occasion to answer the standing objections to his view of deliberative democracy, and to close with the digital transformation that turned out to run through nearly every interview in the book – a theme he noticed himself, and named as central to the collection. One section was on experts, common sense, and the chain of translation that carries specialised knowledge into public reasoning: the same distinction he’d first sketched over a hotel breakfast, worked out now in far more detail, at the very end of his working life.

有一处他写道，在如今正在倒退的各民主国家里，失去的是「一种仍可辨认的意见冲突——一场关于哪种立场得到更好理由支持的争辩」。这是他在任何直接为我写下的文字里，最接近用他自己的话道出我的前提的一次。在序言后文里，他再次点出同一种丧失，描写那些退守进早已与自己一致的人群的人：他们失去的不仅是公共领域的包容性，还有真假共同标准的观念。而他在这里没有做的——正如1999年时没有做的那样——是当支撑一个道德断言的话语分崩离析之后，继续追问这种丧失对它意味着什么。而这，正是我自博士论文以来在自己的哲学工作中试图探究的东西。

At one point, he writes that what’s been lost, in the democracies now regressing, is ‘a conflict of opinions that remains recognisable as a dispute about which position is backed by the better reasons.’ That’s as close as he ever came, in anything he wrote for me directly, to stating my own premise in his own words. He names the same loss again later in the foreword, describing people who retreat into whichever group already agrees with them and lose their grip not just on the public sphere’s inclusiveness but on the idea of a shared standard of true and false. What he doesn’t do here, any more than he did in 1999, is follow that loss when it concerns a moral claim once the discourse behind it comes apart. And that is what I tried to explore in my philosophical work since my dissertation.

他仍然相信，专家与外行之间的鸿沟，原则上总能被一条恰当的翻译链条弥合。这是他唯一从未修正的东西：他修正过公共领域理论，修正过真理理论，却从未修正那个信念——在合适的条件下，人们仍能抵达彼此。我不认为这些条件在今天有任何接近满足的迹象——但这分歧，我永远没有机会与他进行了。

He still believed the gap between expert and layperson could always, in principle, be closed by the right chain of translation. It was the one thing he never revised: he revised his theory of the public sphere, his theory of truth, but never the conviction that under the right conditions people can still reach each other. I am not persuaded that these conditions are as anywhere close to being met today – but that’s a disagreement I never got to have with him.
