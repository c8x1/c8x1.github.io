一句笨话

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年9月20日·3分钟阅读

顾家二郎提亲的信随班船到了，回信也得赶明早的船走。宋姨捧着信进了桂婶的铺子，把纸页摊在灯下，一笔小楷写得又圆又稳。「你听听这句，」她念道，「『自埠头一见，三月不知米味。』这样的词，装是装不出来的。」桂婶把信从头看到尾，又折了回去。「宋姨，这话你讲反了。真心的人写得出这样的信，我认。可你倒过来讲『写得出，所以真心』，那就是另一回事。我经手的信，一年里写得漂亮的有百来封，真心的，两只手数得过来。惯写的男人一年到头都在写，给阿禾一封，沿河有姑娘的人家也各有一封。你手里这封，和别的几十封出自同一个模子。」

The betrothal letter from the Gu family's second son had come in with the packet boat, and the answer had to go out on the morning boat. Aunt Song carried it into Aunt Gui's shop and spread the page under the lamp; the small regular script was round and even. "Listen to this," she read, "'Since I saw her at the wharf, rice has lost its taste for three months.' No one fakes words like that." Aunt Gui read the letter from beginning to end, then folded it back. "Aunt Song, you have it backwards. That a sincere man can write such a letter — that much I grant. But turning it around to 'he wrote it, therefore he is sincere' is another matter. Of the letters that pass through my hands, a hundred a year are beautifully written; the sincere ones I could count on two hands. The men who write from habit write all year long — one to your A-He, and one to every household along the river with a daughter of age. The letter in your hand came out of the same mold as a few dozen others."

宋姨的脸沉了下来。「你是因为大郎。当年那门亲你押了名，赔了双倍，招牌摘了三个月，如今看死顾家满门。」桂婶给她续了茶，不躲这话。「秤上坠着铁，是真的。顾家的亲事到我手里，我这杆秤先往沉里坠，这我认。可你的秤也不空——你和顾家娘子自小换着衣裳穿，你的秤先往轻里坠。同一封信，落在两杆秤上，读出的斤两不一样。秤不一样，不等于没有准头；信，还得一句一句看。」

Aunt Song's face darkened. "This is about the elder brother. You staked your seal on that match, paid back double, took your signboard down for three months — and now you have written off the whole Gu family." Aunt Gui refilled her tea and did not dodge it. "There is iron hanging on my scale, that is true. When a Gu proposal reaches my hands, my scale tips heavy before I have read a word; I own that. But yours is not empty either — you and Madam Gu traded dresses as girls, so yours tips light. The same letter set on two different scales reads off two different weights. That the scales differ doesn't mean there's no true reading; a letter still gets weighed line by line."

桂婶把信重新摊开，指腹停在一行上。「旁的都是好话，好话是刻出来的模子，家家能用。独独这一句：『那日在埠头，见阿禾把最后半块饼掰给拦路的老黄狗。我想，肯这样待一条狗的人，待人必不会差。』宋姨，这话说得笨，也不讨巧。惯写的男人想不到往这儿编，他们练熟的都是姑娘家爱听的话，谁肯去编一条老狗？这句笨话，模子里倒不出来。它旁的姑娘用不上——看了多半要皱眉。」宋姨怔住了。「那黄狗……上月阿禾真喂过它半块饼。就一回，没旁人看见。」

Aunt Gui spread the letter open again and rested her fingertip on one line. "The rest is fine talk, and fine talk is a carved mold; any household can use it. This sentence stands alone: 'That day at the wharf I saw A-He break her last half piece of bread for the old yellow dog that blocks the path. I thought, a person who will treat a dog that way will not treat people badly.' Aunt Song, the sentence is clumsy and it flatters nothing. A man who writes from habit would not think to counterfeit here — what he has practiced are the words girls love to hear; who would go to the trouble of inventing an old dog? This clumsy line cannot be poured out of a mold. It is of no use to any other girl — most, reading it, would frown." Aunt Song sat very still. "The yellow dog... last month A-He truly fed it half a piece of bread. Once. No one else saw."

宋姨把笔拿了起来。「你要不肯押，我自己写回信。船不等人。」桂婶按住纸，从柜台最里头取出那枚三年没动过的印，在印泥里转了半圈。「押。媒钱记十成，先收七成。」「为何？」「我称出几成，就先收几成。这条秤上，这封信七成；剩下三成，等亲事满一年你来补。若一年内生变，你拿信到行里来，我双倍赔，招牌摘三个月，从头再挣。」

Aunt Song picked up the brush. "If you will not stake your seal, I will write the answer myself. The boat waits for no one." Aunt Gui held the paper flat, took from the innermost shelf the seal that had not moved in three years, and turned it half a circle in the vermilion paste. "Staked. The fee is set at ten parts; I collect seven now." "Why seven?" "I collect what I have weighed. On this scale the letter weighs seven parts. The other three you bring when the match has held a year. If it breaks inside the year, take the letter to the guild — I pay back double, my signboard comes down for three months, and I build again from nothing."

回信写好，落了印。宋姨盯着那点朱红，又问：「万一看错了呢？」桂婶把印擦净，收好。「那就赔。这行手艺教人掂量，不教人打包票。」天没亮，班船的梆子敲过埠头，回信随船走了。

The reply was written and the seal set upon it. Staring at the dot of vermilion, Aunt Song asked one more question: "And if you have read it wrong?" Aunt Gui wiped the seal clean and put it away. "Then I pay. This trade teaches a person to weigh; it does not teach guarantees." Before daybreak the packet boat's clapper sounded down at the wharf, and the answer went out with it.

转年秋天，阿禾过了门。满一年那天，宋姨把欠下的三成媒钱送到铺子里。桂婶收了钱，拆开案头一封新到的信——沿河的提亲信，总也断不了。

The next autumn A-He crossed the threshold into the Gu household. On the day the year turned, Aunt Song brought the three remaining parts of the fee to the shop. Aunt Gui took the coins and slit open a new letter that had come in that morning — along the river, betrothal letters never stop coming for long.

注解：这里讲的是贝叶斯定理（Bayes' theorem）：新证据不清空旧判断，而是按「这件事为真时它会出现的分量」与「为假时它照样出现的分量」之比，把旧判断重新校一遍；同一封信在两杆秤上读数不同，说的正是起点判断不同，同一证据会落到不同结论。只是要坦白一条边界：它写的是理性人应当如何掂量；人心自动冒出来的常是另一套——直觉最容易丢的，恰是「满镇子这样的信还有几十封」那一步。

Note: This is Bayes' theorem: new evidence does not empty out an old judgment but re-weighs it by the ratio between how much the evidence appears when the thing is true and how much it appears anyway when it is false — and the same letter reading differently on two scales is the point that different starting judgments send the same evidence to different conclusions. One honest limit: the theorem is a rule for how a rational person ought to weigh; what springs up in a mind unasked is often another thing — and the step intuition drops first is "how many letters like this fill the town."
