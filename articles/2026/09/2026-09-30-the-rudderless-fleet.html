无舵的船队

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年9月30日·4分钟阅读

每年开春头一场涨水，石桥上头的人家就把糊了一冬的河灯放下去，一夜之间，河面上有了一支无舵的船队。灯是半片松木托一粒豆大的火，火外糊一圈油纸；没有舵，也没有人划，水往哪里送，就往哪里去。桥下三里，河水绕过长洲，分成南北两汊，到洲尾又合成一股。合流往下，是镇上年年看灯的地方：灯到这里从不摊满一河，倒像自己归置过似的，两三里水亮得稠，接着一段黑水，再往下又亮起来，一段亮一段黑，排到河湾尽头。鱼梁照着亮水下，亮到哪里，梁就扎到哪里；春鱼打上来，北汊南汊对半分，这也是老规矩。

On the night of the first spring freshet, the households above the stone bridge set out the river lamps they had pasted and dried all winter, and by dark the water carried a fleet without a single rudder. Each lamp was half a pine shaving bearing a bead of flame behind a collar of oiled paper; there was no rudder and no rower, and where the water sent it, there it went. Three li downstream the river split around the Long Shoal into a north channel and a south channel, and at the shoal's tail the two waters closed back into one. Below the junction was where the town watched, year after year. The lamps never spread thin over the whole river; they settled as if someone had tidied them, two or three li of water crowded with light, then a stretch of black, then light again, on down to the bend. The fish weirs were set wherever the light gathered, and the spring catch was halved between the north-channel folk and the south-channel folk, which was also an old rule.

单看一盏，谁也说不准它的去处。它顺水而下，晃着打着旋，末了在某处滩唇上停住；停在哪，连放它下水的主人来年也认不出自己那一盏。丰年放灯上千；油紧的年月，一炷香才点一盏，一盏一盏地顺下去，起初东一盏西一盏，像随手撒的。可不管快慢，攒到天亮，亮段还是那几段亮段，黑段还是那几段黑段，年年落在老地方。守灯屋的阿汀数得出亮段有几段，却从不敢说她盯着的下一盏会停在哪里；镇上放灯最久的老人，也一样。灯怎么晃，是灯自己的事，没有人教过它。

Watch one lamp alone, and nobody could call where it would end. It rode down with the current, swaying and turning, and stopped at last on some lip of shoal; a year on, even the hand that had set it afloat could not pick its own lamp out of the crowd. In fat years a thousand went down together; in lean years, when oil ran short, one lamp for every stick of incense, one and then one, and at first they looked like things flung out carelessly, here one and there one. Yet fast or slow, by dawn the bright reaches were the same bright reaches and the dark the same dark, in the old places. A-Ting, who kept the lamp house, could say how many bright reaches there were, but she never dared say where the next lamp she watched would stop; neither could the oldest lamp-setter in town. How a lamp swayed was the lamp's own affair; nobody had taught it.

坏就坏在对半分上。北汊人咬定灯走的是北汊，南汊人咬定是南汊，年年吵；今年开春，两边的头面人索性顶到灯屋门口。北汊的把话撂下：「灯走我们汊里，梁上的鱼倒只落一半，亏不亏心？」南汊的拍拍胸口：「数就数。」两下头一回想到一处去。北汊出杉木，南汊出劳力，在两道汊口各下一张拦帘，帘上只留一个口，灯得一盏一盏过；口子边守着人，把灯捞起来，在松木托上写个北字或者南字，再放回水里。灯一盏一盏地过帘，账一页一页地厚，两边都笃定，这本账到头来帮自己说话。阿汀娘家在北汊，婆家在南汊，那晚坐在门槛上，一句话没说。

The trouble lived in the halves. The north-channel folk swore the lamps rode their channel; the south-channel folk swore the opposite; the quarrel ran for years, and this spring the leading men of both sides met at the lamp-house door. A north man laid it down: "The lamps ride our water, and half the fish off those weirs walks away — does that sit well with you?" A south man thumped his chest: "Then count them." For once the two sides wanted the same thing. The north gave cedar and the south gave hands, and at the mouth of each channel they set a woven screen with a single gap, so that the lamps had to pass one by one. A man stood at each gap: he lifted each lamp out, wrote a "north" or a "south" on the pine float, and set it back on the water. Lamp by lamp the fleet went through; page by page the tally thickened; both sides were certain the book would end by speaking for them. A-Ting, born north and married south, sat on the doorsill and said nothing.

下梢看灯的人守到半夜，就明白出了事。灯照旧一盏一盏下来，照旧一盏一盏停住，可它们不再归置自己：光摊平了，从这岸匀匀铺到那岸，没有亮段，也没有黑段，一整条河淡淡浮着火。有心的老人还看出一桩事：那夜的灯下得太直，一片片寻常木片似的，不晃了。老人们守到天亮，亮段到底没有回来；梁没敢下，没人说得出往哪里扎。这个春鱼季就这么空过去，北汊南汊各亏各的。倒是那本账清清楚楚——南汊多出三百一十七盏。南汊人捧着账要七成，北汊人只问一句：七成，分的什么？两下这才哑了。吵的东西没了。

Down at the lower bend the watchers knew by midnight that something had gone wrong. The lamps still came one by one, still stopped one by one, but they no longer tidied themselves: the light lay level, spread even from bank to bank, no bright reach and no dark one, a whole river faintly afloat with flame. One sharp-eyed old man saw something else besides: the lamps came down too straight that night, like plain chips of wood, without a sway in them. The old men waited to dawn and the bright reaches never came back; the weirs stayed ashore, for nobody could say where the stakes should go, and the spring season emptied past them, the north channel and the south each bearing its own loss. The tally, at least, came out clean: the south had three hundred and seventeen lamps the more. The south folk carried the book about asking for seven parts in ten, and the north folk asked one question — seven parts of what? After that, both sides went quiet. The thing they had quarreled over was gone.

镇上有人把这笔账算到人心上，说帘口的手太贪，看灯的眼太毒，河灯不乐意了。隔年阿汀做主又放了一夜：拦帘照旧下，灯照旧捞、照旧写字，只是字一律封进灯屋的木箱，不点验，不开看；守帘的换了村里最敬水的两位老人，落头一盏之前还烧了香。那一夜，光仍旧匀匀摊了一河。再隔一年，帘下得稀，十盏里捞三四盏，其余从帘缝里滑过去；那晚河面终于又有亮段，只是淡，像隔着雨看火。三夜的水面在阿汀心里摆齐了：账越厚，亮段越淡。坏掉亮段的从来不是看灯的心思，是帘口那只捞灯的手。

Some in town laid the loss on the human heart: the hands at the screens had been too greedy, the watching eyes too sharp, and the river lamps had taken offense. The next year A-Ting had her way and they spent one more night on it: the screens went down as before, the lamps were lifted and lettered as before, only every lettered float went straight into a wooden chest in the lamp house, uncounted, unopened, and the watch at the gaps was kept by the two oldest men in the village, the ones most reverent toward water, who burned incense before the first lamp went down. That night the light lay level across the river just the same. The year after, the screens were set sparse, three or four lamps lifted in ten and the rest slipping through the weave, and that night the reaches came back at last — faint, though, like fire seen through rain. A-Ting set the three nights side by side in her mind: the thicker the tally, the fainter the reach. What undid the bright reaches was never the heart that watched; it was the hand at the gap.

第四年开春，两汊的人一同上水，拆了拦帘，杉木抬回来修了灯屋的墙。放灯照旧放得慢，一炷香一盏；后半夜，亮段一段一段回来了，还在老地方，一处不差。梁照旧下在亮水下，春鱼照旧对半分。分鱼那晚，阿汀娘家的兄弟和婆家的叔公坐上同一张桌，婆母把最肥的一段推过桌面；没有人提那口木箱，它还封在架子上，往后再没开过。两汊后来还是吵，为水，为滩，为滩边一棵老槐，只是再没人提数灯的事。镇上添了一句新话，都说是阿汀先说的：一条河在一夜里只给你一样，要么每一盏灯的来历，要么那一整条灯河。

In the fourth spring the two channels went upriver together and took the screens out, and the cedar came home to mend the lamp-house wall. The lamps went down slow that year, one to a stick of incense; after midnight the bright reaches came back stretch by stretch, in the old places, not one of them shifted. The weirs went into the bright water as always, and the catch was halved as always. On the night of the split, A-Ting's brother from her mother's house and her husband's uncle sat down at one table, and her mother-in-law pushed the fattest share across the boards; nobody spoke of the chest, which stayed sealed on its shelf and was never opened again. The two channels went on quarreling — over water, over the flats, over an old locust tree on the bank — but never again over the counting of lamps. The town added one saying, and they said A-Ting said it first: a river can give you only one thing in a night, either the origin of every lamp, or the whole river of light.

注解：这说的是量子力学里的波粒二象性（wave-particle duality）：光子、电子这样的东西，用一种方式去看，是一粒一粒、各有落点的实体；用另一种方式去看，是万千次落点攒出来的一幅明暗图景。要弄清每一粒走的是哪条道，就得设卡去截，而截过的那一夜，图景必然散掉；至于显出哪一面，只取决于怎么看——看的人用不用心、开不开那口木箱，都不相干。

Note: This is wave-particle duality in quantum mechanics: looked at one way, things like photons and electrons arrive one by one, each at a single spot of its own; looked at another way, they are a pattern of bright and dark bands built up out of thousands of arrivals. To learn which way each one traveled you must stop and mark them, and on the night you do, the pattern must scatter; which face shows itself depends only on the way of looking — the watcher's reverence, or whether anyone ever opens the tally box, makes no difference.
