第四张嘴

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年9月16日·3分钟阅读

初三的大水退下去，河谷里只剩泥。我爹从泥里拖出半扇闸门，那是我们家磨坊剩下的最大一块。上游温嫂的磨坊还立着，门板泡胀了，一仓春粮发了芽。镇上的人后来追着问：是谁把话传岔了？摆渡的老关，还是桑伯？问了一个春天，谁也不是。这件事，得从初二夜里的三张嘴说起。

After the flood of the third, the gorge held nothing but mud. My father dragged half a sluice gate out of it, the biggest piece of our mill that was left. Upstream, Widow Wen's mill still stood, its doors swollen shut, a storehouse of spring grain sprouting where the water had sat. The townsfolk kept asking afterward: who garbled the message, old Guan the ferryman, or Uncle Sang? They asked all spring; it was neither. To explain it, you must start with the three mouths of the night before.

我们的河谷里原是四座水磨，隔着崖，喊话传不过去，只能托人带口信：我沿纤道跑短脚，老关的船管下游。开春山上冰坝蓄水，谷里的老规矩只有一条：吊闸同吊，落闸同落。老话说，汛期无对错，只有齐与不齐——半开的河谷最凶，水全灌进开着的闸道，把磨连根拔走，落了闸的也要被漫顶的水淹了仓。自打罗叔去年秋天走了，罗家磨再没人应声；按规矩，不回话的一律当落闸算。今年，只剩三张嘴。

Four watermills once strung this gorge, cliffs between them; a shout carried nowhere, so words traveled by hand: I ran the towpath for the short legs, and old Guan's ferry took the lower reach. When the spring ice dam swelled in the mountains, the valley's old rule was a single line: lift the gates together or drop them together. The saying went that in flood season there is no right and wrong, only together or not — a half-open gorge is the cruelest, the whole surge pouring through the open races and tearing those mills out by the roots, while even the shut ones lose their storehouses to the water that climbs over. Since Uncle Luo died last autumn no one has answered at Luo's mill, and by the rule, silence counts as a dropped gate. This year there were only three mouths.

初二清早，桑伯把我叫进磨房，叫我听两遍、复述两遍：「初三卯时，冰坝要垮，三座磨一齐吊闸。」我一字不差地带给爹。同一个时辰，老关摇船把桑伯的话捎往下游。傍晚温嫂的回话到了：桑伯亲口叫她落闸死守，还说中游若敢吊闸，出了事自己担。爹在灯下坐了半宿。同一个人，同一天，说了两句相反的话。

At dawn on the second, Uncle Sang called me into the mill and made me listen twice and say it back twice: "First light on the third, the ice dam gives way — all three mills lift their gates together." I carried it to my father word for word. The same hour, old Guan rowed Sang's word down to Widow Wen. By evening her reply came back: Sang himself had told her to drop the gates and hold fast, and if the middle mill dared to lift, it would answer for whatever followed. Father sat under the lamp half the night. One man, one day, two opposite words.

爹叫我再跑一趟，问桑伯到底怎么说的。磨房里熬着姜汤，桑伯额头烫手，眼睛亮得吓人，有一瞬他把我认成了死去的罗叔。他说：「吊闸。叫下游别慌。」当晚温嫂家也遣人去问，带回来的话是：桑伯说冰坝到不了下游，落闸，中游在瞎咋呼。两句都是真话。他烧糊涂了，上午一个主意，天黑一个主意，谁去问，撞上的就是哪个时辰的他。我带着「吊闸」回去，温嫂的捎话人带着「落闸」回去，两边都觉得自己手里是多数。

Father sent me up again to ask what Sang had actually said. Ginger broth was boiling in the mill; Sang's forehead burned, his eyes shone frighteningly bright, and for one moment he mistook me for dead Uncle Luo. He said, "Lift. Tell the lower mill not to be afraid." That evening Widow Wen's man went up too, and brought back word that Sang said the dam would never reach the lower mill — drop the gates, the middle mill is raving at shadows. Both messages were honest. The fever had him; one mind in the morning, another after dark, and whoever came asking met whichever hour of him was speaking. I carried "lift" home, her messenger carried "drop" home, and each side believed it held the majority.

那夜爹摆开三只碗：桑伯说吊闸，我亲耳听的；温嫂说落闸，她的捎话人亲耳听的；罗家磨不应声，当落闸算。三只碗里有一只烫手，谁也指不出来。谷里的老话说，容得下一句错话，得有四张嘴——罗叔在时，谁说岔了都对得出来；三张嘴里错一句，就是死题。爹算到鸡叫，末了只认一条：信自己儿子亲耳听来的。天亮前他把最后的话送下去：卯时吊闸。温嫂回了一个词：疯话。

That night Father set out three bowls: Sang says lift, and I heard it with my own ears; Wen says drop, and her messenger heard it with his; Luo's mill gives no answer, which counts as drop. One of the three bowls scalded the hand, and no one could point at which. The saying went that to bear one false word takes four mouths — while Uncle Luo lived, any slip could be checked against him; among three mouths, one false word is a dead problem. Father reckoned until the rooster crowed, and in the end trusted a single thing: what his own son had heard in person. Before first light he sent the last word down: we lift at dawn. Widow Wen sent back a single word: "Madness."

卯时，只有我们家的闸升起来。洪头下来，一线水全灌进我们一条闸道，磨房先抖，后离了基石，爹攥着纤绳才没跟着下去。温嫂落了闸，可漫顶的水淹了她一仓春粮。桑伯自己昏在镇上郎中家，他家的磨没了人守，顶子叫回流掀了。清明后他退了烧，把初二那两回话记得清清楚楚，两回说的都是实话。镇上的人不肯罢休，非要找出个坏人，没有找到。爹后来只说过一句：那晚三张嘴就算说成了一句话，也未必是救命的那句；可说不成一句话，就一定是死。

At first light, only our gate rose. When the crest came down, the whole river funneled into our one open race; the millhouse shuddered, then tore loose from its foundation stones, and only the tow rope kept Father from going down with it. Wen had dropped her gate, but the water that climbed her weir drowned a storehouse of spring grain. Sang himself lay unconscious at the town healer's; his mill stood untended, its roof taken off by the backwater. After Qingming the fever broke, and he remembered both messages of that day with perfect clarity — both had been honest. The townspeople would not let it rest; they meant to find a wicked man, and found none. Father said only one thing about it afterward: had the three mouths spoken as one that night, the shared word might still not have been the saving one; but failing to speak as one was certain death.

注解：这就是拜占庭将军问题（the Byzantine generals problem）：散在各处、只能托人捎话的伙伴，要在错话流通时统一行动；口口相传之下，容一句错话至少得有四张嘴，只剩三张嘴时一句错话便无解——除非每句话都带着按了手印、改无可改的字据。发错话的人未必存坏心，一场高烧、一个听岔的音同样致命；齐心也只保证大家做同一件事，不保证那件事一定对。

Note: This is the Byzantine generals problem: partners scattered out of earshot, who can only pass word by courier, must act as one while false words circulate; with nothing but word of mouth, bearing one false word takes at least four voices — with only three, one false word is unsolvable — unless every word travels as a note sealed with a thumbprint no one can alter. And the one who sends the false word need not be wicked: a high fever or a misheard syllable kills just as surely; acting as one only promises that everyone does the same thing, not that the thing is right.
