背书人

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年9月10日·1分钟阅读

唐陌被召进城东商会的大厅时，白老正坐在长桌尽头。老人把一枚铜印推过来，说：「这座城里的规矩，你该知道。每个商人都要拿到背书才能做生意——自己给自己背书也行，找人背书也行。没背书的，不许开张。」

When Tang Mo was summoned to the East Guild hall, Elder Bai sat at the far end of the long table. The old man slid a brass seal across the wood and said, "You know the rules of this city. Every merchant must hold an endorsement to do business—self-endorsement is fine, having someone else endorse you is fine. Without one, you may not open shop."

唐陌接过铜印。白老说：「但总有人自己不愿或不能背书自己。你的差事，就是替这些人背书。记住，你只背书那些不给自己背书的人，而且要背书他们每一个人。」

Tang Mo took the seal. Elder Bai said, "But there are always people who will not or cannot endorse themselves. Your duty is to endorse them. Remember: you endorse exactly those who do not endorse themselves—and you must endorse every one of them."

唐陌觉得这差事简单。头三天，他给十几个没给自己背书的商人盖了印，人人都满意。第四天傍晚，一个年轻商人拦住他，问了个怪问题：「你自己呢？你给自己背书了吗？」

Tang Mo thought the duty simple. For three days he stamped seals for a dozen-odd merchants who hadn't endorsed themselves, and everyone was satisfied. On the fourth evening, a young merchant stopped him and asked a strange question: "What about you? Have you endorsed yourself?"

唐陌愣住了。他想了一夜，第二天去找白老。「如果我给自己背书，」他说，「那我就是给自己背书的人。可我的差事是只背书那些不给自己背书的人——所以我不该给自己背书。」「可如果你不给自己背书，」白老缓缓道，「那你就是那种不给自己背书的人。而你的差事是背书每一个这样的人——所以你必须给自己背书。」

Tang Mo froze. He thought about it all night and went to Elder Bai the next day. "If I endorse myself," he said, "then I'm someone who endorses himself. But my duty is to endorse only those who don't endorse themselves—so I shouldn't endorse myself." "But if you don't endorse yourself," Elder Bai said slowly, "then you're someone who doesn't endorse himself. And your duty is to endorse every such person—so you must endorse yourself."

唐陌握着铜印，手心全是汗。不背书自己，他买不了粮、租不了房、出不了城门——没有背书的人在这城里寸步难行。背书自己，他便背弃了差事的根基，整座城的信任从此崩塌。白老叹了口气：「上一任背书人就是在这一步疯的。他走之前留了句话：这差事不是留给一个人的，是留给一个矛盾的。」

Tang Mo gripped the seal, palms slick with sweat. Without endorsing himself he could buy no grain, rent no room, pass through no gate—without an endorsement, a person could not move an inch in this city. Endorsing himself, he would betray the very foundation of his duty, and the city's entire system of trust would collapse. Elder Bai sighed. "The last man to hold this seal went mad at this very step. Before he left he said: this post isn't meant for a person. It's meant for a contradiction."

注解：这是罗素悖论（Russell's paradox）的结构：令 R 为所有不包含自身的集合所构成的集合，则 R 属于自身当且仅当 R 不属于自身（R ∈ R ⟺ R ∉ R），矛盾不可消解；其根源在于朴素集合论的无限制概括原理——允许对任意性质直接构造集合，当性质回指自身时便陷入恶性循环。

Annotation: This is the structure of Russell's paradox: let R be the set of all sets that are not members of themselves; then R is a member of itself if and only if it is not a member of itself (R ∈ R ⟺ R ∉ R), an irreconcilable contradiction. The root lies in the unrestricted comprehension principle of naïve set theory—allowing a set to be formed for any property whatsoever, and when that property refers back to the set itself, a vicious circle ensues.
