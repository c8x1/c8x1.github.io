再系一个结

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年9月15日·3分钟阅读

谷地里的人把誓约系在带子上。谁许了谁一支舞，两条带子便并到一起打一个结，各人收各人的。到了秋分夜，全镇进谷仓跳一整晚的连奏：琴声不许断，一曲终了场上必留一人，下一位舞伴从长凳上起身接上，一环扣一环直到天亮。结在舞毕当场解开；琴声一停，谁带上还剩着结，那两家就算断了往来，往后赶集撞见也只当不认识。今年谷仓冷得早，琴师塞林（Serin）的手指已经拢不住弓。他同如今守着舞会的露薏莎（Luisa）讲定：这一夜他从第一个音符拉到最后一个，弓子一旦放下，就再举不起来。

In the valley, promises were carried on ribbons. When two people pledged each other a dance, the two ribbons were laid together and one knot tied into both, and each went home wearing their own. On the night of the equinox the whole town crowded into the barn for the unbroken medley: the fiddle was never to fall silent, and when a tune ended one dancer stayed on the floor while the next partner rose from the benches, the night holding together link by link until dawn. Each knot was untied the moment its dance was done; when the music finally stopped, any knot left on a ribbon meant the two households were finished with each other, and would pass at market as strangers. The cold came early this year, and Serin the fiddler could no longer close his fingers on the bow. He struck his bargain with Luisa, who now kept the ball: he would play from the first note to the last, and once the bow came down, it would not rise again.

舞会前一日，长桌上摊开全镇的带子，露薏莎一条一条数过去。数着数着她看明白一件事：结是并着两条带子打的，一个结必定同时落进两条带子，从来没有只落在一条里的。米拉（Mira）数自己那条，越数越慌，说带的是五个结，单数；露薏莎让她重数——单数的带子从不单来——才发现有一个结绕了两圈，其实是六个。她再把全桌带子从头牵到尾：一条牵着一条，整桌连成一整片，没有哪条能单独摘出去。末了数出单数的只有两条：她自己的，九个；约纳斯（Jonas）的，七个。

The day before, the town's ribbons lay spread down the long table, and Luisa counted them out knot by knot. Somewhere in the counting one thing came clear: a knot is tied across two ribbons at once, and no knot was ever tied into one alone. Her sister Mira counted hers in a panic and came up with five, an odd number; Luisa had her count again — odd ribbons never came singly, that much the valley knew — and one knot turned out to be wound twice round, which made six. Then she pulled the whole table's ribbons end to end: each held on to the next, the lot of them in one single piece with no strand that could be lifted out by itself. In the end only two ribbons carried an odd count: her own, with nine knots, and Jonas's, with seven.

夜里她睡不着，回到桌边用指尖沿着带子走那条长链。每过一个结，指头便翻到另一条带上；站在链子中段的人，结总是成双地用掉，进一支，出一支，谁也没法单留一个；能把结单个用掉的，只有链头和链尾。守会人起头，这是老规矩；她既起了头，长链无论中途怎么绕，尾必定落在约纳斯带上的结，回不到她手上。谷里另有老话，守会人开的头要由守会人收回来，她母亲那些年都是这样，最后一支舞牵着米拉退场。如今这个收场要交给约纳斯：去年冬天在教堂门口丢下她的人，天一亮就套车出谷，再不回来。她发过誓，此生不同他共一支舞。

She slept badly, and went back to the table in the night to walk the long chain with a fingertip. Over each knot the finger passes onto another ribbon; a dancer in the middle of the chain spends knots in pairs, one to come in on and one to go out on, and nobody inside it can be left holding a single knot. Only the head of the chain and its tail can spend one alone. The keeper opens the ball, that was the old rule; and once she had opened it, however the chain wandered, it was bound to end on a knot of Jonas's ribbon and come no farther home. There was a saying in the valley besides, that the keeper who opens the night brings it home again; her mother had done it that way every year, leading Mira off the floor in the last tune. Now that ending belonged to Jonas — the man who had left her at the church door last winter, who would hitch his cart at first light and be gone from the valley for good. She had sworn never to share a dance with him again.

第二天她把数出来的事讲给镇上人听，人们不服。有人说干脆限每人只下场一回，铁匠布伦（Brun）把带子抖开给他看：十个结，许的是十家邻居，一个也省不得。有人又说多备些曲子、把夜拉长，总能接得上；露薏莎摇头，难的从来不是曲子的数目，而是结的形状。米拉不肯信，拿自己的带子起头走了一遍：链子走到一半死在场上，指头底下剩一个孤零零的结，没有去处。

The next morning she laid her counting before the town, and the town pushed back. Let each person be allowed onto the floor only once, someone said; Brun the blacksmith shook out his ribbon in answer: ten knots, pledged to ten households, not one of them to be spared. Add more tunes, said another, stretch the night and it will all come right; Luisa shook her head — the trouble was never the number of tunes, but the shape of the knots. Mira refused to believe it and tried walking the chain from her own ribbon: halfway along, the thread died on the floor, one lone knot left under her finger with nowhere to go.

黄昏她把两条带子摆到一处。要看明白的事其实很小：要把单数翻平，新结必须落在两个单数之间，落到别处，单数不过挪个窝。在他和她中间再系一个结，九变十，七变八，全镇的带子便都齐了双数，长链就能首尾相衔，从她的带子起，回到她的带子收，最后一支舞还是她的。代价是同他跳一支舞，跳完解开；若一时心软系上两个，单数又都回来，一切照旧。她想起自己起的誓，也想起母亲的收场。天黑透前她走到井台边，约纳斯正往车上捆行李。她把两个带尾递过去，没有说话。他看了一会儿，接住，同她一起打了那个结。

At dusk she set the two ribbons side by side, hers and his. What she had to see was small enough: to turn an odd ribbon even, the new knot must fall between two odd ones; tied anywhere else, the oddness merely moves house. One more knot between him and her — nine to ten, seven to eight — and every ribbon in town would come out even, the long chain could take its own tail in its mouth, setting out from her ribbon and coming home to it, and the last dance would be hers again. The price was one dance with him, danced and untied; a second knot, tied out of softness, would turn both odd again and leave everything where it stood. She thought of her oath, and she thought of her mother's last tune. Before full dark she walked to the well, where Jonas was lashing his crates to the cart. She held out the two ends without a word. He looked at them a while, took them, and tied the knot with her.

那一夜琴没有断过。布伦下了十回场，回回是新的舞伴。中段有一支慢曲，露薏莎同约纳斯把新结用掉、解开，退下去时谁也没提冬天。天快亮时，长链绕回她的带子，最后一支舞，她牵着米拉退了场；塞林把弓横在膝上，再没有举起来。晨光里，捆好行李的车出了谷口，全镇的带子上，一个结也不剩了。

The fiddle did not stop once that night. Brun took the floor ten times, a new partner every time. Somewhere in the middle came a slow tune: Luisa and Jonas spent the new knot, untied it, and stepped down without a word about the winter. Toward dawn the chain came round to her ribbon again, and in the last tune she led Mira off the floor; Serin laid the bow across his knees and never lifted it again. By first light the loaded cart was out of the valley mouth, and there was not a single knot left on any ribbon in town.

注解：这串带子与结对应欧拉路径（Eulerian path）：每段誓约恰好走过一遍的长链能否连成，不凭心愿，只看各条带上结的单双，以及整把带子是否连成一片——单数恰好两处时，链必始于其中一处、终于另一处；两处之间补一个结翻成全双，链便首尾相衔成环。它只管每段誓约恰好一次，人像布伦那样反复下场并无妨碍；只是单数结的人若多过两个，一夜连奏装不下，得分几段才收得完。

Note: The ribbons and their knots stand for Eulerian paths: whether every promise can be threaded into one unbroken chain depends on nothing but whether each ribbon holds an even or an odd number of knots, and on whether the whole bundle hangs together — with exactly two odd ribbons, the chain must begin at one and end at the other; one extra knot between them turns every count even, and the chain can close into a ring. It asks only that each promise be kept exactly once, while a dancer may take the floor as often as the chain needs, as the blacksmith does; with more than two odd ribbons, though, one unbroken night would not be enough, and the night would have to break into parts.
