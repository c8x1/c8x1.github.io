我们需要一种更好的方式来描述那些常被称为「癌症」的东西

Matthew R Cooperberg·Aeon·2026年9月30日·15分钟阅读·原文

威廉（William）来找我，是想听第三份诊疗意见。他53岁，身体健康，有一位妻子和两个十几岁的孩子，工作繁忙，日程表是典型的被工作与家庭塞满的那种忙乱。他特意抽出时间做了件正确的事：去初级保健医生（primary care provider）那里做了常规体检和血液检查——这是他多年来的第一次。几周之后，他发现自己坐在一间陌生的泌尿科医生（urologist）诊室里，站在深渊边缘，耳边嗡嗡作响的，也许是医学中最令人闻风丧胆的三个词：「你得了癌症（cancer）。」这个诊断预示着疼痛、痛苦与死亡：仅在美国，癌症每年就夺走超过600,000人的生命——足以让一座城市人去城空。

William came to me for a third opinion. He was 53 and healthy, with a wife and two teenage children, a busy job, and a typically hectic work-and-family schedule. He’d taken time to do the right thing and had visited his primary care provider for a routine health exam and blood tests, his first in many years. A few weeks later, he’d found himself in an unfamiliar urologist’s office, at the edge of an abyss, ears ringing with what may be three of the most dreaded words in medicine: ‘You have cancer.’ This diagnosis portends pain, suffering and death: in the United States alone, cancer kills more than 600,000 people a year, enough lives to empty a city.

然而，接到癌症诊断，未必还意味着它曾经的含义。威廉的处境其实极为普遍：每年有超过200万美国人收到这一诊断，而对其中许多人来说，这一发现对寿命或生活质量几乎没有威胁——除非算上治疗本身带来的不良影响。然而，被贴上「C字眼」（C-word）标签的患者，却要面对实实在在的临床、心理、社会和经济后果。这种脱节——这个词在我的诊室里唤起的联想，与它在当代医疗实践中常常描述的东西之间的脱节——正是筛查实践中争议与乱象的根源；而鉴于我们对细胞生物学的理解日益加深，它也要求我们重新思考：当我们贴上「癌症」这个标签时，我们究竟在说什么。

Yet, receiving a cancer diagnosis doesn’t necessarily mean what it once did. William was in very broad company: more than 2 million Americans a year receive the diagnosis and, for many, the finding entails little to no threat to length or quality of life, except through the ill effects of treatments. However, patients labelled with the ‘C-word’ face substantial clinical, psychological, social and economic consequences. This disconnect – between what the word evokes in my exam room and what it often describes in contemporary practice – drives controversies and variability in screening practices, and in light of our growing understanding of cellular biology, demands that we rethink what we really mean when we apply the label ‘cancer’.

威廉的癌变位于前列腺（prostate）——此前他只是隐约知道自己体内有这么一个器官，而切除它或对它进行放疗，都有损害泌尿、肠道和性功能的风险。活检（biopsy）显示，他的前列腺肿瘤属于我们称为「1级分组」（grade group 1）的类型，这种肿瘤几乎没有癌症的分子标志特征，也没有立即转移（metastasis）或致死的风险。然而，在迅速扩大的医疗团队抛出的一堆相互矛盾的意见的围困之下，威廉发现自己一头扎向了治疗，并与一种从未体验过的持续焦虑缠斗。到他来到我的诊室时，他已经请了病假，还向我吐露：吊诡的是，几十年来他第一次抽起了烟。

William’s cancer was in his prostate, an organ of whose existence he was previously only vaguely aware, but whose removal or radiation risked impairments to urinary, bowel and sexual function. His biopsy had revealed a prostate tumour we designate ‘grade group 1’, which features few molecular hallmarks of cancer and no immediate potential for metastasis or death. Nonetheless, beset with many competing opinions from his rapidly expanding medical team, William found himself rushing headlong toward treatment, and wrestling with a constant anxiety he’d never felt before. By the time he reached my office, he had taken medical leave from work and confided that, perversely, he was smoking for the first time in decades.

上世纪90年代我刚进医学院时，觉得癌症既迷人又可怕，恰恰是因为它所代表的那种背叛。大多数人类疾病意味着某个身体系统的失灵。无论起因是外伤、感染、血供退化、机械磨损还是其他原因，总有一个或多个器官无法再正常运转。在这类情况下，我们的医学任务原则上是简单的：支持失灵的系统，防止进一步的损伤，并尽可能修复或替代缺失的功能。

When I started medical school in the 1990s, I found cancer fascinating and terrifying exactly because of the betrayal it represented. Most human disease represents failure of one body system or another. Whether due to trauma, infection, degradation of blood supply, mechanical wear-and-tear or other causes, one or more organs stop performing optimally. In such cases, our job in medicine is simple in principle: support the failing system, prevent further damage, and repair or replace missing function as much as possible.

癌症则截然不同：尽管它最终也会导致系统衰竭，但它的起源，是某个器官一隅的细胞生长得过了头、增殖过快，并最终探索和殖民身体的其他部位。横行的病毒或细菌显然是恶性入侵者，可以被理解并标记为外来的敌对之物。而癌症，是一个人自己的细胞。它们行为不端，却仍然属于自我本身。癌症化疗之所以远比细菌感染的抗生素治疗毒性更强，恰恰是因为，要在人体细胞中持续区分「正常」与「癌」，要困难得多。

Cancer is fundamentally different: although ultimately it also causes system failures, its origin lies in cells in a corner of a given organ thriving more than they should, growing too quickly, and eventually exploring and colonising other parts of the body. A marauding virus or bacterium is clearly a malign invader that can be understood and labelled as foreign and hostile. A cancer is a person’s own cells. They are behaving badly but are still intrinsic to the self. Chemotherapy for cancer is so much more toxic than antibiotics for bacterial infection precisely because it is harder for treatments to consistently distinguish ‘normal’ from ‘cancer’ among a body’s cells.

那个名词，那个首字母大写的专有名词，依旧阴森地高悬着

The noun, the proper noun with the capital letter, still looms, menacing

在加州大学旧金山分校（University of California, San Francisco）癌症中心和旧金山退伍军人事务医疗中心（San Francisco Veterans Affairs Medical Center）执业时，我每周的每一次门诊都会遇到处于威廉这种境况的男性。其中消息较灵通的人往往已经想明白：并非所有肿瘤都需要治疗。但许多人仍然被「癌症」这个词进入房间的那一刻击得晕头转向——光线如何骤然黯淡，空气如何骤然凝重——以及在那次就诊的余下时间里，他们实际上几乎什么也没听进去。为这些患者做咨询，与做手术一样，是我工作中同等重要、同样需要拿捏分寸的一部分；诊室里恰当的言辞，其重要性不亚于手术室里恰当的器械。然而，尽管我们已经开发出机器人辅助的精准手术系统、聚焦度越来越高的放疗平台和靶向性奇佳的药物，我们的语言却基本上还停留在19世纪。

In my practices at the University of California, San Francisco Cancer Center and the San Francisco Veterans Affairs Medical Center, I see men in William’s situation in every clinic, every week. The better-informed among them have often already figured out that not every tumour needs treatment, but many are still reeling from the moment the word ‘cancer’ entered the room – the way the light suddenly dimmed and the air thickened – and how little they really heard during the rest of the visit. Counselling these patients is as important and nuanced a part of my job as is surgery, and the right words in the office are no less critical than the right tools in the operating room. But even as we have developed robot-assisted precision surgical systems, increasingly focused radiation delivery platforms and marvellously targeted pharmaceuticals, our language is essentially stuck in the 19th century.

我和许多同事都努力在这些咨询对话中穿插一些旨在安抚患者及其亲人的形容词：低级别（low-grade）、低危（low-risk）、无威胁（nonthreatening）、生长缓慢（slow-growing）、惰性（indolent）。我们已经相当熟练地用这些修饰语把患者从深渊边缘拉回来，但那个名词，那个首字母大写的专有名词，依旧阴森地高悬着。经过多年这样的患者咨询——这类咨询随时间推移变得容易了些，但也仅止于此——我加入了日益壮大的前列腺癌专科医生和研究者行列，开始敦促同行认真对待一个问题：是否应当更新我们的词汇，让我们所说的话重新与患者实际听到的对齐。

Many colleagues and I try to pepper these counselling conversations with adjectives intended to reassure both patients and their loved ones: low-grade, low-risk, nonthreatening, slow-growing, indolent. We’ve grown relatively facile using these modifiers to pull patients back from the edge of the chasm, but the noun, the proper noun with the capital letter, still looms, menacing. After many years of these patient consultations – which have grown easier over time, but only to an extent – I am among a growing cadre of prostate cancer specialists and researchers who have started to challenge our colleagues to take seriously the question of whether to update our vocabulary to bring what we say back in line with what patients hear.

我的职业前辈们能在人的尺度上迅速认出癌症，并带着心照不宣的恐惧：乳房里的肿块，腋窝里又摸到一个；发黄的眼睛，提示胆管受到挤压；止不住的小出血，因为白血病（leukaemia）已占据了骨髓。莫名其妙的体重下降。挥之不去的疼痛。而在我的时代，我们通常是通过一张纸或电脑屏幕上的文字，向患者呈现诊断：一份病理报告，一种从玻璃切片上读出的占卜，以墨水或像素的形式，为一个此刻通常感觉良好的人预言未来。可是，异常行为究竟从哪一刻起开始威胁全身？我们如何判定细胞跨越的那些门槛——从变异，到异常，再到敌人？从医学院毕业至今的几十年职业生涯里，我们对癌症复杂性的认识已愈发精深，但我们仍然远不能定义癌症真正开始的那个时刻，也不能定义它随后首次威胁健康与生命的那个时刻。

My professional forebears would recognise cancer at human scale quickly and with a knowing dread: the lump in the breast accompanied by another in the armpit, the yellowed eyes indicating a squeezed bile duct, the minor bleeding that wouldn’t stop because leukaemia had taken over the bone marrow. The unintended weight loss. The nagging pain. In my time, in contrast, we typically present a patient with the diagnosis over a piece of paper or text on a computer screen: a pathology report, a divination read from glass slides and rendered in ink or pixels to predict the future for a patient who today usually feels just fine. But at what point does aberrant behaviour threaten the whole body? How do we determine the thresholds cells cross from variant to abnormality to enemy? In the decades of my career since school, our appreciation of cancer’s complexity has grown more sophisticated, but we are still nowhere near being able to define the true moment a cancer begins, nor the subsequent moment it first threatens health and life.

绵延数千年的医学传统，早已认识到那些能够生长、扩散和致死的可见肿块。公元前5世纪，希波克拉底（Hippocrates）及其同时代人首次将其命名为karkinos——「螃蟹」——得名于进行性肿瘤沿皮下淋巴管（lymphatic channel）蔓延时可见的那一条条触须。在此后的几千年里，人类对这种疾病的认识断断续续地推进，但癌症无一例外是在变得可见、可触及或出现症状时才被发现的，而到那时，手术治愈已殊为罕见。即便在麻醉术与抗菌术问世之前那个难以想象的年代手术尚能挺过，手术也无法根除已经转移的癌症；于是癌症复发、进展——而且几乎总是致命。

Medical traditions stretching over thousands of years recognised visible growths with the capa­city to grow, spread and kill. Hippocrates and his contemporaries in the 5th century BCE first named the karkinos – ‘the crab’ – after the visible tendrils that progressive tumours could be seen to propagate along lymphatic channels under the skin. In the ensuing millennia, progress in understanding the disease came in fits and starts, but cancers were universally detected when they were visible, palpable or symptomatic, by which point cure with surgery was successful only uncommonly. Even when survivable in the hard-to-imagine pre-anaesthesia, pre-antisepsis era, surgery couldn’t eradicate cancer that had already metastasised; as a result, cancers recurred and progressed – and were nearly always lethal.

显微镜技术发明后的几个世纪里，现代病理学逐渐成形，让研究者和医生得以看见这个敌手的隐秘面目：那些背弃了自己在整个机体中本职的微小细胞，一门心思地繁殖与迁徙。到19世纪晚期，改良的显微镜使早期病理学家得以对癌症——癌（carcinoma）——进行分类，依据不仅是其起源器官，还有细胞本身的外观，从而为现代分级（grading）与分期（staging）体系奠定了基础。

In the centuries after the development of microscopy, modern pathology evolved to allow researchers and physicians to see the hidden face of the adversary: the minute cells devolved from their proper roles in the broader organism and hellbent on reproduction and migration. By the late 19th century, improved microscopes allowed early pathologists to categorise cancers – carcinomas – based not just on their organs of origin but also on the appearance of the cells themselves, laying the foundation for modern grading and staging.

筛查的非预期后果是过度诊断——检出那些永远不会威胁生存的病变

The unintended consequence of screening has been over-diagnosis – the detection of lesions that would never threaten survival

然而，即便在显微镜时代的大部分时间里，癌症最初仍是因为临床症状明显才被发现的，而显微镜下的洞见，只有在对通常已相对晚期的癌症进行活检或手术之后才能获得。在那几十年里，病理学家的工作大体上是对临床医生已经做出——或至少已经怀疑——的诊断加以确认和细化。然而，随着20世纪中叶癌症筛查的问世，一切都变了。按照定义，筛查就是在其他方面健康的人群中寻找早期癌症。宫颈癌的巴氏涂片检查（Pap smear，1950年代）、针对乳腺癌和结肠癌的乳腺钼靶摄影与结肠镜检查（mammography、colonoscopy，1960年代）、针对前列腺癌的前列腺特异性抗原检测（prostate-specific antigen，PSA，1990年代）相继普及，使癌症得以通过活检早期确诊：比它们引发临床问题早数年甚至数十年。随着时间的推移——并无任何特别的意图，也没有经过正式的讨论——癌症的基本诊断，逐渐从外科医生或肿瘤科医生的临床判断，转变为病理学家的组织学判断，为我们今天面临的裂痕埋下了伏笔。

Even throughout most of the microscopy era, however, cancers were still first detected based on clinically apparent symptoms, and the microscopic insights were gained only after biopsy or surgery for a cancer that was usually already relatively advanced. In these decades, the pathologist largely confirmed and refined a diagnosis made – or at least suspected – by a clinician. With the advent of cancer screening in the middle of the 20th century, however, everything changed. Screening, by definition, entails looking for early cancer in otherwise healthy people. The widespread availability of Pap smears for cervical cancer (1950s), mammography and colonoscopy for breast and colon cancer (1960s) and prostate-specific antigen (PSA) testing for prostate cancer (1990s) allowed cancers to be diagnosed early, by biopsy: years or even decades before they would cause clinical problems. Over time – and without any particular intention or formal conversation – the fundamental diagnosis of cancer shifted gradually from a clinical determination by a surgeon or oncologist to a histologic determination by a pathologist, setting the stage for the rift we face today.

这些年也是「抗癌战争」（War on Cancer）拉开序幕的年代，确诊癌症就意味着治疗癌症。到我于本世纪初开始接受培训时，筛查已在全世界挽救了数以百万计的生命。然而，筛查的非预期后果是过度诊断（over-diagnosis）——查出那些若不被发现便永远不会引发症状或威胁生存的病变。在我步入执业之时，情况已日渐清晰：过度诊断滋生过度治疗；筛查纵然救人一命，也通过不必要的手术、放疗和其他治疗的副作用伤害着许多其他人——这些副作用有时是永久性的，偶尔甚至是致命的。此外，这一诊断还可能意味着人寿和健康保险费用的上涨，并且始终与抑郁症乃至自杀发生率的上升相关联。因此，筛查被证明极具争议，在医疗研究者之间——尤其是在全科医生与专科医生之间——引发了火药味十足的论战，也令筛查建议和实践出现剧烈的钟摆式摇摆。

These years also marked the advent of the War on Cancer, and diagnosing a cancer meant treating that cancer. By the time I started training early in the millennium, screening had already saved millions of lives worldwide. However, the unintended consequence of screening has been over-diagnosis – the detection of lesions that would never cause symptoms or threaten survival if left undiscovered. As I entered practice, it was becoming clear that overdiag­nosis begets overtreatment and, even as screening saves lives, it harms many others through the side-effects, sometimes permanent and occasionally lethal, of unnecessary surgery, radiation and other treatments. Moreover, the diagnosis may also mean increased costs for life and health insurance, and remains associated with increased rates of depression and even suicide. So screening has proved highly controversial, driving vitriolic debates among healthcare researchers, particularly between generalists and specialists, and wild pendulum swings in screening recommendations and practices.

癌症筛查的演进，与分子医学的一场革命相伴而行。过去半个世纪，我们的社会在研究上投入的巨资带来了丰厚回报。如今，我们对致癌过程（carcinogenesis）的理解前所未有，也前所未有地看清了每天都在上演的那场军备竞赛何等壮观复杂——在威廉的身体里，在我的身体里，在你的身体里，在每一只动物的身体里——交战双方，一边是单个细胞的繁殖与演化目标，另一边是整个机体的目标。我们如今对癌症起源、演化和结局的理解，理应促使我们重新考虑：究竟哪些人才真正需要背负这个诊断。

Cancer screening has evolved in concert with a revolution in molecular medicine. Our society’s massive investment in research over the past half-century has paid tremendous dividends. We now enjoy an unprecedented understanding of the process of carcinogenesis and of the spectacular complexity of the arms race happening every day – in William’s body, in mine, in yours, and in every animal’s – between the reproductive and evolutionary goals of individual cells and those of whole organisms. What we now understand about cancer’s origins, evolution and outcomes should drive a reconsideration of which individuals really need to bear the diagnosis.

那些我们依据显微镜下外观称之为「癌症」的不规则细胞团，其实时时刻刻都在每个人的身体里生长着。要理解为什么它们中的绝大多数并无危害，我们需要回到地球生命的早期历史。单细胞生物几乎在地球刚刚变得宜居时就出现了，而在随后长得难以想象的20亿年里，这些细胞为生存而竞争，演化出无数策略来压倒——有时是吞掉——自己的邻居，并适应新的栖息地。最终，演化压力促使一些细胞群开始紧密协作，直到它们开始作为多细胞生物（multicellular organism）进行繁殖；这些新近特化的细胞能够为机体的生命贡献特定功能，而关键在于，它们再也无法独自存活了。

The irregular clusters of cells we term ‘cancer’ based on their microscopic appearance are growing at all times in every body. To understand why the overwhelming majority of these are harmless, we need to return to the early history of life on Earth. Single-celled organisms emerged just about as soon as the planet became habitable, and for the next inconceivably long stretch of 2 billion years, these cells vied for survival and evolved innumerable strategies to outcompete – and sometimes consume – their neighbours and to adapt to new habitats. Eventually, evolutionary pressures drove some groups of cells to work closely together, until they started reproduc­ing as multicellular organisms; these newly specialised cells could contribute specific functions to the life of the organism and, critically, could no longer survive on their own.

近两个世纪前，细胞理论家马蒂亚斯·施莱登（Matthias Schleiden）写道：「每个细胞都过着双重生活：一种是独立的，只关乎它自身的发育；另一种是附属的，就它已成为某个［整体］不可分割的一部分而言。」这种独立与合作之间的张力，构成了全部多细胞生命的基础——也解释了细胞与身体之间那份脆弱的契约。一个人体内包含数以万亿计的细胞，每一个都围绕同一套遗传下来的DNA编码构建。这些细胞彼此不断沟通——也与另外数以万亿计、同样构成一个健康身体的非人类细菌和真菌细胞沟通——通过无数局部和远端的信号，调节彼此的行为与生长，协作以最大化整个人的繁殖成功率。

Nearly two centuries ago, the cell theorist Matthias Schleiden observed that ‘each cell leads a double life: an independent one, pertaining to its own development alone, and another incidental, insofar as it has become an integral part of a [whole].’ This tension between independence and cooperation underlies all multicellular life – and explains a cell’s fragile pact with the body. A human being contains trillions of cells, each built around the same inherited DNA coding. These cells communicate constantly with each other – and with trillions more nonhuman bacterial and fungal cells that also comprise a healthy body – through myriad local and distant signals, regulating each other’s behaviour and growth, cooperating to maximise the reproductive success of the whole person.

然而，每一个这样的细胞，都携带着它单细胞过去的基因记忆，以及那份古老的复制使命。事实上，每一个细胞之所以能安分守己，全靠一套复杂得令人眼花缭乱的内部遗传控制机制，以及与其他细胞的外部相互作用。即使成年后停止了生长，每天仍有约1%的细胞——数十亿个——耗损老化，需要通过细胞分裂来更替，而每一次分裂都需要生产出一份完整且最好是完美无缺的DNA拷贝。

And yet, every one of these cells carries the genetic memory of its single-celled past and its ancient imperative to replicate. In fact, each is kept in line only though a bewilderingly complex set of internal genetic controls and external interactions with other cells. Even after an adult stops growing, about 1 per cent of cells – billions – wear out and need to be replaced every day through cell division, requiring production of a complete and preferably perfect copy of the DNA.

我困在中间：不得不按我们如今在生物学上的理解向患者解释癌症，而他们仍然在文化层面上理解它

I am stuck explaining cancer as we now understand it biologically to patients who still understand it culturally

我们的DNA持续遭受辐射、环境污染，以及由不健康饮食、吸烟、慢性压力等因素驱动的炎症的攻击。此外，细胞每分裂一次，就会发生数以千计的复制错误。校对机制会纠正其中绝大多数，但总有漏网之误。这种不完美对整个物种至关重要——如果没有一定的突变率，演化便无从谈起。但对个体而言，错误却可能致命。相对少量的未被纠正的突变，就足以把一个细胞推向不受束缚的狂飙生长。这样的事每天都在发生，在每一只动物身上。

Our DNA is continuously assaulted by radiation; environmental pollution; and inflammation driven by unhealthy diets, smoking, chronic stress and other factors. Furthermore, every time a cell divides, thousands of copying mistakes occur. Proofreading mechanisms correct the vast majority of these, yet errors do slip through. This imperfection is critical for a whole species, for without some rate of mutation, evolution would not be possible. For an individual, on the other hand, mistakes can be deadly. A relatively small number of uncorrected mutations can send a cell hurtling toward unfettered growth. This happens daily, in every animal.

身体会监视这些叛逆者；及早识别并铲除基因叛乱，是威廉的免疫系统（immune system）的一项基本职责。这场军备竞赛在每一天的每一小时上演：身体某处的一些细胞开始生长过快或模式错乱，免疫细胞便识别并消灭它们。任何一个异常细胞都胜算渺茫，但经年累月，在数十亿次失败的尝试中，总有一些突变逃过侦测、站稳脚跟、开始增殖。不过，这些早期事件中的大多数并不构成临床意义上的癌症——它们没有引发症状或扩散的能力。

The body monitors for such rogues; early identifi­cation and eradication of genetic uprisings is a fundamental job of William’s immune system. The arms race unfolds in every hour of every day: individual cells in some part of the body start to grow too quickly or in the wrong pattern, and immune cells identify and kill them. The odds are stacked against any one aberrant cell, but over the course of years, among billions of false starts, some mutations evade detection, take hold, and propagate. Most of these early events, however, do not define a clinically meaningful cancer in that they have no ability to cause symptoms or to spread.

随着时间推移，一些肿瘤会获得让细胞生长加速的突变，以及损害错误核查的其他突变。一旦足够多的调控信号被解除，细胞便回归其繁衍与扩散的原初使命。微观尺度上的演化会偏爱那些传播最快、迁徙最远的细胞，而无论整个机体付出什么代价。以乳腺腺体细胞为例——它们唯一的职责就是原地驻守、分泌乳汁——要迁入淋巴管和血管通道，并在骨骼或肺这些陌生环境中落地生根，就需要对它们原本被设定的行为做出大量改变。而在此全程，免疫系统都会持续遏制（即便不能根除）初发的肿瘤。只有那些学会了无限生长、四处远行并躲避免疫系统的罕见例外，才会威胁机体；这些病变，才配得上「癌症」一词所承载的历史与文化恐惧。

Over time, some tumours acquire mutations that allow cellular growth to accelerate and others that impair error-checking. With enough regulatory signals removed, cells return to their primordial imperative to reproduce and spread. Evolution at micro-scale will favour those that can propagate fastest and travel farthest, no matter the cost to the organism as a whole. For breast gland cells, for example – whose one job is to sit in place and produce milk – to migrate into lymphatic and blood channels and thrive in the unfamiliar environments of bones or lungs requires many changes to their originally programmed behaviour. All along the way, the immune system will continue to contain, if not eradicate, incipient tumours. Only those rare exceptions that learn to grow indefinitely, travel and evade the immune system threaten the organism; these are the lesions that merit the historical and cultural terror carried by the word cancer.

然而，我们取得的巨大科学进展，并没有转化到公众的语言之中。作为一名医生，我卡在中间，不得不按我们如今在生物学上的理解向患者解释癌症，而他们仍然在文化层面上理解它。生物学的解说无法完全取代历史的共鸣，翻译所能做的终究有限。

Yet the huge scientific progress we have made has not translated in the public vernacular. As a physician, I am stuck explaining cancer as we now understand it biologically to patients who still understand it culturally. Biologic explication cannot fully supplant historical resonance, and translation goes only so far.

致癌过程是一个分子层面的连续谱。细胞获得最初那些突变时，显微镜下看不到任何变化。最终，当足够多的变化累积起来，一个显微镜下可见的新生物（neoplasm，「新生长物」）出现了；而在那之后的某个时点，这个新生物可能获得转移的能力。在某些类型的癌症中，这种情况频繁且迅速地发生；在另一些类型中则发生缓慢，甚至根本不发生。至关重要的是，有症状或可经其他途径临床检出的肿瘤，其出现可能早于也可能晚于第一批细胞转移——这正是筛查之所以关键的原因：在治愈机会的窗口期内，识别出潜在致命的癌症。

The process of carcinogenesis represents a molecular continuum. Cells acquire their first mutations without any visible changes under the microscope. Eventually, enough changes accumulate that a microscopically visible neoplasm (‘new growth’) emerges and, at some point after that, the neoplasm might acquire the capacity for meta­stasis. In some types of cancer, this happens frequently and very quickly; in others, it happens slowly, if at all. Critically, the appearance of a symptomatic or otherwise clinically detectable tumour may happen before or after the first cells metastasise – which is the reason screening is critical to identify potentially lethal cancers within the window of opportunity for cure.

威廉决定接受前列腺癌筛查，由此引发的连锁反应最终把他带到了我的诊室——这是一个绝佳的例子。当前列腺癌引发症状时，几乎总是已错过治愈时机，而死于前列腺癌，既痛苦又漫长。因此，当PSA——一种无创血液检测——在1990年代上市时，被广泛采用。前列腺癌的诊断率一飞冲天；多年来，它一直是美国及其他国家男性中最为常见的确诊癌症，遥遥领先。许多原本致命的癌症被及时发现并治愈，前列腺癌的死亡率下降了一半。

William’s decision to undergo screening for prostate cancer, setting off the cascade that landed him in my office, is a perfect example. By the time a prostate cancer causes symptoms, it is nearly always too late to cure it, and dying of prostate cancer is both painful and protracted. Therefore, when PSA – a noninvasive blood test – hit the market in the 1990s, it was widely adopted. Diagnosis rates skyrocketed for prostate cancer, which for years has been by far the most common cancer diagnosed among men in the US and other countries. Many otherwise lethal cancers were found and cured, and prostate cancer mortality rates fell by half.

这类发现如此常见，以至于「偶然瘤」（incidentaloma）一词已跻身临床用语

These findings are so common that the term ‘incidentaloma’ has joined the clinical vernacular

然而，到我接受住院医师培训时，已几乎没有疑问：每有一人因手术和放疗免于死亡，就有多个像威廉这样不具威胁的病例经活检检出，并仍被施以激进治疗。在众多因此被过度治疗的男性中，太多人承受着长期副作用。部分由于这一过度诊断问题，2012年，美国预防服务工作组（US Preventive Services Task Force）建议反对任何基于PSA的常规筛查。这一建议达到了减少前列腺癌过度诊断的预期效果，但也推高了死亡率，尤其是黑人男性的死亡率，逆转了此前长达20年的下降趋势。我遇到过被教导说PSA是劣质筛查试验之典型的医学生，也开始见到更多从未被提供过这项检测的不可治愈肿瘤患者——更糟的是，有些人即便主动要求检测，也被拒绝了。

By the time of my residency training, however, there was little question that, for every death prevented through surgery and radiation, multiple non-threatening cases like William’s were detected through biopsy and were still treated aggressively. Of the many men thus overtreated, far too many suffered long-term side-effects. In part because of this problem of overdiagnosis, in 2012 the US Preventive Services Task Force recommended against any routine PSA-based screening. This recommendation had the intended effect of reducing prostate cancer overdiagnosis, but also drove an increase in mortality rates, particularly for Black men, reversing a 20-year decline. I met medical students who had been taught that PSA was the paragon of a bad screening test, and I began to see more men with incurable tumours who had never been offered one – or worse, had been refused a test even when they’d asked for it.

1级分组前列腺癌——级别最低的类型，没有立即扩散或致死的风险——极其常见。在七八十岁死于非癌症原因的男性中，约有一半尸检时可发现这类细胞；在30多岁的男性中，这一比例约为20%。它们并不是临床意义上的癌症，或许更应被理解为正常男性衰老的一个特征。威廉的低级别前列腺癌之所以被检出，是因为他在人到中年时做了一次恰当的PSA筛查；但在此之前，它很可能已经存在多年，而距威胁他的生命，还有许多年。

Grade group 1 prostate cancer – the lowest-grade form, with no immediate potential to spread or cause death – is exceedingly common. Among men who die in their 70s or 80s of non-cancer causes, these cells are found on autopsy in about half; among men in their 30s, the figure is about 20 per cent. They are not clinically meaningful cancers and may be better understood as a feature of normal male ageing. William’s low-grade prostate cancer was detected because he underwent an appropriate mid-life PSA screening, but it had likely been present for years before and was still many years away from threatening his life.

除了通过明确筛查发现的众多癌症之外，如今还有很大一部分是在或多或少偶然的情况下被发现的。我可能为一位疑似肾结石（kidney stone）疼痛的患者开具CT扫描，结果发现除了结石，肾脏、肝脏、胰腺或别的什么器官还有一处异常。这类发现如此常见，以至于那个最初带着调侃意味的词「偶然瘤」（incidentaloma）已经进入了临床用语。偶尔，发现这类病变算是因祸得福。我曾照顾过一位年轻患者，他在南亚旅行时染上的阿米巴感染反倒救了他的命：若不是他因持续不断的腹痛接受影像检查，我们多半来不及发现他那颗硕大的高级别肾癌（kidney cancer），等发现时就太迟了。然而更多的时候，我们查出并激进治疗的是一些小小的病灶——无论它们在显微镜下是何等模样，若不被诊断出来，它们永远不会造成任何问题。

In addition to the many cancers detected through explicit screening, another large propor­tion are detected today more or less accidentally. I may order a CT scan for a patient with pain suggestive of a kidney stone, only to find that in addition to the stone there is an abnormality in the kidney, liver, pancreas or some other organ. These findings are so common that the originally tongue-in-cheek term ‘incidentaloma’ has worked its way into the clinical vernacular. Finding such lesions is occasionally fortuitous. I once took care of a young patient whose life was saved by an amoebic infection he picked up while travelling in South Asia; had he not been imaged for unrelenting abdominal pain, we likely would not have discovered his large, high-grade kidney cancer before it was too late. Much more often, however, we find and aggressively treat small lesions that would never have caused any problem if left undiagnosed, no matter their appearance under the microscope.

公共领域关于筛查的论战之所以变得怨愤而部落化，是因为双方都相信自己是在保护患者免遭不同版本的灾难。外科医生、肿瘤科医生和晚期癌症患者看到的是转移、疼痛、错失的窗口与死亡。初级保健医生或公共政策制定者看到的则是治疗并发症、生活质量的永久性下滑、无谓的焦虑，以及种种成本。双方都是对真实苦难的反应，但后者的叙事更容易消散为无形的统计数字，而前者的共鸣更有力，也更难被原谅。一位患者质问：「你为什么劝我接受放疗，害我落下永久性的肠道问题？」另一位则质问：「你为什么不做个简单的检查？它本可以让我免受多年病痛，看着我孙子孙女长大。」

Public screening debates have grown bitter and tribal because both sides believe they are protecting patients from different versions of catastrophe. The surgeon, the oncologist and the advanced cancer patient see the metastases, the pain, the missed window, the death. The primary care physician or public policymaker sees the complications of treatment, the permanent declines in quality of life, the needless anxiety, and the costs. Both are reacting to real suffering, but the latter narratives more easily diffuse into intangible statistics, whereas the former resonate more powerfully and feel less forgivable. One patient asks: ‘Why did you talk me into the radiation that has left me with permanent bowel problems?’ while the other demands: ‘Why didn’t you order a simple test that could have saved me years of pain and let me watch my grandkids grow up?’

这两种情况我都经常见到：有的患者承受着他们根本不需要的治疗所带来的毁灭性副作用，另一些患者的不可治愈癌症本可以早得多地被发现——其中有些人甚至主动要求过筛查检测，却被拒绝了。我对过度诊断和过度治疗的保留态度，并没有让我变成筛查怀疑论者；但如今的筛查检测，应当只是通往诊断的可能旅程的第一步。威廉略为升高的PSA，本应触发十余种血液、尿液或影像检测之一——这些检测旨在预测潜在致命的前列腺癌是否存在。阴性结果意味着，要么前列腺正常，要么只是1级分组癌症；无论哪种情况，我们都可免去做活检——并多少是明白地告诉患者：如果存在的只是低级别病变，我们和他都完全乐得不去知道它。

I regularly see both situations: patients suffering devastating side-effects of treatment they never needed, and others with incurable cancers that could have been found much earlier – some of whom had even asked for a screening test and were refused. My reservations about overdiagnosis and overtreatment have not made me a screening sceptic, but a screening test today should be only the first step on a possible journey to a diagnosis. William’s slightly elevated PSA should have prompted one of a dozen blood, urine or imaging tests designed to predict the presence or absence of potentially lethal prostate cancer. A negative test implies either a normal prostate or a grade group 1 cancer, and in either case we forego the biopsy – telling the patient more or less explicitly that if there is only low-grade disease present, both we and he are perfectly happy not knowing about it.

对于后者，正确的道路是主动监测（active surveillance）——推迟治疗，密切随访；一旦出现进展迹象，再以充分的治愈预期着手治疗。

For the latter, the right path is active surveillance – deferring treatments and following carefully with every expectation of cure if there are signs of progression.

这一模式近30年前由我所在的中心和其他少数几家机构率先开创，但直到最近才在社区诊疗中渐成气候，且落实情况仍然参差不齐。在美国，太多财务、法律和文化因素都偏向治疗而非观察——在咨询门诊中承认这一现实，多少有些难堪。

This paradigm was pioneered at my centre and a few others nearly 30 years ago, but has only recently gained traction in community practice, and implementation is still highly variable. In the US, too many financial, legal and cultural factors favour treatment over observation, a somewhat embarrassing reality to acknowledge in counselling visits.

我们既不需要、也不想知道每一个过分亢奋的细胞——它们虽有生长的欲望，却没有远行的装备

We neither need nor want to know about every over-eager cell with the desire to grow but without the toolkit to travel

到威廉第一次来我诊室时，他已经在一家医院被排上了手术日程，又在另一家医院被极力推向放疗。许多处于完全相同境地的患者，就是迈不过那个词、那个诊断这道坎；他们无法接受这样的想法：在我看来不必治疗的东西，在他们眼里却是一个危及生命的诊断。尽管我竭尽全力安抚，我的任何形容词都驯服不了「癌症」这个名词，而太多患者最终另找了一位愿意提供不必要治疗的医生。

By the time William had his first visit with me, he had already been booked for surgery at one hospital and strongly pushed toward radiation at another. Many patients I meet in exactly this situation simply cannot get past the word, the diagnosis; they cannot reconcile with the idea that I would not want to treat what they see as a life-threatening diagnosis. Despite my best efforts at reassurance, none of my adjectives can tame the noun ‘cancer’, and too many patients wind up finding another doctor to offer unnecessary treatment.

其他例子比比皆是。皮肤基底细胞癌（basal cell carcinoma）如此普遍，以至于癌症登记系统根本不做统计。它们几乎总能通过简单切除治愈。如果放任不管，它们也可能长得相当大、损毁容貌，但尽管顶着「癌」的标签，它们几乎从不扩散。

Other examples abound. Basal cell carcinomas of the skin are so pervasive that cancer registries do not even track them. They can almost always be cured by simple excision. If neglected, they can grow quite large and can be disfiguring, but almost never spread, despite the carcinoma label.

乳腺导管原位癌（ductal carcinoma in situ）按定义并不侵犯周围组织，近期的试验提示，至少一部分此类病变可以像低级别前列腺癌那样加以监测。在甲状腺癌（thyroid cancer）的问题上，病理学家们已经跨过了这座桥。一组曾被归类为甲状腺癌的病变被重新命名为「伴乳头状核特征的非浸润性滤泡性甲状腺肿瘤」（noninvasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features）——所幸缩写为NIFTP——因为它们配不上「癌」这个字眼的全部分量。同样，一些曾经被称为癌症的小而低级别的膀胱病变，如今被标注为「低度恶性潜能的乳头状尿路上皮肿瘤」（papillary urothelial neoplasms of low malignant potential）。

Ductal carcinoma in situ of the breast by definition does not invade, and recent trials have suggested at least some such lesions can be monitored much like low-grade prostate cancer. In the case of thyroid cancer, pathologists have already crossed this bridge. A subset of lesions once classified as thyroid carcinoma were renamed ‘noninvasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features’ – mercifully abbreviated to NIFTP – because they don’t justify the full weight of ‘carcinoma’. Likewise, some small, low-grade bladder lesions once called cancer are now labelled ‘papillary urothelial neoplasms of low malignant potential’.

与此同时，我们可能正冲向一个过度检出加速、危机四伏的新时代。迄今为止，所有筛查手段都源自针对特定器官系统的检测。而现在，面向「忧心忡忡的健康人」——而且还得是富裕人群——推销的商用全身MRI扫描，必将使我们每年发现的偶然瘤成倍增加。与此同时，所谓的多癌种早期检测（multi-cancer early detection）试验，承诺仅凭一次抽血就能揭示多种癌症的分子信号——DNA片段。如果它们效果良好，或许能挽救生命。但它们也加剧了筛查本已提出的问题：当检测沿着癌症之旅不断回溯——从可触及的肿块，到异常细胞，再到分子层面的先兆——我们命名的究竟是什么？我们既不需要、也不想知道每一个稍显过火的细胞：它有分子层面的生长欲望，却缺少远行所需的基因装备。

At the same time, we may be careening toward a new, fraught era of accelerated overdetection. All screening efforts to date have emerged from organ system-specific tests. Now, commercially available whole-body MRI scans marketed to the ‘worried well’ – and well-off – will surely multiply the number of incidentalomas we find every year. In parallel, so-called multi-cancer early detection tests promise to reveal molecular signals – fragments of DNA – of many cancers from a single blood draw. If they work well, they may save lives. But they also raise the stakes for the question that screening has already raised: when detection moves backward along the cancer journey from palpable lump, to abnormal cells, to molecular harbingers, what exactly are we naming? We neither need nor want to know about every slightly over-eager cell with the molecular desire to grow but without the genetic toolkit required to travel.

如果说「癌症」的边界是模糊的，那么「正常」的边界同样如此。外观正常的细胞常常携带许多癌症典型的突变。生物学在钟形曲线上运转，临床风险亦步亦趋。简言之，我们并不需要把某种异常定性为癌症，才能证明治疗的正当性。我们会常规切除结肠息肉（colon polyp）——它们可能预示癌症，也可能永远不会——因为风险通常微不足道。更极端的例子是，一些携带遗传性BRCA突变的女性选择预防性乳房切除术和卵巢切除术——这是对生活质量影响重大的大手术——只因为可能在未来罹患致命癌症的阴魂实在压得太重。

If the borders around ‘cancer’ are blurry, the boundaries of ‘normal’ are too. Normal-appearing cells often feature many cancer-typical mutations. Biology operates across bell curves, and clinical risk follows suit. In short, we don’t need to designate a given abnormality a cancer to justify treatment. We routinely remove colon polyps that may or may not presage cancer because the risks are typically negligible. In a more extreme example, some women with inherited BRCA mutations elect prophylactic mastectomy and oophorectomy – major surgery with substantial quality-of-life implications – because the spectre of possible future lethal cancer looms so darkly.

医学里那些希腊语和拉丁语的行话切口，长久以来服务医生多过服务患者。把大脑中一块新近描述却尚未了解的区域称作蓝斑（locus coeruleus），便平添了几分「蓝色之地」（blue place）所不具备的权威感。今天，我们对疾病的理解在加深，对患者自主权（patient autonomy）的承诺也在加深。但这一脉络下的一项非常晚近的进展——开放式医疗记录（open medical records）——却让患者直面他们可能读不懂的语言。其结果是，那「你患了癌症」的一刻，往往只属于独自面对一块屏幕的患者。

Medicine’s Greek and Latin shibboleths long served physicians better than patients. Dubbing a newly described but not-yet-understood region of the brain locus coeruleus lent an air of authority that ‘blue place’ would not have done. Today, our understanding of disease has grown along with our commitment to patient autonomy. But a very recent development in this vein, open medical records, exposes patients to language they may not understand. The result is that, all too often, the ‘you have cancer’ moment is lived only by a patient alone with a screen.

做活检时，我会尽量在我们再次交谈之前，预先提醒患者病理报告上可能出现哪些措辞，但我能提前解释的终究有限。这个问题之所以愈发棘手，是因为我们这些癌症专科医生所栖身的边境地带，我们的词汇尚未演化出足以治理它的语言。「正常」与「癌症」的二元语言，曾与疾病在临床上自行显形的漫长时代相称；但在筛查检测和偶然扫描远早于临床命运就把生物学上的可能性识别出来的时代，它已不堪重负。病理学家描述的是显微镜下的所见，但诊断不只是一个描述。当一个词从病理报告上跃起——无论跃入我的诊室，还是患者的客厅——它就变成了预后，变成了身份，变成了恐惧，变成了危机。

When I perform biopsies, I try to forewarn patients of the language they may see on the pathology report before we talk again, but there are limits to how much I can explain in advance. This problem is all the worse because we cancer specialists inhabit a borderland our vocabulary has not yet evolved to govern. The binary language of ‘normal’ vs ‘cancer’ suited the long epoch in which disease announced itself clinically but is badly strained in an era in which screening tests and incidental scans identify biological possibility long before clinical destiny. Pathologists describe what they see through the microscope, but a diagnosis is more than a description. When a word rises off a pathology report, whether into my exam room or the patient’s living room, it becomes prognosis, identity, fear, crisis.

筛查提供了一个变革性的机遇，但唯有当我们能把「真」癌症——那些确有扩散和杀伤能力的——与冒牌者区分开来并单独命名时，它才能在人群中安全推行。研究者和肿瘤科医生常常谈到要区分「小猫」与「狮子」。有些小猫日后可能长成狮子，但那只是少数，而且通常能在狮子逃出兽笼之前就被识别出来。

Screening offers a transformative opportunity but can be implemented safely across populations only if we can distinguish and name ‘real’ cancers – those with the ability to spread and kill – as separate from the pretenders. Researchers and oncologists alike often speak of distinguishing ‘kittens’ from ‘lions’. Some kittens may evolve into lions, but these are the minority and can usually be identified before the lion escapes the enclosure.

我们不该对这群小小的不满分子发动一场无谓的内战

We shouldn’t embark on a needless civil war against these minor malcontents

现代医学热爱分类，却在肿瘤学上离奇地短了板。我们的国际诊断编码系统，在「与其他无毒爬行动物接触」这个条目下设有18个编码，而面对前列腺癌这一横跨广阔生物学谱系的疾病，却只设了一个编码——不论其侵袭性如何，也不论其临床意义如何。如果我们不在命名异常与疾病的边界时多加小心，我们就可能用「癌症」这个名字本身伤害患者。

Modern medicine loves classification yet falls oddly short in oncology. Our international system for diagnostic coding recognises 18 codes in the heading of ‘contact with other nonvenomous reptiles’ yet only a single code for the huge spectrum of biology that is prostate cancer, regardless of aggressive­ness or clinical relevance. If we’re not careful naming the boundary between abnormality and disease, then we risk wounding our patients with the name ‘cancer’ itself.

那么，在2026年，我的同行们当初真的应该把癌症的诊断交到威廉手上吗？当我们四分之一个世纪以来所学的一切都在声嘶力竭地说「并非如此」时，我还有义务去认可那个诊断吗？我们已越来越有能力分辨狮子与幼猫。但数千年之后，karkinos——那只螃蟹——仍在文化记忆中庞大如初，病理报告上的「癌」（carcinoma）一词，仍然意味着疼痛、失去与死亡。我仍会遇到专为安排手术而挂号的患者，当我暗示那可能是错误的第一步时，他们看我的眼神里满是难以置信。

In 2026, then, should my colleagues really have handed William a cancer diagnosis? Am I required to validate that diagnosis when everything we have learned over a quarter-century screams otherwise? Increasingly, we can tell the lions from the kittens. But after thousands of years, the karkinos still looms large in cultural memory, and the term ‘carcinoma’ on a pathology report still connotes pain, loss and death. I still meet patients who book appointments specifically to set up surgery and look at me with incredulity at the suggestion that that would be the wrong first step.

或许，现在正是医学界重新考虑如何定义癌症的时候，正是让诊断回归公众持久认知的时候。在始终萦绕于公众想象的那个意义上，癌症会扩散、会致死。前体病变（precursor lesion）——无论分子层面的还是病理层面的——并非「正常」，但我们并不缺少用来标注它们的其他词汇，也有安全随访它们的工具。它们中的大多数永远成不了什么像样的叛乱，而我们不该对这群小小的不满分子发动一场无谓的内战。如果我们干脆拒绝诊断低级别病变，从而一劳永逸地解决过度诊断问题，我们就能更广泛、更有信心地开展筛查。这样的改变将增进患者与医生之间、初级保健医生与会诊专科医生之间的信任。重新标注也很可能挽救成千上万条生命。

Perhaps it is high time for the medical community to reconsider how we define cancer, and to bring the diagnosis back into line with enduring public perception. Cancer, in the sense that has always haunted the public imagination, spreads and kills. Precursor lesions – molecular or pathological – are not ‘normal’, but we have no shortage of alternative vocabulary with which to label them, as well as the tools to safely follow them. Most will never amount to any meaningful insurgency, and we shouldn’t embark on a needless civil war against these minor malcontents. If we permanently address overdiagnosis by simply declining to diagnose low-grade lesions, we can screen much more broadly and with greater confidence. Such a change would improve trust between patients and their doctors, and between primary care providers and their consulting specialists. Relabelling would also very likely save thousands of lives.

无论我们给它挂上什么标签，威廉的前列腺都需要被关注。他需要我的咨询和监测。有朝一日，我或许需要引导他走向治疗。但在他走进我的诊室、已经围绕一个词重新安排了自己整个人生的那一天，他不需要的，是活在一种尚未威胁到他的疾病的完整阴影之下。修改这个标签，不仅能减少过度治疗，还将最终让我们的语言与我们的科学、与我们的分类体系、与患者的切身处境重新对齐。癌症是一场会扩散、会杀戮的细胞叛乱。而微观边境地带的其余部分，理应得到另一个、更温柔的名字。

William needs attention to his prostate no matter what labels we hang on it. He needs my counselling and monitoring. Someday, I may need to guide him toward treatment. But what he did not need, on the day he walked into my office having already rearranged his life around a word, was to live in the full shadow of a disease that was not yet threatening him. Revising the label would not only reduce overtreatment but would also finally realign our language with our science, with our systems of classification and with patients’ lived reality. Cancer is a cellular rebellion that spreads and kills. The rest of the microscopic borderland deserves a different, gentler name.
