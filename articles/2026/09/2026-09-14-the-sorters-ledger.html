分拣者的账簿

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年9月14日·2分钟阅读

学者踏进图书馆时，注意到两翼书架的奇异秩序。东翼全是借阅频仍的书，封面磨亮，书脊柔软；西翼全是落灰的孤本。他走到借阅台前，对管理员说：「这太整齐了。你一定花了不少力气。」

管理员抬起头。「没有。我只是读每本书的借阅记录，然后把它放到该去的一侧。」

「仅此而已？」

「仅此而已。」

When the scholar stepped into the library, he noticed the strange order of the two wings. The east wing held only frequently borrowed books, their covers polished, their spines soft from use; the west wing held nothing but dust-covered rarities. He walked to the circulation desk and said to the librarian, "This is too tidy. You must have put in a great deal of effort."

The librarian looked up. "No. I simply read each book's borrowing record, then place it on the side where it belongs."

"That's all?"

"That's all."

学者在东翼走了一圈，又去西翼走了一圈。每一本书都在它该在的位置——没有人翻错架，没有人找错书。他回到借阅台：「你没有多请人手，没有追加预算，甚至没有搬动几次——你只是看了一眼记录，指了个方向。这秩序几乎像是凭空出现的。」

管理员把一摞书推到面前，翻开第一本的借阅卡。「凭空，」她重复了一遍，语气没有起伏，「你觉得是凭空。」

The scholar walked a round of the east wing, then another of the west. Every book sat where it should — no one pulled from the wrong shelf, no one searched in vain. He returned to the desk. "You haven't hired extra hands, haven't asked for a larger budget, haven't even moved things many times — you just glanced at a record and pointed. This order almost seems to appear from nothing."

The librarian pulled a stack of books toward her and opened the first one's borrowing card. "From nothing," she repeated, her tone flat. "You think it's from nothing."

学者注意到借阅台下面有一本厚账簿。他弯腰抽出来，翻开。每一页都写满了借阅记录，但大半条目被墨线划去——粗重的、不可辨认的、一层叠一层的划痕。有些纸页被划得几乎透了。

「这是什么？」他问。

「我的账簿，」她说。「我读一本书的记录，就抄一条进去。不抄进去，我就记不住；记不住，就没法分。」

「那这些划掉的——」

「满了就划掉。账簿只有一本。要给新记录腾地方，就得把旧的抹掉。」

The scholar noticed a thick ledger beneath the circulation desk. He bent down, pulled it out, and opened it. Every page was filled with borrowing records, but most entries had been struck through with lines of ink — heavy, illegible, layer upon layer of crossings-out. Some pages were nearly worn through from the scoring.

"What is this?" he asked.

"My ledger," she said. "Every time I read a book's record, I copy an entry in. If I don't write it down, I can't remember; if I can't remember, I can't sort."

"And these crossed-out lines —"

"When it's full, I strike them out. There's only one ledger. To make room for new entries, I have to erase the old ones."

学者盯着那些被划去的条目。「划掉之后还能恢复吗？」

「不能。墨线划下去，就是永久的。」

他沉默了一会儿。「所以你创造的秩序——东翼和西翼的整齐——全靠这些划掉的东西。你每摆对一本书，就得毁掉一条记录。」

「是。」她翻到下一页，继续抄写新条目。「而且划线本身也不干净。墨会渗，纸会破，我的手会抖。这些划痕本身就是乱。」

The scholar stared at the crossed-out entries. "Can they be recovered after you strike them through?"

"No. Once the ink line goes down, it's permanent."

He was silent for a moment. "So the order you create — the neatness of the east wing and the west — all depends on these crossed-out entries. For every book you place correctly, you have to destroy a record."

"Yes." She turned to the next page and continued copying new entries. "And the crossing-out itself isn't clean, either. The ink seeps, the paper tears, my hand shakes. These marks are their own kind of disorder."

学者把账簿放回台面。「馆务会怎么说？」

「他们说再扩两个分馆。同样的方法，同样的排序。」她的笔停了一下。「可我的账簿已经划到最后一百页了。要扩，就得划得更快、更狠。」

「你可以拒绝。」

「我按规则排序，」她说，「记录多的往东，记录少的往西。这不是我想不想的问题。只要有人送书来，我就得读、得抄、得划。唯一能停下的办法是关馆。」

The scholar set the ledger back on the desk. "What does the library board say?"

"They want to expand to two more branches. Same method, same sorting." Her pen paused. "But my ledger is down to the last hundred pages. To expand, I'll have to strike out faster, harder."

"You could refuse."

"I sort by rule," she said. "More records go east, fewer go west. This isn't a question of whether I want to or not. As long as books come in, I have to read, copy, and strike. The only way to stop is to close the library."

学者在一张空椅上坐下。「我刚才说你创造的秩序像凭空出现的。现在我明白了——它不是凭空出现的。它从你的账簿里搬走的。东翼和西翼确实整齐了，可你的账簿比原来更乱。划痕、渗墨、破损的纸页——这些乱加起来，不少于书架上的整齐。」

「不少，」她确认。

「那别的馆呢？」学者问。「听说有几家也用了你的法子。」

「都一样，」她说。「有的馆换了更厚的账簿，有的馆雇了人来划——可划掉的东西不会少。没有一个馆做到过真正的、不付代价的整齐。」

The scholar sat down in an empty chair. "I said earlier that the order you create seems to appear from nothing. Now I understand — it doesn't appear from nothing. It's taken from your ledger. The east wing and the west wing are indeed neat, but your ledger is more chaotic than before. The crossed-out lines, the seeping ink, the torn pages — the disorder they add up to is no less than the neatness on the shelves."

"No less," she confirmed.

"What about other libraries?" the scholar asked. "I've heard several adopted your method."

"All the same," she said. "Some switched to thicker ledgers, some hired people to do the crossing-out — but the amount crossed out never decreases. Not one library has achieved truly costless order."

注解：麦克斯韦妖（Maxwell's demon）指一个按规则逐个测量并筛选个体的存在，将快慢两类分入两侧，看似在不做功的情况下降低系统的熵、违反热力学第二定律。但一旦计入其记忆的代价——测量必须写入记录，账簿终会写满，而擦除旧记录是逻辑不可逆的、必然付出熵的代价——筛选者与被筛选者作为整体的熵并不减少，第二定律依然成立。

Note: Maxwell's demon refers to a being that measures and sorts individuals one by one according to a rule, separating the fast from the slow into two sides, apparently lowering the system's entropy and violating the second law of thermodynamics without doing work. But once the cost of its memory is accounted for — measurement must be written into records, the ledger fills, and erasing old records is logically irreversible and necessarily exacts a price in entropy — the entropy of the whole system, sorter and sorted together, does not decrease, and the second law still holds.
