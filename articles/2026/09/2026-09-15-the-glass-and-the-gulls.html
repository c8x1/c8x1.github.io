玻璃管与海鸥

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年9月15日·3分钟阅读

在我们那座海湾，没人比我更会看天。十个年头，每天天没亮我就站上防波堤，看云从岬角后面怎样翻出来，看风在水面写下些什么。船主们养成了习惯：解开缆绳之前，先来敲我的门。我说走，他们便走；我说歇，网就晒在院子里。十个年头，我没有说错过一次。

In our bay, nobody read the sky better than I did. For ten years I was on the breakwater before first light, watching how the clouds came over the headland and what the wind wrote on the water. The skippers fell into the habit of knocking at my door before they loosed a single line: if I said go, they went; if I said stay, the nets dried in the yard. In ten years I was never wrong once.

我的本事不落纸面，全在身上。海鸥清早蹲满南堤，中午之前必有风；退潮时滩涂泛起那股甜腥的藻味，天就要放晴；灯塔的雾若贴着水面走，夜里的浪就温顺。每一条征兆都是拿真实的下午换来的，哪一年、哪一场风，我记得清清楚楚，像记得自家门槛。

My craft never touched paper; I carried it in my bones. Gulls crowding the south mole at dawn meant wind before noon; the sweet weedy stink rising off the flats at ebb meant the sky would clear; lighthouse fog walking low along the water meant a gentle night swell. Every sign had been paid for on real afternoons — I remembered the year and the particular gale the way you remember your own doorstep.

表哥阿川和我合撑一条船，他这辈子只信一根玻璃管。管里的水银往下走，他就收帆；水银稳住，他就睡觉，统共就这一条。我笑他：天空在我眼前摊成一本书，在他那儿只剩一行字。他不恼，只说：管子不哄人。那年夏末，城里的行商来寻船，要把一批盐赶在大风季之前送到北边的港口，运费开到平日的三倍。我们的柴油机咳了一整个夏天，那笔钱意味着一台新机器，还有一整个安生的冬天。阿川指着日历说这趟日子太贴边，我指着他那根玻璃管说：有你在，怕什么。

My cousin Achuan and I worked one boat between us, and he trusted a single thing: the glass. When the mercury slid down he took in sail; when it held, he slept. That was the whole of his art. I teased him that the sky lay open before me like a spread book while he got by on one line, and he never took offense. The glass does not lie, he said. Late that summer a merchant from the city came looking for a boat to run salt north before the gale season, pay at three times the going rate. Our diesel had coughed all summer; that money meant a new engine and one whole winter clear. Achuan tapped the calendar and called the dates too tight. I tapped his glass and said, with you aboard, what is there to fear.

第三天清晨，我们贴着一条陌生的海岸走。海鸥密密地蹲满一道朝东的石嘴，姿势和蹲在我们南堤上一模一样，我差点笑出声——老朋友，原来也到了这儿。日头升高，风把滩涂的气味送上来，甜腥的，熟得像自己的名字。阿川盯着玻璃管，水银从天亮起就没停过。他说：进港吧，前面有个避风的湾子。我说：这种天在我们那儿，傍晚准转向；误了交货的日子，行商一个子儿也不给。他把帆索塞进我手里：那好，这片海听你的。

On the third morning we ran along a coast we had never seen. Gulls sat shoulder to shoulder on an east-facing spur of rock, in the very posture they took on our south mole, and I nearly laughed out loud — old friends, this far from home. As the sun climbed, the wind brought up a smell off the flats, sweet and weedy, familiar as my own name. Achuan watched the glass; the mercury had been sinking since dawn. Put in, he said, there is a sheltered cove ahead. Weather like this backs off by evening where I come from, I told him, and miss the delivery day and the merchant pays nothing. He put the sheets in my hands. Then this sea is yours.

转向没有来，来的是从正北压下来的一整面黑墙。我们在浪谷里干了半夜，最后是阿川把我从横摆的吊杆底下拽开，他的左臂替他记住了那一下。天亮时我们蹭进一座陌生的小港：桅杆裂了，半舱盐化成了卤水。行商验完货，付的钱刚够修桅杆，新机器从此免谈。那个冬天我们靠赊账过活。阿川的胳膊接上了，却抬不过肩；他再也没有跟我出过海。

The backing wind never came. What came was a black wall bearing down out of the true north. We worked half the night in the troughs, and it ended with Achuan hauling me clear of the swinging boom — his left arm caught it instead. At dawn we crept into a strange little harbor with a cracked mast and half the cargo melted into brine. The merchant looked over the ruined salt and paid just enough to fix the mast; the new engine was never spoken of again. That winter we lived on credit. Achuan's arm knitted crooked and would not lift past his shoulder, and he never sailed with me again.

后来我在那座小港躺了半个月，翻来覆去只想一件事。海鸥蹲在哪儿，取决于那道石嘴怎样折弯清晨的风；滩涂冒不冒那股味，取决于那片泥滩自己的性子。这些是海湾的私事，跟天空的脾气不相干。天空真正的口信寥寥几句，玻璃管里那句算一句——它难看，笨拙，却在每一片海边作数。阿川的一行字，比我的一千个下午走得更远。

I lay two weeks in that little harbor, turning one thing over. Where the gulls sit depends on how a spur of rock bends the morning wind; whether the flats smell at the ebb depends on the temper of that particular mud. Those are a bay's private affairs and say nothing about the sky's temper. The sky's true messages are few, and the one in the glass is among them — plain, clumsy, and faithful on every coast. Achuan's single line traveled farther than my thousand afternoons.

回村以后，船主们起初还来敲门，我说走他们照旧走，那个秋天，从我们湾里出去的船一条也没有出事。可北边的事情一传回来，敲门声就稀了。我不怨谁。南堤还是我的南堤，雾还是贴着水面走；只是遇上远航来问天的人，我把他们领到阿川门口，自己退回我的岸边。

Back home the skippers still knocked at first, and when I said go they went, and that autumn not one boat out of our bay came to grief. But word of the north found its way back, and the knocking grew thin. I hold it against no one. The south mole is still my south mole, and its fog still walks the water; only now, when strangers bound for far ports come asking about the sky, I walk them to Achuan's door and step back to my own shore.

注解：这说的是「过拟合」（Overfitting）：把见过的东西记到分毫不差，连只在本处成立的偶然也一并收下当作章法，于是在旧地盘上从不出错，头一回踏上陌生的海岸就全线失灵，对应「偏差—方差」权衡里高方差的那一端。故事只演了失事，没演药方：现实里，多看几片海，或把几桩旧事扣下不学、留作考题，都能治这个病；而阿川那一行字站在另一端，叫欠拟合，靠放过细节换来安稳。

Note: This is overfitting: a mind that takes in everything it has seen down to the last local accident, so it is never wrong on its home ground yet fails on the first strange coast, the high-variance end of the bias-variance tradeoff. The fable shows the wreck but not the remedies; in real life, seeing more coasts, or holding some old cases back as a test instead of learning them, can cure it, while Achuan's one line sits at the opposite end, underfitting, trading away the details for steadiness.
