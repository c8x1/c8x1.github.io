哲学

世界哲学研究中的认知收敛

Takeshi Morisato·Aeon·2026年9月11日·13分钟阅读·原文

谁来决定什么算作哲学？你或许会认为，如果西方传统中的哲学家多读一些黑人哲学家和亚洲哲学家的著作，他们就会对其他知识传统更加包容。然而，苏格兰第一位黑人哲学教授汤米·柯里（Tommy Curry）指出，恰恰相反，对少数族裔思想家的消费可能给世界哲学的诸多领域——包括非洲哲学和亚洲哲学——带来毁灭性的后果。

Who gets to decide what counts as philosophy? You might think that, if philosophers in the Western tradition read more Black and Asian philosophers, they would be more inclusive of other intellectual traditions. The first Black philosophy professor in Scotland, Tommy Curry, has argued that, on the contrary, consumption of minority thinkers could create devastating consequences for many fields of world philosophies, including Africana and Asian philosophy.

为了解释这一点，柯里在2010年代的一系列文章中将这一现象命名为「认知收敛」（epistemic convergence）。这一概念本身可以追溯到杜波依斯（W E B Du Bois）的「双重意识」（double consciousness）、法农（Frantz Fanon）的「社会发生学」（sociogeny）、休伊·牛顿（Huey Newton）所说的「反动派」（reactionary）、史蒂夫·比科（Steve Biko）的「黑人意识」（Black consciousness），以及西尔维娅·温特（Sylvia Wynter）对「社会发生原则」（sociogenic principle）的改造，类似的类似概念不胜枚举。秉承这一激进黑人传统，柯里指出，白人学术界成功地将同一批黑人思想家整合进来，并非为了批判性地挑战其传统的人性观念——正是这一观念将这些思想家边缘化——而是将他们整合进一个更大的叙事之中，而这一叙事持续为其自身的正当性进行辩护和维护。这就制造了「审讯式环境」（interrogationist milieu），在其中，非洲裔人民的历史著作不断被白人学术界的标准所衡量，尤其是被其自由主义意识形态所衡量。他们被塞进既有的叙事框架之中。他们对建制中任何白人至上主义要素的激进挑战都被仔细删改、修正，并整合进我们在西方哲学史中所认为的「正当」哲学思想形式的延续之中。他们的语言——无论这一语言本身在许多情况下是否是被迫用来讲述他们描述自身种族化（非）存在的生活经验的——也被改写以避免冒犯（例如不使用如今已过时的技术性词汇「Negro」，该词在1960年代有不同含义），通过白人类比加以柔化，或干脆删除。柯里认为，这在我们的黑人思想研究中造成了一场「方法论的与失责的危机」（methodological and derelictical crisis）。

To explain this point, Curry termed this phenomenon ‘epistemic convergence’ in a series of articles in the 2010s. The idea itself can be traced back to W E B Du Bois’s idea of ‘double consciousness’, Frantz Fanon’s ‘sociogeny’, Huey Newton’s term ‘reactionary’, Steve Biko’s ‘Black consciousness’, and Sylvia Wynter’s adaptation of the ‘sociogenic principle’, to name but a few similar concepts. In line with this radical Black tradition, Curry argues that white academia has managed to integrate the same list of Black thinkers, not to critically challenge its traditional concept of humanity that has marginalised these thinkers, but rather to integrate them into the larger narrative that continues to justify and sustain its legitimacy. This creates the ‘interrogationist milieu’, where the historical works of people of African descent are constantly measured by the standards of white academia and, especially, in relation to its liberal ideology. They’re slotted into pre-existing narratives. Their radical challenge to any white-supremacist elements of the establishment is carefully redacted, revised, and integrated into the continuation of what we think of as the ‘proper’ form of philosophical thought in the history of Western philosophy. Their language – regardless of the fact that this language itself was, in many cases, forced upon them to speak about their lived experience in the process of describing their racialised (non-)being – is also paraphrased to avoid perceived offence (by, for example, not using the now archaic technical term ‘Negro’, which carried different connotations in the 1960s), softened through white analogy, or removed. This, Curry argues, creates a ‘methodological and derelictical crisis’ in our study of Black thought.

我们可以用大量篇幅逐一讨论我所提到的每一位黑人思想家及其对种族主义的分析，但我们可以在柯里早期一篇题为《论失责与方法》（On Derelict and Method，2011年）的文章中看到他们所欲表达的要旨。柯里指出，现代学术界中自由主义的兴起使我们得以考察和引用非洲哲学的著作，但仅限于它们揭示「传统欧洲思想之不足」的范围内。换言之，非洲哲学因其对盎格鲁-欧洲过往的批判而开始获得越来越多的认可。然而，由于它携带着深深根植于其所批评的那个传统之中的隐性偏见——而这一传统至今仍然支配着何谓哲学讨论的话语权——将黑人思想家整合进传统白人/西式学术界的整个过程，最终反而验证了欧洲哲学所认为的「人之存在的正当形式」（或哲学中的任何其他主要概念）。换言之，通过阅读黑人思想家的著作，我们对白人建制和白人哲学的信仰不降反升，而激进哲学的潜在影响力则被柔化，转化为一种为西方哲学家服务的「种族疗法」（racial therapy）。

We could spend pages on each of the Black thinkers I’ve mentioned and their analyses of racism, but we can see the essence of what they were trying to say in an early article of Curry’s titled ‘On Derelict and Method’ (2011). Curry argued that the recent rise of liberalism in the modern academy has allowed us to examine and cite the works of Africana philosophy insofar as they highlight the ‘inadequacies of traditional European thought’. Africana philosophy, in other words, has begun to enjoy increasing validation for its critique of the Anglo-European past. But, because it comes with tacit prejudices deeply rooted in the very tradition it criticises, which still dictates the terms of what counts as philosophical discussions, the whole process of integrating Black thinkers into the traditionally white/Western-style academy ends up validating what European philosophy thinks of as the proper form of being human (or any other major conceptions in philosophy). In other words, by reading the works of Black thinkers, our faith in the white establishment and white philosophy paradoxically increases, and the potential impact of radical philosophy is softened and turned into a kind of ‘racial therapy’ for Western philosophers.

柯里指出，我们可以在两个相互影响的过程中察觉这种「欧洲式人类学的人性秩序」（European anthropological order of humanity）。一方面，我们会试图回避和柔化我们前辈大师们的种族主义观点：亚里士多德（Aristotle）对女性和儿童的看法；大卫·休谟（David Hume）臭名昭著的种族主义脚注（及文章），涉及非洲裔及其他族群；伊曼努尔·康德（Immanuel Kant）、黑格尔（G W F Hegel）、马丁·海德格尔（Martin Heidegger）和雅克·德里达（Jacques Derrida）也都曾对非西方哲学及其人民发表过糟糕的言论。但我们将这些段落视为其次，认为它们不及他们严谨哲学思考的首要重要性，便直接跳过——尽管历史上恰恰是这些段落妨碍了西方及西方化的人们认真对待非西方哲学及其（自身的）人民。没有任何一位非西方哲学家在阅读西方哲学时被鼓励以其自身的方式思考其哲学。另一方面，那些严肃挑战了占主导地位的西方关于何为人类或如何做哲学的观念的非洲裔思想家，被接纳进我们的学术讨论之中，其方式却是让我们继续这一验证白人大师的过程，并继续以与那些白人大师大致相同的方式从事哲学研究。一切照旧，而非西方哲学的部分只是为课程增添了一些漂亮的「色彩」。

Curry suggests that we can detect this ‘European anthropological order of humanity’ in two mutually influential processes. On the one hand, we will try to avoid and soften the racist views of our past masters: Aristotle’s views on women and children; David Hume’s notorious racist footnote (and essay) about people of African descent and beyond; Immanuel Kant, G W F Hegel, Martin Heidegger and Jacques Derrida, too, all said terrible things about non-Western philosophies and their people. But we skip right over these sections as secondary to the primary importance of their rigorous philosophical thinking, regardless of the fact that these passages historically discouraged Western and Westernising people from taking non-Western philosophies and their (own) people seriously. None of these non-Western philosophers were encouraged to think about their philosophy on its own terms when reading Western philosophy. On the other hand, Africana thinkers who seriously challenged the dominant Western conception of what it means to be human or how to do philosophy are accommodated in our academic discussion in such a way that we continue this process of validating the white masters, and carry on doing philosophy in much the same way as those white masters did. It’s business as usual, and the non-Western-philosophy component gives some pretty ‘colours’ to the curriculum.

柯里说，杜波依斯被当作「黑人的黑格尔」来读，法农被当作「黑人的萨特」来读——我还要补充，温特也被当作「黑人的波伏娃」来读（或被视为与第二波女权主义协同运作的黑人女权主义的一个碎片）。我们对黑人思想家的当代研究，由大量白人（或白人面具的）学者所主导，他们并非专攻激进黑人传统，结果在无意中维护了现状。这一对黑人思想家的验证过程——一个拔去他们尖刺的过程——并未表达他们生活经验的整体，而是将他们作为新奇之物纳入欧洲中心主义的哲学经典之中。这一过程是高度选择性的。他们变成了某种新奇之物，既补偿又论证了我们对欧洲知识传统正确性的深厚信仰。这种知识整合还使得「多少内容能够启发白人知识分子」成为我们在学术界被允许研究的黑人思想的基本条件。我们只想读黑人哲学中那些帮助我们批判和改进对白人思想家诠释的部分，而略去那些呼吁彻底改造欧洲中心主义哲学传统的部分。

D u Bois is read as the Black Hegel, and Fanon as a Black Sartre, says Curry – and I would add that Wynter is also read as a Black Beauvoir (or as a fraction of Black feminism that works in tandem with second-wave feminism). Our contemporary study of Black thinkers is conducted by a great number of white(-masked) scholars, who do not specialise in the radical Black tradition, and end up unwittingly supporting the status quo. This validation process of Black thinkers, one that removes their sting, does not articulate the totality of their lived experience, but instead incorporates them as novelties in the Eurocentric canon of philosophy. It’s highly selective. They become novelties that somehow both compensate for and justify our profound faith in the correctness of the European intellectual tradition. This intellectual integration also makes the principle of ‘how much of the content can inspire white intellectuals’ the basic condition of what we are allowed to study as Black thinking in the academy. We want to read the parts of Black philosophy that help us critique and improve our interpretations of white thinkers, but omit the parts that call for a complete overhaul of the Eurocentric philosophical tradition.

我们如何以杜波依斯的方式察觉这种「漂白」（whitewashing）——法农所称的黑人天才的「乳白化」（lactification）？柯里指出，我们可以看到一种「人道主义倾向」，在其中我们将欧洲哲学史维持为始终是我们当代包容性人性观念之根基的事物。换言之，西方哲学家期望黑人思想家或多或少与白人自由主义者相同。他们必须温和，比他们的一些同代人更少对抗性，并且必须愿意与白人哲学家的历史合作。我们还期望黑人思想家没有自己的原创思想谱系。正如我们不得不发明「含族」（Hamite）这一新「种族」，以便将黑人法老保留为白人人类的（衍生性）一部分一样，黑人哲学思想被认为不能独立于欧洲思想史而以自身的方式存在。

How do we detect this ‘whitewashing’ in Du Boisian terms, what Fanon dubbed the ‘lactification’ of Black geniuses? Curry argues that we can see a ‘humanist inclination’ where we maintain the history of European philosophy as something that has always been the foundation of our contemporary, inclusive conception of humanity. Western philosophers, in other words, expect Black thinkers to be more or less the same as white liberals. They have to be peaceful and less antagonistic than some of their contemporaries, and they have to be willing to work with the history of white philosophers. We also expect Black thinkers not to have their own genealogy of original ideas. Just as we had to invent the new ‘race’ of Hamite to retain Black pharaohs as a (derivative) part of white humanity, Black philosophical thought was supposed not to exist apart from the history of European thought in its own right.

在当下，西方哲学家继承了这一倾向，他们假定阅读黑人思想家时必须始终将其视为与白人哲学中正在发生的事情进行对话。我们可以将杜波依斯的双重意识解读为某种可以通过黑格尔的主奴辩证法来解决的问题。例如，法农的新人文主义几乎总是被解释为与欧洲存在主义或后殖民理论相一致。任何进入欧洲学术界课程的黑人思想都必须被去激进化，驯化到足以让白人消化的程度。当授课者——包括我自己（尽管我在非西方和西方学术界都接受过教育）——无法通过自身生活经验来代表黑人视角时，情况尤为如此。我是日本人，不是非洲黑人。

Western philosophers inherit this tendency, in our present, by supposing that Black thinkers must be always read as if in dialogue with what is happening in white philosophy. We can read Du Bois’s double consciousness as something we can solve through Hegel’s master-and-slave dialectic. Fanon’s idea of new humanism is almost always interpreted in consonance with European existentialism or postcolonial theories, for instance. Any Black thought that makes it on to the curriculum in the European academy has to be deradicalised and made tame enough for white consumption. This is particularly the case when the instructors, including myself (even though I was educated in both the non-Western and Western academy), do not represent Black perspectives through our lived experience. I am Japanese, not a Black African.

盎格鲁-欧洲人热衷于发掘那些独立得出与他们非常相似结论的印度思想家

The Anglo-Europeans delight in uncovering Indian thinkers who independently arrived at conclusions very similar to their own

这种收缩而安全的非洲哲学研究的最终结果是，黑人思想家总是被描绘为服务于启蒙运动后欧洲哲学的普遍化工程。黑人思想家就是这样被白人学术界所奴役，被呈现为对欧洲哲学传统的统治地位毫无威胁的形象。即使在最好的情况下，这种研究非西方哲学的方式也意味着无法将非洲思想家理解为从其自身高度特定的历史、文化和语言语境中涌现出来的。

The net result of this contracted and safe study of Africana philosophy is that Black thinkers are always portrayed as serving the universalisation project of post-Enlightenment European philosophy. This is how Black thinkers become enslaved to the white academy and get presented as unthreatening to the domination of the European philosophical tradition. Even at its best, this way of approaching non-Western philosophy involves a failure to understand Africana thinkers as emerging from their own highly specific historical, cultural and linguistic contexts.

这一洞见当然也适用于其他殖民语境，包括亚洲哲学。例如，印度哲学家弗林达·达尔米亚（Vrinda Dalmiya）将盎格鲁-欧洲哲学的这种殖民倾向描述为「我们也有」（we have it too）路径。盎格鲁-欧洲人热衷于发掘那些独立得出与他们非常相似结论的印度思想家。但这种兴奋几乎从未导向对他们自身哲学方式的根本批判，也未承认当代印度知识分子可能拥有一种更好的方式来理解他们的世界及其中的位置。这仅仅源于西方思维的自我指涉模型，它将欧洲的一种文化特殊视角抽象为普遍模型。在此，我们假定每一种可能的文化特定的思维框架都可以还原为这一普遍模型，且我们将我们的思维框架投射到我们在其他文化中发现的传统之上是合理的。

This insight is certainly relevant to other colonial contexts too, including Asian philosophies. The Indian philosopher Vrinda Dalmiya, for instance, has described this colonial tendency of Anglo-European philosophy as the ‘“we have it too” approach’. The Anglo-Europeans delight in uncovering Indian thinkers who independently arrived at conclusions very similar to their own. But this delight hardly ever arrives at the fundamental critique of their own way of doing philosophy or acknowledging the possibility that contemporary Indian intellectuals had a better way of understanding their world and place within it. This simply comes from the self-referential model of Western thinking, which abstracts a culturally particular perspective of Europe as the universal model. Here, we assume that every possible particular cultural framework of thinking is reducible to this universal model, and that it makes sense for us to project our frameworks for thinking on to the traditions we find in other cultures.

例如，相对于犹太-基督教的历史观，亚洲的历史犹如一个庞大的多元宇宙。当亚洲的知识分子回溯其知识传统时，他们并非在一个由公元前/公元后——即BCE/CE——所划分的单一时间线（也即一种先前宗教性的人类历史想象）之内来看待自身；在这条时间线中，所有其他亚洲知识传统都须存在。相反，他们每一个都有自己关于历史的理解，与西方对应物截然不同。这就是为什么对一位亚洲哲学专家来说，了解「朝代」的复杂谱系及其历史观念非常重要。

For instance, Asian histories exist like a massive multiverse vis-à-vis the Judaeo-Christian conception of history. When intellectuals in Asia look back on their intellectual traditions, they do not conceive of just a single chronological line of history that is divided by BC/AD, or BCE/CE, (namely, a previously religious imagination of human history), within which every other Asian intellectual tradition exists; instead, each of them has their own conception of history that does not look like the Western counterpart. This is the reason why it’s very important for an Asian philosophy specialist to know the complex genealogy of ‘dynasties’ and their conceptions of history.

对于我们这些身处亚洲的人来说，思考多种宗教传统及其各种历史观念如何总是归属于特定的地点和时间，是非常自然的事情。仅在日本，当我们要考察属于日本知识传统的那一长串哲学家中的任何一位时，就必须同时思考神道教、儒学和佛教的历史以及地缘政治史。如果我们聚焦于其中一个知识传统——佛教——来理解佛教内部的思想，我们至少需要研究属于印度、中国、朝鲜和日本的一系列复杂历史时期，以及在这些不同地区出现的各种佛教思想流派。最重要的是，这种佛教哲学的多元论模型不能代表日本、朝鲜或中国哲学的全部。它也不能作为一个完全独立的思想体系而优越于其他传统。

It is quite natural for those of us in Asia to think about how multiple religious traditions, and their various conceptions of histories, always belong to a specific place and time. In Japan, alone, we have to think about the histories of Shintoism, Confucianism and Buddhism along with geopolitical history when we consider any of the long list of philosophers who belong to the Japanese intellectual tradition. If we focus on one intellectual tradition, that of Buddhism, to make sense of philosophical thought within Buddhism, we must at least study a complex range of historical periods that belong to India, China, Korea and Japan, and the various Buddhist schools of thought that emerged within these different regions. Most importantly, this pluralistic model of Buddhist philosophy cannot represent the entirety of Japanese or Korean or Chinese philosophy. Nor can it be better than the other traditions as a completely separate system of thought.

我们在世界其他地方所目睹的一切，对我们谈论哲学的方式影响甚微

What we witness in the rest of the world does not very much affect the way we talk about philosophy

然而，欧洲哲学倾向于通过投射其所认为的正当思考方式，来为一切可能的思维方式奠定基础。非洲和亚洲思想家将此称为「西方我思」（western cogito），即笛卡尔（René Descartes）公理「我思故我在」中的那个「我思」。正如非洲和亚洲思想家经常指出的，这一「我思」的总体概念呈现了一种哲学思维的普遍模型，带有一种论证欧洲人的思考方式是思考世界和我们自身的最佳方式的倾向。它呈现了一种据称人人共享的单一主体性模型，但这只对那些认同犹太-基督教的世界观、（白）人观及其线性历史观的人才有意义。它还得到了白人优于非白人这一人种学假说的支撑——这是一种为奴隶制和殖民主义辩护的科学观点。显然，在这种思维方式和历史意识下，盎格鲁-欧洲哲学被（欧洲思想家）假定为最佳思维形式，而世界上其他文明和未开化的野蛮人只应向这些最文明的、欧洲化（European-nised）的思想家学习。

However, European philosophy tends to ground the foundation of all possible ways of thinking through its projection of what it thinks of as the proper way to think. Africana and Asian thinkers refer to this as the western cogito , namely the ‘I think’ of René Descartes’s axiom ‘I think, therefore I am.’ As Africana and Asian thinkers often point out, this general concept of ‘I think’ presents a universal model of philosophical thinking, imbued with a tendency to argue that the ways Europeans think are the best way to think about the world and ourselves. It presents a singular model of subjectivity that is supposedly shared by everyone, but which only makes sense to those who have subscribed to the Judaeo-Christian conception of the world, (white) humans and their linear conception of history. It was also backed by the ethnographical assumption that whites are better than non-whites, a scientific viewpoint justifying slavery and colonialism. Obviously, with this mindset and consciousness of history, Anglo-European philosophy was presumed (by European thinkers) to be the best form of thinking, and the rest of the world’s civilisations and uncivilised savages should only learn from them as the most civilised, European(-nised) thinkers.

拉丁美洲哲学中的恩里克·杜塞尔（Enrique Dussel），在其著作《解放哲学》（Philosophy of Liberation，1977年）中将西方我思的这种扩张或自我膨胀称为「商业哲学」（mercantile philosophy）。许多非西方哲学专家，包括万百安（Bryan Van Norden）和杰伊·加菲尔德（Jay Garfield），认为我们可以在西方哲学专家中找到压倒性的证据。重商主义是一种商业安排：我们向他国出售产品，但这种交换不产生互惠利益。我们卖货给他们，唯一目的就是获利。也就是说，在支配他们市场的同时，我们不从我们的商品投放地购入任何东西。杜塞尔说的是，我们对西方哲学理念也做了同样的事，尤其是当我们假定我们在欧洲做哲学的方式应当适用于多数世界中的所有其他人时。他说，这就是原住民哲学在美洲被边缘化的原因。

Enrique Dussel, in Latin American philosophy, called this expansion or self-inflation of the Western cogito ‘mercantile philosophy’ in his book Philosophy of Liberation (1977). Many specialists of non-Western philosophy, including Bryan Van Norden and Jay Garfield, argue that we can find overwhelming evidence of this among specialists of Western philosophy. Mercantilism is a business arrangement whereby we sell our products to other countries in such a way that there is no reciprocal benefit from this exchange. We sell them goods for the sole purpose of making a profit. That is, to dominate their market while we buy nothing from where we drop our merchandise. Dussel is saying that we do the same with Western philosophical ideas, especially when assuming that the way we do philosophy here in Europe should apply to everyone in the rest of the majority world. This is the reason why, he says, Indigenous philosophy has been marginalised in the Americas.

实际上，这是英国高等教育中一个普遍的假设。例如，英国教育部正面临应对大学运营成本的危机，而国际学生入学人数却在下降。即使我们假设这与减少净移民无关，一旦我们向国际学生关上大门——他们的高昂学费无疑是我们机构的财务支柱——我们就立刻想出了向其他国家出口教育的念头。这假定我们的英国教育必须足以与各地区的本土大学竞争，其中一些大学在财务和学术上已成功运营了一个多世纪。（但有谁会想去不在英国本土的英国大学呢？仅东京就有130多所大学。其中一些已存在了一个多世纪。英国大学怎么可能与之竞争？）

This is actually a common assumption in higher education in the United Kingdom. The UK Department for Education, for instance, is facing the crisis of dealing with the cost of running universities while the number of international student enrolments declines. Even assuming that this was not about reducing net migration, the moment we closed the door to international students (whose exorbitant tuition fees are definitely a financial backbone of our institutions), we immediately came up with the idea of exporting our education to other countries. This assumes that our education in the UK must be good enough to compete with other universities in their own regions, some of which have been both financially and academically successful for more than a century. (But who wants to go to UK universities that are not in the UK? There are more than 130 universities in Tokyo, alone. Some of them have been there for more than a century. How can UK universities possibly compete with them?)

同样的膨胀性自我评价也存在于我们的西方哲学实践中，因为我们行业中很多人几乎不假思索地认为，所有人都应当购买盎格鲁-欧洲的理念，而世界上其他地方则没有什么在概念上值得购买的东西。通过推行这种西方理念的单向交易，我们最终培养出了一种共同偏见：我们应该能在西方哲学史中找到关于世界和我们自身需要知道的一切。这就是为什么许多哲学家不认为他们需要通过非洲或亚洲或全球南方其他地区非西方哲学的外部和论争性视角来了解世界和我们自身。我们到那里去只是为了谈论应当被欣赏的西方哲学。但我们在世界其他地方所目睹的一切，对我们谈论哲学的方式影响甚微。

The same inflationary self-assessment is present in our practice of Western philosophy because a lot of us in our profession almost automatically think that everyone should buy Anglo-European ideas, but that there is nothing worth conceptually buying from the rest of the world. Through pursuing this one-way transaction of Western ideas, we end up fostering the shared bias that we should be able to find everything we need to know about the world and ourselves in the history of Western philosophy. This is the reason why many philosophers do not think they need to learn about the world and ourselves through the exterior and polemic lens of non-Western philosophies in Africa or Asia or any other parts of the Global South. We just go there to talk about Western philosophy that is to be appreciated. But what we witness in the rest of the world does not very much affect the way we talk about philosophy.

面对世界哲学的浩瀚海洋，欧洲殖民者的态度是假定从「土著」那里无物可学，相反，他们必须通过欧洲的思维方式来开化。印度哲学家达亚·克里希纳（Daya Krishna）指出，这种对待其他知识传统的问题态度以「神学宿醉」（theological hangover）的形式存活于学术哲学领域，他进一步将其识别为英国殖民主义在印度学术界留下的负面遗产。印度哲学试图镜像欧洲哲学的精华，并将自身传统呈现为不值得严肃哲学对待的东西。换言之，印度的大多数哲学家将西方哲学作为人类思维的巅峰来研究，但这一视角负面影响着他们对自己前殖民时期知识传统的看法，视之为过时和神秘的东西。柯里在对这一跨文化与种族问题的分析中更进一步，他写道：

W hen facing the vast ocean of world philosophies, the European colonial attitude was to assume that there would be nothing to be learned from the ‘natives’ and that, instead, they would have to be civilised through European ways of thinking. The Indian philosopher Daya Krishna argues that this problematic attitude towards any other intellectual traditions has survived in the form of a ‘theological hangover’ in the field of academic philosophy, and he further identifies it as a negative legacy of British colonialism in the Indian academy. Indian philosophy tries to mirror the best of European philosophy and presents its own tradition as unworthy of serious philosophical engagement. Most philosophers in India, in other words, study Western philosophy as the pinnacle of human thinking, but this perspective negatively influences their views on their own precolonial intellectual tradition as something obsolete and mystical. Curry takes a step further in his analysis of this cross-cultural and racial problem when he writes:

我们所需要的是在知识传统中为非洲或亚洲哲学的自主决定开辟一片空间

What is required is creating a clearing for Africana or Asian philosophy’s self-determination among intellectual traditions

如果我们确立了何为像理性人一样思考的「更高人类学秩序」，并以欧洲哲学史为模型，我们就被剥夺了谈论世界哲学其他领域的可能性——例如佛教传统中「无我」（anatman）的悠久历史，它通常只有在考察休谟的内省实验或德里克·帕菲特（Derek Parfit）在《理与人》（Reasons and Persons，1984年）中的佛教式倾向时才被提及。这对亚洲哲学家来说不太成问题。欧洲学者是否理解儒学、道家或佛教的重要性，并不威胁我们的知识传统。但我们必须注意到，对于非洲裔思想家以及一些经历了毁灭性殖民时期的亚洲知识传统来说，情况要严峻和充满敌意得多。

If we establish the ‘higher anthropological order’ of what it is like to think like a rational human being, and model it after the history of European philosophy, we are denied the possibility of talking about any other areas of world philosophies such as the long history of anatman , or ‘no-self’, in the Buddhist tradition, which usually gets a mention only when considering Hume’s experiment in introspection, or Derek Parfit’s Buddhist-like tendencies in Reasons and Persons (1984) . This is less of a problem for Asian philosophers. Whether or not academics in Europe come to understand the importance of Confucianism, Daoism or Buddhism does not threaten our intellectual traditions. But we must be mindful of the fact that the situation is much more dire and hostile for African-descended thinkers, as well as for some Asian intellectual traditions that went through a devastating period of colonialism.

正如法农在《黑皮肤，白面具》（Black Skin, White Masks，1952年）中所指出的，建立在「心理-存在复合体」（psycho-existential complex）之上的内在制裁体系，向被种族化的主体及其社群倾注不安全感，从而使其无法以自身的话语来谈论自我和世界。甚至连将他们生活中发生的事情作为他们的经验来谈论也被否认了。因此，柯里对「认知收敛」的批评目标不能仅仅是呈现一个纯粹的概念难题，而是要呼唤一种存在论层面的觉知：以盎格鲁-欧洲的目光阅读非洲哲学家，犯下了法农所诊断的「所有被殖民人民」面临的一个重大问题。法农说他们是「一个自卑情结已根深蒂固的民族，其本土文化的原创性已被送入坟墓」。这意味着，我们越是带着这种认知收敛的还原性、殖民性解读来阅读非洲和非西方哲学文本，我们对它们就越不尊重或越无知。

As Fanon pointed out in Black Skin, White Masks (1952), the internal sanction system, built on the ‘psycho-existential complex’, pours insecurity into the racialised subject and their communities, thereby making it impossible for them to talk about their self and world in their own terms. Even to talk about what is happening in their lives as their experience is denied. Then, the target of Curry’s criticism in ‘epistemic convergence’ cannot be just presenting a purely conceptual puzzle but calling for an existential awareness that reading Africana philosophers with an Anglo-European gaze commits what Fanon diagnosed as a major problem among ‘all colonised people’. Fanon says that they are ‘people in whom an inferiority complex has taken root, whose local cultural originality has been committed to the grave’. This means that the more Africana and non-Western philosophical texts we read with this reductive, colonial reading of epistemic convergence, the less respectful or knowledgeable we become of them.

为什么坚持要求世界哲学专家讨论非洲或亚洲思想家所发展概念的实际历史生成，及其与社会、历史、语言和存在条件的关系？答案是：以这些思想家自身的方式理解他们的世界和理念，是对抗结构性种族主义的更广泛斗争的一部分——这种种族主义不断试图扭曲原住民思想家并美化盎格鲁-欧洲的方法。要揭示这种同化现象中的一种智识奴役，我们需要进行一种「知识谱系学」（intellectual genealogy）考察，其目标无非是双重过程：一是检查学术界走向认知收敛的倾向，二是为非洲或亚洲哲学在知识传统中的自主决定开辟一片空间。

Why insist that world philosophies specialists talk about the actual historical genesis of concepts developed among Africana or Asian thinkers in relation to their social, historical, linguistic and existential conditions? The answer is that understanding these thinkers’ worlds and ideas on their own terms is part of a wider battle against structural racism, a racism that constantly aims at distorting Indigenous thinkers and glorifying Anglo-European approaches. What is required to call out a sort of intellectual enslavement in this assimilative phenomenon is to conduct an ‘intellectual genealogy’, which aims at nothing but the two-fold process of checking the academic propensity towards epistemic convergence, and of creating a clearing for Africana or Asian philosophy’s self-determination among intellectual traditions.

我们能做些什么来培养阅读多数世界思想家的文化和方法论敏感性？我们如何遏制这种正在同化和同质化大量非西方思想的认知收敛现象？首先，我们应当认识到我们自己可能就是问题的一部分。我们不应将自己从以下可能性中豁免：我们正在助长结构性种族主义，并使那些研究边缘化传统思想家的人举步维艰。马提尼克诗人和政治家艾梅·塞泽尔（Aimé Césaire）在其文章《殖民主义话语》（Discourse on Colonialism，1955年）中写道，希特勒不仅仅是人文主义最大的敌人，他的遗产还活在最受尊敬的人文主义者——法国作家茹勒·罗曼（Jules Romains）及其合作的人类学家和哲学家——身上。这些知识分子通过批评二战期间德国占领和犹太人大屠杀的暴行来将自身呈现为人文主义的捍卫者，但他们对非洲人民和文化的看法与希特勒如出一辙。塞泽尔的建议是，与其将现代史最黑暗的时刻作为与我们无关的他者来指责，不如认识到我们每个人内心都有自我中心主义和危险意识形态的影子，尽管我们看似善意的人道主义倾向。

W hat can we do to build our cultural and methodological sensitivities for reading majority-world thinkers? How can we stem this phenomenon of epistemic convergence that is assimilating and homogenising much of non-Western thought? First, we should recognise that we may be part of the problem. We should not exempt ourselves from the possibility that we are aiding structural racism, and making it difficult for those who work on thinkers from marginalised traditions. The Martinican poet and politician Aimé Césaire wrote in his essay Discourse on Colonialism (1955) that Hitler wasn’t just the biggest enemy of humanism, but that his legacy lives on with the most respected humanist, the French writer Jules Romains, and his associate anthropologists and philosophers. These intellectuals presented themselves as defenders of humanism by criticising the atrocity of the German occupation and the Holocaust during the Second World War, but their views on African people and cultures were very much in line with Hitler’s. Instead of pointing our fingers at the darkest hours of modern history as something other than ourselves, Césaire’s suggestion is to recognise within each of us our self-centeredness and a hint of dangerous ideology, despite our seemingly benign humanist inclinations.

哲学系学生花一年时间只读黑人思想家的著作，将是一个很好的主意

It would be a great idea for a philosophy student to spend one year reading just Black thinkers

当我们拥抱非西方哲学传统时，我们应当检视我们的善意是否出于一种欲望——即验证自己属于那个体系，而该体系尽管有包容性的修辞，却仍在继续边缘化和非人化非西方人民。这就是比科所说的意思：问题不仅仅是保守的种族主义者，还有那些自由主义者，他们规定了南非黑人学生可以如何为自主决定而对抗种族主义。他们实际上被告知要退回队列中等待历史选择他们的命运，而不是拿起武器捍卫他们的尊严和尊重。鉴于当前欧洲学术界的构成——哲学家伦纳德·哈里斯（Leonard Harris）在1990年代曾将其描绘为以中上阶层白人为主，拥抱与三K党（Ku Klux Klan）相同的意识形态追求——在我们谈论世界哲学时，分析我们的方法论和动机可能是一个很好的思维训练。良好的意愿是不够的。

When we embrace non-Western philosophical traditions, we should check if our friendliness is not motivated by our desire to validate ourselves as a part of the system that, nevertheless, continues to marginalise and dehumanise non-Western people despite a rhetoric of inclusivity. This is what Biko meant when he said the problem was not just conservative racists but the liberals who dictated the ways in which Black students could fight against racism for their self-determination in South Africa. They were effectively told to stay back in line and wait for history to choose their fate, rather than to take up arms to defend their dignity and respect. Given the current make-up of academia in Europe, which the philosopher Leonard Harris once characterised , in the 1990s, as predominantly upper-middle-class whites, embracing the same ideological aspirations as the Ku Klux Klan, it might be a good mental exercise to analyse our methodology and motivations when we talk about world philosophies. Good intentions are not enough.

第二，我们应当花更多时间与黑人思想家及亚洲思想家相处。我用第二语言英语学习了八年欧洲哲学。如果你让我教授一门涵盖从柏拉图（Plato）到海德格尔的任何思想家的课程（以欧陆风格），我能做到。我也能够在不对他们进行文化挪用的情况下自如地谈论他们中的任何一位。让我达到这种能力的原因，是我花在阅读他们著作以及与许多在这一传统中完成知识发展的人交流上的时间。我认为，哲学系学生花一年时间只读黑人思想家或世界哲学任何其他子领域的著作，将是一个很好的主意。（如果可能的话，我还建议他们前往这些思想家曾生活过且被尊为其知识经典组成部分的那些土地。）我经常推荐我的学生去苏格兰爱丁堡一家只出售女性作家作品的书店。这一精心策展的空间为少数群体作家留出了更多位置，并对我们的意识产生了某种影响。同样，如果你搭一个小书架，花一年时间用非洲思想家和作家的书来填满它，你就会开始更清晰地听到他们的声音，也许就不太会再仅仅把他们当作推进分析哲学或欧陆哲学进路的工具来阅读。

Second, we should spend more time with Black and Asian thinkers. I studied European philosophy for eight years in English as my second language. If you asked me to teach a course on it including any thinkers from Plato to Heidegger (in continental style), I could. I am also comfortable talking about any of them without culturally appropriating them. What allowed me to achieve this competence is the time I spent with their works and talking with many people whose intellectual development took place within this tradition. I think it would be a great idea for a philosophy student to spend one year reading just Black thinkers or any other subfields of world philosophies. (If possible, I’d also recommend them to travel to the lands in which these thinkers lived and are revered as a part of their intellectual canon.) I often recommend my students to go to a bookstore in Edinburgh, Scotland, that sells the works of only women writers. This curated space gives more room for minority writers and does something to our consciousness. In the same way, if you built a small bookshelf and spent one year filling it with books by Africana thinkers and writers, you would begin to hear their voices more clearly and might feel less tempted to read them solely as an instrument for advancing analytic or continental approaches to philosophy.

最后，我认为我们应当在学习语言方面付出更多努力。在日本，一位法国哲学专家如果不精通法语是不可想象的。一位受人尊敬的学者会从法语翻译数十本书到日语，而且除了其日语著作和文章之外，还会尝试以法语发表一些文章。非西方学者在关于西方知识方面的学术勤奋，与其研究东方哲学的西方同行相比，存在一种不平衡。书写非西方哲学的西方学者至少应当表现出一种意愿，在阅读译文时学习并参阅原文。我们要求学生在研究柏拉图或托马斯·阿奎那（Thomas Aquinas）时懂一些希腊语和拉丁语；我们应当对描述目标子领域世界哲学中思想家的历史和语言更加开放。认知收敛是一种将类西方思维无意识地同化入更广泛经典之中的过程，同时伴随着对那些无法整齐嵌入既有框架的元素的相应回避。是时候更深入地思考我们在研究世界哲学时究竟在做什么了。将非西方思想家转化为我们自身的驯化版本，是一种我们亟需超越的智识殖民主义。

Lastly, I think we should make more effort learning languages. It is unthinkable for a specialist of French philosophy in Japan not to be fluent in French. A well-respected scholar would translate dozens of books from French into Japanese and, in addition to their books and articles in Japanese, they would try to publish some articles in French. There is an imbalance between non-Western scholarly diligence over their knowledge of the West when compared with their Western counterparts studying Eastern philosophy. Western academics writing about non-Western philosophies should at least show a willingness to learn and consult the original when reading its translation. We ask students to know some Greek and Latin when studying Plato or Thomas Aquinas; we should be more open to the history and the language that describes the thinkers in reference to the target subfield of world philosophies. Epistemic convergence is a kind of unconscious assimilation of Western-like thinking into the broader canon, combined with a corresponding turning away from those elements that don’t fit neatly into a pre-existing framework. It’s time to think harder about what we do when we think about world philosophies. Turning non-Western thinkers into tame versions of ourselves is a kind of intellectual colonialism that we need to get beyond.
