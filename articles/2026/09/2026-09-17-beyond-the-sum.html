数目之外

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年9月17日·3分钟阅读

托尔姆镇（Tolm）把人记在两本册子上。荒春里放粮的人家，后代进红册；借粮的人家，后代进灰册。粮是当年就吃尽的，那笔数目却抄在红册的扉页上，长到要占两行墨；数目底下的章程，只认一个把灰行变红的条件：把那笔数目一个子儿不少地交清。三百年里没有一家人凑近过它。最殷实的灰册人家攒了一辈子，也只攒到它的一个零头；儿孙把钱抬进大厅，司秤的过完秤，摇了头，钱又从原路抬回街上。

Tolm kept its people in two rolls. The descendants of the grain-lenders of the hungry spring went into the red roll; the descendants of the borrowers went into the grey. The grain itself was eaten within the year, but the sum of it was copied onto the red roll's first page, a figure long enough to take two lines of ink, and the statute beneath it admitted one condition only for turning a grey line red: the sum paid entire. In three hundred years, no family had come near it. The richest grey houses saved all their lives and reached a fraction; their heirs carried the coins up to the hall, the weigher weighed them and shook his head, and the coins went home by the same street.

可灰册的行，还是出现在了红册里。查籍人的表格记了三百年，平均下来约莫一代人一行；镇子早就不把每一桩当丑闻来办，给这事起了名字。街面上叫它「月光过籍」，想象着梯子和被买通的书记；查籍人管它叫「无凭过籍」，因为他们翻遍卷宗，什么也按不到它头上。没有交割，也没有伪造——笔迹对得上，墨的年头也对得上。过籍的人家不更虔诚，不更走投无路，也不更有大厅里的朋友；事前没有任何记号落在他们身上。看守在大厅里守过整年：门锁着，封条完好，烛芯一寸寸烧到底。过籍落在哪一夜，那一夜看上去就和别的夜没有两样；那户人家是站在广场上开读时才知道的，说不出是哪个时辰，因为没有哪个时辰和其余的不同。

And still, grey lines turned up in the red roll. The auditors' tables put it, across three hundred years, at something like one line to a generation, and the town had long since stopped treating each case as a scandal and given the thing a name. The quarter called it a moonlight transfer and pictured ladders and bribed clerks; the auditors, who kept the only complete record, called it an unwarranted transfer and could pin nothing to it. There was no payment and no forgery — the hand of the writing and the age of the ink always proved true. The crossed families were not the devout ones, not the desperate ones, not the ones with friends in the hall; nothing marked them beforehand at all. Watchmen sat up whole years in the hall with the rolls: doors locked, seals unbroken, the candle burning down. Whatever night a crossing fell on, it looked like every other night; the family itself learned at the quarterly reading, standing in the square, and could not name the hour, because no hour had felt different from the rest.

查籍人还看出一件事，也记在表格里。凡有过籍记录的，落的都是押账最薄的人家——又是那个荒春，借了几袋谷，押书上只占小小一行。八十年前的寒冬立过一道铁契，那时借粮的人家条件加倍；八十年里，铁契底下的行一行也没有动过。押得越深，过籍越稀；深过某个界限，就再没有过。这些也拦不住人想花钱买路。每年秋天，有穿软靴的男人到街面上来卖门路。头一年秋天，卡里家（Kari）把两间临街的屋子抵了钱，交给其中一个，说好开春前他们的行变红。查籍人的表格写着：三百年里，凡是给这种男人付过钱的，行没有动过一根头发丝的宽度。

The auditors noticed one more thing, and kept it in their tables. Every crossing on record had landed on a family carrying one of the thin liens — the hungry spring again, a few sacks borrowed, a small line in the pledge-book. The iron pledge of the hard winter eighty years back had doubled the terms for whoever borrowed then, and in eighty years not one of those lines had moved. The deeper the lien, the rarer the crossing; past a certain depth, never. None of it kept people from trying to buy the crossing. Soft-booted men came through the quarter every autumn selling arrangements. One had taken the Kari family's money the autumn before — two street-front rooms mortgaged for it — promising their line red by spring. In three hundred years, the tables said, no family that had ever paid such a man had seen its line move by the width of a hair.

尤娜（Una）在灰册街靠缝补过活。她的儿子布兰（Bran）的肺，每年从头一场霜起就哨响，一年一年，哨响在胸口坐得越来越低。面包房炉膛上头的暖阁按季出租，那笔租金，她屋里只有那把好剪刀当得出来；广场边上的当铺肯放这个数。那年秋天，软靴男人也敲了她的门。他讲起夜里翻页的人，讲起行可以像领人过院门那样被领过去；又问起孩子，提到那种空气一直暖着的作坊。他开的价钱，是那把剪刀当得出的全部。她把剪刀搁在膝上坐了半夜；秤的一头是她吃饭的家伙，另一头是一个冬天的呼吸。

Una took in mending in the grey quarter. Her boy Bran had lungs that whistled from the first frost every year, and each winter the whistling sat lower in his chest. The loft over the bakery's ovens rented by the season, at a figure only her good shears would raise; the pawnshop off the square would advance that much on them. The soft-booted man came to her door that autumn too. He spoke of the man who turned the pages at night, and of lines that could be led across like a man through a courtyard gate; he asked after the boy, and mentioned workshops where the air stayed warm. The price he asked was the shears' worth, all of it. She sat half the night with them in her lap; on one side of the scale, the tool she ate by; on the other, a single winter's breath.

她把剪刀送进了当铺，暖阁一季的租金就此付清；此后她裁料全凭面包房太太那把剪面粉袋口的旧剪子，一件件活计都慢了下来。布兰那一冬能平躺着睡了，这是三个冬天里的头一回；开春时他长高了一指，哨响退了回去，等着下一场霜。软靴男人赶在烛节前就走了，往没听过他名号的镇子去了。春季开读，卡里家的名字照旧念在灰册里，还是老位置；到夏天，那两间临街屋子的门上换了放债人的锁。紧接着，同一场开读的同一口气里，加尔东家（Galdon）念进了红册：扫烟囱的人家，一架梯子全家合用，什么也没张罗过，开读也从没缺过一回。老加尔东摘下帽子又戴上，像被一滴雨找着了后颈。

She carried the shears to the pawnshop, and the loft over the ovens was paid up for the season; after that she cut with the baker's wife's spare pair, the ones kept for slitting flour sacks, and every piece went slower. Bran slept flat on his back that winter, which he had not managed for three winters, and by thaw he had grown a finger-width, the whistling withdrawn to wait for the next frost. The soft-booted man was gone before Candlemas, off toward towns that had not heard of him. At the spring reading the Kari name came up in the grey roll, where it had always come up, and by summer their two street-front rooms carried a moneylender's lock. Then, in the same breath of the same reading, the Galdons were read in red: chimney sweeps with one ladder among them, who had never arranged anything and had never once missed a reading. Old Galdon took his hat off and put it back on, the way a man does when a single raindrop finds the back of his neck.

查籍人把加尔东家从头到尾查了一遍，像对每一户过籍的人家那样，写下的也是那两句：没有付出，屋顶底下没有添任何新东西。梯子还是那一架；第二天一早，他们照旧扫同一些烟囱，收同样的钱。他们家的女儿当周就签了钟表匠的行册，因为从此签得了；手艺是一年一年学出来的，和哪个学徒都一样。过籍改的是她能签的东西，不是她手上的功夫。尤娜自己的名字，一辈子都念在灰册里；她站在广场上听，像听天气。总查籍人在一封镇上从未见过的信里写过一回：这些过籍若稠起来，托尔姆的炉火一个季节就会烧空、熄灭；三百年来每个早晨都还亮着，靠的正是这一滴一滴的慢。

The auditors went through the Galdons the way they went through every crossed family, and wrote down what they always wrote: nothing paid out, nothing new under the roof. The ladder was still the one ladder; the morning after, they swept the same chimneys for the same coins. Their daughter signed the clockmakers' roll that same week, because now she could, and she learned the trade the way anyone learns it, year over year. The crossing had changed what she could sign, not what her hands could do. Una's own name was read in the grey roll all her life; she stood in the square and listened the way one listens to weather. The chief auditor wrote it once, in a letter the town never saw: if the transfers ever thickened, Tolm's fires would burn through a season and go out; it was the slowness of the drip that kept three hundred years of mornings lit.

注解：这是量子隧穿（quantum tunneling）——能量不够翻过一道墙的微观粒子，仍有机会出现在墙的另一侧；单次既无法预言也无法挑选，定死的只有机会的大小，由墙的厚薄高矮决定，而粒子过去之后能量不多不少、无借无还。墙越厚，这样的事越罕见；到了日常物件的尺寸，几乎就从不发生。

Note: This is quantum tunneling — a microscopic particle with too little energy to climb a wall can still turn up on the far side of it; no single crossing can be predicted or chosen, only the size of the chance is fixed, set by how thick and tall the wall is, and the particle crosses with its energy unchanged, nothing borrowed and nothing repaid. The thicker the wall, the rarer it gets; for things of everyday size, it all but never happens.
