第二只药包

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年9月14日·2分钟阅读

那个孩子活了下来，全村都说是奇迹——因为学徒在最后一刻换掉了手中的药包。孩子的母亲跪在草棚里哭，旁人却窃窃私语：明明只剩两只药包，换与不换应当各占一半，这小子不过是赌徒运气好罢了。

The child survived, and the whole village called it a miracle — because the apprentice swapped the medicine pouch in his hand at the very last moment. The child's mother wept, kneeling in the thatched shed, but the onlookers muttered: with only two pouches left, switching or staying should be fifty-fifty; the boy was merely a lucky gambler.

三天前，瘟疫抽干了这个孩子的最后一丝气力。老药师从三只一模一样的布包中取出一只，交给学徒——三包里只有一包装着救命的退热草，另两包只是苦味的枯叶，谁也辨不出。学徒接过第一只药包，手心全是汗。

Three days earlier, the plague had drained the last strength from the child. The old herbalist took one of three identical cloth pouches and handed it to the apprentice — only one pouch held the fever-breaking herb that could save a life, the other two held only bitter dried leaves, indistinguishable to anyone. The apprentice received the first pouch, palms slick with sweat.

老药师一生辨药无数，鼻息一闻便知哪包藏着退热草。他沉默良久，依着药师行的铁规——既不许直接点破哪包是真药，却必须从学徒未选的两包中弃去一只无效的——若两只都无效，便任弃其一。他拎起第三只药包，当众倒出枯叶，说：「这包救不了人。」随后他问学徒：「你手里那包，还是换作这剩下的一包？」

The old herbalist had discerned medicines all his life; one breath of each pouch told him which held the fever-breaking herb. He fell silent for a long while, then, bound by the iron rule of the apothecaries' guild — he must not directly name the true pouch, yet he must discard one ineffective pouch from the two the apprentice had not chosen — if both were ineffective, either would do. He lifted the third pouch, poured out its dried leaves before everyone, and said: "This one cannot save anyone." Then he asked the apprentice: "Keep the pouch in your hand, or switch to the one that remains?"

棚子里炸了锅。村里的识字人纷纷开口：三包去了一包，剩两包，一真一假，换不换都是一半对一半，何必冒失改主意？孩子的母亲也攥紧学徒的手腕，哭着说别拿命去赌。学徒盯着自己掌心的第一只药包，盯着桌上那孤零零的第二只，胸口像压着磨盘。

The shed erupted. The village's lettered folk spoke up one after another: three pouches minus one leaves two, one true and one false, switching or staying is half against half — why recklessly change your mind? The child's mother gripped the apprentice's wrist, weeping, begging him not to gamble with a life. The apprentice stared at the first pouch in his palm, then at the lone second pouch on the table, his chest pressed as though by a millstone.

他把第一只药包放回桌面，拿起了第二只。退热草就在里面——孩子服下半个时辰后烧便退了。日后有人问学徒为何敢换，他说，不是凭胆量，是老药师的那只手告诉他：当初三只药包里，真药落在自己手中只有三分之一可能；而老师知道一切，他别无选择，只能从未选的两包中拿走一包假的，于是那两包合起来的三分之二指望，全数压进了剩下这一只没被碰过的药包里。

He set the first pouch back on the table and picked up the second. The fever-breaking herb was inside — within half an hour of the child taking it, the fever broke. Years later, when someone asked the apprentice why he had dared to switch, he said it was not courage; it was the old herbalist's hand that told him: of the three pouches to begin with, the true medicine had only a one-third chance of being in his hand; and the old master knew everything, he had no choice — he could only remove one false pouch from the two not chosen, so the two-thirds of hope that had rested across both unchosen pouches flowed entirely into this one remaining, untouched pouch.

村里最有学问的三位老秀才当初都咬定是一半对一半，其中一位还搬出半生所学的律例来论证「换与不换无异」。直到老药师取出一个布袋，装进三颗石子——一颗红、两颗白——让人连摸了一百回，每回照旧弃去一颗白石子再问换不换。百回之中，换而摸中红石子的次数恰是六十六次上下，三位老秀才这才不作声了。

The three most learned elders in the village had insisted it was half against half; one of them even invoked a lifetime of legal reasoning to argue that switching and staying were no different. It was not until the old herbalist brought out a cloth bag, filled it with three pebbles — one red, two white — and had someone draw a hundred times in a row, each time discarding a white pebble and asking whether to switch, that they fell silent: of the hundred rounds, the switchers drew the red pebble roughly sixty-six times.

注解：这是蒙提霍尔问题（the Monty Hall problem）——三只药包如三扇门，真药如车，枯叶如羊；掌握全部信息且受规则约束的老药师必须从未选药包中弃去一只无效的，这使得「换」的胜率并非直觉中的一半，而是三分之二，因为最初那三分之二的概率被压缩进了唯一未碰过的那只药包。

Note: This is the Monty Hall problem — the three pouches are the three doors, the true medicine the car, the dried leaves the goats; the herbalist, holding all the information and bound by rules, must discard an ineffective one from the unchosen pouches, which makes the odds of switching not the intuitive one-half but two-thirds, because the initial two-thirds of probability is compressed into the one pouch that was never touched.
