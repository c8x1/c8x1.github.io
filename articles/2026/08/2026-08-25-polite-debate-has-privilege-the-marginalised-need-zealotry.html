礼貌的辩论是一种特权。被边缘化的人需要狂热

Geertje Bol·Aeon·2026年8月25日·11分钟阅读·原文

1706年，一位英国女子去拜访朋友凯瑟琳（Katherine）。凯瑟琳的丈夫弗朗西斯·阿特伯里（Francis Atterbury）是一位显赫的政治家和主教。听说弗朗西斯刚写完布道文，凯瑟琳的朋友便询问能否带回家拜读。弗朗西斯欣然应允，无疑期待着这位女士的几句恭维。然而，布道文很快被退还，上面附有这位女士大量的批评和修改意见。弗朗西斯大为震惊：他向友人抱怨说，这位女士缺乏「得体的举止」，言辞「冒犯而骇人」。他承认：「我惧怕与她交锋。」

In 1706, an English woman visited her friend, Katherine. Katherine’s husband, Francis Atterbury, was a prominent politician and bishop. Upon hearing that Francis had just finished writing his sermon, Katherine’s friend asked if she could take it home to read it. Francis graciously agreed, no doubt expecting to receive some compliments from the lady. But his sermon was promptly returned to him by the woman with copious criticism and notes for its improvement. Francis was shocked: he complained to a friend that the lady lacked a ‘decent manner’ and that she was ‘offensive and shocking in her expressions’. He admitted: ‘I dread to engage her.’

这位「不得体」而「骇人」的女子正是玛丽·阿斯特尔（Mary Astell，1666-1731）。如今她最为人所知的身份是英国第一位女权主义者，其保守政治与先锋女权主义的悖论式结合长久以来令学者困惑。到1706年，阿斯特尔无疑已习惯了被人斥为冒犯、恼人和失礼。她的大部分党派小册子写于18世纪头十年，字里行间总是布满了对异议男性的犀利攻击（通常——但并非 exclusively——指向辉格自由派，其中最著名的是约翰·洛克（John Locke））。她所攻击的小册子作者之一詹姆斯·欧文（James Owen）斥她过于愤怒、狂热而「毒辣」——这一打发方式意味着他不必真正去应对她那些犀利而棘手的论证。

This ‘indecent’ and ‘shocking’ woman was Mary Astell (1666-1731). Today, she is best known as England’s first feminist, whose paradoxical mix of conservative politics and pioneering feminism has long puzzled scholars. By 1706, Astell was no doubt used to being called offensive, irritating and impolite. She wrote most of her partisan pamphlets in the first decade of the 18th century, always studded with sharp attacks on men she disagreed with (often, but not exclusively, those on the side of the Whig liberals, the most famous of which was John Locke). One of the pamphleteers she attacked, James Owen, dismissed her as overly angry, zealous and ‘virulent’ – a dismissal that meant that he did not have to actually engage with her sharp and difficult arguments.

半个多世纪后，另一位积极参政的女性遭遇了类似的对待。历史学家兼政治理论家凯瑟琳·麦考利（Catharine Macaulay，1731-91）撰写了一本犀利的小册子攻击埃德蒙·伯克（Edmund Burke）——后者至今仍被视作现代保守主义之「父」和有组织的政党的首位主要倡导者。麦考利针对的是伯克那篇如今声名远播的为政党辩护之作《关于当前不满原因的思考》（Thoughts on the Cause of the Present Discontents，1770年）。伯克没有正面回应她的批评，而是写信给友人说：「这位亚马逊女郎是他们当中最勇猛的斗士……比尔灵斯门里再厉害的泼妇也比不过我们这位共和派女杰的爱国式破口大骂。」他借由凶悍女战士和粗声大气、男性化女性的形象来嘲弄麦考利，却又不无敬畏。与阿特伯里如出一辙，伯克承认自己「不敢回答她」。

More than half a century later, another politically active woman received similar treatment. The historian and political theorist Catharine Macaulay (1731-91) had penned a sharp pamphlet attacking Edmund Burke, who remains widely received as the ‘father’ of modern conservatism and the first major proponent of organised political parties. Macaulay had taken issue with Burke’s now-famous defence of party, Thoughts on the Cause of the Present Discontents (1770). Instead of dealing with her critique head on, Burke wrote to his friend that ‘the Amazon is the greatest champion amongst them … no heroine in Billingsgate can go beyond the patriotick scolding of our republican Virago.’ Invoking the image of the fierce female warrior and of the loud, masculinised woman, he ridiculed Macaulay, but not without a sense of awe. Much like Atterbury, Burke admitted he was ‘afraid to answer her’.

但伯克对麦考利的轻慢并非单纯的粗鲁——这与他关于党派政治应当是何种面貌、谁有资格践行它这一更宏大的构想完全一致。正是在引发麦考利攻击的那本册子里，伯克主张，党派政治最好被理解为一种精心选择的、忠诚的友谊，存在于礼貌、理性、志同道合的男性之间，他们彼此「十之八九」意见一致。唯有如此，党派政治才能「在不破坏和谐」的前提下存在。相形之下，麦考利的小册子失礼、激烈而狂热。因此在伯克眼中，麦考利那一路党派政治无疑破坏了和谐。

But Burke’s dismissal of Macaulay was not just rudeness – it was entirely consistent with his broader vision of what partisanship should look like, and who should be allowed to practise it. In the very tract that sparked Macaulay’s attack, Burke argued that partisanship is best conceived of as a well-chosen, loyal friendship between polite, reasonable and like-minded men, who agreed with one another ‘at least nine times in ten’. Only in this way could partisanship exist ‘without violating concord’. By contrast, Macaulay’s pamphlet was impolite, fierce and zealous. In Burke’s eyes, then, Macaulay’s brand of partisanship certainly violated concord and harmony.

伯克并非18世纪唯一一位推崇礼貌、友谊、温和与理性的理论家。大卫·休谟（David Hume）同样强调和谐的重要。党人不该过于讲原则；他应当能「轻松地与同胞达成一致」。讲原则的党人比那些理性而倾向妥协的人「更加狂热」「更加暴躁愤怒」。安东尼·阿什利-库珀（Anthony Ashley-Cooper），即第三代沙夫茨伯里伯爵（Earl of Shaftesbury），也认为守护和谐至关重要。为此他主张培养「沉静、柔和、融洽」的举止，以对抗愤怒、狂热的政治「战争」。这些男性传递的信息很明确：党人应当礼貌、理性、温和而友善。唯有如此，党派政治才能改善政治——即使之更为和谐。唯有如此，党派政治才得以被驯服。像阿斯特尔和麦考利这样失礼、狂热、愤怒而讲原则的声音之所以被视为威胁，恰恰因为它们扰乱了和谐。

Burke was not the only 18th-century theorist to champion politeness, friendship, moderation and reasonableness. David Hume, too, stressed the importance of concord. A partisan shouldn’t be too principled; he should be able to ‘easily agree with his fellow-citizen’. Righteous partisans were ‘more zealous’ and ‘more furious and enraged’ than those who were reasonable and prone to compromise. Anthony Ashley-Cooper, the third Earl of Shaftesbury also thought it was crucial to safeguard concord. For this purpose, he recommended the cultivation of ‘serene, soft, and harmonious’ manners to combat angry, zealous political ‘warfare’. The message of these men was clear: partisans should be polite, reasonable, moderate and friendly. Only then could partisanship make politics better – that is, more harmonious. Only then could partisanship, as it were, be tamed. Impolite, zealous, angry, and righteous voices like those of Astell and Macaulay were threatening precisely because they disrupted concord.

使阿斯特尔与麦考利的共同经历格外引人注目的是，她们来自政治光谱的两端。阿斯特尔是托利党保守派，捍卫英国国教、绝对君主制和宗教正统；麦考利则是激进的共和派和辉格党人，拥护人民主权、宗教宽容和革命。但尽管在几乎一切问题上意见相左，她们对如何从事党派政治却得出了惊人相似的看法。这表明她们的洞见并非源于各自的党派立场，而是源于她们共同的边缘处境。作为女性，她们既不能投票也不能担任公职，而党派政治——撰写小册子、加入政治团体、介入公共辩论——是她们仅有的少数政治参与途径之一。

What makes Astell and Macaulay’s shared experience all the more striking is that they came from opposite ends of the political spectrum. Where Astell was a Tory conservative defender of the Church of England, absolute monarchy and religious orthodoxy, Macaulay was a radical republican and Whig, championing popular sovereignty, religious toleration and revolution. But despite disagreeing on nearly everything, they arrived at remarkably similar views on how to do partisan politics. This suggests that their insight arose not from their partisan commitments but from their shared position on the margins. As women, neither could vote nor hold public office, and partisanship – writing pamphlets, joining political groups, intervening in public debates – was one of the few avenues of political participation available to them.

她们作为女性和党人的经历，使她们看到了身边那些被更充分纳入政治的男性所看不到的东西

Their experience as women and partisans allowed them to see what the more fully politically included men around them couldn’t

我们或许会以为，面对阿特伯里、欧文和伯克之流，阿斯特尔和麦考利会辩称自己其实并不失礼、并不狂热、并不冒犯。然而相反，她们坦然接受了这些名号。在《真正的温和》（Moderation Truly Stated，1704年）这本小册子里，阿斯特尔欣然承认自己慷慨地在行文中「撒入」了「我那煽风点火的雄辩、讽刺与谩骂」。与欧文、沙夫茨伯里或阿特伯里等人不同，她偏爱诚实的「苦胆精华」胜过阿谀奉承、安抚人心的「蜂蜜精粹」。即便真相令人受伤或震惊，把它倾倒出来也胜过隐忍不发。阿斯特尔说，「如果」她自己的文字「显出某种苦涩」，「那仅仅源自真相之朴素与力量，恶人不堪承受。」麦考利同样以不卑不亢的姿态面对伯克或休谟这类嫌她过于直白坦率的人：做一名党人的要义正在于「说出某些大胆而冒犯的真相」。即便这让人「极为冒犯」、令人侧目，也胜过虚伪的礼貌。

We might expect Astell and Macaulay to have defended themselves against the likes of Atterbury, Owen and Burke by arguing that they were not in fact impolite or zealous or offensive. But, instead, they embraced these monikers. In her pamphlet Moderation Truly Stated (1704), Astell gladly admitted that she liberally ‘sprinkled’ her writing with ‘my inflaming Oratory, Satyr and Invective’. Unlike men such as Owen, Shaftesbury or Atterbury, she preferred the honest ‘Quintessence of Gall’ over the flattering, soothing ‘Extract of Honey’. Even if truths were hurtful or shocking, it was still preferable to dole them out rather than to hold back. ‘[I]f there appears any Bitterness in’ her own writings, Astell said, ‘it arises only from the Plainness and Force of Truth, which ill Men cannot bear.’ Macaulay similarly adopted an unapologetic attitude to men like Burke or Hume who found her shockingly honest and direct: the point of being a partisan was ‘to speak some bold offending truths’. Even if it caused one ‘to be very offensive’ and ‘obnoxious’, it was still preferable to dishonest politeness.

为何阿斯特尔和麦考利要拒绝男性同侪所盛赞的那些「美德」？她们作为女性和党人的经历，使她们看到了身边那些被更充分纳入政治的男性所看不到的东西：坚持要求党人礼貌、温和、理性、友善，实际上会排斥被边缘化的政治声音。这可以通过两种方式实现。

Why did Astell and Macaulay reject those ‘virtues’ that their male peers extolled? Their experience as women and partisans allowed them to see what the more fully politically included men around them couldn’t: that insisting that partisans be polite, moderate, reasonable and friendly would actually serve to exclude marginalised political voices. It could do so in two ways.

首先是把关问题。用阿斯特尔的话说：「谁来做裁判」判定什么、谁算是（不）礼貌、（不）温和、（不）理性？裁判将是沙夫茨伯里这类有权势、被充分纳入政治的男性。阿斯特尔转述了沙夫茨伯里《论热情的信》（Letter Concerning Enthusiasm，1708年）的观点，说他主张机智与嘲弄是可接受的，前提是「我们以良好风度待之」。可是，像沙夫茨伯里这样的男性无论走到哪里都能随意施展嘲弄，仍被视为礼貌得体；而像阿斯特尔这样的女性做出同样的行为，却会因举止失当而遭斥责。在她最后一本小册子（1709年）中，阿斯特尔质问沙夫茨伯里：女性或仆人是否也能「以这种方式对待一家之主？」换言之，有权势的男性无论怎样行事都仍可被视为得体理性，而仆人、女性及其他「无足轻重之人」却不行。政治理论家特蕾莎·贝让（Teresa Bejan）在其著作《仅仅是文明》（Mere Civility，2017年）中指出，近代早期厚重的文明观念正是以这种方式运作——通过将文明的门槛抬到只有内部人才能达到的高度，从而排斥异议之声。但贝让主张一种更薄、更简约的文明形式时，阿斯特尔和麦考利走得更远。

First, there was the problem of gatekeeping. As Astell put it: ‘Who shall be the Judge’ of what and who counted as (im)polite, (im)moderate, and (un)reasonable? It would be the powerful and fully politically included men like Shaftesbury who would be the judge. Paraphrasing Shaftesbury’s ‘Letter Concerning Enthusiasm’ (1708), Astell suggested he had argued that wit and ridicule were acceptable, provided ‘we treat it with Good Manners’. But while men like Shaftesbury might be able to dole out ridicule wherever they went and still be considered polite and well-mannered, women like Astell would, for the very same behaviour, be berated for their bad manners. In her last pamphlet, t (1709), Astell challenged Shaftesbury by asking whether women or servants would be allowed to ‘treat the Master of the Family in this Fashion?’ In other words, while powerful men would be allowed to behave however they liked and still be deemed well-mannered and reasonable, servants, women and other ‘Person[s] of No-Figure’ would not. In her book Mere Civility (2017), the political theorist Teresa Bejan shows that thick conceptions of early modern civility functioned in exactly this way – excluding dissenting voices by raising the threshold for civility so high that only insiders could meet it. But where Bejan proposes a thinner, more minimal form of civility, Astell and Macaulay went further.

阿斯特尔和麦考利还识别出另一个问题：处于政治边缘的声音有特殊的理由变得讲原则而狂热。在重大议题上——比如两性平等——保持礼貌或寻求温和妥协是行不通的。麦考利面对伯克时遭遇的正是这个问题：他论证说，只有那些每日亲身参与政治实践的人才够格做出政治判断。相形之下，像麦考利这样未被纳入政治核心的「思辨型」思想家则天真而不胜任。他们「软化到比婴儿的奶性还低」。他们或许能推理政府的「目的」，却无法触及它的实践手段。麦考利拒绝接受这一点：她论证说，自己与权力的距离反而赋予了她相对于被充分纳入的党人更优越的洞察力。

Astell and Macaulay identified another problem: voices at the margins of politics had special cause to be righteous and zealous. On important issues – such as the equality of the sexes – it would not do to remain polite or seek a moderate compromise. Macaulay faced precisely this problem with Burke: he argued that only those involved in the daily practice of politics were qualified to make political judgments. By contrast, ‘speculative’ thinkers like Macaulay, who were not included in the thick of politics, were naive and unfit. They ‘soften[ed] into a credulity below the milkiness of infancy’. They could perhaps reason about the ‘ends of government’ but not with the practical means of it. Macaulay refused to accept this: she argued that her distance from power actually gave her superior insight vis-à-vis fully included partisans.

在1770年对伯克的回应中，麦考利论证说，「实务派绅士」如伯克本人，已被自利和政治权力腐蚀到无法做出公正的政治判断。而保持一定健康距离的「思辨型思想家」如麦考利，则能思考得更清晰，也更善于识破身处政治核心之人的「阴险图谋」。对伯克的一种「温和」回应本可以是承认双方各有重要的政治洞见——事实上，这正是他本人所暗示的。但麦考利拒绝在中间地带与伯克会合，因为中间地带仍然会实质性地压制她的政治声音。在涉及谁被纳入、谁被排斥的争论中，礼貌、理性、温和是行不通的。对于身处麦考利之位的人来说，狂热和讲原则不是性格缺陷，而是一种将她的声音排除在合法政治言说之外的体制的唯一恰当回应。

In her 1770 reply to Burke, Macaulay argued that ‘practical gentlemen’ like himself were too corrupted by self-interest and political power to make unbiased political judgments. But with some healthy distance, ‘speculative thinkers’ like Macaulay could think more clearly and were better equipped to spot the ‘sinister views’ of those in the thick of politics. A ‘moderate’ response to Burke would have been to concede that both sides have some important political insight – indeed, this was what he himself gestured at. But Macaulay refused to meet Burke on the middle ground, because the middle ground would still effectively silence her political voice. When it came to arguments about who was included and who excluded, it would not do to be polite, reasonable and moderate. For someone in Macaulay’s position, zeal and righteousness were not character flaws, but the only adequate response to a system that defined her out of legitimate political speech.

阿斯特尔表示赞同。整个18世纪，人们倾向于把狂热定义为「过度的或不节制的热情」，而她却赞颂「狂热者」，说其「热忱而勤勉」「坚定而狭隘」。阿斯特尔具有颠覆性的观点是，在原则和信念上的不动摇是值得赞美的——在重大事务上，从宗教到女性的社会地位，轻易被说服或太快改变想法都是错误的：「诚实之人」拥有一颗「不屈的良知」，不会「随风气转向」。在政治上，选边站至关重要。而如果「诚实之人」认为对方阵营有道理，那么「他应当投奔过去。」麦考利 likewise 赞美那些「对事业保持不屈依附」、被「如此炽热而诚实的狂热所驱动」的党人。讲原则的狂热意味着采取明确立场，不寻找中间地带，热烈地为自己的事业辩论。这与其男性同侪所推崇的政治美德恰恰相反。

Astell agreed. Whereas, throughout the 18th century, people tended to define zeal as ‘excessive or immoderate enthusiasm’, she praised the ‘Zealot’, who was ‘warm and assiduous’ and ‘stiff and narrow-Spirited’. Astell’s subversive point was that inflexibility in one’s principles and beliefs was admirable – on important matters, from religion to women’s position in society, it was wrong to be quickly dissuaded, or to change one’s mind too readily: ‘an Honest Man’ had an ‘Inflexible Conscience’ and did not ‘Veer with the Times’. When it came to politics, it was important to take sides. And if the ‘Honest man’ thought the other side had a good point, then ‘he ought to go over to them.’ Macaulay, too, praised partisans with ‘inflexible attachment to the cause’, and who were ‘actuated by so warm and honest a zeal’. Righteous zeal meant taking a decided stance, not looking for middle ground, and zealously arguing one’s cause. It was directly opposed to the political virtues their male peers recommended.

讲原则而狂热的党人非但没有摧毁政治辩论，反而维系了它

Far from destroying political debate, righteous, zealous partisans in fact sustained it

对阿斯特尔和麦考利而言，政治辩论中讲原则的狂热更可取，这是显而易见的。首先，因为处于边缘的党人从温和、礼貌的理性中最受损。如果他们礼貌地承认对手有道理，那么什么都不会改变。这些美德只会维持现状，而阿斯特尔和麦考利寻求的是挑战并改变现状。阿斯特尔想要对抗那些有害而无据的「习俗」——一个近似我们今日所谓「社会规范」的概念——它们将女性描绘为低劣的造物，难以进行政治论证、缺乏智识。「习俗，那无情的洪流，裹挟一切奔腾向前」，必须加以遏制。麦考利同样担忧「大多数世人过于钟爱既成的建制」，因此「有意排斥每一项改革」。党派政治是边缘之人可借助的有力手段——那些永远无法成为伯克所说的参与日常政治的「实务派绅士」的人——用以挑战那些试图将他们拒之门外的偏见与规范。哲学家米莎·切里（Myisha Cherry）在其著作《为愤怒辩护》（The Case for Rage，2021年）中提出了平行的论证，她写道，讲原则的愤怒是民权运动的推动力——在那个语境中，礼貌、温和的平等诉求早已无法带来改变。

To Astell and Macaulay, it was obvious that, in political debate, righteous zeal was preferable. First of all because partisans on the margins had most to lose from moderate, polite reasonableness. If they politely conceded that their opponents had a point, nothing ever changed. These virtues would only maintain the status quo, whereas Astell and Macaulay sought to challenge and change it. Astell wanted to combat the pernicious and ill-grounded ‘customs’ – a term that closely resembles our present-day idea of ‘social norms’ – that painted women as inferior creatures, incapable of political argument and devoid of intellect. ‘Custom, that merciless torrent that carries all before it’ needed to be checked. Macaulay likewise worried that ‘the generality of mankind are too fond of accustomed establishments’, and therefore ‘interested to reject every part of reformation’. Partisanship was a powerful means available to those on the margins – those who could never belong to Burke’s ‘practical gentlemen’ involved in daily politics – seeking to challenge the prejudices and norms that tried to keep them out. The philosopher Myisha Cherry makes a parallel argument in her book The Case for Rage (2021), writing that righteous anger was a driving force in the civil rights movement – a context where polite, moderate appeals for equality had long failed to produce change.

接受讲原则的狂热而非礼貌、温和的理性，更具包容性：它让更多人得以发声并被认真对待。但阿斯特尔和麦考利也论证说，讲原则而狂热的党人非但没有摧毁政治辩论，反而维系了它。毕竟，这些「热忱」「不屈」之人即便在不受欢迎时仍坚持与对手辩论。作为一名讲原则、狂热的党人，阿斯特尔设想在来世的天国与她的「敌人」相遇，在那里「他们会意识到，他们的获准进入，在某种程度上有赖于我们为他们所作的不懈代求」。换言之，正是凭借她不断的辩论与劝说，对手们才能改进自身及其观点。「这，」她在其代表作《基督教信仰》（The Christian Religion，1705年）中思忖道，「无疑将是一种甜蜜的复仇。」麦考利似乎也是一位锲而不舍的政治对话者。她的一位相识说，「虽然她打牌、谈论各种话题，」她「总是回到」政治上来，即便在聚会上也是如此——「对一位女士而言是种古怪的性情」。

Accepting righteous zeal instead of polite, moderate reasonableness was more inclusive: it allowed for a greater number of people to make their voices heard and be taken seriously. But Astell and Macaulay also argued that, far from destroying political debate, righteous, zealous partisans in fact sustained it. After all, these ‘warm’ and ‘inflexible’ people kept arguing with their opponents even when it was unwelcome. As a righteous, zealous partisan herself, Astell envisioned meeting her ‘enemies in heaven hereafter’ where ‘they shall be sensible that their admission is in some measure owing to our own importunate intercessions for them’. In other words, it was only due to her constant arguments and persuasions that her opponents would improve themselves and their views. ‘This,’ she mused in her magnum opus The Christian Religion (1705), ‘will be no doubt a sweet revenge.’ Macaulay seems to have been a persistent political interlocutor too. An acquaintance of hers said that ‘though she plays at cards and talks of different subjects,’ she ‘always returns’ to politics, even at parties – ‘an odd character for a lady’.

既然讲原则、狂热的党人推崇诚实胜过礼貌，他们也就乐于为了劝说而冒险冒犯。麦考利论证说，正如暴政与自由相对立，狂热也与形式、或「外在表象或模样」以及「对既定规则的遵从」相对立。对麦考利而言，狂热意味着无视外在表象或顺从。狂热往往意味着大胆、坦诚，乃至失礼。用阿斯特尔的话说，它涉及「说出任何男子都不会说的话」，换言之，说出「伤人的真相」。

Since righteous, zealous partisans favoured honesty over politeness, they were also happy to risk causing offence in pursuit of persuasion. Macaulay argued that, just as tyranny stood opposed to liberty, so zeal stood opposed to formality, or ‘outward appearance or semblance’ and ‘conformity to established rule’. For Macaulay, zeal thus implied a disregard of outward appearance or conformity. To be zealous often meant being bold, truthful, even impolite. As Astell put it, it involved ‘speaking truths which no man would say’, in other words, speaking ‘the Truth that wounds’.

说到这里，讲原则、狂热的党人彼此劝说的画面或许会唤起令人忧虑的景象：一片吵嚷却并无真正的对话发生。即便这样的党人会说个不停，他们真的会彼此倾听吗？阿斯特尔和麦考利的著述表明，她们确实始终认真对待对手的观点。她们最具党派色彩、最为狂热的著作往往针对一两位她们所反对的男性：逐一检视对方的论证，标注页码和引文，并说明它们为何在每一点上都错了。在《真正的温和》中，阿斯特尔如此对待欧文——而鼓吹温和与理性的欧文，却只用一句话打发了阿斯特尔的整本著作。麦考利对伯克的两次回应也深入交锋了他的论证，而伯克嘲弄她，却从不屑于回应她的论证。这不是巧合。一位想要劝说对手的讲原则、狂热的党人，必须认真对待对方的论证；而一位看重和谐胜过真相的礼貌、温和的党人，却可以打发一个令人不安的争议之声然后继续前行。

At this point, the image of zealous, righteous partisans trying to persuade one another might conjure up worrying images of shouting without any genuine dialogue taking place. Even if such partisans would keep talking, would they really listen to one another? Astell and Macaulay’s writings show that they did in fact always take their opponents’ views seriously. Their most partisan and zealous works often targeted one or two men they disagreed with: they went through their arguments, one by one, including references to page numbers and quotations, and explained why they were wrong on all counts. In her pamphlet Moderation Truly Stated, Astell gave Owen this treatment – whereas Owen, the proponent of moderation and reasonableness, dismissed Astell’s entire work in one sentence. Macaulay’s two responses to Burke also engaged with his arguments in depth, whereas Burke ridiculed her but never deigned to respond to her arguments. This wasn’t a coincidence. A righteous, zealous partisan who wanted to persuade her opponents had to take their arguments seriously; whereas a polite, moderate partisan who valued concord over truth could afford to dismiss an uncomfortable, controversial voice and move on.

今天，讲原则而狂热的党人既显得不讲道理，又似乎无法改变想法。如果没有人改变想法，政治辩论又有何意义？但对阿斯特尔和麦考利而言，讲原则的狂热与一定程度的开放心态和批判性思考是相容的。她们当然不认为所有立场都同等有效，但仍然认真对待它们。阿斯特尔和麦考利都赞美不屈，并警惕那些随风摇摆的党人。正如哲学家蕾娅·伊皮（Lea Ypi）所指出的，不轻易改变想法实际上能强化政治承诺，并能确保党派事业随时间延续。换言之，它能确保党人不至于过早放弃。

Today, righteous, zealous partisans seem as unreasonable as they are incapable of changing their minds. And what is the point of political debate if no one ever changes their mind? But to Astell and Macaulay, righteous zeal was compatible with a degree of open-mindedness and critical thinking. They certainly did not consider all standpoints as equally valid, but nonetheless took them seriously. Both Astell and Macaulay praised inflexibility and were wary of partisans whose views swayed with the wind. As the philosopher Lea Ypi points out, not being quick to change one’s mind can actually bolster political commitment and can ensure that partisan projects persist over time. In other words, it can ensure that partisans don’t give up too easily.

但即便党人应当有所不屈，讲原则的狂热难道不会封死开放心态的可能性吗？如果我们将开放心态定义为把任何其他立场都视作同等有效，那么讲原则、狂热的党人确实算不上开放。但如果我们像哲学家杰里米·范特尔（Jeremy Fantl）在《开放心智的局限》（The Limitations of the Open Mind，2018年）中那样定义它——即「如果我能给出一个论证，其每一步你都认为有说服力，且你凭自认也无法在其中找到破绽，你便愿意收回自己的信心」——那么讲原则的狂热与开放心态当然相容。阿斯特尔认为，如果英国国教徒不认为异见者错了，「他就应当投奔过去」。党人的讲原则立场永远不应妨碍他们批判地审视自己的信念，也不应排除改变想法的可能：阿斯特尔建议读者「不要凭任何人的空口白话接受其观点，也不要不经检验就吞下他的论证。」

But even if partisans should be somewhat inflexible, would righteous zeal not foreclose the very possibility of open-mindedness? If we define it as taking every other standpoint as equally valid, then righteous, zealous partisans would indeed not be open-minded. But if we define it, as the philosopher Jeremy Fantl does in The Limitations of the Open Mind (2018), as the willingness ‘to pull back on your confidence if I offer an argument each step of which you find compelling and in which you cannot, by your own admission, find a flaw,’ then righteous zeal is certainly compatible with open-mindedness. Astell believed that, if a member of the Church of England did not think that dissenters were mistaken, ‘he ought to go over to them’. A partisan’s righteousness should never prevent them from looking critically at their own beliefs, nor preclude a change of mind: Astell advised her readers to ‘receive no man’s opinion on his bare word, nor to swallow his arguments without examining them.’

真正威胁政治辩论的是情感性极化，而非分歧本身

It is affective polarisation, not disagreement itself, that actually threatens political debate

更重要的是，阿斯特尔和麦考利对「党派即友谊」的拒斥，实际上起到了防范回音室的作用。我们在伯克那里看到的那种以友谊为基础的温和模式，反倒有陷入群体思维之虞，因为对朋友的忠诚和避免冒犯的愿望会妨碍坦诚的异议。阿斯特尔写道，这些朋友「期望你在原则、党派和情感上对错都追随他们」，并希望「受到奉承」。他们「不会指出你的过失，生怕得罪你」。相比之下，阿斯特尔和麦考利不仅批评对立阵营的党人，也批评同阵营的同道：阿斯特尔在《真正的温和》中批评了托利党人查尔斯·达文南特（Charles Davenant），麦考利则与同道激进派约翰·威尔克斯（John Wilkes）有过分歧。两位女性都明确偏爱坦诚、公开的敌人，而非伪装成朋友的「隐匿之敌」。换言之，如果你身边围绕的都是礼貌、忠诚、奉承你且避免冒犯的朋友，你的观点就永远得不到检验；如果你与挑战你的对手坦诚交锋，你的观点就能得到检验。

What’s more, Astell and Macaulay’s rejection of partisanship as friendship actually protects against echo chambers. It is the moderate, friendship-based model that we find in Burke that risks groupthink, because loyalty to one’s friends and a desire to avoid offence prevents frank disagreement. Astell wrote that these friends ‘expect to be followed right or wrong in their principles, party, and passions’, and wished to ‘be flattered’. They would not ‘tell you of your Faults for fear of disobliging you’. By contrast, Astell and Macaulay were not only critical to partisans of opposing sides, but also criticised their co-partisans: Astell critiqued the Tory Charles Davenant in Moderation Truly Stated, and Macaulay had a disagreement with the fellow radical John Wilkes. Both women had a decided preference for open, honest enemies over ‘concealed enemies’ in the guise of friends. In other words, if you surround yourself with polite, loyal friends who flatter you and avoid offence, your views never get tested; if you engage honestly with your opponents who challenge you in turn, they do.

这一对讲原则的狂热的辩护出自两位女性，而非出自把持党派理论的男性，绝非偶然。在深度分歧面前放弃讲原则与狂热，是一种并非人人可得的奢侈——尤其对处于政治边缘之人而言最不可得。同样，坚持要求党人放弃讲原则与狂热、转而追求理性、礼貌与温和，这本身就是一种特权。正如阿斯特尔和麦考利所指出的，它有排斥或迫害那些在政治中最受压迫、也最需要讲原则之狂热、最有理由如此的人的风险。

That this vindication of righteous zeal came from two women, not from the men who dominated partisan theory, is no accident. To forgo righteousness and zeal in the face of deep disagreement is a luxury not available to all – least of all to those positioned at the margins of politics. Likewise, to insist that partisans should dispense with righteousness and zeal in favour of reasonableness, politeness and moderation is itself a privilege. As Astell and Macaulay pointed out, it risks excluding or persecuting those who are most oppressed in politics, and who have most need of, and cause for, righteous zeal.

阿斯特尔和麦考利的诊断在今天尤为紧迫。当政治光谱的各方都在呼吁以更温和、更理性、更礼貌的辩论作为极化的解药时，何为合理政治言说的标准仍由内部人设定，而表达讲原则、狂热或愤怒的声音仍被视为对民主生活的威胁，而非对它的贡献。与18世纪一样，今天这同样在压制边缘的党派之声——那些最需要讲原则之狂热的声音。诚然，讲原则的狂热不太可能缓解意识形态极化——即党人远离中心的极化。但极化这一事实本身未必有害于政治。讲原则的狂热可以对抗一种更令人担忧的极化：情感性极化——即完全拒绝与对手接触。而真正威胁政治辩论的，正是情感性极化，而非分歧本身。

Astell and Macaulay’s diagnosis is especially urgent today, when we hear calls for more moderate, reasonable, polite debate from all sides of the political spectrum as the cure for polarisation. But the terms of what counts as reasonable political speech are still set by insiders, and voices expressing righteousness, zeal or anger are still viewed as threats to democratic life rather than as contributions to it. Just as in the 18th century, today this also works to silence partisan voices on the margins – those with most need of righteous zeal. It is certainly true that righteous zeal is unlikely to reduce ideological polarisation – where partisans move away from the centre. But the mere fact of such polarisation need not be detrimental to politics. Righteous zeal can combat a more worrying form of polarisation, affective polarisation – the refusal to engage with one’s opponents at all. And it is affective polarisation, not disagreement itself, that actually threatens political debate.

也许在一个理想世界里，人人被平等纳入，每个人的声音都被倾听，我们便不需要讲原则、狂热的党人。但那不是我们所处的世界。政治生活仍以声音与地位的深度不平等为特征。坚持礼貌、温和与理性，或许能让已被充分纳入者之间的政治辩论更为和谐。然而它同时也压制了边缘之人的声音——而引入更广泛的声音，恰恰是改善政治辩论所需要的。从边缘写作的阿斯特尔和麦考利向我们表明，讲原则的狂热不是对政治的威胁，而是真正政治包容与坦诚政治辩论的前提。

Perhaps in an ideal world, where everyone is equally included, and where everyone’s voices are heard, we wouldn’t need righteous, zealous partisans. But that is not the world we live in. Political life remains marked by deep inequalities of voice and standing. Insisting on politeness, moderation and reasonableness may make political debate more harmonious among those already fully included. Yet it simultaneously silences those on the margins – and bringing in a wider array of voices is precisely what is needed to improve political debate. Writing from the margins, Astell and Macaulay show us that righteous zeal is not a threat to politics, but rather a condition of genuine political inclusion and frank political debate.
