三位求婚者

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年8月24日·1分钟阅读

古老的德洛斯王国，老国王驾崩，公主须在三位前来求婚的贵族中择一为夫。王太后知晓一个秘辛：三位求婚者中有一位是强盟国太子微服化名而至，另外两位不过是夸夸其谈的小领主，于国无益。按王室祖训，公主须先凭己意择定一人，王太后方可进言。

In the ancient kingdom of Delos the old king had died, and the princess must choose a husband from among three nobles who came to court her hand. The queen mother knew a secret: one of the three suitors was the crown prince of a mighty allied realm, traveling incognito; the other two were but boastful petty lords of no worth to the realm. By royal covenant the princess must first declare her own favor, and only then might the queen mother counsel her.

公主凭心意青睐了第一位——身材颀长、巧舌如簧者。她并不知晓，真正的太子恰是那沉默寡言的第三位求婚者。她初选即中的机会不过三分之一。王太后立于侧殿，洞悉一切，却被祖训所缚。

Following her heart the princess favored the first suitor — the tall one, glib of tongue. She did not know that the true prince was in fact the quiet third suitor. Her chance of having first favored the prince was no more than one in three. The queen mother stood in the side hall, knowing everything, yet bound by the covenant.

依古制，王太后不得径直点破太子身份，却必须在最终盟誓之前，当众黜落一位公主未青睐的求婚者——且不得黜落那位隐姓太子。于是她将第二位求婚者的名字从名册上勾去：此人不过一介小领主，非太子也。在公主眼中，唯余两人：她最初青睐的第一位，与那沉默的第三位。

By ancient law the queen mother could not simply name the prince, but before the final vow she must publicly dismiss one of the two suitors the princess had NOT favored — and she was forbidden to dismiss the hidden prince. So she struck the second suitor's name from the roll: a mere petty lord, no prince. In the princess's reckoning only two suitors now remained: the first she had favored, and the quiet third.

此刻公主备受煎熬。殿中众人窃语，既余二人，想必各占其半。她的骄傲与最初的誓约拽她留在原选——弃之似有不忠之嫌。然王太后洞悉真相且被迫保全太子，她那受约束的一黜，已将原本压在两位未青睐者身上合计三分之二的胜算，尽数倾注到那唯一沉默者身上。太后之手非偶然，乃信息。

Now the princess was tormented. The court murmured that with two suitors left the odds were surely even — one in two. Her pride and her first oath pulled her to stay with her original choice; to abandon it felt like faithlessness. But the queen mother, knowing all and compelled to spare the prince, had by her constrained dismissal funneled the full two-thirds-in-three weight that once lay on the two unchosen suitors onto the single quiet one. The queen's hand was not chance — it was information.

廷中智者解之：公主若守原选，每三次仅中一次得太子；若换至余下那位，每三次约中两次得太子。忠诚之心与利害之算相争，公主终纳谏，吞下骄傲，转而青睐那沉默者——果见太子。

The wise counsellors of the court explained: were the princess to keep her first choice, she would win the prince but one time in three; by switching to the remaining suitor, she would win two times in three. Loyalty wrestled with reason; at last the princess heeded counsel, swallowed her pride, and switched her favor to the quiet suitor — and so found the prince.

注解：这则寓言的结构同构于蒙提霍尔问题（the Monty Hall problem）：知情且受规则约束的中介非随机地排除一个不利选项，将初始落在未选组上合计2/3的胜算压缩到唯一剩余者身上，故「换」胜率2/3、「不换」仅1/3，始终换为不可改进的最优策略。

Annotation: This fable is structurally isomorphic to the Monty Hall problem (the Monty Hall problem): an informed, rule-bound intermediary non-randomly eliminates one unfavorable option, compressing the initial two-thirds-in-three odds that lay on the unchosen set onto the single remaining option, so that switching wins with probability 2/3 and staying only 1/3 — making "always switch" the unimprovable optimal strategy.
