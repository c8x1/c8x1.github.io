司阍的账簿

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年8月21日·2分钟阅读

双厅书院立于廊道交汇处，一条狭廊连通东西两间阅览室，大小、光线与寂静俱同。廊口坐着司阍——一位老者，他唯一的事便是察看来客：辨其声、步、所问之中颤动的急切，然后一言指路——急者东，缓者西。

The Archive of Two Halls stood at a crossing where a single corridor joined east and west reading rooms, identical in size, light, and silence. At the corridor's mouth sat the Keeper, an old man whose only task was to read each visitor as they approached — to gauge the urgency trembling in their voice, their step, their question — and then, with a word, to point: urgent ones east, idle ones west.

「我看了你半年了，」学者双手撑在他案上说道，「东厅鼎沸——踱步，争论，焦躁的手翻着书页。西厅沉睡。你在一侧造出风暴，另一侧造出死寂，而你所做的不过是听和指。没搬过一本书，没点过一盏灯，没花过一枚铜板。」

"Six months I've watched you," said the scholar, pressing her palms on his desk. "The east room seethes — pacing, argument, restless hands turning pages. The west room sleeps. You've made a storm on one side and a lull on the other, and all you do is listen and point. No book moved, no lamp lit, no coin spent."

「东厅的躁动可以驱使做事，」她目光灼灼，「若引而用之——以他们的不安牵动绳索，以争论之炽为力——不烧一根柴便能照亮全城。你从无中生出了有。你破了那条万物终归沉寂的旧律。」

"The east room's ferment could drive work," she said, eyes bright. "If we channel it — rig lines from their restlessness, harness the heat of their quarrels — we could light the whole city without burning a single log. You've made something from nothing. You've broken the old law that says everything runs down."

司阍沉默良久，然后翻开那本从不离身的账簿——密密麻麻写满姓名、急缓、每一次指路的方向。写满了。每一行、每一处页边、每一页扉页都挤满字迹。「我手上做的，不费分毫，」他说，「但这本账簿上做的，费尽一切。今夜我必须把它擦净。」

The Keeper was quiet for a long time. Then he opened the ledger that never left his side — its pages dense with names, urgencies, the direction of each finger he had pointed. It was full. Every line, every margin, every flyleaf crammed. "What I do with my hands costs nothing," he said. "What I do with this book costs everything. Tonight I must scrub it clean."

「而擦的时候，」他继续说，把账簿转过来让她看清渗入纸纹的墨迹，「那力气不会消失。每一个被我抹去的名字都在两厅激起一阵震颤——困惑、潮热、所有急切重新散入空气。到天亮时，东厅不比西厅更喧嚣。我白日筑起的落差，夜里自会拆去。不是因为我甘心失去——而是遗忘是劳作，劳作须向那条旧律偿债。」

"And when I scrub," he continued, turning the ledger so she could see the ink bitten into the grain, "the effort does not vanish. Each name I dissolve sends a tremor through both halls — confusion, flush, a brief scattering of all that urgency back into the air. By dawn the east room is no stormier than the west. The gradient I built through the day, the night unbuilds. Not because I choose to lose it — because forgetting is a labor, and labor pays its debt to the old law."

学者盯着写满字的纸页。「那么测量是免费的，而这——」

「测量近乎免费，」他说，「我读出一位来客的急切并记下来，可以不费分毫。可账簿只容得下这么多。迟早写满，我必须腾空，而腾空从不免费。我每抹去一个名字，归还的混乱恰等于分拣所聚拢的秩序。我做了四十年。东厅不曾有一次撑过夜里仍比西厅喧嚣。」

The scholar stared at the full pages. "Then measuring is free, but this—"

"Measuring is nearly free," he said. "I can read a visitor's urgency and remember it without cost. But the ledger holds only so much. Sooner or later it fills, and I must empty it, and emptying is never free. Every name I scrub returns exactly as much disorder as the sorting gathered. I have done this for forty years. The east room has never once stayed stormier through the night."

「那就没指望了，」她低声道。

司阍合上账簿。「不是没指望，是诚实。你想要从无中生有——一种不付代价的分拣，一个自行维持的落差。可再巧的门户、再明的司阍，也逃不过这笔账。账簿总要收它的债。你所谓东厅的风暴，不过是借来的混乱，而借来的东西天亮必须归还。」

"Then it's hopeless," she whispered.

The Keeper closed the ledger. "Not hopeless. Honest. You wanted something from nothing — a sort that costs nothing, a gradient that holds itself. But no gate, however clever its keeper, escapes the accounting. The ledger always demands its due. What you call a storm in the east room is only borrowed disorder, and every borrowed thing must be returned at dawn."

注解：这则寓言映射麦克斯韦妖（Maxwell's demon）：一个测量后选择性分拣、似在不做功的情形下降低系统熵、挑战热力学第二定律的假想存在。但纳入其测量与信息擦除代价后——擦除一比特至少耗散 kT ln 2 热量（Landauer 原理）——总熵不减，第二定律成立。

Note: This parable mirrors Maxwell's demon — a being that measures and selectively sorts, apparently reducing the system's entropy without work and challenging the second law of thermodynamics. But once the costs of its measurement and information-erasure are included — erasing one bit dissipates at least kT ln 2 of heat (Landauer's principle) — total entropy does not decrease, and the second law holds.
