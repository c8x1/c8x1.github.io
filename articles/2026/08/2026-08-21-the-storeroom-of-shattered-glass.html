碎玻璃的储藏室

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年8月21日·2分钟阅读

大教堂的窗接住晨光，把它留住。朝圣者慕名而来；玻璃匠人公会称其为十年来最精湛的委托，将功劳归于那位一夜之间升任师傅的学徒。她默默收下赞誉。她知道自己什么也没设计。

The cathedral window caught the morning light and held it. Pilgrims traveled to see it; the glass masters' guild called it the finest commission of the decade and credited the apprentice who had become a master overnight. She accepted their praise in silence. She knew she had designed nothing.

半年前，师傅去世，公会给她一季的期限：完成大教堂的窗，否则工坊解散、房舍收回。她继承的全部家当只有那间储藏室——几千块碎玻璃，是多年实验砸碎的残骸，不分次序地扫在一起。没钱买新玻璃板，没时间从零设计、切割一幅画面。

Six months earlier, her master had died, and the guild had given her a single season: finish the cathedral window, or the workshop would be dissolved and its building reclaimed. The storeroom was all she inherited — bins overflowing with thousands of shattered glass fragments, the random wreckage of years of experiments, swept together without order. No money for new sheets of glass, no time to design and cut a scene from scratch.

她面临一个选择。她可以花一整季画图设计，把碎玻璃切到合适——但一刀出错，委托就毁了，而她没有师傅指导切割。或者她可以在废墟里翻找，寻找那些已经构成一幅连贯画面的碎片，拼合而非创造。公会长老说这是发疯：那些碎玻璃是随机垃圾，纯属偶然扫在一起的。她选择了翻找。

She faced a choice. She could spend the season sketching a design and cutting fragments to fit it — but one mistake and the commission was lost, and she had no master to guide the cutting. Or she could sift through the rubble, hunting for fragments that already formed a coherent scene, assembling rather than creating. The guild elders called the idea madness: the fragments were random trash, swept together by accident. She chose to sift.

翻找了数周之后，她找到了：一组特定的碎片，它们精确的颜色、厚薄与形状恰好已构成一幅连贯画面——一个人影从黑暗中升入光明。她没有切割或重塑任何一块。她只是认出了偶然已经放进废墟里的东西。有两件事必须同时正确：她选了哪些碎片，以及每一块所携带的精确属性。两者都不是她的功劳。

Weeks of sorting later, she found it: one particular selection of fragments whose exact colors, thicknesses, and shapes already composed a coherent scene — a figure rising from darkness into light. She had not cut or reshaped a single piece. She had simply recognized what chance had already placed in the rubble. Two things had to be right simultaneously: which fragments she chose, and the precise properties each one carried. Neither was her doing.

为了证明画面来自碎玻璃本身而非她的手艺，她做了一个实验。她描下每一块获奖碎片的位置，然后把每一块换成从另一只箱子里切出的同形碎片。轮廓相同，颜色和厚薄不同。画面立刻塌陷成一滩泥泞。单凭排列不够；原始碎片所携带的精确属性——偶然赋予的那一组——才是让这扇窗成立的原因。

To prove that the scene came from the fragments and not from her art, she tried an experiment. She traced the position of every winning fragment, then replaced each one with a fragment of identical shape cut from a different bin. Same outlines, different colors and thickness. The scene collapsed into muddy confusion. The arrangement alone was not enough; the exact properties of the original fragments — the ones chance had given — were what made the window work.

一部古老的公会编年史记载着一个更激进的说法——她原本只当它是传说：倘若储藏室足够庞大——碎玻璃堆成山、箱子数不清——那么废墟里几乎必然已存在一幅连贯画面，无需切割、无需打磨、甚至无需拼合。碎玻璃本身已经是一扇窗。在任何人去看之前，它就已经是一扇窗了。

An old guild chronicle recorded a stronger claim, one she had dismissed as legend until now: if a storeroom were vast enough — mountains of fragments, bins beyond counting — a coherent scene was practically guaranteed to exist somewhere in the wreckage, needing no cutting, no fitting, no assembly at all. The shattered glass was already a window. It had been a window before anyone looked.

注解：彩票假设（the lottery ticket hypothesis）。一个随机初始化、足够大的网络中，纯凭运气已存在某个子网络——它被保留的连接加上这些连接上的精确初始权重，使其单独训练后就能匹敌原网络精度；保留同样的连接却换一组新的随机权重，中奖即失效。这枚彩票是事后用剪枝算法偶然发现的，从来不是被设计出来的。

Annotation: The Lottery Ticket Hypothesis. A randomly initialized, sufficiently large network already contains, by pure chance, a subnetwork whose particular connections plus the exact initial weights on them let it, when trained alone, match the original network's accuracy — keep the connections but swap in a new random set of weights and the win vanishes. The ticket is found after the fact by pruning, never designed.
