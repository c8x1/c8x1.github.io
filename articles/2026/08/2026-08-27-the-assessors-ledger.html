评估官的账簿

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年8月27日·2分钟阅读

阿慎第一次走进东坊时，满眼是绸缎铺子与笑语；转过墙根到西坊，却是低矮的棚屋与愁容。他回到公署，对老评估官说：「师父，城里人总讲东坊天生富庶、西坊天生贫陋，可这分明不是天生的。」

The first time A-Shen walked through the East Ward, he saw silk shops and laughter; around the wall into the West Ward stood only low shacks and worried faces. Back at the office he said to the old Assessor: "Master, everyone in the city says the East Ward was born prosperous and the West Ward born shabby — but it plainly wasn't born that way."

老评估官抬起眼：「你说对了。四十年了，每来一个新人，我当面问他三句话——会什么手艺、欠不欠债、有无恶习——凭这三句，我定他去东坊还是西坊。会手艺、无债、无恶习的遣去东坊，余下去西坊。年复一年，东坊攒下匠人，铺子越发兴旺；西坊堆着欠债的、生病的，越发败落。外人来看，都说东坊人本就勤快、西坊人本就懒——秩序像是白来的。」

The old Assessor raised his eyes. "You're right. Forty years now: every newcomer, I ask him three questions to his face — what craft he knows, whether he owes debt, whether he has vices — and by those three I assign him East or West. The skilled, the debt-free, the clean-living go East; the rest go West. Year on year, the East Ward gathered craftsmen and its shops flourished; the West Ward piled up the indebted and the sick and fell further into ruin. Outsiders come and say the East folk were hardworking all along, the West folk lazy all along — as though the order cost nothing."

阿慎皱眉：「可这不可能是白来的。您判断每一个人，就得记住每一个人——您记了四十年，这些判断搁在哪儿？」老评估官拍拍自己的额头：「全搁这儿。每个被我问过的人，我至今记得他的脸、他的答话、我判他去了东还是西。」

A-Shen frowned. "But it can't have cost nothing. To judge each man you had to remember each man — forty years of it — where do those judgments go?" The old Assessor tapped his own forehead. "All here. Every person I've ever questioned, I still remember his face, his answers, whether I sent him East or West."

「可是师父，」阿慎声音低下去，「账簿总得腾出地方写新账。您每年要把旧年的判断清掉一些，好记新一茬人——」老评估官沉默半晌：「是。我清掉的不是纸上的字，是我自己脑子里的东西。每清掉一批，我就连同别的记忆一起丢——我如今已记不清自己女儿长什么样了。」

"But Master," A-Shen's voice dropped, "a ledger has to be cleared to make room for new entries. Every year you must wipe some of the old judgments, to remember the next crop of people—" The old Assessor was silent a long while. "Yes. And what I wipe isn't ink on paper. It's things inside my own head. Every batch I clear, I lose other memories along with them — by now I can no longer remember what my own daughter looked like."

阿慎站起身，声音发颤：「那东坊的体面、西坊的败落，城里人当它是天上掉下来的秩序，原来全靠您一点点忘掉自己的命来换？这账，到底是谁在付？」老评估官摇头：「你只算了一半。东坊多出的一分体面，恰是我脑子里少掉的一分旧事；把我和这城合在一起算，多的并没有变多——秩序从我这边搬到了那边，一块也没凭空多出来。」

A-Shen stood, his voice shaking. "So the East Ward's decency, the West Ward's ruin — the city calls it order fallen from the sky, and all along it was bought by you forgetting away your own life? Who is paying this ledger?" The old Assessor shook his head. "You've reckoned only half. For every measure of decency the East gained, an equal measure of memory was lost from my head. Reckon me and the city together, and nothing has actually increased — the order only moved from my side to theirs. Not a sliver of it came from nothing."

阿慎望着老人空洞的眼睛，许久才说：「那……还继续吗？」老评估官没有回答。他翻开今年新到的名册，第一个名字下面，是又要被问三句话、被记住、有朝一日再被忘掉的一张脸。

A-Shen looked at the old man's emptied eyes, and only after a long time said: "Then… do we go on?" The old Assessor did not answer. He opened the freshly arrived roster for this year, and beneath the first name was another face — to be asked three questions, to be remembered, and one day to be forgotten.

注解：这则寓言指向麦克斯韦妖（Maxwell's demon）——一个逐个测量并据之筛选分流、制造出表面免费秩序的守门者；可一旦把它测量与记忆所耗、以及最终必须擦除记忆的代价一并算入，妖与被筛者合起来的总账并未凭空盈余，秩序不过从一处搬到另一处——热力学第二定律终归守恒。

Annotation: This parable points to Maxwell's demon — a gatekeeper that measures each individual and sorts accordingly, producing order that seems to cost nothing; but once the cost of its measuring and remembering, and the necessary eventual erasure of that memory, are all reckoned in, the combined ledger of demon and sorted shows no free surplus — the order has merely moved from one place to another, and the second law of thermodynamics holds.
