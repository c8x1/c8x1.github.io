最后一缸靛蓝

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年8月22日·3分钟阅读

我们的染村里曾经有十一种颜色，而没有哪位师傅会凭手艺或情分挑选徒弟。每年春天，够年纪上工的孩子名字被放进一只陶罐，师傅们依次抽签，抽到谁，谁就坐到那条板凳上七年，而那位师傅会的三四种颜色，便是他这辈子唯一会染的颜色。我们管这叫「抽签」，我们信它，就像信风——不是因为它公正，而是因为它不属于任何人。

In our dye-village there were once eleven colors, and no master chose an apprentice by skill or by love. Every spring the names of the children ready for the bench went into a clay jar, and the masters drew them out, one by one. Whichever hand a child's name landed in, that was the bench they sat at for seven years, and the three or four colors that master knew were the only colors they would ever learn. We called it the draw, and we trusted it as we trust the wind — not because it is just, but because it is no one's.

九岁那年，抽签把我分到了林师傅的板凳前。他会靛蓝、茜草、黄梣——十一种里的三种，也是最朴素的三种。排在我后面那个男孩被分到街对面芮师傅那里，他们研一种要等上一个月才定色的漆红。我想要那种红。可陶罐已经开口了，陶罐是没法跟它讲理的。我学会了靛蓝，学得很好——不是因为靛蓝比漆红更值得活过漆红，而是因为靛蓝就是分给我的那条板凳。

The draw put me at Old Lin's bench when I was nine. He knew indigo, madder, and weld — three of the eleven, and three of the plainest. The boy whose name came up after mine sat across the lane at Master Rui's, where they ground the lacquer-red that took a month to set. I wanted that red. But the jar had spoken, and a jar cannot be argued with. I learned indigo, and I learned it well, not because indigo deserved to outlive the lacquer-red but because indigo was the bench I was given.

在有一千个染人的城里，一种颜色也许会一时失宠，过一代又回来，因为总还有别的人手攥着它。我们村最多时不过四十条板凳。在这里，冬天芮师傅过世了，而那年春天没有抽到任何徒弟上他的板凳——陶罐把所有孩子送去了别处——漆红就随他一起没了。不是因为它不如别的颜色。只是那一季的抽签，碰巧没有一个孩子落进他的作坊。一条板凳空着，一种颜色就离开了这个世界。

In a city of a thousand dyers, a color might fade from favor and return a generation later, because there are always other hands holding it. Our village was never more than forty benches. Here, when old Master Rui died one winter and no apprentice had been drawn to his bench that spring — the jar had sent all the children elsewhere — the lacquer-red died with him. Not because it was a lesser color. Because the draw, that season, had simply failed to land a child in his workshop. One bench empty, and a color left the world.

接着是那场火，我三十岁那年。一夜之间它吞掉了东巷——芮师傅的旧作坊，守着海绿和琥珀色的两位师傅，还有那个独自记得梅紫的女人。那夜风向自西来，风饶过谁，谁就活下来。天亮时，十一种只剩四条板凳。有七种颜色并不真的是被烧掉的；它们只是再也没有谁会了。火并没有挑出更高贵的颜色。它挑中的，是恰好站在上风口的那些。

Then the fire came, the year I turned thirty. It took the east lane in a single night — Rui's old workshop, the two masters who kept the sea-green and the amber, the woman who alone remembered plum. The wind had been from the west, and whatever the wind spared, lived. By morning we had four benches where we had had eleven. Seven colors did not burn, exactly; they simply had no one left who knew them. The fire had not chosen the worthier colors. It had chosen the ones standing upwind.

同一年，梅师傅带上了她的靛蓝——也就是我染了二十年的那种靛蓝——翻过山脊去了谷里的小镇，在那里支起一条板凳。她带了三个徒弟，那三个又各带三个，不出两代，那镇上除了靛蓝再没别的颜色，他们出手的每一匹布都是蓝的。而在我们村，我成了最后一个还下靛蓝缸的人，等我那双手再也抬不动，村里就一种蓝也没有了。同一种颜色，在一个从未见过它的镇上占满了所有板凳；在它出生的地方，却被抹得干干净净。

The same year, old Mei took her indigo — my indigo, the one I had carried for twenty years — and walked over the ridge to the valley town, to set up a single bench there. She taught three apprentices, and they taught three, and within two generations no one in that town dyed anything but indigo; every bolt of their cloth came out blue. Meanwhile, back in our village, I was the last who still dipped a vat of indigo, and when my hands gave out, we had none. One color, holding every bench in a town that had never known it; the same color, erased from the place where it was born.

如今我是村里最老的染人，仅剩的四种方子被我收在床边一只铁盒里。我只需把它们抄出来，在每一个徒弟手里塞一张，从此再没有哪种颜色会因抽签而失传。可我们村向来认一个理：颜色只活在染人的手里——纸上的墨是尸首，尸首教不会浸染。于是我开着铁盒坐着，拿不定自己的主意。要是我写下方子，我救下了颜色，可我也就毁了抽签，而抽签是让我们之所以成为我们的东西。

I am the eldest dyer now, and I keep the four recipes that remain in a tin box beside my bed. I have only to copy them out and press a sheet into every apprentice's hands, and no color would ever again be lost to the draw. But our village has always held that a color lives only in a dyer's hands — that ink on paper is a corpse, and a corpse cannot teach the dip. So I sit with the box open and I do not know my own mind. If I write the recipes, I save the colors, but I break the draw, and the draw is the thing that has made us what we are.

最后我到底没有写。去年秋天，陶罐把三个新来的孩子全数给了我这条板凳。靛蓝已经在我们仅剩的四条板凳里占了三条——当一样东西已经攥住了大多数的手，抽签几乎没法不把它再往前送。街对面那间还勉强撑着茜草和黄梣的作坊，一个孩子也没分到，到明年春天，那两种也许就没了。那时村里将只剩下靛蓝，而等到只剩一种颜色，连抽签也无事可裁了。我为茜草和黄梣哀伤。可我没法说陶罐不公。它只是碰巧，而碰巧不欠任何一种颜色一句道歉。

In the end I did not write them down. Last autumn the jar gave all three of the new children to my bench. Indigo was already in three of our four remaining benches — and when a thing already holds most of the hands, the draw can hardly send it anywhere but onward. Not one child went to the workshop across the lane where madder and weld still hung on, and by next spring those two may be gone. Then the village will have nothing left but indigo, and when only one color remains, even the draw will have nothing left to decide. I mourn the madder and the weld. But I cannot say the jar was unfair. It was only chance, and chance owes no color an apology.

注解：这个概念是遗传漂变（genetic drift）。在小群体中，传递过程中的随机抽样误差使某种属性的频率在代际间随机波动——与优劣、与适应无关——最终某一种被固定、其余丢失，多样性下降；群体越小，这一过程越快，而一旦只剩一种，过程即停止。

Note: The concept is 遗传漂变（genetic drift）. In a small group, random sampling error during transmission causes a trait's frequency to fluctuate between generations — independent of quality or adaptation — until one variant is fixed and the rest are lost, reducing diversity; the smaller the group, the faster this proceeds, and once only one variant remains, the process stops.
