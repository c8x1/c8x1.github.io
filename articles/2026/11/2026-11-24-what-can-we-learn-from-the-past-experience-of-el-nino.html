我们能从厄尔尼诺的历史经验中学到什么？

Dagomar Degroot·Aeon·2026年11月24日·14分钟阅读·原文

就在我写下这些文字之时，太平洋中东部大片海域的表层水温已经连续攀升了数月。厄尔尼诺（El Niño）指的正是太平洋这一海域反复出现且持续经年的变暖现象。如今水温相对其长期均值飙升至如此高位，2026年的厄尔尼诺有可能成为1000年来最强的一次。

As I write these words, surface water temperatures across a great swath of the central and eastern Pacific Ocean have been rising for months. El Niño is the name for the recurring and sustained warming of this part of the Pacific. And temperatures are soaring to such highs, relative to their long-term averages, that the El Niño of 2026 may be the strongest in 1,000 years.

海水变暖将如何在今后数月重塑全球天气，目前尚不确定，更遑论生态系统乃至人类群体将如何应对。未来向来混沌难明。然而，就厄尔尼诺而言，过去或许能教我们该期待什么——而且不只是晚近的过去，还包括科学家、历史学家和考古学家近年重新发掘出来的深远过去。

It’s still uncertain how the warming ocean will reshape global weather in the months to come, let alone how ecosystems – and human populations – will respond. The future is always murky. Still, when it comes to El Niño, the past could teach us what to expect. And not just the recent past, but also the deep past recently recovered by scientists, historians and archaeologists.

2015年11月的全球海表温度距平，当时2014-16年厄尔尼诺事件正处于峰值，而该事件是气象仪器记录到的最强事件之一。图片由美国国家海洋和大气管理局（NOAA）提供。

Global sea-surface temperature anomalies in November 2015, at the peak of the 2014-16 El Niño event, one of the strongest recorded by meteorological instruments. Courtesy NOAA

我是一名环境史学家。我与气候学家合作，理解气候在过去如何变迁；我研究各个社群如何理解并适应这些变化。我的研究表明，即便面对当今急速变暖气候中最极端的事件，历史也能帮助我们加以理解。

I’m an environmental historian. I work with climatologists to understand how climates have changed in the past. I study how communities understood and adapted to the climate’s changes. And my research suggests that history can help us understand even the most extreme events in today’s rapidly warming climate.

例如，过去可以帮助我们看清厄尔尼诺为何如此难以预测，甚至难以界定；它还能让我们看到，尽管厄尔尼诺在过去曾导致数以百万计的人死亡，它也曾让另一些人积聚起财富与权力。

The past can help us see why El Niño is so hard to forecast, for example, or even define. It can show us that, while El Niño has contributed to the deaths of millions of people in the past, it has also enabled others to amass wealth and power.

厄尔尼诺会扰乱全球天气格局，这一点无可争议；但历史告诉我们，它既是一种自然实在，也同样是一种人类造物。这一背景或许能帮助我们经受住考验——不仅是未来数月里2026-27年厄尔尼诺的影响，还有变暖星球上厄尔尼诺的未来。

El Niño indisputably scrambles worldwide weather patterns, but history teaches us that it’s as much a human creation as a natural reality. And this context may help us weather not only the effects of the 2026-27 El Niño in the months to come, but also El Niño ’s future on a warming planet.

一股浩大的冷水洋流沿南美洲西海岸蜿蜒北上。每年12月，这股洋流都会中断约一个月。涌入的暖水带来热带鱼群，而且——由于暖水比冷水更易蒸发——还带来丰沛的降雨。

A vast current of cold water snakes up the western coast of South America. In December each year, that current breaks down for about a month. An influx of warm water delivers tropical fish and – because warm water evaporates more readily than cold water – abundant rainfall.

人们最早在何时注意到这种一年一度的水温与降水逆转，已无从知晓。但我们确实知道，在16世纪西班牙征服印加帝国（Inka Empire）之后的某个时候，渔民们将其命名为厄尔尼诺，字面意思是「男孩」，若首字母大写，则指「基督圣婴」（The Christ Child）。至少命名的缘由看似清楚：洋流的逆转恰好发生在圣诞节前后。

It’s impossible to know when people first noticed this annual reversal of water temperatures and precipitation. We do know that, sometime after the 16th-century Spanish conquest of the Inka Empire, fishermen named it El Niño , meaning literally ‘the boy’ or, when capitalised, ‘The Christ Child’. At least the reason for the name seems clear: the reversal of current happens around Christmas.

印加帝国的殖民核心地带——即今天的秘鲁（Peru）——在19世纪赢得独立。临近该世纪末，秘鲁政府效仿许多国家，成立了一个地理学家学会，用以描绘本国的环境并盘点本国的资源。1892年，学会副会长卡米洛·卡里略（Camilo Carrillo）将这种一年一度的水温转换——连同「厄尔尼诺」这个术语——介绍给了科学界。

The colonised heartland of the Inka Empire, now known as Peru, won its independence in the 19th century . Towards the end of that century, Peru’s government joined many of its counterparts by establishing a society of geographers to describe the country’s environment and to catalogue its resources. In 1892, the society’s vice president, Camilo Carrillo, introduced the annual switch in water temperatures – and the term El Niño – to the scientific community.

因此，当厄尔尼诺进入科学命名体系时，它还是一个在多个方面都很小的循环：时间上（每年只涉及一个月）、空间上（只影响秘鲁海岸），以及被感知到的重要性上（只有少数当地科学家对它感兴趣）。

When it entered scientific nomenclature, El Niño was therefore a cycle that was small in time (involving just one month every year), space (affecting only the Peruvian coast), and perceived importance (interesting only to a handful of local scientists).

厄尔尼诺其实是太平洋上空一座庞大大气压力跷跷板的海洋表征

El Niño is actually the oceanic expression of a colossal seesaw of atmospheric pressure over the Pacific

研究者只是逐渐才探测到太平洋海温中一种规模大得多的振荡——它每两到七年发生一次。这种振荡最终也被冠以「厄尔尼诺」之名。到20世纪末，预测其爆发时间与全球影响的工作，已成为国家支持的大科学的典型范例。

Only gradually did researchers detect a much bigger oscillation in Pacific Ocean temperatures, one that happened every two to seven years. This oscillation was eventually branded as El Niño. By the end of the 20th century, the effort to forecast its onset and worldwide impacts became a quintessential example of big, state-supported science.

现代意义上的厄尔尼诺似乎不难定义：它是中东部太平洋的一次剧烈变暖——变暖限于热带，持续数月，且往往在年末前后达到峰值。它向大气输送巨量的热量与水汽，热带急流再将它们重新分配，使各地区的天气格局重新洗牌。

The modern version of El Niño seems easy to define. It’s a dramatic warming of the central and eastern Pacific Ocean – a warming that’s confined to the tropics, lasts for many months, and tends to peak around the end of the year. It transfers a stupendous quantity of heat and moisture into the atmosphere, which the tropical jet stream redistributes, reshuffling weather patterns from one region to another.

但是，究竟是什么造成了太平洋海水的反复变暖？它们又究竟如何影响各区域的天气格局？

But what causes these recurring warmings of Pacific Ocean water? And how, exactly, do they affect regional weather patterns?

直到最近，科学家才得以回答这些问题。原来，厄尔尼诺实际上是太平洋上空一座庞大大气压力跷跷板的海洋表征，这座跷跷板被称为南方涛动（Southern Oscillation）。当空气在东太平洋上空密集堆积——换句话说，那里的气压偏高——而西太平洋气压偏低时，这一气压差便会强化沿赤道持续向西吹拂的风。

Only recently have scientists been able to answer those questions. It turns out that El Niño is actually the oceanic expression of a colossal seesaw of atmospheric pressure over the Pacific Ocean, known as the Southern Oscillation. When air is tightly packed together over the eastern Pacific – when atmospheric pressure there is high, in other words – and when it’s low over the western Pacific, the pressure difference reinforces persistent winds that blow west along the equator.

这些被称作信风（trade winds）的风，能把大量温暖的表层海水推向西太平洋的热带海域，以至于更深、更冷的海水得以升上东太平洋的表层。这种现象被称为拉尼娜（La Niña）。而当太平洋的气压差回落时，信风便会减弱，西太平洋的暖水随之向东奔涌——这便是我们所说的厄尔尼诺。

These trade winds , as they’re called, can push so much warm surface water into the tropics of the western Pacific that they allow deeper, colder water to reach the top of the eastern Pacific. This is called La Niña. But when the difference in Pacific Ocean pressure dips, the trade winds falter, and the warm water of the western Pacific surges east. This we call El Niño.

科学家已经认识到，南方涛动涉及海气之间相互强化的关系，也就是所谓的反馈（feedback）。通常，西太平洋的暖水在高耸入云的风暴中蒸发，释放出热量；空气向东流动、冷却、变密、下沉，随后又向西涌回，并在途中升温。

Scientists have learned that the Southern Oscillation involves reinforcing relationships, known as feedbacks , between the air and sea. Usually, hot water in the western Pacific evaporates in towering storms, releasing heat. The air flows east, cools down, grows denser, falls, then surges back to the west, warming up.

这一环流被称为沃克环流（Walker Circulation），它强化了南方涛动的气压差，并为信风提供动力。但在厄尔尼诺期间，暖水、蒸发与降雨转移到东太平洋，削弱了横跨太平洋的气压差，进而削弱了信风。这一模式有助于解释与厄尔尼诺相伴的最重要天气现象：西半球部分地区暴雨成灾，而澳大拉西亚（Australasia）则陷入干旱。

This pattern, known as the Walker Circulation, strengthens the pressure differences of the Southern Oscillation and fuels the trade winds. But during an El Niño, warm water, evaporation and rains shift to the eastern Pacific, weakening transpacific differences in pressure and, in turn, the trade winds. This pattern helps explain the most important weather associated with El Niño: torrential rains across parts of the Western Hemisphere, and drought in Australasia.

如今，厄尔尼诺看起来不再像一个事件，而更像横跨太平洋、永不停歇地摇来摆去的跷跷板的一端。但这座跷跷板为何如此运作？科学界尚无共识，不过人类社会与自然界留下的历史痕迹，为我们提供了线索。

Today, El Niño seems less like an event, and more like one end of a seesaw that perpetually teeters across the Pacific. But why does the seesaw behave as it does? There is still no scientific consensus, but the historical traces of both human societies and the natural world provide us with clues.

关于厄尔尼诺（El Niño）最重要的问题，涉及其对全球天气格局的影响——换言之，即赤道太平洋变暖与世界各地干旱、洪水、风暴或热浪之间形成的遥相关（teleconnections）。

T he most important questions about El Niño concern its impacts on global weather patterns – or in other words, the teleconnections that form between the warming of the equatorial Pacific and droughts, floods, storms or heatwaves around the world.

这些遥相关通常被呈现为可预测、且历次厄尔尼诺之间保持一致。它们被绘制在地图上，据称展示了随着厄尔尼诺在太平洋上增强，各地区的气温或降水会如何变化。

These teleconnections are usually presented as predictable and consistent from one El Niño to the next. They’re visualised in maps that supposedly depict how regional temperatures or precipitation change as El Niño intensifies in the Pacific Ocean

厄尔尼诺事件期间的典型降水格局。图片来源：美国国家海洋和大气管理局（NOAA）

Typical rainfall patterns during El Niño events. Courtesy NOAA

但没有哪两次厄尔尼诺事件的遥相关完全相同。原因之一是，热带太平洋异常暖水的范围、强度与持续时间逐次不同；另一个原因是，厄尔尼诺只是影响区域天气的众多因素之一，其作用通常会随着与热带太平洋距离拉远而减弱。

But no two El Niño events have exactly the same teleconnections. One reason is that the extent, intensity and duration of unusually warm water in the tropical Pacific differs from one El Niño to the next. Another is that El Niño is only one of many influences on regional weather, and its effects generally weaken with distance from the tropical Pacific.

由于厄尔尼诺对区域天气的影响从未完全相同，我们不可能精确预测未来的厄尔尼诺将如何影响世界上的大多数人口与生态系统。而要拼合出20世纪之前的厄尔尼诺历史——那时船只刚开始定期测量海表温度——难度更大。至于19世纪中叶之前则难上加难：那时科学家才开始系统地从雨量计收集数据，而这些雨量计所在之处，正是厄尔尼诺必然引发强降水的地区。

Because El Niño’s impacts on regional weather are never identical, it’s impossible to predict exactly how a future El Niño will impact most of the world’s people and ecosystems. But it’s even harder to piece together the history of El Niño before the beginning of the 20th century, when ships began to regularly measure sea-surface temperatures. And it’s harder still before the middle of the 19th century, when scientists began to systematically gather data from rain gauges where El Niño reliably generates heavy precipitation.

数十年的研究如今已揭示出厄尔尼诺历史中的宏观规律，足以让我们对未来大致有数

Decades of research now reveals broad patterns in El Niño history that could give a rough sense of what’s in store

这是一项挑战，但并非不可能。气候学家——还有少数历史学家与地理学家——通过查阅他们所称的「社会档案」（archives of society）与「自然档案」（archives of nature），来估计19世纪中叶之前厄尔尼诺的时机、强度与影响。

It’s a challenge, but not impossible. Climatologists – and a handful of historians and geographers – estimate the timing, strength and impacts of El Niño before the mid- 19th century by consulting what they call the ‘archives of society’ and the ‘archives of nature’.

社会档案由过去的人们创造。其中包括直接描述天气的传世文本，例如水手在船上写下的航海日志；也包括形形色色的证据来源——不仅有文献，还有器物、遗迹乃至口述传统——它们所记录的人类活动如此依赖天气，以至于可以作为天气的代用指标。

The archives of society were created by the people of the past. They include surviving texts that describe weather directly, such as the logbooks that sailors wrote aboard ships. They also include diverse sources of evidence – not only documents but also artefacts, ruins, even oral traditions – that record human activities so dependent on the weather that they can serve as proxies for it.

与社会档案不同，自然档案由地球本身创造（当然，对它们的解读仍由人来完成）。这些档案是自然界中或对太平洋水温变化作出响应、或对可能与之相关的区域温度与降水极端状况作出响应的部分。这些响应会在自然档案中留下印记，例如改变树木或珊瑚生长轮的构成。每一处印记都可作为遥远过去厄尔尼诺状况的代用指标。

Unlike the archives of society, the archives of nature were created by Earth itself (even if they are, of course, interpreted by people). These archives are parts of the natural world that responded either to changes in the water temperature of the Pacific Ocean, or to the regional extremes in temperature and precipitation that can be associated with those changes. The responses leave a mark in natural archives, changing the composition of growth rings in trees or corals, for example. Each mark serves as a proxy for El Niño conditions in the distant past.

社会档案与自然档案都无法提供海表温度或天气的直接测量值，因此厄尔尼诺的历史必然充满不确定性。以树木为例，其生长（或停止生长）的幅度终究有限。这正是树木年轮无法精确衡量过去厄尔尼诺事件强度的一个原因（也是我们难以断定今年的厄尔尼诺是否确为千年之最的一个原因）。

Neither societal nor natural archives can provide direct measurements of sea-surface temperatures or weather, so histories of El Niño are inevitably full of uncertainty. Trees, for example, can only grow (or stop growing) so much. It’s one reason why their rings can’t precisely measure the strength of past El Niño events (and one reason why we can’t easily pin down whether this year’s El Niño really is the strongest in a millennium).

尽管如此，数十年的研究如今已能揭示厄尔尼诺历史中的三大宏观规律，让我们得以粗略预见未来将发生什么——不仅关乎来年，更关乎我们在一个变暖世界中的未来。

Still, decades of research can now reveal three broad patterns in El Niño history that could give us a rough sense of what’s in store – not only in the coming year, but in our future on a warming world.

最重要的是，厄尔尼诺通过骤然改变水在何处可得、水量几何以及人们能够如何使用水，塑造了人类的过去。

A bove all, El Niño shaped humanity’s past by suddenly changing where water was available, how much there was, and how people could use it.

人类生活的根基——食物、住所与迁徙——都在不同程度上依赖水。人类群体能以多种不同方式利用水，因此学会了在迥异的环境中繁衍生息。但对他们而言，适应水的骤然紊乱似乎要困难得多——无论是强烈的厄尔尼诺事件，还是总体上趋于更强厄尔尼诺状况的长期趋势，都可能引发这种紊乱。

The foundations of human life – food, shelter and movement – depend to varying degrees on water. Human populations can use water in many different ways, so they have learned to thrive in very different environments. But it seems to have been much harder for them to adapt to the sudden disruptions in water that a strong El Niño – or that a trend towards stronger El Niño conditions overall – could bring about.

这是厄尔尼诺人类历史中的第一个规律。

This is the first pattern in El Niño’s human history.

早在4000多年前，当最早的国家与帝国确立之时，我们便已能看到这一规律的轮廓。当时，厄尔尼诺状况似乎变得更频繁、更强烈、更持久。原因可能是地球自转摆动的一个数千年周期，它改变了不同季节的日照量，从而削弱了信风。

We can already see its outlines a little more than 4,000 years ago, when the first states and empires established themselves. At that time, El Niño conditions seem to have grown more frequent, intense and long-lasting . The reason may have been a multimillennial cycle in the wobble of Earth’s rotation that altered the amount of sunlight associated with different seasons, weakening the trade winds.

这一切听起来都很有说服力，直到——不可避免地——新证据出现

It all sounds convincing until – inevitably – new evidence emerges

自然档案与社会档案表明，随着厄尔尼诺增强，一些社群走向衰落。例如在秘鲁（Peru），人口似乎遗弃了神庙土墩——一种曾承载精美建筑与广场的巨型平台。人们很容易想象，强厄尔尼诺状况引发的汹涌洪水使这些构筑物无从维护。但考古证据也暗示，还有其他变化可能部分造成了人口迁移，包括新的贸易网络与新近培育的作物。两种趋势的巧合，究竟能在多大程度上揭示因果关系？

Natural and societal archives suggest that some communities withered as El Niño intensified. In Peru, for example, populations appear to have deserted temple mounds, giant platforms that supported elaborate buildings and plazas. It’s easy to imagine that torrential floods sparked by strong El Niño conditions made it impossible to maintain these structures. But archaeological evidence hints at other changes that might have been partly responsible for population movements, including new trade networks and newly cultivated plants. How much can the coincidence of two trends really tell us about causation?

也许能说明不少问题——前提是类似的巧合在别处也出现。而事实似乎正是如此。例如，将近3000年前，移居者在特乌马（Teouma）建立了一个社群，该地位于今瓦努阿图（Vanuatu）的埃法特岛（Efate）。附近沼泽沉积物中人类排泄物与叶片的化学痕迹表明，这些移民曾在降水日渐增多的时期种植芋头——一种块根作物。然而到大约2600年前，趋于更强或更频繁的厄尔尼诺状况似乎使当地降水更加反复无常。这一趋势与埃法特岛上聚落和耕作的衰落看起来存在相关。

Maybe quite a lot, if similar coincidences pop up elsewhere. And it seems they do. A little under 3,000 years ago, for example, settlers established a community at Teouma, on the island of Efate in today’s Vanuatu. Chemical traces of human waste and leaves in nearby swamp sediments show that the settlers cultivated taro, a root vegetable, during a period of increasing rainfall. By around 2,600 years ago, however, a trend towards stronger or more frequent El Niño conditions seems to have made local rainfall more erratic. The trend appears to correlate with a decline in settlement and cultivation on Efate.

你会注意到，上文用了许多限定性措辞，谈论可能的变化与潜在的相关关系。深历史（deep history）正是如此。自然档案与社会档案让我们得以识别可能的趋势、大致确定其发生时间，并为一种趋势如何引发另一种趋势构建出令人信服的理论。这一切听起来都很有说服力——直到新证据不可避免地出现，迫使我们重新断代某个趋势，或为其提出另一种成因。

You’ll notice a lot of conditional language about possible changes and potential correlations. Deep history is like that . Natural and societal archives can let us identify probable trends, figure out roughly when they happened, and craft convincing theories for how one trend caused another. It all sounds convincing until – inevitably – new evidence emerges that compels us to re-date a trend, or propose another cause for it.

总体而言，来自自然与社会档案的证据，年代越是接近当下，就越丰富、越精确。过去一千年的证据使我们得以辨认的，不仅是人类历史与厄尔尼诺（El Niño）历史中的趋势，还有一个个具体的事件。

In general, evidence from the archives of nature and society is more abundant and precise the closer it dates to our present. Evidence from the previous millennium lets us identify not just trends in human and El Niño history, but discrete events .

与强厄尔尼诺状况部分相关的灾难性饥荒，似乎已夺去数百万人的生命

Catastrophic famines partly linked to strong El Niño conditions appear to have killed millions

例如，到了16世纪，洞穴沉积物、树木年轮以及政府官员撰写的报告，已足以让我们重建具体厄尔尼诺年份对印度（India）夏季风可能造成的影响。这些证据揭示出16世纪末、17世纪中叶和末期以及18世纪末横扫印度的一系列毁灭性旱灾。

By the 16th century, for example, cave formations, tree rings and reports written by government officials let us reconstruct the likely impacts of individual El Niño years on the Indian summer monsoon. They reveal a series of devastating droughts across India in the late 16th century, the middle and end of the 17th century, and the late 18th century.

这些旱灾或许有一部分要归因于一系列爆炸性火山喷发。喷发将大量硫送入上层大气，其冷却效应改变了各纬度之间以及陆地与水体之间的温差。正是这些温差帮助形成了带来季风雨的风；温差一旦改变，降雨也随之改变。

It may be that a series of explosive volcanic eruptions were partly responsible for these droughts. The cooling effect of the sulphur they launched into the upper atmosphere altered temperature differences between latitudes, and between land and water. These differences in temperature help create the winds that bring monsoon rains; when they changed, the rains did too.

但这些旱灾在时间上同样与厄尔尼诺状况相合。事实上，两者之间可能确有联系，因为强喷发造成的新风场格局，似乎会使东太平洋相对于西太平洋变暖。无论如何，厄尔尼诺本身也能让暖水与上升气流沿热带太平洋东移，从而削弱印度季风。

But the droughts also coincided with El Niño conditions. In fact, there may be a connection, because the new wind patterns created by powerful eruptions seem to warm the eastern Pacific, relative to the west. Regardless, El Niño can also weaken the Indian monsoon by shifting warm water and rising air eastward across the tropical Pacific.

季风雨一旦失约，收成也可能随之落空。当然，乡村人口与国家有许多自我保护的办法。例如，水井与粮仓储存着水和种子，贸易网络则可用于粮食的紧急输入。然而，当旱灾持续过久——尤其当国家开战之时——这些体系便会崩溃。

When the monsoon rains failed, harvests could too. Of course, rural populations and states had many ways of protecting themselves. Wells and granaries stored water and seeds, for instance, while trade networks could allow for emergency food imports. Still, these systems broke down when droughts lasted too long – and especially when states waged war.

在某些厄尔尼诺事件中、在印度的某些地区，旱灾要等到军队向已然耗竭的乡村索取粮食之后，才演变为大范围饥荒。在印度另外一些地区，当普遍的营养不良与旱区外流的人口相互叠加、助推流行病蔓延时，死亡人数便急剧攀升。总而言之，与强厄尔尼诺状况部分相关的灾难性饥荒，似乎在16、17、18世纪的印度各地夺去了数百万人的生命。

In some El Niño events and in some parts of India, droughts provoked widespread famine only when armies demanded food from the already depleted countryside. In other Indian regions, the death toll soared when widespread malnutrition joined migration from drought-stricken regions to encourage the spread of epidemic disease. All told, catastrophic famines partly linked to strong El Niño conditions appear to have killed millions across 16th-, 17th- and 18th-century India.

凡是厄尔尼诺引发极端天气的地方，当地社群都深受其害。然而，另一些社群却以他们的损失为代价兴旺起来，这正是我们从厄尔尼诺历史中可以辨认出的第二种模式。

Everywhere El Niño sparked extreme weather, communities suffered. Yet some prospered at their expense, and this is the second pattern we can discern in El Niño’s history.

17世纪中国明朝（Ming dynasty）的崩溃，似乎提供了一个鲜明的例证。树木与珊瑚的生长年轮表明，在16世纪末和17世纪初，厄尔尼诺状况一再削弱东亚季风。起初，旱灾同时袭击了中国的东北地区——彼此联系松散的女真（Jurchen）诸部的家园——以及明朝的腹地华北。

T he collapse of China’s Ming dynasty in the 17th century seems to provide a striking example. Growth rings in trees and corals indicate that El Niño conditions repeatedly contributed to a weakening of the East Asian monsoon in the late 16th and early 17th centuries. At first, droughts afflicted both northeastern China – home of the loosely connected Jurchen peoples – and northern China, the Ming heartland.

在中国东北多样的环境中，女真人狩猎、捕鱼、耕种，这种多样化的生计策略使他们对旱灾有了一定的抵御能力。他们还越过长城劫掠明朝境内，夺取囤积的粮食。最终，华北的旱灾愈演愈烈，飙升的粮价与饥荒动摇了明朝政权。而在女真人的土地上，旱情似乎让位于高于常年的降雨。丰沛降雨所提供的食物，也许有助于把女真各社群整合为一个统一的「满洲」（Manchu）国家——这个国家足够强大，得以利用明朝的虚弱征服中国，建立起新的清朝（Qing dynasty）。

In the diverse environments of northeastern China, the Jurchen hunted, fished and farmed, a varied subsistence strategy that gave them some protection from drought. They also raided across the Great Wall into Ming territory, where they seized stockpiled grain. Eventually, droughts intensified in northern China, where soaring food prices and famine helped to destabilise the Ming regime. But drought seems to have yielded to above-average rainfall in Jurchen territories. Food provided by plentiful rain may have aided efforts to consolidate Jurchen communities into a united ‘Manchu’ state that was strong enough to exploit the Ming dynasty’s weakness, conquer China, and establish a new Qing dynasty.

事实上，征服与殖民主义创造了一种手段，使厄尔尼诺的影响对某些社群被放大，对另一些社群则被减轻、甚至被反过来利用。这种脆弱性的再分配，从未像19世纪末那样清晰而惨烈——彼时异常强烈的厄尔尼诺状况，恰逢全球帝国主义的时代。

Indeed, conquest and colonialism created a means by which the impacts of El Niño could be amplified for some communities, but alleviated or even exploited for others. This redistribution of vulnerability was never clearer or more catastrophic than in the late 19th century, when exceptionally strong El Niño conditions coincided with a global age of imperialism.

殖民主义与危机之间的确切关联，各地并不相同

The exact connection between colonialism and crisis differed from place to place

1876年，旱灾席卷印度，并在1877-78年的强厄尔尼诺中进一步加剧。彼时正值英属印度（British Raj）的鼎盛年代。殖民官员曾强迫许多印度农耕社群改种茶叶、棉花之类的经济作物，早已颠覆了他们的生活与生计。此刻，粮食已不敷分配。能收上来的谷物，要么从最贫困的社群转运到人们付得起更高价钱的地方，要么被对市场干预不屑一顾的殖民官员出口到印度之外。

In 1876, drought spread across India, then intensified with a strong El Niño in 1877-78. It was the heyday of the British Raj. Colonial officials had upended lives and livelihoods in many Indian farming communities by forcing them to plant cash crops such as tea or cotton. Now, there wasn’t enough food to go round. What grain could be harvested was either redirected from the poorest communities to areas where people could pay more, or exported out of India by colonial administrators who scorned market interventions.

随之而来的饥荒夺去了数千万人的生命。

Tens of millions died in the ensuing famines.

「最后的兽群」（The Last of the Herd），霍勒斯·哈拉尔（Horace Harral）作，刊于1877年10月号《图画报》（The Graphic）。图片由维基媒体（Wikimedia）提供。

‘The Last of the Herd’ by Horace Harral from the October 1877 edition of The Graphic . Courtesy Wikimedia

在19世纪末，凡是厄尔尼诺引发旱灾的地方，类似的悲剧都接连上演，只是殖民主义与危机之间的确切关联因地而异。例如在厄立特里亚（Eritrea）和埃塞俄比亚（Ethiopia），意大利侵略者带来了感染牛瘟（rinderpest）的牛只，这种致命疾病在1890-91年另一场强厄尔尼诺到来之前便已蔓延至当地牛群。牛群的大批死亡加剧了粮食短缺，浩大蝗群的到来亦是如此。

Similar tragedies played out wherever El Niño sparked drought in the late 19th century, though the exact connection between colonialism and crisis differed from place to place. In Eritrea and Ethiopia, for instance, Italian invaders arrived with cattle infected by rinderpest, a deadly disease that spread to local cattle before the arrival of another strong El Niño in 1890-91. Widespread mortality among cattle intensified food shortages, as did the arrival of immense locust swarms.

埃塞俄比亚或许有三分之一的人口死于此难。

Perhaps a third of Ethiopia’s population perished.

然而，美国（United States）却常常受益于厄尔尼诺状况。1877-78年，世界各地的旱灾推高谷物价格，美国农民向欧洲出口了数量惊人的小麦。随之流入的以黄金为后盾的信贷强化了美国的财政，并帮助政府应对南北战争（Civil War）的债务。一个世纪后，在1972年的又一次强厄尔尼诺期间，秘鲁鳀鱼渔业因海水变暖与连年过度捕捞而崩溃，使家禽养殖者失去了鱼粉；这却让美国农民得以改种大豆这一替代品而获利。在更为强烈的1982-83年事件中，倾盆大雨重创了加利福尼亚（California）等南部各州，但总体而言，温暖的冬季天气为美国经济注入了强劲的刺激。

T he United States, however, often benefitted from El Niño conditions. As worldwide droughts drove up grain prices in 1877-78, US farmers exported enormous quantities of wheat to Europe. The resulting inflow of gold-backed credit strengthened US finances, and helped the government manage its Civil War debts. A century later, during another strong El Niño in 1972, the collapse of the Peruvian anchovy fishery, owing both to warming water and years of overfishing, deprived poultry producers of fishmeal. But that allowed US farmers to profit by planting soy, a substitute. In the even stronger 1982-83 event, torrential rains devastated California and other southern states, but overall, the US economy received a powerful stimulus from mild winter weather.

正是在这个时候——20世纪末——科学家和记者创造了现代的厄尔尼诺概念：一种每隔几年重现一次、带来可预见灾害的全球性事件。这是我们历史中的第三种模式。厄尔尼诺不仅是一种环境实在，也是一种文化实在：一种被制造出来的威胁，是我们这个时代独有的产物。

It was around this time – the late 20th century – that scientists and journalists created the modern concept of El Niño as a global event that recurred every few years to cause predictable disasters. This is the third pattern in our history. El Niño is not just an environmental reality; it’s also a cultural reality: a manufactured threat that is a unique product of our time.

在某种意义上，这种威胁正是1957-58年国际地球物理年（International Geophysical Year，IGY）的产物。国际地球物理年期间，一场观测地球与近地空间的全球性活动，成为美苏两国政府科技竞争的竞技场。国际地球物理年恰逢一次厄尔尼诺（El Niño）事件，史无前例的观测帮助科学家开始认识太平洋变暖的真实规模。

In some respects, that threat was a creation of the 1957-58 International Geophysical Year (IGY). During the IGY, a global campaign to observe Earth and near-Earth space served as an arena for scientific and technological competition between the US and Soviet governments. The IGY fortuitously coincided with an El Niño, and unprecedented observations helped scientists begin to understand the true dimensions of Pacific Ocean warming.

不同厄尔尼诺事件的增强方式各不相同

It turned out that different El Niño events strengthened in different ways

冷战政治持续塑造着厄尔尼诺研究。1959年的古巴革命（Cuban Revolution），连同对家禽养殖者而言日益重要的秘鲁（Peru）鳀鱼渔业，共同促使美国支持美国与拉美科学家展开合作。这些合作——其中许多以厄尔尼诺研究为重点——本意在于展示与美国机构合作的价值。

Cold War politics continued to shape El Niño science. The 1959 Cuban Revolution joined the growing importance – for poultry farmers – of the Peruvian anchovy fishery to spur US support for collaborations between US and Latin American scientists. These collaborations, many of which focused on the study of El Niño, were supposed to demonstrate the value of partnership with US institutions.

当各国海军部署核潜艇以充实新近建成的核武库时，它们开始资助海洋学研究，部分原因是为了更好地了解水下战场。在此之前，海洋学家只能乘船偶尔造访海洋环境。自1950年代起，新的资金与技术使他们得以布放仪器，对海洋环境进行连续监测，一次可持续数月乃至数年。

When navies deployed nuclear submarines to reinforce newly developed nuclear arsenals, they began to fund oceanographic research partly to better understand the underwater battlespace. Until then, oceanographers had been able to visit marine environments only occasionally, in ships. Beginning in the 1950s, new funds and technology allowed them to deploy instruments to monitor environments continuously, for months or years at a time.

国际地球物理年期间斯普特尼克1号（Sputnik 1）的发射，还预示着一个新时代的来临：气象卫星自高空对海洋保持不间断的监视。计算机模型很快便似乎不仅能够预报全球变暖，还能预报南方涛动（Southern Oscillation）的状态。到1980年代初，多位知名科学家相信，厄尔尼诺终于可以提前数月得到预测。

The launch of Sputnik 1 during the IGY also heralded a new age in which weather satellites kept the oceans under constant supervision from above. And computer models soon seemed capable of forecasting not just global warming, but also the state of the Southern Oscillation. By the early 1980s, prominent scientists believed that El Niño could finally be predicted months in advance.

然而，1982-83年的强厄尔尼诺令绝大多数气象学家和海洋学家大感震惊。事实证明，不同的厄尔尼诺事件以不同的方式增强，各自对区域天气格局的影响也截然不同。由于这场始料未及的厄尔尼诺引发洪水、干旱与风暴，导致数千人丧生、造成数十亿美元的损失，并令全球经济蒙受估计达数万亿美元的增长损失，科学家们加倍努力，试图预报厄尔尼诺及其遥相关（teleconnection）。

Yet the strong El Niño of 1982-83 came as a shock to the overwhelming majority of meteorologists and oceanographers. It turned out that different El Niño events strengthened in different ways, and each had distinct impacts on regional weather patterns. Because the unanticipated El Niño led to floods, droughts and storms that killed thousands of people, inflicted billions of dollars in damage, and cost the global economy trillions in estimated lost growth, scientists redoubled their efforts to forecast El Niño and its teleconnections.

厄尔尼诺作为全球灾难的观念，在美国大众媒体的历史中根深蒂固

The concept of El Niño as a global disaster had deep roots in the history of US mass media

1985年，作为联合国机构的世界气象组织（World Meteorological Organization）启动了一项为期十年的计划，在热带太平洋各处布设海洋监测浮标。科学家还开始同时利用社会档案与自然档案，重建厄尔尼诺的久远历史。到1997年初，他们已积累了足够的认识，得以在又一次海洋变暖发生前数月成功做出预测。美国国家海洋和大气管理局（NOAA）与美国国家航空航天局（NASA）的科学家向美国记者发出警示，后者随即掀起一场媒体轰动：厄尔尼诺被重新想象为一场迫近的全球性浩劫。

In 1985, the World Meteorological Organization – a United Nations agency – launched a decade-long project to scatter ocean-monitoring buoys across the tropical Pacific. Scientists also began to use both societal and natural archives to reconstruct the deep history of El Niño. In early 1997, they had learned enough to successfully predict another ocean warming months before it happened. Scientists from the US National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) and the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) alerted US journalists, and they created a media sensation: an El Niño reimagined as a looming, worldwide catastrophe.

颇具讽刺意味的是，正是美国记者塑造了这种充满威胁意味的厄尔尼诺新形象。在美国，1997-98年厄尔尼诺带来的经济利益将再度远远盖过其造成的损害。然而，厄尔尼诺作为全球灾难的观念，在美国大众媒体的历史中早已根深蒂固。

It’s ironic that American journalists developed this threatening new view of El Niño. In the US, the economic benefits of the 1997-98 El Niño would again far outweigh the damage. But the concept of El Niño as a global disaster had deep roots in the history of US mass media.

自19世纪以来，美国记者便一直通过把自然现象描绘成耸人听闻、往往带有威胁色彩的事件——唯有胆识过人的科学家方能参透的事件——来刺激读者的神经。例如，彗星或行星一次例行而近距离的接近地球，会被渲染成一场与死亡擦肩的遭遇，由驻守遥远天文台、颇具冒险家气派的科学家们记录在案。

Since the 19th century, US reporters had thrilled audiences by portraying natural phenomena as sensational, often threatening events – events that could be understood only by plucky scientists. A close but routine approach of a comet or planet to Earth, for example, became a brush with death recorded by swashbuckling scientists in far-flung observatories.

这类新闻报道之所以引起共鸣，正因为其身处的文化深受「末日净化」这一宗教观念的浸染。这也有助于解释，为什么20世纪对大气变化的科学研究——不仅是全球变暖，还包括核冬天（nuclear winter）、臭氧层空洞、酸雨，甚至小行星撞击或火山喷发的潜在后果——都依赖美国的资金与机构，却又尤其令美国公民感到惊恐。

It was journalism that resonated in a culture shaped by religious concepts of a purifying apocalypse. And it helps explain why, in the 20th century, the scientific investigation of changes in the atmosphere – not just global warming, but also nuclear winter, the ozone hole, acid rain, and even the potential consequences of asteroid impacts or volcanic eruptions – relied on US funding and institutions, while coming to particularly alarm US citizens.

此时，一些美国记者借「El Niño」这个西班牙语名称来唤起一种外来威胁的形象，其中几人甚至将其比作来自南美洲（South America）的移民。随着1997-98年厄尔尼诺升温为19世纪以来最强的事件，「都怪厄尔尼诺」与「别怪我，要怪就怪厄尔尼诺！」成了随处可见的口号，从汽车保险杠贴纸到T恤衫无所不在。尽管科学家尚无法准确预测厄尔尼诺将如何影响世界上大部分地区的天气，更遑论各地人群将作何反应，情形依然如此。

Now, some US journalists used the Spanish name of El Niño to conjure an image of a foreign menace that several compared to migrants from South America. As the 1997-98 El Niño warmed into the strongest event since the 19th century, ‘Blame it on El Niño’ and ‘Don’t blame me, blame El Niño!’ were common slogans on everything from bumper stickers to T-shirts. That was true even though scientists couldn’t predict exactly how El Niño would affect weather in most parts of the world, let alone how populations would react.

正是在1997年，我们今天对厄尔尼诺的理解——一部分是冷战遗存，一部分是科学成就，还有一部分是媒体怪物——就此形成。

It was in 1997 that today’s understanding of El Niño – part Cold War relic, part scientific achievement, and part media monster – was born.

厄尔尼诺过往的历史规律告诉我们，热带太平洋变暖对于暴露于干旱与洪水之中的社区可能是一场灾难。但这些历史同样揭示：厄尔尼诺灾难的成因，与其说在于极端天气的猛烈程度，不如说在于一些社区遭另一些社区剥削的残酷方式。它们还表明，今天人们想象中的厄尔尼诺——一个对天气具有可预测影响的全球威胁——与其说是对现实的准确反映，不如说是现代科学、媒体与地缘政治共同营造的产物。

T he patterns in El Niño’s past teach us that the warming of the tropical Pacific can be disastrous for communities exposed to droughts and floods. But they also reveal that El Niño disasters are caused less by the severity of weather extremes, and more by the brutality of how some communities are exploited by others. They suggest that today’s imagined El Niño – a global threat, with predictable impacts on weather – is less an accurate reflection of reality than a creation of modern science, media and geopolitics.

在未来数年乃至数十年里，厄尔尼诺对人类的影响很可能主要由人际关系与观念塑造，而非由自然塑造。但如今的人类活动已包括规模如此之巨的温室气体污染，以至于相对于19世纪末的平均温度，地球已升温约1.5°C。这种污染正在急剧放大厄尔尼诺对天气的影响：热浪愈发酷烈，不断加剧的蒸发令干旱日益恶化；部分由于变暖的大气能容纳更多水汽，暴雨也愈发猛烈。

In the coming years and decades, it’s likely that El Niño’s impacts on populations will be shaped primarily by human relationships and ideas, not by nature. But human actions now include greenhouse gas pollution on such a scale that Earth has warmed by about 1.5°C, relative to its average temperature in the late 19th century. This pollution is supercharging the impacts of El Niño on weather. Heatwaves are growing hotter, and intensifying evaporation means droughts are getting worse. Partly because a warm atmosphere holds more water, downpours are ever more intense.

全球变暖似乎并不能终结南方涛动，以及与之相伴的厄尔尼诺与拉尼娜（La Niña）之间亘古以来的拉锯。不过，它可能导致更加强烈的厄尔尼诺事件，进而引发更加极端的天气。

Global warming doesn’t seem capable of ending the Southern Oscillation, and with it the age-old seesaw of El Niño and La Niña. Still, it could lead to even more intense El Niño events that cause even more extreme weather.

减少温室气体排放从未像现在这样紧迫。但厄尔尼诺的历史也就适应气候极端事件给我们上了一课更深刻的道理。今天，世界似乎日益被再度兴起的大国竞争所塑造：被军国主义，以及超级大国与其附庸之间的殖民式关系所塑造。历史表明，这样一个世界可能是数以百万计的人无谓丧生的世界——不仅死于战争，也死于本可预防的气候灾难。

It’s never been more urgent to reduce greenhouse gas emissions. But El Niño’s past also teaches us a deeper lesson about adapting to climatic extremes. Today, the world seems increasingly shaped by renewed great power competition: by militarism and colonial relationships between superpowers and their patrons. History suggests that such a world could be one in which millions die unnecessarily, not just through war but in preventable climate disasters.

这是来自过去的警告，今天的各国政府若能引以为鉴，方为明智——在未来的数月与数年里，厄尔尼诺将再度重塑从世界一端到另一端的环境。

It’s a warning from the past that today’s governments would be wise to heed, as El Niño reshapes environments from one end of the world to another in the months and years to come.
