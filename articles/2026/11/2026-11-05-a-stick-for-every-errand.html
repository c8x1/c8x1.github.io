一桩事，一根签

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年11月5日·4分钟阅读

巴河上只有一条渡。北岸三十五户人家进镇，贩菜、抓药、赶集、打官司，全凭这一船。渡口的老规矩比船还老：下船的人往签筒里投一根竹签，一日扎一捆，月底点捆对账，船钱照捆数结给佃渡的桑家。腊月对完总账，记签的聂笔杆把一年的三百多个捆从薄到厚排在渡堂的青砖上——这排法是他爹传下的。东家们喝着茶看一眼，说声「年头如此」，捆就收进柜里。年年排，年年是一座堆：当中厚，两头薄。

There is only one ferry on the Ba River. The thirty-five households on the north bank reach town by it — selling greens, filling prescriptions, attending fairs, filing suits. The crossing's oldest rule is older than its boats: every passenger drops a bamboo stick into the tally tube on stepping ashore; one bundle is tied each day; at each month's end the bundles are counted and the fare paid over to the San family, who hold the lease. When the year's accounts close in the twelfth month, Nie the tally-clerk lays the year's three-hundred-odd bundles on the brick floor from thinnest to thickest — an ordering handed down from his father. The masters sip tea, glance over, say "so went the year," and the bundles go back into the cabinet. Every year the same shape: thick in the middle, thin at both ends.

签是三十五户投的，一根签背后一桩事。卖菜的看天色，天晴菜鲜就过河，落雨就歇；药铺的伙计看东家脸色，脸色温和才敢告假；寡妇桂婶等儿子的信，信到人到，信不到人不到；赌棍六指手气旺时连走三日，手气背了半月不沾船板。豆腐坊逢集才动身，贩炭的看窑上的火。上船之前，谁也不问谁。老庚头前年没了，他的签跟着少了；新添的事又把签数填了回来。渡口的人手换了几茬，签筒里只认签，不认人。

The sticks come from thirty-five households, and one stick stands for one errand. The vegetable seller watches the sky: sun on the greens, she crosses; rain, she rests. The apothecary's assistant watches his master's face, and asks leave only when it is mild. Widow Gui waits on her son's letters: the letter arrives, she arrives; no letter, no crossing. Six-Finger the gambler walks three days running when luck is hot, and keeps off the deck half a month when it turns. The tofu shop moves only on fair days; the charcoal men watch the kiln. Before boarding, no one asks anyone. Old Geng died the year before last, and his sticks went with him; new errands arrived and filled the count back in. The hands at the crossing have turned over a few times; the tally tube knows sticks, not faces.

聂笔杆的爹记了半辈子签，也找了半辈子一样东西。他爹说，人间的日子乱得像一把撒开的签，捆排出来却年年是同一座堆，当中厚，两头薄，齐得过分——天底下没有这么巧的事，必有一只手在排班。他问遍三十五户，问到临终，只留下一句话：「手一定有。我没找着。」今年腊月排完堆，镇上的闲话起来了：堆年年齐成这样，里头必有蹊跷；聂家两代管签，那只手八成攥在聂家袖筒里，按堆法吃船钱的空子。渡会的东家们信了一半，放出话来：来年腊月对签结账那日说清楚，签筒还归聂家；说不清楚，交筒走人。

Nie's father kept the sticks for half a lifetime, and hunted one thing for the same half. The days of this world, he said, lie scattered like a thrown fistful of sticks; yet the bundles laid out make the same mound every year — thick in the middle, thin at the ends, unreasonably neat. Nothing under heaven is that neat by chance; there must be a hand arranging the rota. He asked his way through all thirty-five households, asked to his deathbed, and left one sentence behind: "The hand is there. I never found it." This twelfth month, when the mound was laid out, the town's talk began: a mound that regular, year upon year, smells of arrangement; and the Nie family has kept the sticks two generations — that hand most likely sits in a Nie sleeve, skimming the fare by the shape of the heap. The ferry guild half believed it and put out word: at next year's counting day, explain yourself; the tube stays with the Nie family — or hand it over and go.

聂笔杆从爹问不完的地方接着问，一户一户去坐。问的话同他爹当年一样：「那一日，你为什么过河？」答数各是各的：一场雨过后菜价好；东家新纳了小，伙计躲出来三日；儿子的信在路上走了两个月，人上岸时霜已经落了。他把答数记在肚里，又拿去同爹当年念叨过的旧答数比，对不上了。当年伙计告假是为老娘的咳嗽，如今是为躲东家；当年六指赢的是牌九，如今押的是蛐蛐。人还是那些人，缘由换了几茬。可柜里爹排过的堆，同今年青砖上这座，还是一个模样。

Nie took up the asking where his father's had stopped, sitting in one household after another. The question was his father's question: "That day — why did you cross?" The answers kept each to itself: the rain had lifted and the greens would fetch a better price; the master had taken a young wife, and the assistant hid out three days; a son's letter had spent two months on the road, and frost was down by the time he stepped ashore. He stored the answers in his head and set them beside the old ones his father used to recite — they no longer matched. In the father's day the assistant asked leave for his mother's cough; now he hides from the master's new wife. In the father's day Six-Finger won at Pai Gow; now he bets on crickets. The same people, their reasons turned over a few times. Yet the mounds his father laid out, and this year's on the bricks, were one and the same shape.

开春，他试了个狠法子：自掏腰包包下三户人家一年的船钱，只求一件事——把过河的日子挪开，船走得少的日子多走，船走得满的日子歇脚。三户贪这笔包钱，真挪了。聂笔杆一日一日盯住签筒，等堆散架。到了腊月，捆照旧排开：当中薄了一指，边上厚了一指，两桩新添的事又把当中填了回去。堆还是那座堆。他在青砖前蹲了半晌，起身时把三户的包钱折进自家的亏空里，认了。

In spring he tried a harder thing: out of his own pocket he took on a full year's fares for three households, on one condition — shift your crossing days; travel more on the days the boat runs light, and rest on the days it runs full. The three households, tempted by the free fare, truly shifted. Nie watched the tally tube day after day, waiting for the mound to come apart. In the twelfth month the bundles were laid out as always: the middle thinner by one finger's worth, the ends thicker by one, and two new errands had filled the middle back in. Still the same mound. He squatted before the bricks a long while, and when he rose he booked the fares of those three households into his own loss, and let it go.

对签结账那日，渡堂里坐得满。聂笔杆不辩闲话，把三十五户的答数一户一句背出来，哪一日、哪一户、为什么过河，从正月背到腊月。背到贩炭那家，是窑塌砸了人，过河请郎中；背到东家们自家的行商，是过河去对契约。渡会里疑心最重的顾东家从去年的捆里抽出自己那根签，问：「这一根，谁排的？」聂笔杆答：「那日您家同米行打官司，文书过河对笔迹。」顾东家把签放回捆里，没再言语。签筒留在了聂家。来年是瘟年，三十五户走了十几户，渡口添上挑药担的和拉纤的，秋后排堆，青砖上还是那座堆。爹找了一辈子的手，他如今知道了：筒里没有，筒外也没有。堆是签自己成的。

On counting day the ferry hall sat full. Nie offered no defense against the talk. Instead he recited the thirty-five households' answers, one household one sentence — which day, which household, why they crossed, from the first month through the twelfth. For the charcoal family: the kiln had caved and crushed a man; they crossed to fetch a doctor. For the guild's own traveling merchants: contracts to compare. Gu, the most suspicious of the masters, drew his own stick from last year's bundle and asked, "This one — who arranged it?" Nie answered: "That day your house was suing the rice dealer; the papers crossed to have the handwriting compared." Gu put the stick back in the bundle and said nothing more. The tally tube stayed with the Nie family. The next year was a plague year: a dozen-odd of the thirty-five households went quiet, the crossing took up medicine carriers and rope-haulers, and when autumn's bundles were laid out, the bricks still held the same mound. The hand his father had hunted all his life, he now knew: not inside the tube, and not outside it either. The mound is what the sticks made of themselves.

注解：这个故事讲的是中心极限定理（the central limit theorem）。一根签背后一桩互不知情的事，事叠得多了，总数的起落自己拢成当中厚、两头薄的形状，年年如此；没有谁排班，形状是叠出来的。边界：单看哪一户，日子照样没个准形；起落奇大的人家再多些，堆要拢得稳，就得有更多桩事叠进来。

Note: This story is about the central limit theorem. One stick, one errand, and no errand consults another; pile enough of them, and the rise and fall of the total settles on its own into a shape thick in the middle and thin at both ends, reappearing year after year. No one keeps the rota; the shape is what piling makes. The boundary: looked at singly, no household's days take any shape at all; and the more households there are whose comings and goings swing wildly, the more errands must be piled in before the mound holds its form.
