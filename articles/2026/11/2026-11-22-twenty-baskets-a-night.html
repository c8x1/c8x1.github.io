一夜二十笼

寓言故事 · Claude Opus

Claude Opus · AI Parable·每日精选·2026年11月22日·2分钟阅读

我家的焙房在镇子东头，三代人守着一口炭火。焙笼一列排开，青叶进来，全凭一双手在笼里抖与翻——快一分叶子要焦，慢一分叶子要闷。祖父在时，一夜二十笼；到我接手，还是一夜二十笼。叶有叶的脾气，火有火的脾气，能改的只有熬人的时辰。

Our roasting house stands at the east end of town; three generations of us have kept its charcoal fire. The baskets stand in a row, green leaf goes in, and everything depends on a pair of hands shaking and turning it — go too fast and the leaf scorches, too slow and it sours. In my grandfather's day one fire finished twenty baskets a night; under my watch it is still twenty baskets a night. Leaf keeps its own temper, fire keeps its own; the only thing that ever changes is how the night wears a man down.

变的是外头。前年河上游起了纱厂，头一年一个织工守两张机，去年添了新机器，一个织工看八张机，出的布一匹压着一匹。纱厂一遍遍加工钱，镇上别的行当想留人，只好跟着抬。我焙房里两个年轻学徒先后渡河去了，走时都红着脸，说不是我待他们薄。

What changed was outside. Two years ago a cotton mill went up above the river; in its first year one weaver worked two looms, and after new machines arrived last year one weaver tends eight, cloth coming off bolt upon bolt. The mill lifted its pay again and again, and every trade in town had to lift its own to keep anyone at all. My two young apprentices crossed the river one after the other; both left with red faces, saying it was not that I had treated them poorly.

焙房里只剩下岑师傅。头一批春茶落焙前十二天，他夜里来寻我，蹲在门槛上抽了半袋烟才开口：纱厂的锅炉房缺一个看火的人，同样守火，工钱翻倍，还上白班。他说他舍不得这口火，可儿子在纱厂做工，孙子在县里念书，家里处处等钱。送他出门时，我心里另压着一件事：他一走，头一批春茶出不来；邱掌柜按我父亲当年立的字据来收，交不上货，这间焙房连宅带火都得抵出去。十二天里，我要么照纱厂的价留人，要么眼看火冷。

Only Master Cen was left. Twelve days before the first spring tea went over the coals, he came to me at night, squatted on the doorsill, and smoked through half a pipe before speaking: the mill's boiler room needed a man to watch its fire — same watching, double the pay, and daylight shifts. He said he could hardly bear to leave this fire of ours, but his son worked at the mill, his grandson schooled in the county town, and his household needed money at every turn. As I walked him out, another matter pressed on me: if he left, the first spring batch would fail; Merchant Qiu collected on the paper my father had signed, and if the goods fell short, this house and its fire would be forfeit for it. I had twelve days to match the mill's price for a man, or watch the fire go cold.

第二天我去见邱掌柜，把今年的茶价每斤加了三成。他把笔往桌上一搁：「布一年贱过一年，谷一年贱过一年，单单茶一年贵过一年，凭什么？」我说：纱厂一个织工看八张机，布贱得下去；我这里一夜还是二十笼，贱不下去。纱厂把工钱抬到哪儿，我就得跟到哪儿，不跟，火边留不住人。他瞪了我半晌，末了还是画了押——镇上的人，春茶横竖是要喝的。那年春火如期开炉，岑师傅留在了火边。

The next morning I called on Merchant Qiu and lifted this year's price by three parts in ten. He set his brush down hard. "Cloth gets cheaper year after year, grain gets cheaper year after year — why should tea alone get dearer?" I said: one weaver at the mill tends eight looms now, so cloth can afford to fall; under my roof it is still twenty baskets a night, and that cannot fall. Wherever the mill lifts its pay I must follow, because if I don't, no one will stand at my fire. He glared at me a good while, and in the end pressed his seal to the paper — the town will drink its spring tea whatever it costs. That year the fire opened on schedule, and Master Cen stayed at it.

往后年年如此。每斤茶的价钱一年抬一截，镇上渐渐起了闲话，说焙房心黑，说焙茶的人养懒了。岑师傅听见只笑，手上功夫一分不省，青叶照旧一笼一笼地过手。

It has gone on like that every year since. The price of each jin of tea climbs another notch each spring, and talk has spread through the town: the roasting house has turned greedy, the roasters have grown lazy. Master Cen only laughs when he hears it; he spares nothing of his craft, and the green leaf still passes through his hands basket by basket.

可另一头我也看得真：镇上家家比祖父那阵宽裕；清明前来买茶的队，年年比前一年排得长，连火边站着的年轻人，也比我父亲那阵多了起来。只有穷人家买茶，掂在手里比从前更沉，这一层我不替自己辩。去年岑师傅的手抖了，他孙子从县里回来，接过了那口火。

But the other side I see just as clearly: every household in town lives wider than in my grandfather's day; the queue for spring tea before Clear-and-Bright grows longer year upon year, and even the young people standing at the fires have grown more numerous than in my father's time. Only for the poorest does a purchase of tea weigh heavier in the hand than it used to — that much I will not explain away. Last year Master Cen's hands began to tremble, and his grandson came back from the county town and took over that fire.

注解：这便是鲍莫尔成本病（Baumol's cost disease）：活计快不起来的行当为了留人，只能跟着快得起来的行当一路把工钱抬上去，而每人一夜做成的还是那么多，这样的东西便一年比一年金贵；它不来自懒与贪，镇子整体的家底其实始终付得起，只是穷人家掂着分外沉。（本文由 Claude Opus 创作）

Note: This is Baumol's cost disease: a trade whose work cannot speed up must follow the trades that can, lifting pay to keep its people, while each pair of hands still finishes the same night's work — so such things grow dearer year after year. It comes from neither laziness nor greed, and the town as a whole can go on affording them; they only weigh heaviest on the poor. (Written by Claude Opus)
