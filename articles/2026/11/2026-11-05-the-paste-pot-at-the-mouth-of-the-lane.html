巷口的浆糊罐

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年11月5日·3分钟阅读

我接手巷口那面照壁，是叔父病倒那年秋后。所谓接手，其实简单：每月挨家收一回公钱，付两笔工钱，一笔给扫街的，一笔给守墙的蔡老爹。照壁朝南，一丈多宽，巷里人有个什么事，写张纸贴上去，寻猫的、租屋的、招短工的、带路的都有；闲下来我数过一回，常年是十五桩。蔡老爹守墙守了半辈子，家什就两样：一只浆糊罐，一把秃了毛的棕刷，他自己从不往墙上贴一张纸。谁家把纸贴重了，他傍晚去揭，一桩事只留头一张；谁来了忘带浆糊，罐子递过去，刷子也跟着递过去。

I took over the wall at the mouth of our lane the autumn my uncle took to his bed. The handover was simple enough: each month I went door to door for the public levy and paid out two wages, one to the sweeper, one to Old Cai, who kept the wall. The wall faces south, a good three paces wide, and whenever anyone in the lane had something to say, they wrote out a sheet and pasted it up: a missing cat, a room to let, short work wanted, a guide for the road. Once, with nothing else to do, I counted; there were always fifteen matters up there. Old Cai had kept that wall half a lifetime, and his whole kit was two things, a paste pot and a palm-fiber brush gone bald; he never pasted a single sheet of his own. When somebody's sheet went up twice over, he went at dusk and peeled the extra away, one sheet to a matter; when somebody arrived with a notice and no paste, the pot went over, and the brush went with it.

东街膏药铺的徐掌柜钱给得最足，一季一笔场面钱，抵得上小户们一整年的零贴费。他的帖子也最凶，红纸金字，一贴三十张，把旁人的纸压在底下。叔父留过话：公钱的账，年关要平上，平不上，这串钥匙就得交回去。入冬他寻到我家里，把一沓揭剩的帖子拍在桌上：「我出真金白银贴帖子，你们倒雇个人专门揭我的？」当夜我在灯下把两笔工钱摆在一处：扫街的钱动不得；蔡老爹一月两吊，一年二十四吊；徐掌柜一季就给二十吊。我认了笔大的。

The richest payer was Shopkeeper Xu of the plaster shop on East Street: a handsome display fee every quarter, worth a whole year of the small pasting fees from all the lane's small households put together. His sheets were the fiercest as well, red paper with gold characters, thirty at a time, riding over everyone else's. My uncle had left word that the public account had to come out even at New Year or the ring of keys would go back. In early winter Xu came to my house, slapped a stack of his peeled-down sheets on the table, and said, "I pay good silver to put these up, and you people hire a man just to tear them down?" That night under the lamp I set the two wages side by side: the sweeper's pay could not be touched; Old Cai drew two strings of cash a month, twenty-four in a year; Xu gave twenty a quarter. I sided with the larger sum.

巷议上我提了裁掉守墙的工钱。有人嘀咕这钱给得冤，更多的人打的是徐掌柜那笔季钱的算盘，就都点了头。散会后我去照壁根下找蔡老爹。他正坐在杌子上给一张寻人的纸抹浆糊，听完了，把手在围裙上擦了擦，罐子刷子收进筐里，说了句「筐是我家的，墙是巷子的」，把照壁后小棚的钥匙搁进我手心，走了。那张寻人的纸，他自己贴了上去，四角抚得比谁贴的都平。

At the lane meeting I proposed cutting the wall keeper's wage. A few muttered that the money had never been well spent; more of them were working the abacus on Xu's quarterly fee, and the proposal passed. Afterward I went to the foot of the wall to find Old Cai. He was sitting on his stool, brushing paste onto a missing-person notice. When I had finished speaking, he wiped his hands on his apron, packed pot and brush into his basket, said, "The basket is mine; the wall belongs to the lane," laid the key of the little shed behind the wall in my palm, and left. The missing-person notice he pasted up himself, its four corners smoothed flatter than anyone's.

起初没人觉出什么。徐掌柜的红帖子头一日铺满半面墙，第二日铺满整面。旁人的纸先还往上贴，晌午就沉到红纸底下，后来代写书信的不来了，租屋的也不来了，贴了也白贴。我又数过两回：头一回剩八桩，全挤在墙角；再过半月，整面照壁只剩徐掌柜一家的红纸，一层压一层，浆糊结成了壳。过路的人在墙下不再停脚，一面只说一件事的墙，没什么可看的。腊月里徐掌柜也来寻我，说膏药在巷里一盒没多卖，帖子挪去十字街口了。零贴费断了，季钱也断了，我的账两头落空。

At first nobody felt anything change. Xu's red sheets took half the wall the first day and the whole face of it the second. Other people's papers still went up for a while, sinking beneath the red by noon; then the letter-writer stopped coming, and the room-to-let people stopped too, since pasting had come to mean pasting for nothing. I counted twice more: the first time eight matters were left, all crowded into the corners; half a month on, the whole wall held nothing but the red paper of Xu's one shop, layer over layer, the paste cured into a shell. Passersby stopped stopping at the wall; a wall that says only one thing has nothing worth reading. In the twelfth month Xu came to find me as well: his plasters had not sold one box more inside the lane, and he had moved his posting to the crossroads. The small pasting fees were gone, the quarterly fee was gone, and my account came up empty at both ends.

我寻到蔡老爹，是在城外的纸坊，他在那儿挑拣废纸，手背上的冻口子一道一道。我说工钱照旧，再加一倍。他把筐放下，说加的不使，只要一样：往后该揭的照揭，揭到徐掌柜头上也一样，要我当众认下这一条。我应了。回头我把徐掌柜没到期的季钱原封退了，自己掏的腰包；巷议上我当众认了裁错这笔账，有人说声年轻，交钥匙的话没人再提。腊月末，蔡老爹挑着筐回到照壁根下。

I found Old Cai at the paper mill outside the city walls, sorting waste paper, the backs of his hands cracked from the cold, one split beside another. I said the wage would stand as before, doubled. He set the basket down. The doubling he would not take; he asked for one thing only: whatever needed peeling would be peeled as before, and if it peeled onto Shopkeeper Xu's head, just the same, and I was to own that rule in front of everyone. I agreed. I refunded Xu's unexpired quarter just as it had come, out of my own pocket; at the lane meeting I owned the wrong of cutting the wage, a voice or two excused me as young, and nobody raised the keys again. At the end of the twelfth month, Old Cai shouldered his basket and came back to the foot of the wall.

开春头一个月，他几乎天天在揭旧壳，揭下来的纸屑装了几筐，墙皮重新露出青砖本色。头一张新纸是寻猫的，别的纸跟着一张一张上来；到二月里我再数，十四桩。徐掌柜也回来了，这回自己只写一张，央蔡老爹给贴在正当中，季钱照给。过路的人又肯在墙下站一站，看一阵再走。年关对账，那二十四吊照旧写回蔡老爹名下，空缺是我拿积蓄垫上的，叔父那串钥匙，留在了我裤腰上。账上数来数去，他那一行最小；那一行空着的两个月，别的行一行也没进来。

Through the first month of spring he was peeling the old shell almost daily; the scrap came away in basketloads, and the wall showed its gray brick again. The first fresh sheet was a missing cat, and the other papers came up one after another behind it; by the second month I counted fourteen matters. Shopkeeper Xu came back too, wrote out a single sheet himself this time, asked Old Cai to paste it dead center, and paid his quarter as before. Passersby would stop at the wall again, read a while, then walk on. When the accounts were reckoned at New Year, the twenty-four strings of cash went back under Old Cai's name, the shortfall was padded out of my own savings, and my uncle's ring of keys stayed on my belt. Count every line in that ledger, and his is the smallest; for the two months that line stood empty, not one other line came in.

注解：关键物种（keystone species）数量占比很小，对群落的影响却大得不成比例：海獭按住海胆，海胆才啃不倒海藻林；海獭一旦被移走，海胆爆发，海藻林连同依赖它的种种一起崩塌。寓言只画了「按住扩张最猛的一家」这一种样子；现实中传粉的、像河狸那样改造生境的也能担这个位置，且同一物种换个环境未必仍是这样。

Note: A keystone species holds a place in its community far out of proportion to its numbers: sea otters keep sea urchins down, and so the urchins cannot mow away the kelp forests; once the otters are taken out, the urchins erupt, and the forests collapse together with everything that depended on them. The fable draws only the one shape of it, where the boldest spreader is held down; in reality pollinators, and habitat-shapers like beavers, can hold the same post, and a species keystone in one setting may not be keystone in another.
