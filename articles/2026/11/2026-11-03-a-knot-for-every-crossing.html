每穿一趟，打一个结

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年11月3日·3分钟阅读

我在海门码头边的大货行当写字的丫头，凳子摆在堂中间，四面柜台围着我。这行有条不成文的规矩：谁打我身边过，谁顺口一句就算吩咐——掌印的、验货的、跑船的，谁都有份。于是我一天到晚在堂里穿来穿去，差事一句一句落到我头上。没人替我排过路线，我自己也排不出来：上一刻往东走，下一刻就被人喊回西头。堂门开着，我的腿就不停；门板一下，我坐下，像一截被放回匣里的墨锭。

I was the copy girl at the great trading house by the wharf, my stool set in the middle of the hall with counters on every side. The house kept an unwritten rule: whoever passed me could speak a word and it counted as an order — the seal keeper, the goods inspector, the ship runners all had a claim on my legs. So I crossed and recrossed the hall from morning till night, my errands handed to me one voice at a time. No one ever drew my route, and I could not have drawn it: bound east one moment, called back to the west end the next. While the front doors stood open my legs never rested; once the boards went up, I sat down like an ink stick returned to its box.

西窗底下坐着织花边的耿婆婆，腿脚钉在机子跟前，一坐一整天。她识不得几个字，也不管堂里的事，却做了一件谁都想不到的事：每见我打她窗前那块亮地里穿过去一趟，她就在手边一根多余的经线上挽一个结。一天下来，线上密密一片。我起先只当是老人家解闷。到了季末，她把线头两头一挽，撂进堂里卖杂线的筐底，另起一根新的。

By the west window sat old Granny Geng at her lace loom, her feet planted in one place all day. She could barely read and minded nobody's business, yet she alone did a thing nobody would have thought of: every time she saw me pass through the bright strip of floor before her window, she tied one knot in a spare warp thread at her elbow. By day's end the thread was crowded with them. I took it at first for an old woman's idle pastime. When the season closed she looped the two ends together, dropped the thread into the basket of odds and ends kept for sale, and started a fresh one.

那年秋天船期乱了，行里传下话来要裁人。领班的佟先生先拿我的腿做文章。他说，像样的堂面，人走的是直线；一个丫头成天满堂乱转，是堂规有毛病。他当着东家的面押上一季俸钱，同我赌：他跟着我抄三天，抄下每一句打发我的话，午铃响之前，先写下我那时站在哪一块砖上。他抄了三天。忙时吩咐一层摞一层，前一句还没落纸，后一句已经把我喊回头；他抄漏的、抄串行的，比我手里的单子还多。第三天午铃响，他写下那块砖空着，我人立在他背后。一季俸钱，他当场认了。

That autumn the shipping schedules fell into disorder, and word came down that the house would cut staff. Head clerk Tong opened his case with my legs. In a hall worth its rules, he said, people walk straight lines; a girl who wheels about the floor all day is a defect in the rules themselves. Before the master he staked a season's pay on a wager: he would follow me for three days, copy down every instruction thrown at me, and before the noon bell write down in advance the very floor tile I would be standing on. He copied for three days. In the busy hours the instructions came stacked layer on layer; before one reached his paper, the next had already called me back. What he missed and what he misread outnumbered the slips in my own hand. On the third day, at the noon bell, the tile he had written down stood empty, and I was standing behind him. He paid the season's pay on the spot.

有人说丫头腿勤快，不过因为人年轻，心又热。堂里那辆没人使唤的空茶车替我把这话堵了回去：它没有腿，也没有心，过路人擦它一下，它挪半寸，一天下来也能歪出半间堂，车印和我的脚印一样没个准头。后来行里换过人来顶我的缺，出身体面，穿得也光鲜；堂里一样忙的那几日，她穿堂的趟数同我没有什么两样。换人不换堂：堂里忙，人转得急；堂里闲，人转得缓。冬至前后货船不来，我能在一条凳子上坐住半晌。

Some said a young girl's busy legs came of nothing more than youth and warm blood. The idle tea cart answered that for me: it had no legs and no heart, yet every passerby who brushed it moved it half an inch, and by day's end it had wandered a good part of the hall, its wheel marks as wayless as my footprints. The house once brought in a girl to take my place, well born and finely dressed; in a week just as busy, her crossings ran no differently from mine. Change the person and the hall remains the hall: when trade runs hot the whole floor quickens, and when it cools the floor slows. Around the winter solstice, when no cargo boats came in, I could sit half a morning on one stool.

裁谁的名册，东家没有照佟先生拟的那份。他反倒问我：堂里到底还有多少只手在做活，绸缎柜后身、账房里间这些他看不见的地方，报上来的数目可信不可信。我想起筐底那根挽满结的线，取回来，一结一结数给他看。那根线前半截密，一天多少结我记得；越往后越稀，最后一个月短到不足前头一半。堂里点卯的日子手数是实的，一只当差的手一天差我五六趟，这把尺是我拿实数折出来的；拿它量线上的结数，量出的手只有线头那些日子的七成。绸缎柜上报十二只，线量出来八只。东家不信，让人守着耿婆婆新起的那根线又数了三天，结数还是落在八上。名册照着看不见的手重写，我的名字留下；虚报人手的绸缎柜，裁掉的是柜头。

For the cut, the master did not use the list Tong had drawn up. He asked me instead: how many hands were actually at work in the places he could not see — behind the silk counter, in the inner counting room — and whether the numbers reported up could be trusted. I remembered the knotted thread under the odds and ends, fetched it back, and counted the knots out to him, one by one. The front half of that thread was dense, and I remembered how many knots a day it held; it thinned as it went on, until the last month carried less than half of the beginning. On roll-call days the count of hands was solid fact, and from those days I had worked out my yardstick: one working hand sent me five or six times a day. Measured with it, the thread gave no more than seven hands in every ten of the early season. The silk counter reported twelve; the thread measured eight. The master would not take this on faith. He had watchers stand over the fresh thread Granny Geng had started and count for three days; the knots still settled on eight. The staff list was rewritten to the number of the unseen hands. My name stayed on it; where the rolls had been padded, it was the counter's head who was let go.

那根挽满结的线，东家收在身边，当堂里忙闲的凭据。耿婆婆照旧见一趟穿堂挽一个结；问她为什么，她说不上来，只说手上有点事做，花边织得匀。至于我，到今天也没人说得出我下一刻站在哪块砖上；东家却每天傍晚听一回当日的结数，就知道这一天堂里握着多少只手。大门敞开进货的时候，满堂连人带柜台一齐朝一边倾，那是整座堂自己在喘气，与我无关；我不过在过道里侧一侧肩，照旧走我说不准、旁人也说不准的那条道。腊月封海，门板下得早，堂里静下来，我的凳子头一回被坐得发热；耿婆婆手里那根新线，光溜溜躺了一个月，一个结也没有。

The thread full of knots the master kept by him, as the house's gauge of busy and slack. Granny Geng went on tying one knot for every crossing; asked why, she could not say, only that with something in her hands the lace came out even. As for me, no one to this day can say which tile I will be standing on next; yet each evening the master listened to the day's count of knots and knew how many hands the hall had held that day. When the great doors swung wide for a cargo haul and the whole floor leaned one way, people and counters together, that was the hall breathing, not me; I only turned a shoulder in the aisle and kept to a road neither I nor anyone else could call out in advance. In the twelfth month the sea closed to shipping, the boards went up early, the hall fell still, and my stool grew warm under me for the first time. The fresh thread in Granny Geng's hands lay smooth for a month, without a single knot.

注解：把满堂看不见的手换成流体里数不清的热分子，把写字的丫头换成一粒悬浮的微粒，就是布朗运动（Brownian motion）——微粒受周围分子热运动的不断撞击，走出无规则、永不停止的碎步，下一步算不出，能算的只有趟数与快慢；爱因斯坦（Einstein）1905年给出机制，佩兰（Perrin）1908年以实验对上数目，并由此推算出看不见的分子的多少。敞开大门时满堂一齐歪过去，那是宏观的对流在卷，同微粒自己的碎步是两码事。

Note: Swap the hall's countless unseen hands for the countless warm molecules of a fluid, and the copy girl for a speck suspended in it, and you have Brownian motion — a particle nudged without pause by the molecules around it into an irregular, never-ceasing dance whose next step cannot be worked out, though its pace and count can; Einstein gave the mechanism in 1905, Perrin's experiments matched the numbers in 1908, and from such counts the abundance of the unseen molecules was reckoned. The wide-open door that leans a whole hall over at once is convection, a different thing from the speck's own small dance.
