没人说过的那匹马

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年11月14日·4分钟阅读

秋集又开了。我把黑马牵进场口，背上驮着两捆山货；十年来它驮什么都是这一个走法，不快不慢。屠家的摊子在最里头，我打摊前过，他把背转了过去，十年了，年年是这个转法。集上说起我，有两种话：一说我眼毒，十年前一搭眼牵回一匹十秋不倒的马；一说我没信义，握手握热了又改口。两种话我都认，不辩。散集前，一个后生拽住我的袖子，问该牵哪一匹。我朝场边那张小桌努了努嘴，桌后还坐着宋老先生：「去给他添一碟酒，要问，问他旁的两匹；你手里那匹，他一个字也不会给你。」

The autumn fair opened again. I led my black horse in through the gate with two bundles of mountain goods on its back; for ten years it has carried everything in the same gait, neither fast nor slow. The Tu family's stall sits at the far end, and when I passed it the old man turned his back, ten years running. When the fair talks about me there are two versions: one says my eye is sharp, that ten years ago I picked out in a single glance a horse that has stood sound through ten autumns; the other says I have no honor, that I warmed a man's hand with a handshake and then took my word back. I answer to both and argue with neither. Before the fair broke up a young buyer caught my sleeve and asked which horse he should take. I tipped my chin at the small table at the edge of the ground, where old Master Song still sat behind it: "Go pour him a dish of wine, and if you ask, ask about the other two; the one in your hand, he will not give you one word on."

十年前那个秋天，婆娘咳了一整月，山那边府城的医生开了方，说药要赶在头场雪前取回来；来回四天的路，雪一封山，就得等开春。我在砖窑背了三年坯，工钱全在怀里焐着。那年马市上，挑得出三匹能走山路的：枣红的高大，毛亮得照得见人影，屠先给我斟了茶；青灰的一路昂着脖子；黑马夹在当中，不高不矮，过路的人眼皮都不为它多抬一下。三匹都亮，亮得一样。我顺着毛色和那盏茶，把手伸给了屠。市上有句老话：银钱没过手，话过了手，事就算成了。

Ten years ago that autumn my wife had coughed through a whole month. The physician in the prefecture town beyond the mountains wrote out a prescription and said the medicine had to come back before the first snow; the road was four days there and back, and once snow shut the pass it stayed shut till spring. Three years of carrying brick at the kiln were knotted in cash inside my shirt. That morning the fair held three animals that could take the mountain road: a tall bay whose coat shone enough to show your reflection, and Tu poured me tea before I had said a word; a gray that carried its neck high the whole time; and a black wedged between them, neither tall nor short, that passersby did not lift an eyelid for. All three shone, and shone the same. I followed the coat and the tea and put my hand into Tu's. The fair has an old saying: the silver has not crossed, but the word has, and the matter is done.

宋老先生的桌子在场边，一碟酒换一个问题，这规矩他行了三十年。场上每一匹马都过过他的手，哪匹的蹄补过，哪匹的气嗓早破，他心里都有数。规矩的下半截是：他只说买主没握过手的马；买主定了的那匹，他绝不开口。他的酒钱是几位会首凑的——马市靠的是回头客，买马的人在别处折了本钱，来年就不来了；可握过手的交，会首也不替人翻，各人的话各人担。我添了酒，问旁的两匹。他掀了掀眼皮：「青灰那匹，气嗓是破的，上三十里坡你就听见了。」说完便合了嘴。旁边贩骡子的笑出了声：「剩下两匹，对半的运气，犯得上为一句闲话得罪屠？」我答不上来。那一夜我把这句话拆了装，装了又拆。

Master Song's table stands at the edge of the fair; one dish of wine buys one question, a rule he has kept for thirty years. Every horse on the ground has passed through his hands; which hoof was mended, which wind was broken, he knows it all without notes. The rule has a second half: he speaks only of horses no buyer has shaken on; for the horse a buyer has settled, he never opens his mouth. His wine is paid for by the fair's headmen — a fair lives on buyers who come back, and a man ruined by a hidden flaw does not return; but a deal already shaken on, the headmen will not turn over for anyone; each man carries his own word. I set out the wine and asked about the other two. He lifted his eyelids: "The gray's wind is broken; take it up thirty li of slope and you will hear it." Then he closed his mouth. A mule dealer standing near laughed out loud: "Two horses left, an even chance either way, and worth offending Tu over one stray sentence?" I had no answer. That night I took the sentence apart and put it back together, over and over.

后半夜我睡不着，摸到客栈后院，把三匹马在心里过了一遍。青灰划掉了。枣红还在我手里；从清早到此刻，它没添一根好毛，也没掉一根坏毛，宋老先生的嘴对它闭着，它就还是清早那匹不知深浅的马。旁两匹却不一样了。三匹里头，好马一匹，坏马两匹。我挑枣红，凭的是毛色和一盏茶；毛是喂出来的，茶是生意上的礼数，两样都做不得凭据，那一伸手，跟闭着眼睛差不多。好马若落在旁两匹里——两头对一头——宋老先生的话就骂不中它；他骂了青灰，黑马便把旁两匹的指望一并接了过去。枣红若真是好的，他骂哪匹都使得，我改口就是自坑。我担保不了黑马，谁也担保不了；我只数得清，指望宽的那一边不在屠的摊上。

Past midnight I gave up on sleep, went to the yard behind the inn, and walked the three horses through my head. The gray was crossed off. The bay was still in my hand; from daybreak to this hour it had not grown one good hair or shed one bad one, and Master Song's mouth stayed shut about it, so it remained exactly the horse of unknown depths it had been at daybreak. The other two were no longer what they had been. Of the three, one horse was sound and two were rotten; I had chosen the bay on a coat and a cup of tea, and a coat can be fed into any horse and tea is a dealer's manners, so neither was evidence — that first reach of my hand was close to reaching with my eyes shut. If the sound one stood among the other two — two heads against my one — the old man's sentence could not have fallen on it; he damned the gray, so the black took over everything the other two had ever promised. If the bay was the sound one, he could have damned either of them, and my switching would be my own ruin. I could not promise the black was sound; nobody could. I could only count where the wider promise lay, and it did not lie in Tu's stall.

天一亮我去了黑马主人的摊上。是个外乡老倌，要价同屠家一样，我怀里三年的窑钱一枚不剩，全数过了手。牵马回来，打屠的摊前走，他正给旁人斟茶，见了我们，茶壶放下，人转身进了里头。集上那一整天的话我都听见了：改口的人靠不住。这话不冤，我认。第三天动身，药箱捆在鞍上，雪是追着我们进山的；封山那日天擦黑，我们已经站在山的这一边。婆娘的药，赶上了。

At daylight I went to the black horse's owner, an old man from out of town who asked the same price as Tu. Three years of kiln money crossed his table, every coin of it. Leading the black back I passed Tu's stall; he was pouring tea for someone else, saw us, set the kettle down, and walked inside. I heard the fair's talk all day long: a man who takes back his word cannot be trusted. The words were not unjust, and I own them. On the third day we set out with the medicine chest strapped to the saddle, and the snow chased us into the mountains; on the day the pass closed we were already standing on this side of it, in the last gray light. My wife's medicine was in time.

开春，婆娘下了炕，能自己生火熬药了。青灰那年冬天卖给了个拉炭的，没添酒钱，也没多问一句；开春我在炭道上遇见它，满车的炭，走十步喘一口。枣红没出这个市，屠给它重新钉了掌，贩到下河去，后来那边来的脚夫告诉我：年前就瘸了，左前蹄，蹄壳里一道旧裂，漆盖着。我在它跟前站过半个时辰，愣是没看出来。黑马这些年驮盐驮柴，没瘸过一回。

In spring my wife got off the kang and could build her own fire and brew her own medicine. The gray was sold that winter to a coal hauler who poured no wine and asked no questions; I met it on the coal road in spring, loaded full, breathing once every ten steps. The bay never left that market. Tu had it shod afresh and sent it downriver, and a porter coming back from there told me it had gone lame before New Year: the near fore, an old crack in the hoof wall, hidden under paint. I had stood beside it for half an hour and never seen it. The black has carried salt and firewood these ten years and come up lame not once.

后生从宋老先生桌那儿回来，在屠家摊前站了一小会儿。屠家的孙子给他斟了茶。他没接，绕过自己握过手的那匹，把里头那匹牵出来，当面数了银子。屠家上下没人抬眼看他。场口上他追上我，问：「老丈，你当年那匹，是怎么牵的？」我朝他手里的缰绳努了努嘴：「跟我这匹一样，是没人说过的那匹。」

The young buyer came back from Master Song's table and stood a little while in front of the Tu stall. Tu's grandson poured him tea. He did not take it; he walked past the horse he had shaken on, led out the one standing behind it, and counted out his silver to its owner's face. No one in the Tu stall raised an eye to him. At the gate he caught up with me and asked: "Old uncle, that horse of yours back then — how did you come by it?" I tipped my chin at the rope in his fists: "Same as this one — the horse nobody had spoken of."

注解：这就是蒙提霍尔问题（the Monty Hall problem）：三样东西里藏着一样好的，你先闭眼认下一样；知情的主持人必须从你没认的两样里划掉一样坏的，还要让你重选——你手里那样照旧是三分之一，那三分之二全落在没被划掉的那样上，换过去更划算。这笔账成立的前提，是主持人真的知情、也真被规矩绑住；他若是瞎划，或另存自己的心思，账就不是这么算的了。

Note: This is the Monty Hall problem: among three options one hides the prize; you commit blindly to one, and a host who knows the truth must strike out one losing option from the two you did not take and let you choose again — your own pick keeps its one chance in three, and the two chances in three settle entirely on the one option left untouched, so switching is the better bet. The edge holds only while that host truly knows and is truly bound; if he strikes at random, or with a scheme of his own, the arithmetic is no longer the same.
