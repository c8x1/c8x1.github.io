历史上的平等观念，助我们想象一个平等的世界

Teresa M Bejan·Aeon·2026年11月10日·15分钟阅读·原文

平等在美国《独立宣言》（Declaration of Independence）所宣示的各项「不言自明」的真理中位列第一，这绝非偶然。「人人生而平等」这一主张在那里不仅仅是一个可以从中推出革命性后果的抽象前提，更是一个具体的实践基础：美洲殖民地居民正是站在这个基础之上，才得以宣称自己与最伟大的国王平起平坐，并要求他承担责任。五十年后，托马斯·杰斐逊（Thomas Jefferson）对自己的这件手笔依然颇为得意，甚至预言说，载入《宣言》的那种平等的「明白无疑的真理」，终有一天会把全人类团结起来。

It’s no accident that equality comes first among the ‘self-evident’ truths articulated in the United States Declaration of Independence. The claim that ‘all men are created equal’ serves there not simply as an abstract premise from which revolutionary consequences ought to follow, but as a concrete practical foundation on which the American colonists could claim to stand as equals with the greatest king and hold him to account. Still impressed by his own handiwork 50 years later, Thomas Jefferson even prophesied that the ‘palpable truth’ of equality enshrined in the Declaration would one day unite all of humankind.

然而在今天，显而易见的是，「平等」一词对不同的人意味着截然不同的东西。曾经似乎能够提供共同立足点的东西，如今却成了争议的又一个来源。如何解释这种转变？我怀疑，正是「平等」这种貌似简单的概念语言本身扮演了重要角色。因此，更好地理解「平等话语」——即把这种语言应用于人类及其相互关系——或许能为我们当下的困境投下一线光亮。

Yet today, it’s evident that the word ‘equality’ means very different things to different people. What once seemed capable of providing common ground is now just another source of controversy. What explains this shift? I suspect that the deceptively simple, conceptual language of ‘equality’ has itself played a substantial role. A better understanding of ‘equality talk’ – as the application of this language to human beings and their relations – might therefore shed some light on our predicament.

让我们先考察一群通常以比大多数人思考（和言说）得更清楚而自豪的人：职业哲学家。他们在谈论平等的时候，究竟在做什么？

L et’s begin by examining a group of people that generally prides itself on thinking (and speaking) more clearly than most: professional philosophers. What are they doing when they talk about equality?

他们往往是在谈论人：谈论我们从根本上看是什么样子，以及我们在道德、政治和社会上应当如何被对待。当一位学院派哲学家说「所有人都是平等的」时，她通常是在说：尽管存在种族与性别、经济阶层或个人功绩上的差异，每个人在某些根本方面仍然是相同的。一些哲学家强调人类的理性能力与道德能动性，另一些则强调我们共同面临苦难与暴力死亡的脆弱性。尽管如此，他们普遍同意：这种相同性——无论其基础为何——应当对我们的社会和政治安排产生影响，以至于人与人之间任何持续存在的不平等，都必须给出某种正当理由，或予以纠正。

Often, they’re saying something about human beings: about what we’re fundamentally like, and how we should be treated morally, politically and socially. When an academic philosopher says that ‘all people are equal’, she’s generally saying that, despite differences of race and gender, economic class or individual merit, every person is somehow the same in some fundamental respect(s). While some philosophers will stress the human capacity for reason and moral agency, others emphasise our mutual vulnerability to suffering and violent death. Still, all generally agree that this sameness – whatever its underlying basis – ought to matter for our social and political arrangements, such that any persistent inequalities between human beings must require some justification or redress.

但在这里，非哲学家可能会感到疑惑：专业人士为什么坚持使用「平等」这种技术性语言，而不是「相同」这类更直白的说法？我认为，一个原因在于他们借重了前一个概念在数学上的共鸣——定量的精确与超越性的真理。这些共鸣反过来又使不平等问题显得相对容易处理——仿佛其存在可以被测量，因而可以被公认。其他政治价值（如自由）抗拒被还原为某种共同的度量，而平等的量化逻辑恰恰许诺了一个客观标准，社会进步可以据此加以评判。

But here, a non-philosopher might wonder why the professionals insist on using the technical language of ‘equality’ as opposed to something more straightforward like ‘sameness’? One reason, I think, is that they are trading on the former concept’s mathematical resonances of quantitative precision and transcendent truth. Those resonances, in turn, make the problem of inequality seem relatively tractable – as something the existence of which can be measured, hence agreed upon. While other political values, like liberty, resist reduction to a common measure, the quantitative logic of equality thus promises an objective standard against which social progress can be judged.

现代「平等话语」中这种鲜明的数学色彩并非新事物。想想《宣言》中「不言自明」的说法。它暗指欧几里得（Euclid）的《几何原本》（Elements，约公元前300年），在其中，平等的传递性——「等于同物之物彼此亦相等」——位列所谓公理（axiomata，即「有价值的观念」）之首。这些几何命题的真理可以被直接接受而无需证明，从而成为推理的共享起点，由此再推演出不那么显然的结论。

The distinctly mathematical aspect of modern ‘equality talk’ isn’t new. Consider the Declaration’s language of ‘self-evidence’. This was an allusion to Euclid’s Elements ( c 300 BCE), in which the transitive property of equality – ‘things that are equal to the same thing are also equal to one another’ – comes first among the so-called axiomata , or ‘worthy notions’. The truth of these geometric propositions could, in turn, be accepted without proof, and so serve as a shared starting point from which to reason to less-obvious conclusions.

《宣言》问世两百年后，我们看到哲学家罗纳德·德沃金（Ronald Dworkin）在《认真对待权利》（Taking Rights Seriously，1977）一书中，把所有人对平等的权利描述为「公理性的」，从而把人的基本平等从政治争议的议题中拿掉了。于是，就让形而上学家和神学家去争论其基础何在，或者争论非人类动物或人工智能是否也应被算作平等的道德主体吧。政治哲学家则可以着手处理重要得多的工作：确定这种平等对我们的社会、政治和经济关系意味着什么。与杰斐逊相似，德沃金后来也赋予他的公理以世界历史性的意义，称其为「政治论证中的一块高原」，是现代性的典型特征。他的意思是，当代大多数政治意识形态——从自由主义到功利主义，从自由至上主义到共产主义——都必须从一个共同的前提出发，即共同体的所有成员「都重要，而且同等重要」。因此，无论分歧多大，政治对手都应当认识到，自己是在一块共同的地基之上进行建构。

Two hundred years after the Declaration, we likewise find the philosopher Ronald Dworkin in Taking Rights Seriously (1977) describing the right of all persons to equality as ‘axiomatic’, and thereby taking the basic equality of human beings off the table as a topic of political controversy. Let metaphysicians and theologians quarrel about its basis, then, or whether nonhuman animals or artificial intelligence ought to count equally as moral persons. Political philosophers could get on with the altogether more important business of determining what this equality implied for our social, political and economic relations. Much like Jefferson, Dworkin would also later lend his axiom world-historical significance, as providing ‘a kind of plateau in political argument’ characteristic of modernity. By this, Dworkin meant that most contemporary political ideologies – from liberalism to utilitarianism, and libertarianism to communism – must begin from a shared premise, that all ‘members of the community matter, and matter equally’. And so, whatever their differences, political opponents ought to recognise themselves as building upon a common foundation.

今天，德沃金那种把平等视为普遍前提的历史乐观主义似乎更难维持了。尽管如此，他的「平等主义高原」形象在学院哲学家中依然流行，对他们来说，基本平等已成为道德思想中一道不可磨灭的分界线，区分了「（1）贵族被认为比农民……更有价值的前现代世界」与「（2）拒斥这些粗鄙偏见的现代世界」。此外，如果说同类案件在逻辑上应被同样对待，那么「平等者」在政治上当然也理应被「平等地」对待——例如，向每个人分配等量的福祉、权利或资源。德沃金在牛津的同事、分析马克思主义者G·A·科恩（G A Cohen）因此可以径直「想当然地认为，正义要求人们在某个方面拥有等量的东西」。剩下的问题便是阿马蒂亚·森（Amartya Sen）提出的那个：「什么的平等？」（Equality of what）——着重号系原文所加。

Today, Dworkin’s historical optimism about equality as a universal premise seems harder to sustain. Nevertheless, his ‘egalitarian plateau’ image remains popular among academic philosophers, for whom basic equality has come to mark an indelible ‘divide in moral thinking between (1) a premodern world in which nobles [were] regarded as having greater worth than peasants … and (2) a modern world that repudiates these crude prejudices.’ Moreover, if like cases are to be treated alike as a matter of logic, then surely ‘equals’ ought to be treated ‘equally’ as a matter of politics, too – for instance, by distributing to each the same amount of welfare, rights or resources. Dworkin’s Oxford colleague, the analytic Marxist G A Cohen, could thus simply ‘take for granted that there is something which justice requires people to have equal amounts of.’ The only question remaining was that posed by Amartya Sen : ‘Equality of what ?’ (My emphasis.)

然而，有时恰恰需要不平等的对待才能尊重一个人的基本平等——这一观念对许多20世纪晚期的自由主义者来说同样是直觉上可信的，他们支持平权行动之类的政策。德沃金本人对这一直觉给出了极具影响力的表述，即区分「平等地」对待人——对所有人适用同样的标准或分配同样的数量——与「当作平等者」对待人，即以一种对其差异作出回应的方式对待他们。这一洞见乍听之下或许与古老得多的马克思主义原则相似：「各尽所能，按需分配。」但正如历史学家达林·麦克马洪（Darrin McMahon）在《平等》（Equality，2023）一书中指出的，马克思和恩格斯本人对平等一贯持批判态度，将其斥为又一种「流行的偏见」，认为它终将被到来的共产主义革命所涤除。

And yet, the idea that unequal treatment might sometimes be required to respect a person’s basic equality was also intuitive to many late-20th-century liberals, who supported policies like affirmative action. Dworkin himself framed this intuition influentially in terms of a distinction between treating people ‘equally’ – by applying the same standards or distributing the same amount to everyone – and treating them ‘as equals’, in a way responsive to their differences. This insight may, at first, sound similar to the much older, Marxist principle, ‘From each according to his ability, to each according to his needs.’ But as the historian Darrin McMahon observed in Equality (2023), Marx and Engels were themselves consistently critical of equality, dismissing it as a just another ‘popular prejudice’ to be dispelled by the coming communist revolution.

无论如何，此后成为当代社会平等主义或「关系平等主义」理论之核心的，是德沃金的区分而非马克思的原则，尤其是在与哲学家伊丽莎白·安德森（Elizabeth Anderson）相关联的那类理论中。在这种观点看来，平等的要义不在于确保权利或资源在数量上完全相同的分配，而在于实现一个正义的社会，其中每个人都能作为平等者立足，不存在任何劣势或优势的等级关系。但问题一如既往出在细节上：关系平等主义者在正面意义上对「作为平等者相处」究竟是什么样子——具体来说，何时以及何种形式的不平等对待可能参与其中——所说的话出奇地少。

In any case, it is Dworkin’s distinction, not Marx’s, that has since become central to contemporary theories of social or ‘relational’ egalitarianism, especially those associated with the philosopher Elizabeth Anderson. On this view, the point of equality is not to ensure a quantitatively identical distribution of stuff , whether of rights or resources, but rather the realisation of a just society in which every person can stand as an equal , without any hierarchical relations of inferiority or superiority. But the devil, as ever, is in the details, with relational egalitarians saying surprisingly little in positive terms about what relating as ‘equals’ actually looks like – and, specifically, when and what forms of unequal treatment might be involved.

在近来把「公平」与「平等」当作两种社会正义进路相互对置的论战中，人们也会遇到类似的困难。前者的支持者坚称，与后者不同，公平拒绝一刀切的做法，因为它把某些差异——例如种族、残障或社会经济处境——纳入考量。然而一经追问，公平的辩护者往往也以惊人的德沃金式措辞来定义「公平的」对待，例如让弱势者与特权者站上「同等的立足点」。结果便是公平的进步派支持者与其保守派批评者之间一场奇特的合流：双方都把自己偏爱的进路说成是真正平等主义的进路，一方坚持平等的地位要求不平等的对待，另一方则主张，在一个平等者的社会中，同样的规则应当对每个人一视同仁地适用。

One encounters a similar difficulty in recent debates pitting ‘equity’ against ‘equality’ as an approach to social justice. Proponents of the former insist that, unlike the latter, equity rejects a one-size-fits-all approach by taking certain differences – for instance, of race, disability or socioeconomic circumstances – into account. When pressed, however, equity’s defenders also tend to define ‘equitable’ treatment in strikingly Dworkinian terms, for instance as placing the disadvantaged on an ‘equal footing’ with the privileged. The result has been an odd convergence between equity’s progressive proponents and their conservative critics, with each presenting their preferred approach as the truly egalitarian one, with one side insisting that equal standing requires unequal treatment, and the other that, in a society of equals, the same rules ought to apply equally to everyone.

难怪平等话语会让人如此晕头转向！即便在哲学家中，它也一贯许诺多于兑现。对一些人来说，平等意味着我们在所有要紧的方面都相同，因此必须适用同样的标准；对另一些人来说，它意味着我们各不相同，而这些差异值得尊重。就连德沃金那个据称起了澄清作用的「当作平等者对待」的公式，细究起来也不过是说：当情形适合人们的具体境况时，我们应当不平等地对待他们。如果这就是平等的全部要求，那么一位世袭贵族也不会有什么可反对的。

N o wonder, then, that equality talk can feel so disorienting! Even among philosophers, it consistently promises more than it delivers. For some, equality means that we are the same in all the ways that matter, hence the same standards must apply; for others, it means that we are all different in ways deserving of respect. Even Dworkin’s supposedly clarifying formula of treating people ‘as equals’ reduces, under scrutiny, to the suggestion that we ought to treat people unequally when it is appropriate to their particular circumstances. If this is all equality demands, a hereditary aristocrat would find little to disagree with.

不过，像麦克马洪这样的历史学家也许会宽慰我们说，平等的含义一直以来就是「难以捉摸的」。例如在法语中，说 ça m'est égal 可以是「对我来说都一样」，但也可以是说我们的差异无关紧要，它们是无差别之事。英语中「平等话语」的种种变体还可能更让人困惑。两百五十多年前，塞缪尔·约翰逊（Samuel Johnson）的《英语词典》（A Dictionary of the English Language，1755）初版时，为形容词「equal」提供了不下八条释义，其中包括「漠然的」「在体量或卓越程度上与另一物相像」以及「足以胜任任何用途」。

Here, however, a historian like McMahon might reassure us that the meaning of equality has always been ‘elusive’. In French, for instance, to say ça m’est égal can mean ‘it’s all the same to me’, yes, but it can also mean that our differences don’t matter, they’re matters of indifference. The varieties of English ‘equality talk’ can be more confusing still. When it was first published more than 250 years ago, Samuel Johnson’s A Dictionary of the English Language (1755) offered no fewer than eight definitions for the adjective ‘equal’, including ‘indifferent’, ‘like another in bulk, [or] excellence’, and ‘adequate to any purpose’.

尽管如此，在这重重含义之中，我们会发现平等话语在近代早期英语中做着某种独特的事情。具体而言，形容词「equal」——最初源自拉丁语 aequalis——持续不断地为英语使用者提供一种更有学养、社会地位更高的替代词，替代的是一个更常见的盎格鲁-撒克逊词汇「alike」（与德语 gleich 同源）。然而，与我们今天主要在数学意义上理解平等不同，在近代早期英语的耳朵听来，它更接近于「均平」——即处于同一水平或保持平衡，双方既不高也不低于对方。

Still, amid this multiplicity, one finds equality talk doing something distinctive in early modern English. Specifically, the adjective ‘equal’ – deriving originally from the Latin aequalis – consistently presented English speakers with a learned and socially elevated alternative to a more common Anglo-Saxon term, ‘alike’ (cognate with the German gleich ). Whereas we today understand equality primarily in mathematical terms, however, to early modern English ears it meant something more like evenness , in the sense of being on a level or balanced , with neither side higher nor lower than the other.

且看14世纪的威克里夫圣经（Wycliffe Bible），它如此呈现圣保罗对哥林多人（Corinthians）的仁爱劝谕：「但为着今世的均平（aequalitas），你们的富余可以补足他们的缺乏，好使他们的富余也可以补足你们的缺乏，从而成就均平。」在这段经文中，富人与穷人被造成「平等」，靠的不是在任何特定时刻拥有数量完全相同的财富，而是通过互惠的给予，让他们的持有物随时间推移而相互平衡。一个人的富余必须满足另一个人的需要，并预期将来会有反转。在这里，作为均平的平等这一历史语义，清楚地让人联想到「调平」古代商人称量贵金属与货物所用天平或秤杆的过程。（事实上，英语单词「level」最初正源自拉丁语 libra，意为秤。）

Consider the 14th-century Wycliffe Bible, which presented St Paul’s counsels of charity to the Corinthians as follows: ‘but of evenness [ aequalitas ] in the present time your abundance fulfil the need of them, that also the abundance of them be a fulfilling of your need, that evenness be made.’ Rich and poor were thus to be made ‘equal’ in this passage not by possessing identical amounts of wealth at any given moment, but by counter-balancing their holdings over time through reciprocal giving. One person’s abundance must meet another’s need in expectation of a future reversal. Here, the historical sense of equality-as-evenness clearly recalls the process of ‘levelling’ the scales or balances used by ancient merchants to weigh precious metals and trade goods in the marketplace. (Indeed, the English word ‘level’ derives originally from the Latin libra , meaning scale.)

如果我们直接转向古代希腊世界，会发现平等概念与平衡活动之间同样的实践性关联。这种关联或许反过来可以解释，为什么欧几里得这样的希腊数学家要在著作中如此费力地把「平等」的观念与「相同」「相似」等其他概念关系区分开来。在《形而上学》中，亚里士多德（Aristotle）也以纯然否定的方式定义平等，从而把它区别出来：「所谓平等，就是既不大也不小、而其本性本可为大或为小的东西。」换言之，平等自有其非同寻常之处。与相同不同，它是人们在通常预期会出现差异之处的差异缺席。

If we turn directly to the ancient Hellenic world, we find the same practical association between the concept of equality and the activity of balancing. This association may explain, in turn, why Greek mathematicians like Euclid took so much trouble to distinguish the idea of ‘equality’ ( isotes ) from other conceptual relations like ‘sameness’ and ‘similarity’ in their works. In the Metaphysics , Aristotle also distinguished equality by defining it in exclusively negative terms: ‘The equal, then, is that which is neither great nor small, but would naturally be either great or small.’ In other words, there was something exceptional about equality. Unlike sameness, it was the absence of difference where one would normally expect to find it.

我认为，这一定义可以帮助我们更好地理解亚里士多德在《政治学》中的著名论断——「正义就是某种平等」——以及他的断言：当时的政治中至少有两种相互竞争的平等定义在发挥作用。在别处，他把它们分别定义为「算术的」平等与「几何的」平等：前者在各方存在财富或德性等差异的情况下仍分配数量完全相同的份额，后者则按照各人差异的正比分配不同的数量。于是在《政治学》中，这两种平等以相互竞争的策略的面目重新出现，用以在公民之间——具体说是在贫穷的多数与富裕的少数之间——平衡政治权力。民主派支持前一种原则，主张每个成年男性公民都应当被赋予严格相等的政治分量，如同等值的钱币；寡头们则坚持，公民应当像市场上的金属一样被称量，然后按照各自不等的财富或德性，把政治权力按比例分配给每个人。在古代希腊，由公民同胞选举官员被视为一种寡头程序，与之相对的、真正民主的替代方案是抽签，即以抽签选拔，它赋予每个人一个严格相等（随机）的行使权力的机会。

This definition can, I think, help us make better sense of Aristotle’s celebrated observation in the Politics , that ‘justice is a kind of equality’, as well as his assertion that there were at least two competing definitions of equality in play in contemporary politics. Elsewhere, he would define these as ‘arithmetic’ and ‘geometric’ equality, respectively: the former distributing numerically identical shares to the parties despite their differences, for instance of wealth or virtue, and the latter assigning different amounts to each in direct proportion with their differences. In the Politics , then, these two kinds of equality re-appear as competing strategies for balancing political power between citizens, specifically between the poor many and the rich few. While democrats favoured the former principle – claiming that every adult male citizen should be assigned strictly equal political weight, like coins of equal value – oligarchs insisted that citizens should be weighed instead, like metals in the marketplace, with political power then distributed to each in proportion with his unequal wealth or virtue. In ancient Greece, the election of political officeholders by their fellow citizens was thus regarded as an oligarchic procedure, in contrast with the properly democratic alternative of sortition, or selection by lot, which afforded each a strictly equal (random) chance of exercising power.

不过请注意，尽管存在分歧，古代的民主派与寡头们都同样可以拥抱「平等」这一政治理想，而无需同时认定每个公民——更遑论每个人——也必须在某种意义上是「平等的」。即便在古代世界经济体上最具包容性、参与度最高的城邦，即民主的雅典，雅典的民主派也把某些人——即本地出生的成年男性公民——看得比其他人更尊贵、更有分量，无论后者是女人、儿童、奴隶或「蛮族人」，还是其他希腊人，包括像亚里士多德这样的外邦定居者。因此，即便在雅典，平等的要义也在于确保任何不平等的部分或个人，都无法通过压倒或支配其余部分而使城邦失衡。

Notice, however, that, despite their differences, ancient democrats and oligarchs could alike embrace ‘equality’ as a political ideal without also committing to the view that every citizen, let alone every human being, must be somehow ‘equal’ as well. Even in the most economically inclusive and participatory polity in the ancient world, namely democratic Athens, Athenian democrats regarded certain people – namely native-born adult male citizens – as worthier and weightier than other ones – be they women, children, slaves or ‘barbarians’, or even other Greeks, including resident aliens like Aristotle. The point of equality then, even in Athens, was to ensure that no unequal part or person could over-balance the polity by outweighing or dominating the rest.

到目前为止，我的历史考察似乎印证了德沃金的「平等主义高原」观念。古代希腊人固然拥有精深的政治平等观念，但他们同样明确相信人在基本上是不平等的。那么，现代平等主义想必标志着与这段无可救药的等级制历史的彻底决裂吧？

S o far, my historical survey would appear to vindicate Dworkin’s ‘egalitarian plateau’ idea. While the ancient Greeks undoubtedly possessed sophisticated ideas about political equality, they also clearly believed in the basic inequality of human beings. Surely, then, modern egalitarianism must mark a radical break with this irredeemably hierarchical past?

且慢。因为在古代世界的其他地方，人们会发现与德沃金的基本人类平等「公理」惊人相似的表述——就在罗马帝国。且看以下摘自3世纪初的帝国法学家乌尔比安（Ulpian）、保存于查士丁尼（Justinian）《学说汇纂》（Digest）中的文字：「就市民法而言，奴隶被视为无物；但就自然法而言，人人皆平等（aequales）。」

Not so fast. Because elsewhere in the ancient world one finds statements remarkably similar to Dworkin’s ‘axiom’ of basic human equality – namely, in the Roman Empire. Consider the following extract from Ulpian, an imperial jurist of the early 3rd century CE, preserved in Justinian’s Digest : ‘As far as concerns the civil law, slaves are regarded as nothing; but as far as concerns the natural law, all humans are equal ( aequales ).’

在这里，我们发现了许多今天被哲学家视为典型现代主张的东西：所有人（乃至奴隶）在自然意义上平等。但结合语境，乌尔比安显然另有所指。他并不是说所有人都应当在法律或政治上被当作平等者对待，更不是说必须给予他们相同的权利或资源。乌尔比安的意思毋宁是：即便是被奴役的人，也同样受制于他所谓的「自然」法，而这种法毫无例外地适用于每一个人。

Here, we find what many philosophers today regard as a characteristically modern claim: that all humans (even slaves) are naturally equal. But, in context, Ulpian clearly meant something different. He was not saying that all people ought to be treated as equals, legally or politically, let alone that they must be afforded the same rights or resources. Ulpian’s point, rather, was that even enslaved people were equally subject to something he called ‘natural’ law, which applied to every human being without exception.

因此，对乌尔比安来说，人的「平等」看来更接近于「无差别」。的确，正如一位现代法语使用者宣称 ça m'est égal 一样，自然法对罗马帝国内部主导生活的权力、财富或身份上的巨大差异视若无睹。换言之，它就像太阳、雨水或死亡一样，无差别地落在每个人身上，无论男女、自由人或奴隶。但这种「自然」视角并未因此否认或抹除罗马生活（或法律）中特有的种种等级。它只是识别出一种自然正义的（必然有限的）视角，从这一视角看去，那些等级可以被当作无关紧要之物。

For Ulpian, then, it seems that the ‘equality’ of human beings meant something more like indifference . Indeed, much like a modern French speaker declaring ça m’est égal , the law of nature took no notice of the extreme differences of power, wealth or status otherwise governing life within the Roman Empire. In other words, and much like the sun or rain or death, it fell indifferently on everyone, male or female, free or slave. But this ‘natural’ did not therefore deny or otherwise erase the many hierarchies characteristic of Roman life (or law). It simply identified a (necessarily limited) perspective of natural justice from which those hierarchies could be regarded as irrelevant.

这一关于自然平等观念之罗马起源的观察，对另一个更为人熟知的现代平等主义来源故事提出了挑战。在许多现代读者看来，《独立宣言》中「受造」的圣经语言暗示，人的平等原本是一个宗教观念，或属于犹太教，或属于基督教。然而，若查考《希伯来圣经》，平等的概念语言明显缺席。在《创世记》中，圣经中的上帝照着自己的形象造男造女。但这种神性的相似，并不能排除圣经其他地方记载的种种为神所认可或允许的等级形式，包括奴隶制与父权制。

This observation of the Roman origins of the idea of natural equality challenges another, more familiar story about the sources of modern egalitarianism. To many modern readers, the biblical language of ‘creation’ found in the Declaration of Independence suggests that the equality of human beings was originally a religious idea, whether a Jewish or a Christian one. Yet if one consults the Hebrew Bible, the conceptual language of equality is conspicuously absent. In Genesis, the biblical God creates human beings, male and female, ‘in his own image’. But this divine likeness, in turn, does nothing to preclude the many forms of divinely sanctioned or permitted hierarchy, including slavery and patriarchy, found elsewhere in the Bible.

直到后来，即公元3、4世纪，我们才看到圣西普里安（St Cyprian）、拉克坦提乌斯（Lactantius）等罗马基督教护教士为抵抗迫害，刻意地把圣经中人类按「上帝的形象或样式」受造的记述，与罗马法律中作为普遍法律正义标准的「平等」语言结合在一起。其结果，是在拉丁基督教世界出现了一种全新的、影响深远的方式，用以谈论神圣正义。与罗马的市民法不同，基督教的上帝已被揭示为真正「不以貌取人」。因此在他眼中，奴役与自由、贫与富、男与女这些尘世区隔，终有一天将一文不值。所有人都将「平等地」——即依照同一个神圣标准——接受审判，然后按照各自的灵性功过，成比例地受到奖赏或惩罚。

It is only later, in the 3rd and 4th centuries CE, that we find Roman Christian apologists like St Cyprian and Lactantius resisting persecution by deliberately combining the biblical account of human creation in ‘God’s image or likeness’, on the one hand, with the Roman legal language of ‘equality’ as a universal standard of legal justice, on the other. The result was an entirely new – and highly influential – way of talking about divine justice in Latin Christendom. Unlike the Roman civil law, the Christian God had been revealed to be truly ‘no respecter of persons’. Thus in his eyes, the worldly distinctions of slave and free, rich and poor, male and female, would one day count for nothing. All would instead be judged ‘equally’ – that is, by the same divine standard – and then rewarded or punished in proportion with their spiritual merit.

我们现在可以看出，为什么在其历史的大部分时间里，「平等话语」不仅与等级制相安无事，而且从根本上支撑着它。这并不是因为希腊民主派、罗马法学家和基督教神学家对一个自己并不信奉的理想口惠而实不至，也不是因为他们无力把自己信念中的平等主义逻辑贯彻到底。而是因为，过去的人远不像我们这样容易从「所有（或某些）人是平等的」这一前提，滑向「因此应当在社会的或评价的意义上把他们当作平等者对待」这一结论。在上帝眼中、在法律眼中、在民主的抽签中「平等」，只是意味着我们的某些差异在那一特定目的下不应计较。由此并不能推出：那些差异必须在一切目的下都无关紧要，也不能推出所有人因此在德性或价值上相似到足以在社会或政治上被当作同侪对待。事实上，强调前一种平等，往往恰恰是把某些个人或社会群体之间显见的悬殊反衬得更加分明，从而将他们安放进等级关系之中。

W e can now see why, for most of its history, ‘equality talk’ proved not only consistent with hierarchy, but fundamentally supportive of it. It’s not because Greek democrats, Roman jurists and Christian theologians were paying lip service to an ideal they didn’t believe in, or that they were incapable of following through on the egalitarian logic of their own commitments. It’s because people in the past moved much less easily than we do from the premise that all (or some) people are equal towards the conclusion that they should, therefore, be treated as equals in a social or evaluative sense. To be ‘equal’ in the eyes of God, or of the law, or of a democratic lottery, simply meant that certain of our differences shouldn’t matter for that particular purpose. It did not follow that those differences must therefore be deemed irrelevant for every purpose, or that all humans were thus sufficiently alike in virtue or value to be treated socially or politically as peers. Indeed, emphasising the former equality was often a way of bringing the manifest disparities between certain individuals or social groups into sharper relief, thus fitting them for hierarchical relations.

然而，正是历史上平等含义的这种繁衍增生——作为均平与缺席、平衡与比例、无差别与对等——一方面解释了上述断裂，另一方面又能解释「平等话语」如何在近代早期的英格兰终于获得一种全新而异常强大的政治力量。而伴随着这种力量，英格兰的普通男女开始利用它的种种歧义，不仅要求平等的正义，更要求与特权者平等的地位。

And yet, the same proliferation of historical meanings – as evenness and absence, balance and proportion, indifference and parity – that accounts for this disconnect, can in turn explain how ‘equality talk’ finally gained a new and uniquely powerful political force in early modern England. And, with that force, ordinary Englishmen and women began to exploit its many ambiguities in order to demand not only equal justice, but equal standing with the privileged.

要定位这场转变的确切时刻，我们必须把目光越过1776年之夏，回到1646年之夏。当时，一个名叫约翰·利尔伯恩的人因诽谤罪受审于上议院。今天，利尔伯恩主要作为英国内战期间活跃的平等派运动的领袖被人铭记。而平等派之所以被铭记，是因为他们在1647年的普特尼辩论（Putney Debates）中要求实行成年男子普选权，比这成为现代英国的现实早了几个世纪。然而，此前一年六月被传唤到贵族们面前时，利尔伯恩做了一件相对不起眼的小事。他只是拒绝在他的贵族法官面前下跪、摘帽。他的理由？上帝创造「世上曾经呼吸过的每一个具体的男人和女人……在权力、尊严、权威与威严上一律平等、彼此无异」。因此，未经他人「共同同意或允准」，任何人都不得以「权威、统治或长官之权」凌驾于他人之上。

To locate the precise moment of transformation, we must look beyond the summer of 1776, back to the summer of 1646, when a man called John Lilburne found himself on trial for libel before the House of Lords. Today, Lilburne is remembered chiefly as a leader of the Leveller movement active during the English Civil War. And the Levellers are remembered for demanding universal manhood suffrage at the Putney Debates of 1647, several centuries before it became a reality in modern Britain. Summoned before the Peers the previous June, however, Lilburne had done something comparatively trivial. He simply refused to kneel or to remove his hat before his aristocratic judges. His reason? That God had created ‘every particular and individual man and woman that ever breathed in the world … all equal and alike in power, dignity, authority and majesty.’ Hence no one could claim any ‘authority, dominion or magisterial power’ over another without their ‘mutual agreement or consent’.

还谈什么平衡！还谈什么无差别！在那一刻，利尔伯恩竟设法把人人在上帝面前的平等，变成了这样一个要求：像他这样的平民，也应当与那些自诩高人一等者在尘世间平起平坐、互为同侪。以这种方式，利尔伯恩的命题提供了一个更早——而且确实更激进——的版本，其「拉平」逻辑后来出现在美国《独立宣言》中。首先，它明确把女性也包括在被造而平等者之列，认为她们对自身如何被治理享有同等的发言权。（在美国，这一补充要再等七十多年，直到1848年的塞尼卡福尔斯宣言（Seneca Falls Declaration）。）

So much for balance! So much for indifference! In that moment, Lilburne somehow managed to turn the equality of human beings before God into a demand that even commoners like himself could stand on the same level, as peers, with their supposed betters here on earth. In this way, Lilburne’s proposition offered an early – and indeed more radical – version of the levelling logic found later in the US Declaration of Independence. For starters, it explicitly included women among those created equal and entitled to an equal voice in how they were to be governed. (In the US, this addendum would have to wait more than 70 years, until the Seneca Falls Declaration of 1848.)

利尔伯恩是如何做到的？首先，他抹平了一个更早的英语平等话语通常加以保留的概念区分：一方面是「是平等的」——意指服从于或被同一标准评判——另一方面是「是一个平等者」，即在社会与评价意义上作为某人的同侪。事后看来，我们可以看到，利尔伯恩的这一努力还得益于近代早期英语中一种更广泛的概念松动：许多承自希腊语和拉丁语的古典区分已开始消融。例如，在1598年出版的最早的亚里士多德《政治学》英译本中，译者径直抹掉了「平等的」（isos）与「相似的」（homoios）这两个哲学术语之间的区分，把两个词都译作「equal」。

How did Lilburne do it? Well, he started by collapsing a conceptual distinction that earlier English equality talk had generally preserved, that between being ‘equal’ – in the sense being subject to or judged by the same standard – and being ‘an equal’ in the social and evaluative sense of being someone’s peer. In hindsight, we can see that Lilburne was assisted in this effort by a broader conceptual looseness in early modern English, by which many of the classical distinctions inherited from Greek and Latin had started to dissolve. For instance, in the earliest English translation of Aristotle’s Politics , published in 1598, the translator simply erased the philosophical distinction between being ‘equal’ ( isos ) and being ‘similar’ ( homoios ) by translating both terms as ‘equal’.

这种含混无疑冒犯了哲学的精确性。然而在政治上，它却创造了机会。利尔伯恩在《大宪章》（Magna Carta）中注意到了一个这样的机会，具体说，是其中向王国内每一个自由人保证的权利：受同侪审判的权利，即 per pares。在近代早期英语中，「peer」当然可以指任何社会地位上的平等者，无论高低。但在政治用语中，它也日益可以指英格兰贵族的一员。如果每个「生而自由的」英格兰男女都有权受「同侪（或平等者）的合法裁决」的审判——如利尔伯恩所说——那么，他或她当然也必定有资格享有共和国中「与最显赫的贵族同等的特权」，一如在受造之中那样。

Such slippages undoubtedly offend against philosophical precision. Politically speaking, however, they created opportunities. Lilburne noticed one such opportunity in Magna Carta, specifically in its guarantee to every free man in the realm the right to a trial per pares , or by his peers. In early modern English, ‘peer’ could of course mean a social equal of whatever degree, high or low. But increasingly in political usage, it could also mean a member of the English aristocracy. If every ‘freeborn’ Englishman and woman had the right to be tried by ‘the lawful judgment of [their] peers (or equals)’, as Lilburne put it, then surely he or she must also be ‘entitled unto an equal privilege’ with the greatest Peer in the Commonwealth, as in Creation.

随后，平等派运动把这种概念歧义推向极致，借助无数的小册子、抗议和请愿，不仅要求英格兰平民在法律面前与贵族受到「平等的」对待，还要求享有贵族身份的全部政治好处。托马斯·雷恩伯勒（Thomas Rainborough）在普特尼通过激进扩大选举权而提出的「平等发言权」的著名要求，遵循的是同一套拉平逻辑：「因为我确实认为，英格兰最贫穷的人，也和最伟大的人一样，有一条命要活。」1649年平等派妇女呈交下议院的一份引人注目的请愿书也是如此，其中的女性请愿者宣告：

The Leveller movement subsequently pushed this conceptual ambiguity to the hilt across countless pamphlets, protests and petitions, in which they demanded not only that English commoners be treated ‘equally’ with aristocrats before the law, but also that they enjoy all of the political benefits of peerage. Thomas Rainborough’s celebrated demand for ‘equal voice’ at Putney, through a radical expansion of the franchise, followed the same levelling logic: ‘For really I think that the poorest he that is in England has a life to live as the greatest he.’ So did a remarkable 1649 Leveller women’s petition presented to the House of Commons, in which the female petitioners declared:

就这样，即便平等派运动失败了，它谈论平等的崭新方式却留存了下来。我们在托马斯·霍布斯（Thomas Hobbes）的《利维坦》（Leviathan，1651）中，在所谓「第一位英国女权主义者」（也是铁杆托利党人）玛丽·阿斯特尔（Mary Astell）的著作中，以及在美国《独立宣言》中，都能找到这种拉平逻辑的种种版本。在这些政治结论迥异的文本中，平等的语言持续履行着它独特的功能：把大人物削回本来尺寸，同时把边缘者和被排斥者抬举起来与之相会。因此，主张平等不再仅仅是要求同一部法律、或同一位上帝以同样的标准无差别地适用，而是要求在社会中被承认为一个平等者：即一个有资格作为同侪发言、评判并被人倾听的人。

In this way, even as the Leveller movement failed, its new way of talking about equality lived on. We find versions of its levelling logic in Thomas Hobbes’s Leviathan (1651), or in the writings of the so-called ‘first English feminist’ (and staunch Tory) Mary Astell , as well as in the US Declaration of Independence. Across these very different texts, with their different political conclusions, the language of equality continued to perform its distinctive function, cutting the great ones down to size while raising up the marginal and excluded to meet them. To claim equality was thus no longer merely to demand that the same law, or the same God, apply the same standards indifferently. It was to demand recognition as an equal in society: that is, someone entitled to speak, judge and be heard as a peer.

几个世纪以来，这似乎是平等话语的首要政治功能。但在今天，这种语言已经失去了它大部分的拉平之力。与此同时，谈论平等不再凝聚人心，而是令人幻灭、制造分裂。为什么？

F or centuries, it seems, this was the primary political function of equality talk. But, today, this language has lost much of its levelling power. At the same time, talk of equality no longer unifies, it disillusions and divides. Why?

也许，问题最终恰恰出在当初使这种概念语言如此富有吸引力的东西上。平等绝非简单，更遑论不言自明；它的历史表明，平等随时间累积了数量惊人的含义。而在人应当如何彼此相处这个问题上，这些不同的含义——作为缺席或平衡、无差别或对等——往往处于紧张之中，甚至直接相互矛盾。

Perhaps the problem ultimately lies with what made this conceptual language so appealing in the first place. Far from being simple, let alone self-evident, its history reveals that equality has accumulated a remarkable number of meanings over time. And when it comes to how human beings ought to relate to one another, those different meanings – as absence or balance, indifference or parity – are often in tension, if not outright contradiction.

那么，我们各说各话又有什么奇怪的呢！尽管「平等」一词暗示，更好的测量或推理理应消解我们的分歧。有人也许会由此得出结论——正如许多当代进步派已经做的那样——认为我们最好干脆抛弃平等，改信某种历史上不那么含混的理想。但我认为那将是一个错误。因为在历史上，平等话语的问题所在——即它的概念歧义——也一直是它非凡力量的源泉。

Is it any wonder, then, that we talk past each other! Even as the word ‘equality’ suggests that better measurement or reasoning ought to resolve our disagreements. Now, one might conclude from this – as many contemporary progressives have done – that we would be better off rejecting equality altogether, in favour of some other, less historically ambiguous ideal. But that would, I think, be a mistake. Because historically, the problem with equality talk – namely, its conceptual ambiguity – has also been the source of its uncommon power.

再想想利尔伯恩。他那些受过更好教育的批评者可以（也确实）反驳说，他呈给上议院的「一般性命题」是一个不当推论（non sequitur）。从上帝造男造女、使其平等地服从于他的神圣正义这个前提，并不能推出他们因此就应当在政治上被承认为同侪。但这种批评并未能阻止利尔伯恩和他的平等派同志们继续利用平等的多重含义，得出他们自己的政治结论。而他们的成功并不仅仅是巧妙的双关或概念戏法。他们坚称英格兰平民与贵族一样有能力自主地思考和言说，这种说法之所以可信，依托的是一个人们熟悉的制度：陪审团。当「邻里中的十二人」坐堂审判一位平民同伴时，他们在财富、职业、教育和能力上的差异并未消失，只是在作出裁决的目的上不再计较。因此，陪审员们之所以把彼此视为「同侪与平等者」，是因为制度要求他们作为同侪行动——一起倾听、商议和裁决。

Again, consider Lilburne. His better-educated critics could (and did) object that his ‘general Proposition’ to the Lords was a non sequitur. It simply didn’t follow from the premise that God created men and women as equally subject to his divine justice, that they should therefore be recognised politically as peers. But this criticism did not stop Lilburne and his fellow Levellers from trading on the many different meanings of equality and drawing their own political conclusions. And their success was not simply a matter of ingenious wordplay or conceptual sleight of hand. The plausibility of their insistence that English commoners were just as capable of thinking and speaking for themselves as Peers rested on a familiar institution: the jury. When ‘12 men of the neighbourhood’ sat in judgment over a fellow commoner, their differences of wealth, occupation, education and ability didn’t disappear. They simply ceased to matter for the purposes of reaching a verdict. Hence the jurors came to see one another as ‘peers and equals’ because the institution required them to act as peers, as they listened, deliberated and judged together.

就这样，平等的「实践」先行于平等的观念。但如果平等派的「平等话语」依托于一种特定的社会实践，那么这种实践的边界同样重要。英格兰妇女可以受陪审团审判，却通常不能坐上陪审席；英格兰大西洋世界中被奴役的非洲人，尽管也被承认「同样是受造之人」，却被认为过于「粗野」而无从享有那种特权。因此，几个世纪里，这些群体不得不艰难地援引同样的论证，因为他们缺少能让自己同样作为同侪和平等者去行动——并被看见在行动——的社会实践与制度。

In this way, the ‘doing’ of equality proceeded the idea of it. But if Leveller ‘equality talk’ rested on a particular social practice, the limits of that practice mattered, too. English women could be tried by juries, but they did not ordinarily sit on them; enslaved Africans in the English Atlantic, although admittedly ‘created Men’, were regarded as too ‘brutish’ to enjoy that privilege. For centuries, accordingly, these groups would struggle to leverage the same arguments in the absence of social practices and institutions within which they, too, could act – and be seen to act – as peers and equals.

过去就说这些。那么未来呢？首先，上述分析表明，让「平等话语」变得有意义的工作，首要地不是哲学性的，而是实践性的。尽管如此，哲学家们常常表现得仿佛平等的一切困难都必定是逻辑上的或定义上的：只要我们能足够精确地识别出每个人在哪个方面是「平等的」，我们关于正义的分歧就会消解。但历史表明，更重要的任务，是维系那些从一开始就使平等变得有意义的社会实践与制度。（因此，当代关系平等主义者不愿在正面意义上说明平等究竟要求我们做什么——除了一般性地抵制等级关系——乃是一个严重的缺陷。）

So much for the past. What about the future? Well, in the first place, the above analysis suggests that the work of making ‘equality talk’ meaningful is not primarily philosophical, it’s practical. Nonetheless, philosophers often proceed as though any difficulties with equality must be logical or definitional. If only we could identify the respect in which every person is ‘equal’ with enough precision, then our disagreements about justice would dissolve. But history suggests that the more important task is that of sustaining the social practices and institutions that make equality meaningful in the first place. (Hence, the reticence of contemporary relational egalitarians to spell out what equality demands of us in positive terms, beyond a general resistance to hierarchical relations, is a serious defect.)

那么，一种深谙历史的平等研究进路会是什么样子？首先，它要求我们关注（乃至亲身参与）诸如陪审义务这样的事物——在那些日益罕见的线下场所中，我们与那些和我们不同的人——无论在体量、卓越程度还是意识形态上——作为同侪相遇。今天，这些场所正受到威胁，威胁不仅来自大语言模型或社交媒体这类消除摩擦的技术，也来自愈演愈烈的消费体验按价格分层——迈克尔·桑德尔（Michael Sandel）在2012年将其称为「美国生活的包厢化」。但是，在更为均平的条件下参与这类活动，并不会使我们变得一模一样，也不意味着我们彼此认同。它只是意味着，我们承诺在有限的时间和目的之内，不让我们的差异来决定我们中间谁更重要——或者谁的声音应当被计算在内。

So what would an historically informed approach to equality look like? For one thing, it would require attending to (or even just attending) things like jury service, among the other increasingly uncommon offline sites in which we encounter other people who are not like us – in bulk, excellence or ideology – as peers. Today, these sites are threatened not only by frictionless technologies like large language models or social media, but also by the growing price-stratification of so many consumer experiences – what Michael Sandel in 2012 dubbed the ‘skyboxification of American life’. But taking part in such activities on more even terms doesn’t make us identical, nor does it mean that we agree. It just means that we commit ourselves, for a limited time and purpose, to not letting our differences determine which of us matters – or whose voice ought to count.

因为，如果说历史告诉了我们什么，那就是：平等首先不是一个有待信奉的观念，也不是一项需要申明的党派立场。它是人们做过的事情，也是我们必须去做的事情。唯有如此，我们才被创造为平等——不是靠「自然法则与自然之神的法则」，而是靠我们彼此，每一天。

Because, if history tells us anything, it’s that equality is not primarily an idea to be believed in, nor a partisan commitment to affirm. It’s something people did, and something we must do. Only then are we created equal – not by ‘the Laws of Nature and of Nature’s God’, but by each other, every day.
