哲学

相信与认为

Neil Van Leeuwen & Jacob Eisler·Aeon·2026年11月4日·13分钟阅读·原文

政教分离的观念——如今已深深植根于许多现代自由民主国家——在某些方面看来颇为奇怪。毕竟，政府必须让自己掌握信息，也必须向公民传递信息。正因如此，政府才会求助于气象科学来预测天气模式，求助于经济学来理解增长与衰退的规律，求助于医学科学来改善公共健康。那么，政府为什么不也与宗教机构保持紧密联盟，好知道上帝那边的情况呢？

The idea of separating church and state – now entrenched in many modern liberal democracies – is in some ways strange. After all, governments must keep themselves informed and inform their citizens. That’s why they turn to the science of meteorology to predict weather patterns, to economics to understand patterns of growth and decline, and to the medical sciences to improve public health. So why wouldn’t governments also keep close alliances with religious institutions to know what’s going on with God?

从其他角度看，政教分离同样显得奇怪。在大型人类社会的历史长卷中，它只是个相对晚近的新来者，常与美国开国元勋托马斯·杰斐逊（Thomas Jefferson）联系在一起。1802年，杰斐逊在写给康涅狄格州丹伯里浸信会协会（Danbury Baptist Association）的一封信中申明了这条分离原则：

Separating church and state appears strange from other perspectives as well. In the historical sweep of large-scale human societies, it’s a relative newcomer, often associated with the United States’ founding father Thomas Jefferson. 我们都是杰斐逊这条原则的拥趸——我们中的一位（尼尔·范·列文，Neil Van Leeuwen）是在基督教改革宗教会（Christian Reformed Church）中长大的哲学教授，另一位（雅各布·艾斯勒，Jacob Eisler）则是定期参加犹太会堂礼拜的法学教授。但杰斐逊这封信把该原则的实践说得比实际情况更简单——仿佛面对人们的信念，或曰「意见」，政府可以完全撒手不管。然而，这不可能正确：介入并塑造政治、社会及其他各类信念，其实是政府的一项核心职能。公共教育引导着人们对从数学到历史再到天文学等一切事物的信念。政府还会出于政策原因塑造信念。如果消防部门官员得知人们误以为某栋危楼安全无虞，他们就会设法改变人们对这栋楼安全性的看法。信念也是法律关注的范畴。例如，如果心理治疗师相信某位患者会伤害自己或他人，法律便要求该治疗师上报这一信念。

We’re fans of Jefferson’s principle, one of us (Neil Van Leeuwen) being a philosophy professor who grew up in the Christian Reformed Church, and the other (Jacob Eisler) being a law professor who regularly attends synagogue. But Jefferson’s letter makes putting the principle into practice sound simpler than it is – as though governments could be entirely hands-off when it comes to people’s beliefs, or ‘opinions’. That, however, can’t be right: engaging with and shaping political, social and other kinds of beliefs is actually a core function of government. Public education guides what people believe about everything from mathematics to history to astronomy. Governments also shape beliefs for policy reasons. If fire department officials learn that people believe a building is safe when it isn’t, they will try to change what people believe about the building’s safety. Belief is also a category of legal interest. If a psychotherapist, for example, believes a patient is a danger to himself or others, the therapist is obligated by law to report that belief.

这一切都意味着，杰斐逊的「分离之墙」（wall of separation）不可能被合乎情理地用来禁止政府干预任何类型的信念。毋宁说，杰斐逊——以及他之后这条原则的倡导者们——必定预设了宗教信念有某种独特之处，正是这种独特之处让给予它们不同的法律地位变得合情合理。

All this means that Jefferson’s ‘wall of separation’ can’t sensibly prohibit governments from intervening on beliefs of any type whatsoever. Rather, Jefferson – along with advocates of the principle who came after him – must have presupposed that there is something distinctive about religious beliefs that makes it reasonable to give them a different legal status.

但这个独特之处究竟是什么？是宗教信念关涉上帝吗？不是，因为佛教是一种自有其信念的宗教，而佛教并没有至高神。是宗教信念关涉一般意义上的超自然吗？也不是，因为许多不属于任何宗教的迷信同样关涉超自然。是宗教信念关涉道德，就像生物学家斯蒂芬·杰伊·古尔德（Stephen Jay Gould）曾提出的那样吗？还不是，因为许多宗教信念与道德无关，而许多道德信念也并非宗教性的。再说了，任何政府体制要想成其为政府，都必须预设某些道德信念——比如「谋杀是错的」。这些考量或许会让人灰心，觉得无从阐明我们所关注的那条心理区分——宗教「信念」究竟有何不同？——而这条区分正是让政教分离从一开始就说得通的东西。

But what is this distinctive thing? Is it that religious beliefs concern God? No, because Buddhism is a religion with its own beliefs, yet Buddhism has no supreme deity. Is it that religious beliefs concern the supernatural generally? No, because many superstitions that aren’t part of a religion concern the supernatural too. Is it that religious beliefs concern morality, as the biologist Stephen Jay Gould once suggested? No, because many religious beliefs aren’t about morality, and many moral beliefs aren’t religious. In any case, any system of government must presuppose some moral beliefs – eg, that murder is wrong – to be a government at all. These considerations might make one despair of articulating the psychological distinction of interest – what’s different about religious ‘belief’? – that’s needed to make sense of separating church and state in the first place.

我们认为，这类刻画宗教信念的尝试之所以失败，是因为它们聚焦于信念的内容，即所相信的是什么。但既然任何给定观念既可以以宗教的方式相信，也可以不以宗教的方式相信，这条路就行不通。因此，我们的主张是：宗教性地相信，是一种不同于寻常信而有据的相信（matter-of-fact believing）的态度——是一种与观念相关联的不同方式——而这正是宗教信念的独特之处。换言之，把一个观念神圣化，不同于仅仅把它当作对事物实况的描述。此外，认知科学与宗教人类学中的实证研究，连同与之相伴的哲学分析，能帮助我们揭示我们所追寻的这种态度差异的典型特征。为了有明确的术语，我们把这种差异称为宗教信奉（religious credence）与事实性相信（factual belief）之间的区别。我们将看到，理解这一区别，既有助于理解杰斐逊的分离之墙，也为更一般意义上自由民主国家的政教分离之理据提供了支撑。

Such attempts at characterising religious belief fail, we think, because they focus on belief contents, or what the believing is about. But since any given idea can be believed in a religious way or not, that won’t work. Our suggestion, then, is that religiously believing is a different attitude from ordinary matter-of-fact believing – a different way of relating to ideas – and that this is what’s distinctive about religious belief. In other words, sacralising an idea is different from just taking it to describe how things are. Furthermore, empirical research in cognitive science and anthropology of religion, along with accompanying philosophical analysis, can help reveal characteristic features of the difference in attitude we’re after. To have specific terms, we call that the difference between religious credence and factual belief. Understanding this difference, we’ll see, helps make sense of Jefferson’s wall of separation, and supports the rationale for separating church and state in liberal democracies more generally.

无论它们到底是什么，「信念」都分不同的种类。「信念」一词是多义的——也就是说，它可以指一系列不同但相关的事物——它涵盖的成员五花八门：从平淡无奇的事实性信念（比如冰淇淋店在哪儿），到迷信（比如占星术里的那些），从关于谋杀的道德信念，到关于「万物一体」的哲学信念。如前所述，信念既可以按内容来分类（这个信念是关于什么的？是关于政治吗？还是关于某个大概率为真的科学理论？），也可以按其所体现的态度来分类（一个人如何与给定的观念或内容相关联？）。不过，我们在此聚焦的是信而有据的相信与宗教信奉作为人们处理观念的两种不同方式之间的差异。当然，类信念的态度还有更多种类。但我们专门考察事实性相信与宗教信奉，因为这两种态度的差异对政教分离的心理学最为关键。

Whatever they are, ‘beliefs’ come in different varieties. The word ‘belief’ is polysemous – meaning it can refer to a range of different-but-related things – and it subsumes a motley crew: from humdrum factual beliefs (say, about where the ice-cream store is) to superstitions (like those found in astrology), and from moral beliefs about murder to philosophical beliefs about how ‘all is one’. As mentioned earlier, beliefs can also be categorised by their contents (what is the belief about? Is it about politics? Or maybe about a probable scientific theory?) Or by the attitude they embody (how does a person relate to given ideas or contents?) Here, however, we focus on the differences between matter-of-fact believing and religious credence as distinct ways in which people process ideas. There are, of course, more varieties of belief-like attitudes. But we home in on factual belief and religious credence since the differences in those attitudes matter most to the psychology of separating church and state.

宗教民族志——比如人类学家坦尼娅·卢尔曼（Tanya Luhrmann）的《当上帝回应时》（When God Talks Back，2012）——记载了虔信者把自己的「信念」当作一种选择来谈论。卢尔曼采访过的一位葡萄园教会（Vineyard Church）会友告诉她：「相信与否，永远是一种选择。」在葡萄园教会，一个常见主题就是选择耶稣，也就是选择相信耶稣。而选择「信念」也并非基督教独有。世界各地的人会为了爱情或其他社会激励而改换宗教，这意味着他们在自愿选择持有何种宗教信奉。

Ethnographies of religion, like the anthropologist Tanya Luhrmann’s When God Talks Back (2012), feature the devout talking of their ‘beliefs’ as a matter of choice. One of the congregants from the Vineyard Church whom Luhrmann interviewed told her: ‘There is always a choice to believe what it is.’ And a common theme in the Vineyard is choosing Jesus, which means choosing to believe in Jesus. Nor is choosing ‘belief’ unique to Christianity. People around the world switch religions for reasons of romance or for other social incentives, which means voluntarily choosing what religious credence to have.

但假设有人出100万美元，让你以信而有据的方式相信狮子是食草动物。你能做到吗？我们深表怀疑，哪怕你很想要那笔钱。你可以自愿地想象狮子是食草动物，或者假装相信如此。但如果你知道狮子吃肉，你就无法径直选择相信相反的事。这说明了事实性相信不受意志支配。或者，正如我们中的一位（尼尔）在《作为假扮的宗教》（Religion as Make-Believe，2023）一书中所说：如果你事实性地相信某事，你就不可能不去相信它。请试着花片刻工夫，事实性地相信纽约市位于魁北克、冰岛位于南半球，或者贾斯汀·比伯（Justin Bieber）的母语是斯瓦希里语。

But suppose someone offered you $1 million to believe, in a matter-of-fact way, that lions are vegetarians. Could you do it? We doubt it, even if you wanted the money. You could voluntarily imagine that lions are vegetarians, or you might pretend to believe it. But if you know that lions eat meat, you can’t just choose to believe the contrary. This is one illustration of how factual belief is not under voluntary control. Or, as one of us (Neil) has put it in Religion as Make-Believe (2023): if you factually believe it, you can’t help believing it. Try for a moment to factually believe that New York City is in Quebec, that Iceland is in the Southern Hemisphere, or that Justin Bieber speaks Swahili as his mother tongue.

This gives us our first processing difference between religious credence and matter-of-fact belief:

差异一：宗教信奉是自愿的，而事实性相信是非自愿的。

Difference number 1: religious credence is voluntary, while factual belief is involuntary.

但如果信而有据的相信不是自愿的、不响应意志，那它响应什么？众多心理压力会参与其中，包括偏见、记错之类的扭曲因素。但这不应遮蔽事实性相信的主要驱动力：证据。例子简单得容易被人忽视。如果你就事论事地以为自己启动了洗衣机，一小时后却发现塞进去的衣服还是干的、还是脏的，你亲眼所见的证据便熄灭了你先前的事实性相信。如果你以为超市还开着门，到了却看到店内灯光全灭，这个证据就会改变你的事实性相信。

But if matter-of-fact belief isn’t voluntary, or responsive to the will, what does it respond to? Numerous psychological pressures come into play, including distortions like biases and misremembering. But that shouldn’t obscure the main driver of factual belief: evidence. Examples are so simple they’re easy to overlook. If you think, in a matter-of-fact way, that you started the washing machine and then, an hour later, see that the clothes you loaded are dry and still dirty, the evidence of your eyes extinguishes your prior factual belief. If you think the supermarket is open and then arrive to find its lights are all out, that evidence changes your factual belief.

实证研究显示，这种对证据的敏感性在儿童甚至婴儿身上就已经出现。根据发展心理学家艾莉森·戈普尼克（Alison Gopnik）及其同事的《摇篮里的科学家》（The Scientist in the Crib，1999），幼儿甚至婴儿是通过在不同领域形成微型理论并加以检验来认识世界的——很像科学家的做法。根据发展心理学家保罗·哈里斯（Paul Harris）与梅丽莎·克尼格（Melissa Koenig）的研究，儿童在向他人学习时也表现出证据上的甄别力。其中一点是：儿童更愿意相信过去准确的信息来源，而非过去不准确的信息来源。

Empirical research reveals such evidence-responsiveness already in children and even infants. According to The Scientist in the Crib (1999) by the developmental psychologist Alison Gopnik and colleagues, young children and even babies learn about the world by forming mini-theories in different domains, and testing them – much like scientists do. According to the research of the developmentalists Paul Harris and Melissa Koenig, children are also evidentially discriminating in how they learn from other people. Among other things, children are more likely to believe sources that have been accurate in the past than sources that have been inaccurate.

宗教信奉是否同样响应证据？

Is religious credence similarly responsive to evidence?

且看信仰。《希伯来书》11:1写道：「信就是所盼望之事的确据，是未见之事的凭据。」如果这话不错，那么宗教信奉就不像事实性相信那样依赖证据。例如在《约翰福音》里，我们读到多疑的多马（Doubting Thomas），他要求证据证明眼前之人就是复活的耶稣；他也因此成了「信仰所不是」的典范。在《旧约》中，挪亚则是信仰的典范：在没有洪水将至的任何可见证据时，他建造了一艘巨大的方舟。这样的例子不胜枚举。信仰若要屹立不倒，宗教信奉就必须经得起证据缺席乃至与之相悖的种种明枪暗箭。

Consider faith. In Hebrews 11:1, we read: ‘Now faith is the assurance of things hoped for, the conviction of things not seen.’ If that’s right, then religious credence doesn’t depend on evidence in the way factual belief does. In the Gospel of John, for example, we read about Doubting Thomas, who demands evidence that the man before him is Jesus resurrected; he consequently is an exemplar of what faith is not. In the Old Testament, Noah was an exemplar of faith for building an enormous boat when there was no visible evidence of an impending flood. Examples can be multiplied. If faith is to stand strong, religious credence must endure the slings and arrows of absent, or even contrary, evidence.

当代宗教认知科学印证了这一点。在一系列跨文化研究中，研究人员发现，即使在伊朗和美国这样的宗教社会，人们从幼年起就报告说，自己对他们所信奉的不可见宗教实体（如上帝和天使）的信心，低于对不可见的科学实体（如细菌和氧气）的信心。这种信心差异大概源于人们感到不可见宗教实体所对应的证据贫乏。然而，人们仍以某种意义「相信」（请读作：宗教信奉）那些不可见的宗教实体。心理学家艾米丽·利奎因（Emily Liquin）、S·埃姆伦·梅茨（S Emlen Metz）与塔尼娅·隆布罗佐（Tania Lombrozo）的研究发现：相较于科学信念，被试对宗教信念表现出更低的解释需求，同时又对宗教信念表现出更高的神秘性接受度。所以，人们常常安于把自己的宗教信奉当作无法解释的神秘之物。

Contemporary cognitive science of religion bears out this point. In a series of cross-cultural studies, researchers have found that, from a young age, people, even in religious societies like Iran and the US, report lower levels of confidence in the invisible religious entities they profess, like God and angels, than in invisible scientific entities, like germs and oxygen. That difference in confidence is presumably due to the felt poverty of evidence for the invisible religious entities. Yet people in some sense nonetheless ‘believe’ (read: religious credence) in the invisible religious entities. The psychologists Emily Liquin, S Emlen Metz and Tania Lombrozo conducted studies finding that participants indicated a lower need for explanation when it came to religious beliefs as opposed to scientific beliefs, while at the same time having a higher mystery acceptance for religious beliefs. So people are often fine treating their religious credences as inexplicable and mysterious.

这就给出了宗教信奉与信而有据的相信之间的第二个差异：

That gives us our second difference between religious credence and matter-of-fact belief:

差异二：事实性相信极易受证据影响，而宗教信奉则不受证据影响。

Difference number 2: factual belief is highly vulnerable to evidence, while religious credence is not vulnerable to evidence.

当然，这里存在复杂之处。例如，宗教护教士会为他们信奉的叙事和教义声称握有证据。但声称握有证据，并不等于其信奉真的会受证据牵动；况且，在广大信众的总体背景衬托下，护教士本来就属异类——信众通常并不假装以证据为依据来对待自己的宗教信奉。

There are, of course, complications here. Religious apologists, for example, claim evidence for the stories and doctrines they profess. But claiming evidence is not the same as having one’s credences be vulnerable to it, and apologists are outliers anyway against the broad background of faithful who don’t usually pretend to treat their religious credence in an evidence-based way.

上述差异——自愿选择与证据响应之别——是宗教信奉与事实性相信在生成之初的差异。那么，下游的差异呢，比如它们如何引发行动与行为？

Those differences, voluntary choice versus evidence-responsiveness, are differences in how religious credence versus factual belief arise in the first place. But what about downstream differences, like how they bring about action and behaviour?

这就引出了第三个差异。你听说过「一周一次的基督徒（once-a-week Christian）」这个说法吗？这个短语描述的是这样的人：一个星期里有那么一天，他们的言行仿佛其基督教「信念」为真——其余时间则大体任其沉睡。我们自己成长并参与过不同的宗教传统，凭亲身经历可以说，符合这种区隔化（compartmentalisation）模式的人比愿意承认的要多。即使是每天操练灵修的人，也可能只在神圣或仪式性的时间与场合表现得仿佛上帝真实存在，其余时候则并非真的如此行事。如果你问信教者上帝是否无时无刻不在他们的生活中，他们很可能会说是。但这样的探问，也许就像靠打开冰箱门来查看冰箱里的灯是否总是亮着。在《破除魔咒：作为自然现象的宗教》（Breaking the Spell: Religion as a Natural Phenomenon，2006）中，哲学家丹尼尔·丹尼特（Daniel Dennett）以更直白的方式点出了这一行为模式。他指出，人们在「上帝注视下」会做一些在自己母亲注视下不会做的事，接着又尖锐地问道：「你真的相信上帝在注视着你吗？恐怕未必。」我们想说，宣认宗教信奉的人通常确实对一位监察之神怀有宗教信奉——但顺着丹尼特的话说，他们并没有事实性相信。

This brings us to the third difference. Have you ever heard the expression ‘once-a-week Christian’? The phrase describes people who act as though their Christian ‘beliefs’ are true one day a week – and then largely leave them dormant otherwise. Having grown up and participated in different religious traditions ourselves, we can say from personal experience that more people fit this compartmentalised model than would like to admit. Even those who practise devotion daily may act as if God is real specifically in sacred or ritual times and places, but then not really act this way otherwise. If one asks religious people whether God is ever-present in their lives, they might say yes. But such probes might be like opening the refrigerator to see whether the light is always on. In Breaking the Spell: Religion as a Natural Phenomenon (2006), the philosopher Daniel Dennett highlights this behavioural pattern in even blunter terms. He points out that people do things with God watching that they wouldn’t do with their own mothers watching, and then asks pointedly: ‘Do you really believe God is watching you? Perhaps not.’ We would say that people who profess to religious credence do typically have religious credence in a watchful deity – but, to Dennett’s point, not factual belief.

实证研究与民族志观察进一步印证了宗教信奉的区隔化。一项研究显示，圣经地带（Bible Belt）的人订阅色情网站的比率，与美国其他不那么保守的地区相当——但周日除外，那天该比率显著更低。另一项研究显示，信教者的慈善捐赠与非信教者并无显著差别——除了周日之外。再看另一个宗教语境：实验经济学家埃里克·杜海姆（Erik Duhaime）发现，在一个经济博弈中，马拉喀什麦地那老城（Medina）的摩洛哥店主在宣礼声清晰可闻时有100%选择了最具慈善性的选项，而在宣礼结束后的20分钟里，这一比例降到了50%。

Empirical studies and ethnographic observations further exemplify the compartmentalisation of religious credence. According to one study, people in the Bible Belt start subscriptions to pornography sites at the same rate as in less conservative regions of the US – though not on Sundays, when the rate is significantly lower. According to another study, charitable giving among the religious is not significantly different from among the secular – except for on Sundays. Turning to a different religious context, the experimental economist Erik Duhaime found that, in an economic game, 100 per cent of Moroccan shopkeepers in the Medina of Marrakesh chose the most charitable option while the call to prayer was audible, dropping to 50 per cent in the 20 minutes following it.

关于区隔化，还可以换一种说法：宗教信奉倾向于引导人们的象征性行动——祷告、礼仪、仪式，乃至慈善——却不引导他们日常的工具性行动，比如通勤上班或喂狗。在那些场合，世界就只是世界，超自然信念大体处于沉睡状态。而且，即便某个宗教传统宣称其宗教「信念」应当随时随地支配行动，这种规范的存在及其难以遵行的事实，恰恰证明此类宗教信奉通常并不在神圣场域之外引导行动。（倘若它们当真如此，何必立下这样的规范呢？）

Another way of putting the point about compartmentalisation would be to say that religious credences tend to guide people’s symbolic actions – prayer, liturgies, rituals or even charity – but not their everyday instrumental actions, like commuting to work or feeding the dog. In those cases, the world is just a world, and supernatural beliefs are largely dormant. And even if a religious tradition says its religious ‘beliefs’ should govern actions all the time, the fact that such norms exist and are hard to follow is actually evidence that such religious credences usually don’t guide action outside of sacred settings. (If they did, why have such a norm?)

然而，信而有据的相信并不以这种方式被区隔。在本文所用的意义上，事实性相信是一个人对世界的默认认知地图，无论我们何时行动，它都始终开着。举一些大多数人都以事实性方式相信的观念，有助说明这一点：干燥的混凝土总是硬的；电就是存在的；木头来自树木；如此等等——至少对那些以信而有据的方式相信这些事的人来说是如此。这种不加区隔的特性，也可以借假装游戏作对照来说明：即使一个人假装一块混凝土板是柔软的床，他躺上去时仍会缓缓伸展身体以免受伤，而不是猛然扑倒在上面。这是因为「混凝土是硬的」这一事实性相信，在人假装相反情况时仍在引导行动。每当你执行行动时，你都在依照一个「事物本来如何」的心理模型行事，而一个人对世界样貌的默认心理模型，就是我们所说的事实性相信。

Matter-of-fact believing, however, is not compartmentalised in this way. Factual belief, in the sense at work here, is a person’s default cognitive map of the world, and it is always on, whenever we act. Examples of ideas most people factually believe help to illustrate this point. Dried concrete is always hard. Electricity just exists. Wood comes from trees. And so on – at least for people who believe those things in a matter-of-fact way. This lack of compartmentalisation can also be illustrated through a contrasting reference to pretend play: even if one pretends that a slab of concrete is a soft bed, one still stretches out on it slowly so as not to injure oneself, rather than flopping down on it. That’s because the factual belief that concrete is hard still guides action even when one is pretending otherwise. Whenever you perform actions, you act on a mental model of how things just are, and a person’s default mental model of what the world is like is what we are calling factual belief.

差异三：宗教信奉是区隔化的，而事实性相信不区隔。

Difference number 3: religious credence is compartmentalised, while factual belief is uncompartmentalised.

以上只是宗教信奉与事实性相信之间若干心理加工差异的一幅快照。当然，还有复杂之处和细节有待梳理——包括难以归类的例子，比如阴谋论「信念」，以及准宗教式的意识形态信奉——但它们不应遮蔽整体图景。大体而言，信教者拥有一种双地图式的认知结构，事实性相信与宗教信奉各司其职。事实性相信是那张响应证据、跨场域引导行动的默认认知地图；宗教信奉则是一张被选择的、区隔化的认知地图，在神圣化的仪式性场合才被激活。这些场合可能一天出现数次，可能一周只有一天，甚至比这更少见，但无论如何，区隔化始终存在。

This is all just a snapshot of some of the psychological processing differences between religious credence and factual belief. There are, of course, complications and details to sort out – including hard-to-categorise examples, like conspiracy ‘beliefs’, along with quasi-religious ideological credences – but those shouldn’t obscure the overall picture. Religious people, by and large, have a two-map cognitive structure with factual belief and religious credence playing distinct roles. Factual belief is the evidence-responsive default cognitive map that guides action across settings, and religious credence is a chosen, compartmentalised cognitive map that becomes active in sacralised ritual settings. Those settings may occur as often as several times a day, just one day a week, or even more rarely than that, but compartmentalisation is there in any case.

在回到政教分离问题之前，让我们先就这幅整体心理图景再处理一个问题：普通人（不只是会读会写这类文章的人）是否意识到了事实性相信与宗教信奉之间的心理差异？

Before returning to the issue of separating church and state, let’s just address one more question about this overall psychological picture: are lay people (not just people who read and write essays like this one) aware of the psychological difference between factual belief and religious credence?

证据显示，答案是肯定的——至少是笼统意义上的肯定。

The answer, the evidence suggests, is yes – at least in a vague way.

首先，人们常用「我个人相信……」这类话来表达自己的宗教信奉。但没有人会这样来表达一个信而有据的相信，比如「自行车在车库里」：没有人会说「我个人相信我的自行车在车库里」，而会说「我想我的自行车在车库里」，或者干脆说「它在车库里」。使用「个人」一词的倾向表明，人们直觉地意识到，所表达的这类「信念」是一种不同的态度，是一种有别于信而有据的相信的、与观念相关联的不同方式。关于这一点，也有更系统的实证证据。我们中的一位（尼尔）与同事所做的实验研究显示，英语使用者在描述宗教态度时倾向于用「相信（believes）」一词，而在描述信而有据的相信时只用「认为（thinks）」。

To start, people often express their religious credences by saying things like ‘I personally believe…’ But one would not express a matter-of-fact belief, say, that their bicycle is in the garage, by saying: ‘I personally believe my bicycle is in the garage.’ They would rather say: ‘I think my bicycle is in the garage’ or just ‘It’s in the garage.’ The inclination to use ‘personally’ suggests people have an intuitive awareness that the kind of ‘belief’ being expressed is a different attitude, a different way of relating to ideas from matter-of-fact belief. And there is more systematic empirical evidence for this point, as well. One of us (Neil) together with colleagues has conducted experimental studies showing that English speakers tend to use the word ‘believes’ when it comes to describing religious attitudes but just ‘thinks’ when it comes to describing matter-of-fact beliefs.

此外，这种语言上的分化在其他语言中也有对应现象，包括芳蒂语（Fante，加纳阿肯人的一种方言）、泰语、汉语普通话，以及比斯拉马语（Bislama，瓦努阿图通行的一种克里奥尔语）。进一步的研究显示，人们使用「相信」一词，与把某种态度视为自愿持有、非因证据而持有——例如出于忠诚——并会引发象征性行动（如公开宣告）相关联；而人们使用「认为」一词，则与把某种态度视为因证据而持有、并会引发依赖真实性的工具性行动（如购置房产或进行投资）相关联。这一切都表明，多数人在某种程度上正在追踪前述理论所明确刻画的那些差异——而且他们往往正是用「相信」与「认为」这对词，来分别报告宗教信奉与事实性相信。

Furthermore, such linguistic differentiation has parallels in other languages, including Fante (a dialect of the Akan people of Ghana), Thai, Mandarin and Bislama (a creole spoken in Vanuatu). Further studies show that people’s use of the word ‘believes’ correlates with seeing an attitude as being held voluntarily and not for evidential reasons – for example, out of loyalty – and as giving rise to symbolic action, like public pronouncements. People’s use of the word ‘thinks’, on the other hand, correlates with seeing an attitude as being held for evidential reasons and as giving rise to truth-dependent instrumental action (like buying property or making investments). All of that suggests that most people are at some level tracing the differences that the foregoing theory makes explicit – and they’re often using the words ‘believe’ versus ‘think’ to report religious credence versus factual belief.

让我们回到杰斐逊的观念，也回到更一般的政治领域。事实性相信与宗教信奉之间的心理差异，如何支撑政教分离这条政治原则？我们认为，理解这一差异——连同其全部心理动力学——能让我们看清两条理据；若不理解它，我们便无法把这两条理据清晰阐说。

Let’s return to Jefferson’s idea and to the political realm more generally. How does the psychological difference between factual belief and religious credence support the political principle of separating church and state? We think that understanding the difference – psychological dynamics and all – helps us see two justifications that we couldn’t have articulated clearly otherwise.

第一，宗教信奉不易受证据影响这一事实，有助于论证如下观念：自由民主国家不应设立国教——就强制人们皈依的那种意义而言。（这是「分离之墙」中由所谓美国宪法第一修正案（First Amendment）「设立条款（establishment clause）」所涵盖的部分。）这里的推理很简单。它始于一个认识：政府采取的任何行动，事实上都会以某类「信念」为依据并由其获得辩护。例如，在埃博拉疫情期间对从刚果民主共和国返回的人员实施隔离，依据的是官员们的事实性相信——在此案例中是有据可依的——即这些人有传播疫病的风险。

First, the fact that religious credences are not vulnerable to evidence helps justify the idea that liberal democracies should not establish state religions, in the sense of compelling people to adhere to them. (This is the part of the ‘wall of separation’ captured in the so-called ‘establishment clause’ of the First Amendment to the US Constitution.) The reasoning here is simple. It starts by recognising that any actions a government takes will, in point of fact, be based on, and justified by, ‘beliefs’ of some sort. For example, quarantining people who return from the Democratic Republic of Congo at the time of an Ebola outbreak is based on officials’ factual belief – justified in this case – that those people risk spreading the disease.

重要的是，如果相关信念易受证据影响——正如我们所论证的事实性相信那样——那么它们就可以通过那种以对话为基础的论证与说理而改变，而这种论证与说理正是民主的审议内核。基本上，基于事实性相信的推理与政策制定允许人人参与，因为事实性相信响应证据，而证据性理由为所有人共有。

Importantly, if the beliefs in question are vulnerable to evidence, as we argued that factual beliefs are, then they will be susceptible to change through the kind of dialogue-based argument and reasoning that is the deliberative heart of democracy. Basically, factual belief-based reasoning and policy-making allows everyone to participate, since factual beliefs respond to evidence, and evidential reasons are the property of everyone.

但反过来，如果一种「信念」不响应证据——正如我们所论证的宗教信奉那样——那么以证伪为取向的论证性说理就无法撼动它。只有已经持有该「信念」的人，才会被基于它的决定说服。于是，一种明确带有宗教性质的政策——例如强制推行宗教实践——将难以对接民主决策的界定性实践。简言之，自由民主国家应当排除设立国教的政策，以守护居于其核心的理性共同审议。

But, conversely, if a ‘belief’ is not responsive to evidence, as we argued religious credences are not, then it won’t be movable by falsification-oriented argumentative reasoning. Only people who already have the ‘belief’ will be convinced by decisions based on it. Thus, a policy explicitly religious in nature – imposing religious practice, for example – would lack tractability to the defining practices of democratic decision-making. In short, liberal democracies should exclude religion-establishing policies in order to safeguard the rational communal deliberation that is at their centre.

这一视角也照亮了自由民主国家的政策为何以其现有的方式获得辩护。公民往往如此，而立法者——若要在宪法上站得住脚——则必须如此：即便他们恰好受属灵信念驱动，也应以信而有据的、人人皆可把握的证据来组织论证中的描述性主张。这样的组织方式所产出的公共解释，比各种宗教「信念」的直接碰撞更适合自由民主的共存。这一点在当代政策制定最具争议的领域之一——堕胎法——中显而易见。眼下的美国，堕胎政策从某些州的相当宽松，延伸到另一些州的实质性严苛。然而，尽管许多政治行动者确实受其属灵信念和宗教归属驱动，在这一议题上采取与之相符的立场，他们仍试图诉诸可受理性和证据质疑的信念，来为这些立场辩护。如此一来，关于如何尊重生命神圣性之类理想的辩论，至少在原则上，可以成为把每一个人都包括进来的公共对话。

This perspective illuminates why policy is justified the way it is in liberal democracies. Even when they happen to be motivated by spiritual convictions, citizens tend to, and legislators must (if they are to be constitutionally respectable), frame the descriptive claims in their arguments in terms of matter-of-fact, universally tractable evidence. Such framing yields public explanations that are better suited to liberal democratic coexistence than the mere direct clash of disparate religious ‘beliefs’. This is apparent in one of the most controversial areas of contemporary policymaking: abortion law. Right now in the US, abortion policy runs from fairly permissive in some states to materially restrictive in others. Yet, while it’s true that many political actors are motivated to take positions on this issue that align with their spiritual beliefs and religious affiliations, they still attempt to justify those positions by appeal to beliefs that can be contested by reason and evidence. As a result, debate over how to respect ideals like sanctity of life can, at least in principle, be a matter of public dialogue that includes everyone.

我们在此聚焦于美国的情形，因为美国在政教分离的历史上地位显赫。但美国以第一修正案所阐明的这种分离承诺，在世界各地都有映照：从日本到墨西哥，从葡萄牙到纳米比亚。即便是拥有国教的英国——其国教即英格兰教会（Church of England）与苏格兰教会（Church of Scotland）——也对这些国教在公共领域可为与不可为之事施加了严格限制。同样重要的是，这一切都无碍公民和领导者出于其宗教虔信而更偏好某项政策。但私人动机与公共辩护是两回事。我们眼下的论点是：事实性相信与宗教信奉之间的心理差异，有助于解释为什么一条把这两者拆分开来的原则是有道理的。

We’ve focused on the US case here, because it is prominent in the history of separating church and state. But commitment to the kind of separation spelled out in the US with the First Amendment is mirrored across the globe, from Japan to Mexico and from Portugal to Namibia. Even the United Kingdom, which has state religions in the form of the Church of England and the Church of Scotland, places strict limits on what those state religions can and can’t do in the public sphere. Importantly, again, none of this prevents citizens and leaders from being motivated to prefer one policy over another due to their religious commitments. But private motivation and public justification are different things. Our present point is that the psychological difference between factual belief and religious credence helps explain why a principle that teases apart those two things makes sense.

第二，宗教信奉在心理上被区隔这一事实，有助于论证如下观念：在自由社会中，宗教的「自由行使（free exercise）」是一个合理的理想。（这是「分离之墙」的另一面，同样体现在第一修正案中：政府不应阻止人们选择信奉哪种宗教，或者干脆不信。）

Second, the fact that religious credences are psychologically compartmentalised helps justify the idea that, in a liberal society, ‘free exercise’ of religion is a reasonable ideal. (This is the other side of the ‘wall of separation’, also captured in the First Amendment: government shouldn’t stop people from choosing which religion to follow, if any.)

这个论证是这样运作的。宗教信奉的区隔化，加上它作为一张有别于事实性相信的「地图」而运作这一事实，意味着持有宗教信奉的人在与其宗教之外的人交往时，至少能够把这些信奉暂放一边。（例如，宗教信奉中相信魔鬼存在的人，一般不会在超市里对陌生人施行驱魔。）既然人们能够在公共领域把宗教信奉放到一边，那么出于对隐私与自主的一般考量，他们就应当自由地在私下保有并尊崇这些信奉。公共审议便可以在响应证据的事实性相信、以及响应理由的道德信念的基础上进行。简言之，宗教信奉默认倾向的那种区隔化，使持有多种宗教「信念」的人得以在自由社会中共存，而国家无需对其中任何一种「信念」本身发起挑战。

Here’s how this argument works. The compartmentalisation of religious credence, along with the fact that it operates as a ‘map’ distinct from factual belief, means that people who have religious credences are at least capable of setting them aside when it comes to interacting with people outside their own religion. (For example, people who have religious credence in the existence of demons don’t typically perform exorcisms on strangers in the supermarket.) So, since people are capable of setting their religious credences aside in the public sphere, they should be free to keep and venerate them in private, in light of general considerations of privacy and autonomy. Public deliberation can then proceed on the basis of evidence-responsive factual beliefs, as well as moral beliefs that respond to reasons. In short, the default compartmentalisation to which religious credence is prone makes it possible for people with a plurality of religious ‘beliefs’ to co-exist in a free society without a need for the state to challenge any of those ‘beliefs’ per se.

把这一切贯通起来，我们的论证是：政教分离向信教者提出的交易——人人享有宗教自由，以换取遵守一部阻止政府把他们的特定宗教立为国教的宪法——之所以行得通，正因为宗教信奉在心理上是区隔化的，在与事实性相信不同的层面上运作。而这种默认区隔化的事实本身，恰恰正是让这样一笔交易值得达成的理由。我们猜想，杰斐逊在提笔写那封开头引用过的、致丹伯里浸信会的信——向他们保证不会因其宗教虔信而遭受压迫——之时，就已直觉地意识到这一点。或许，在他那句「宗教纯粹是介于人与其上帝之间的事」里，我们能听到区隔化这一观念本身的先声。

To put this all together, our argument is this: the deal that separation of church and state offers to the religious – namely, that everyone gets religious freedom in exchange for abiding by a constitution that prevents government from establishing their specific religion – can work only because religious credences are psychologically compartmentalised and operate at a different level from factual belief. But the very fact of that default compartmentalisation is precisely what makes such a deal worth striking. Jefferson, we suspect, intuitively sensed this, when he composed his letter to the Danbury Baptists, quoted at the outset, assuring them that they wouldn’t be oppressed for their religious commitments. And perhaps we hear a foreshadow of the very idea of compartmentalisation in his line: ‘religion is a matter which lies solely between man and his God.’

那个短语（「人与其上帝」）中的物主代词，道出了宗教信奉的私人性质。毕竟，宗教信奉是自愿的。这种私人性质使宗教信奉不宜进入公共审议；但也正因这种性质，它恰好适宜领受那种免受政府干涉私生活的自由——而正是这种自由，界定了自由社会的一般特征。

The possessive pronoun in that phrase (‘man and his God’) bespeaks the personal nature of religious credence. Religious credence, after all, is voluntary. That personal nature makes religious credence inapt for entry into public deliberation, but it also makes it ripe for receiving the freedom from government interference into private lives that characterises liberal societies generally.
