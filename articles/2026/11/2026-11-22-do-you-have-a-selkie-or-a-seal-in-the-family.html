你的家族里有海豹人（selkie），还是只有海豹？

Jordan Lee Borgman·Aeon·2026年11月22日·13分钟阅读·原文

1948 年 10 月，在爱尔兰凯里郡（County Kerry），肖恩·奥·穆尔切塔伊（Seán Ó Muircheartaigh）会见了绍萨姆·奥·达利（Seosamh Ó Dálaigh），向他讲述那位来自大海的女人。据奥·穆尔切塔伊说，她是在某个夜晚出现在马海里斯（magharees）上的——那是一片明亮的沙嘴，偶尔从丁格尔半岛（Dingle peninsula）被暴雨冲刷的礁石之间冒出来。她独自一人在海滩上，在一块岩石上坐下，开始梳理自己的头发。

In October 1948, in Country Kerry, Ireland, Seán Ó Muircheartaigh met with Seosamh Ó Dálaigh to tell him about the woman who came from the sea. She had appeared, said Ó Muircheartaigh, one night on the magharees, those bright spits of sand that occasionally pop up amid the rain-lashed rocks of the Dingle peninsula. Alone on the beach, she settled herself on a rock and began combing her hair.

海滩上还有一位农夫——令那女人自己也大吃一惊的是——到天亮时他将成为她的丈夫。他本是来采集海藻的，在她看见他之前先看见了她。那女人固然美丽，却也古怪：他看到那个跌跌撞撞走上丁格尔海滩的生物，根本不是女人。那是一只海豹。

Also on the beach was a farmer, who – much to the surprise of the woman herself – would by daybreak become her husband. He’d come to gather seaweed, seeing her before she saw him. Beautiful as she was, the woman was also strange: the creature he saw stagger onto that Dingle beach was not a woman at all. It was a seal.

等到奥·穆尔切塔伊向奥·达利讲述海豹女人的故事时，他已经 80 岁了。他出生于大饥荒（Great Hunger）浩劫般的余波之中——那场饥荒使爱尔兰多达三分之一的人口或死或迁——而他又熬过了两场世界大战。那些消失者中的大多数，和奥·穆尔切塔伊本人一样，是小农和爱尔兰本土语言，即爱尔兰语（Gaeilge）的使用者。那场饥荒不仅是一场肉体上的种族灭绝，也是一场语言与文化上的灭绝。

By the time Ó Muircheartaigh told Ó Dálaigh about the seal woman, he was already 80. He had been born in the apocalyptic aftermath of the Great Hunger that divested as much as a third of Ireland’s population to death or migration, and he had survived two great wars. Most of the disappeared were – like Ó Muircheartaigh himself – small farmers and native Irish, or Gaeilge, speakers. The famine had been not just a physical genocide: it had been a linguistic and cultural one, too.

奥·穆尔切塔伊和奥·达利都是爱尔兰民俗学委员会（Irish Folklore Commission，简称 IFC）的资深贡献者：前者是著名的讲故事人，后者是同样著名的采集者。该委员会如今是都柏林大学学院（University College Dublin）的国家民俗收藏馆（National Folklore Collection）——一座拥有 300 万页文本与录音的庞然大物。但在 1948 年，历经八个世纪的殖民主义、岛上 99% 森林的消失、[触目惊心的大移民](https://aeon.co/essays/the-irish-experience-and-the-meaning-of-modern-diaspora)以及爱尔兰语的衰微，该委员会正在与时间和记忆的脆弱赛跑。而事实证明，听过海豹化为人上岸的故事的人，多得吓人。

Both Ó Muircheartaigh and Ó Dálaigh were veteran contributors to the Irish Folklore Commission: the former as a renowned storyteller; the latter, an equally renowned collector. The IFC is now the National Folklore Collection at University College Dublin ­– a behemoth of 3 million pages of texts and recordings. But in 1948, following eight centuries of colonialism, the loss of 99 per cent of the island’s forests, a [staggering emigration](https://aeon.co/essays/the-irish-experience-and-the-meaning-of-modern-diaspora), and the decline of the Irish language, the IFC was in a race against time and the fragility of memory. As it turned out, an awful lot of people had heard stories about seals that came to land as humans.

如今，这些变形者被称为「海豹人」（selkie）。Selkie 是一个苏格兰语词汇，经由琼·贝兹（Joan Baez）1961 年翻唱的一首苏格兰传统民歌「Silkie」而广为流传。然而它所描述的现象——生物蜕下一张皮，便能在人与海豹的形态之间变换——始终连贯地出现在横跨凯尔特世界乃至更远之地（远至瑞典）的民间传说中。在贝兹把这个词悄然植入国际通用语汇之前，爱尔兰语和英语里是用一连串各异的称谓来指认海豹人的。在爱尔兰语中，最常见的说法是 _mhaighdean mhara_，即「海洋少女」，兼指海豹人与美人鱼。即便在英语里，许多讲故事的人也用「美人鱼」（mermaid）来同时指称海豹类和鱼类这两种生灵。有时二者靠语境来区分（鱼尾、海豹皮），但比外表特征归类更重要的是：美人鱼也好，海豹人也好，都既不完全是人，也不完全是动物。定义海豹人的，是变形。

N owadays, these shapeshifters are known as ‘selkies’. Selkie is a Scots term that came into widespread use via a Joan Baez cover, ‘Silkie’ (1961), of a traditional Scottish folk song. Yet the phenomenon it describes – of creatures who shapeshift between human and seal forms by shedding a skin – appears consistently in folklore spanning the Celtic world and beyond, even as far as Sweden. Before Baez insinuated the term into international parlance, selkies were identified in Irish and English by a constellation of descriptors. In Irish, the most common is _mhaighdean mhara_, or ‘sea maiden’, referring to selkies and mermaids. Even in English, many storytellers use ‘mermaid’ for both the phocine and the piscine. Sometimes the two are differentiated with context (fish tails, seal skins), but what matters more than phenotyping is that the mermaid or selkie is neither quite human nor animal. What defines the selkie is transformation.

[https://youtube.com/embed/6zZy2Q3QY0Q](https://youtube.com/embed/6zZy2Q3QY0Q) Much of the folklore retains the quality of the best gossip, hyper-local and yet vague. Storytellers name names, offer precise locations for key scenes in the story. And several admit something startling: that they themselves are the descendants of human-seal pairings.

据奥·穆尔切塔伊讲，农夫在海滩上望见那女人之后，又看到了别的东西。一件海豹皮斗篷放在她身旁：那是她变形并重返大海的凭证。接着他做了一个选择——一个会在世世代代间回荡不绝、令海豹人别无选择只能跟他回家的选择。他偷走了海豹人的斗篷，把它藏了起来，就这样把她弄到手做了新娘。撇开这粗暴的开端不谈，夫妻俩生养了一窝孩子，过着任何穷困爱尔兰渔家都要熬的艰辛日子。唯一打破「以寻常人间方式娶得寻常人间妻子」这一幻象的，是孩子们脚趾间的蹼。此外还有一桩事：那女人从不微笑，只是不停地眺望大海。

After the farmer spotted the woman on the beach, Ó Muircheartaigh said, he saw something else. A sealskin cloak lay beside her: her ticket to transformation and a return to the sea. Then he made a choice that would ricochet throughout the generations, and leave the selkie with no option but to follow him home. He stole the selkie’s cloak and secreted it away, thus securing her for a bride. These rough beginnings aside, the couple produced a brood of children and went about the gruelling lives of any impoverished Irish fishing family. The only thing disrupting the fantasy of a normal human wife, acquired in normal human ways, was the webbing between the children’s toes. That, and the fact that the woman never smiled, but kept looking out towards the sea.

如果你的祖母是一只海豹，这意味着什么？民间传说证明，人与海豹人的婚姻曾常见到足以催生出一大批人豹混血的家族谱系——康尼利（Conneely）、加拉格尔（Gallagher）、多德（Dowd）、奥凯恩（O'Kane）、奥谢（O'Shea）、麦克达菲（MacDuffie）、麦克菲（McPhee）、弗莱厄蒂（Flaherty）、格拉文（Glavin）——这些家族的成员天生倾向于长蹼趾，并对大海怀有一种难以言说的渴望。1949 年，铁路工人谢默斯·麦克马瑟纳（Séamas MacMathghamhna）在讲解了奥谢家族的海豹人血统之后，向一位民俗采集者吐露：「我母亲就是奥谢家的人。」凯尔特研究学者格雷戈里·达尔文（Gregory Darwin）已识别出近 50 个家族群体，其成员身上流着海豹人的血。

W hat does it mean if your grandmother was a seal? The folklore attests that marriages between humans and selkies were common enough to generate a host of human-selkie lineages – Conneely, Gallagher, Dowd, O’Kane, O’Shea, MacDuffie, McPhee, Flaherty, Glavin – whose members were predisposed towards webbed digits and an ineffable longing for the sea. (‘My mother was one of them O’Sheas,’ the railway worker Séamas MacMathghamhna confided to a folklore collector in 1949, after explaining the selkie lineage of the O’Sheas.) The Celtic studies scholar Gregory Darwin has identified nearly 50 family groups whose members carry selkie blood.

如今正是家谱里有一只海豹的好时候。当我们的星球在气候灾难中疾驰，从环保活动家、TikTok 网红到学者和新纪元朝圣者，人人都转向了「动物即家人」的修辞。这份热情源于一种文化上的疲惫——对攫取主义的疲惫，对自 19 世纪以来一直纠缠着全球北方文化与地缘政治的支配叙事和人类例外论的疲惫。然而，时代精神在靠近一种扩展的跨物种亲缘观时所带的天真，本身也带来问题。其一，它不太经得起推敲——而要为一个你看不真切的世界观而奋斗，是很困难的。其二，如果我们把与「人类之外」（more-than-human）的关系重新框定为亲密的家族联结，那么当这种联结变得紧张纠结时，我们就需要理解那会发生什么的方法。

Now is a good time to have a seal in the family tree. As our planet hurtles through climate catastrophe, everyone from environmental activists and TikTok influencers to academics and New Age pilgrims is turning towards animals-as-family rhetoric. Such gusto stems from a cultural exhaustion with extractionism and with narratives of domination and human exceptionalism that have dogged the culture and geopolitics of the Global North since the 19th century. Yet the naivety with which the zeitgeist tends to approach an expanded view of interspecies kinship presents its own problems. For one, it doesn’t quite ring true – and it is hard to fight for a worldview that you can’t see clearly. For another, if we reframe our relationship with the more-than-human around intimate familial connection, then we need ways of understanding what happens when that connection becomes fraught.

海豹人的故事堪称家庭失能的极致样本。毕竟，海豹人家庭最扎心的真相在于：这一切的源头是偷窃、暴力、强暴和难以想象的失去。人与海豹结合所生的孩子，往好里说，面对的也是一份斑驳的遗产。2008 年，布莱恩·帕特里克·米勒（Bryan Patrick Miller）为《纽约时报》（The New York Times）撰文，讲述他的高祖父的祖父当年绑架一位 _mhaighdean mhara_（海洋少女）如何诅咒了整个家族，致使这对夫妻的半人孩子们逃过大西洋，远走他乡。在一些沿海社群看来，海豹是堕落的天使或溺亡渔夫的亡魂：与它们交合，往轻里说也是可疑的。

The selkie story is one of family dysfunction par excellence. After all, the gutting truth at the centre of selkie families is that one’s origins lie in theft, violence, rape and unimaginable loss. The children of human-selkie pairings face a mottled inheritance, at best. Writing for _The_ _New York Times_ in 2008, Bryan Patrick Miller recounted how his great-great-great-grandfather’s kidnapping of a _mhaighdean mhara_ cursed the family, causing the couple’s half-human children to flee across the Atlantic. Some coastal communities saw seals as fallen angels or the souls of drowned fishermen: mating with them was dubious to say the least.

无论斑驳与否，海豹人的家谱都必须被正视。家族中有一位海豹人，就迫使你承认一份阴暗而不安的混合遗产，而且一代又一代人持续牵涉其中。唯有细想「成为一家人」意味着什么的这些不安的回响，我们才能更接近于重新想象我们与人类之外世界的关系。

Mottled or not, a selkie genealogy had to be reckoned with. Having a selkie in the family forced an acknowledgement of a mixed inheritance that was dark and disturbing, and in which successive generations continued to be implicated. Only by considering these uncomfortable reverberations of what it means to be family do we move closer to reimagining our relationships with the more-than-human world.

在婚姻中性欲得不到满足的女人，可以对着大海哭泣，从而召来一位床上精力惊人的海豹人

A woman who is sexually unsatisfied in her marriage may cry into the sea and receive a selkie who is an absolute dynamo in bed

在民俗学家中间，海豹人这个母题通常被称为「海豹妻」（Seal Wife）——这个称呼更多道出的是海豹人故事对我们想象力的性别化掌控，而非民间传说本身的实际题材。海豹人的魅力如此之大，男人们甘冒诅咒、行窃、绑架来占有她，全然不顾强制婚姻所预示的必然悲剧。然而民间传说里的海豹人不一定是妻子，不一定是女人，甚至不一定是海豹（参见上文提到的美人鱼）。雄性海豹人偶尔客串出场，扮演被绑架海豹妻那遭抛弃的丈夫、她那些半水生孩子的父亲，在人类丈夫的房外哭嚎着要求放她走。另一些时候，他们以她兄弟的身份出现，或作为人类与其环境日常互动中的角色：他们被误杀、被掳作宠物、偷鱼、发预言、恳求与讨价还价、提供忠告和意见。

Among folklorists, the trope of the selkie is generally known as the Seal Wife – a designation that speaks more to the gendered hold that the selkie story has on our imagination than to the actual folklore topos. The selkie’s allure is such that men will risk curses and commit theft and kidnapping to possess her, regardless of the certain tragedy that forced marriage augurs. Yet the selkies of folklore are not necessarily wives, women or even seals (note the mermaids, above). Male selkies occasionally make cameos as the kidnapped selkie wife’s jilted husband, father of her aquatic children, keening for her release from outside the human husband’s house. At other times, they appear as her brother, or as characters in the quotidian interactions between humans and their environment: they are accidentally killed, kidnapped as pets, steal fish, issue prophesies, plead and bargain, proffer advice and opinions.

雄性海豹人也会以爱人的身份到来。有时他们与人类女子的情缘命中多舛——比如说，这对恋人可能七年才能相会一次——但总体而言，与雄性海豹人的性事是一件纯粹欢乐的事。在婚姻中性欲得不到满足的女人，或是盼着一场青春艳遇的少女，可以对海哭泣，召来一位海豹人——据说，那可是床上精力惊人的绝顶高手。

Male selkies also arrive as lovers. Sometimes their affair with a human woman is star-crossed – the paramours may meet only once every seven years, say – but generally sex with a male selkie is an occasion for straightforward joy. A woman who is sexually unsatisfied in her marriage or a maiden hoping for a youthful adventure may cry into the sea and receive a selkie who is, apparently, an absolute dynamo in bed.

这些故事的迷人之处在于，它们把女性快感放在中心位置——这是海豹妻故事从未做过的（男性快感也同样从未）。海豹妻纵有诱使丈夫行窃的性感魅力，为人称道的却是她操持家务、养育子女的能耐。但即便雄性海豹人的故事，偶尔也会转向黑暗。有时，是人类女子偷走爱人的皮来逼他成婚；另一些时候，则是人类女子遭到雄性海豹人的强暴。

What is fascinating about these stories is that they centre female pleasure in a way that the selkie wife stories never do (or male pleasure, for that matter). Instead, the selkie wife – despite the sensual appeal that incited her husband to theft – is noted for her competence in housekeeping and childrearing. But even the male selkie stories occasionally take dark turns. Sometimes, the human woman steals her lover’s skin to compel him into marriage; other times, the human woman is raped by the male selkie.

民间传说中描述的这些混合关系层层叠叠、纠葛重重，但也与爱尔兰乡村生活的节奏交织在一起。爱尔兰人把自己与人类之外世界相联结的倾向——包括通过默罗（merrow，即美人鱼）、会变形的普卡（púca），当然还有 aos sí（与人相对应的超自然仙灵）这样的生物——数百年来一直有目共睹。就连诺曼人，也因为新臣民对把自己与动物区分开来毫无兴趣而懊恼，着手改革爱尔兰教会，并「教化」这里的人口。

The hybrid relationships described in the folklore are multilayered and fraught, but they are also interwoven with the rhythms of rural Irish life. The Irish tendency to link themselves to the more-than-human world – including through creatures such as the merrow, or mermaid, the shapeshifting _púca_, and, of course, the _aos sí_, the supernatural, fairy analogue to humans – has been noted for hundreds of years. Even the Normans, frustrated with their new subjects’ disinterest in distinguishing themselves from animals, set about reforming the Irish Church and ‘civilising’ the population.

在论文《蜕下海豹皮》（Slipping Off the Sealskin，2015）中，文学批评家兼研究型艺术家克莱尔·勒库图尔（Clair Le Couteur）指出，早期的海豹人故事远比后来的故事更接近性别平等：不仅雄性海豹人出场更多，雌性海豹人也更有权能。他们提出，向着相对扁平的「海豹妻」母题的转变，呼应了人类对自身生态位置看法的变化。雄性海豹人「在很大程度上已被抹去〔并被〕一个简化版的海豹妻故事所取代，在这个故事里，性别与物种被交叉勾连……女性、动物（海豹），乃至自然与超自然两界，被混同为一种简化的、可资榨取的身份，与作为主人公的『人』的能动性相对而立」。

In their paper ‘Slipping Off the Sealskin’ (2015), the literary critic and research artist Clair Le Couteur points out that early selkie stories had far more gender parity than later ones: not only were male selkies better represented, but their female counterparts were more empowered. The change towards the comparably flat Seal Wife trope, they suggest, echoes mankind’s changing view of our ecological place. Male selkies have ‘been largely erased [and] replaced by a simplified Seal Wife story in which gender and species are cross-linked … the female, the animal (seals), and indeed the natural and supernatural realms are conflated into a simplified, exploitable identity formed in opposition to Man’s agency as protagonist.’

然而，即使海豹妻象征着性别剥削与环境剥削，她也绝不束手就擒。偷走她皮的准丈夫们得到的是郁郁寡欢、反叛，有时还有暴力。在一个故事里，一位 _mhaighdean mhara_（海洋少女）放火烧了绑架者的房子，循着皮肉烧焦的气味找回了自己的皮（尽管有传说称，毁掉海洋少女的皮可能导致她死亡，勒库图尔对此也有告诫）。

Yet if the Seal Wife stands for gendered and environmental exploitation, she does not submit without a fight. The would-be husbands who steal her skin are met with unhappiness, rebellion and sometimes violence. In one story, a _mhaighdean mhara_ retrieves her skin by setting her kidnapper’s house on fire and following the smell of singed skin (in spite of the lore, as Le Couteur warns, that destroying a _mhaighdean mhara_’s skin may lead to her death).

海豹人故事中一个格外难办的面向，是它一再坚称这段婚姻是幸福的。说这是父权制的粉饰，不无道理：在一个故事里，讲述者把婚姻描述为幸福，结果只隔了两行就承认，那女人在陆上的岁月里只笑过三次——最后一次是在她离开之时。然而这个故事提出了一个扎人的真相：有时候，爱本身就是问题。你可以爱一个伤害你的人，也仍然可以离开。

One particularly difficult aspect of the selkie story is the repeated insistence that the marriage is a happy one. It’s a fair claim that this is patriarchal whitewashing: in one story, the teller describes the marriage as happy, only to admit two lines later that the woman laughed just three times during her spell on land – the last being at her departure. Yet the story raises a thorny truth: sometimes love is problematic. You can love someone who hurts you, and you can still leave.

人类与动物是亲族这一观念，属于宽泛的「关系本体论」范畴，也就是所谓的泛灵论（animism），尽管这个词在凯尔特研究中的位置并不确定。英国人类学家爱德华·泰勒（Edward Tylor）是这个新兴领域的巨擘，他在《原始文化》（Primitive Culture，1871）一书中造出了这个词。在书中，他把世界人口划分为不同的进化阶段：「泛灵论」是留给那些不开化到只能把握最粗糙宗教形式的野蛮人的。直到一个多世纪之后，努里特·伯德-大卫（Nurit Bird-David）等先驱学者才重新拾起并翻新这个词，称之为「新泛灵论」（new animism），并[论证](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.1086/200061)泛灵论所描述的并非一种「失败的认识论」，而是一种关系性的认识论。

T he notion that humans and animals are kin falls into the broad bucket of relational ontologies known as animism, although the word occupies an uncertain place in Celtic studies. Edward Tylor, a British anthropologist and a towering figure in the nascent field, coined the term in his book _Primitive Culture_ (1871). Here, he divided the world population into stages of evolution: ‘animism’ was reserved for savages so undeveloped they could grasp only the crudest expression of religion. It took more than a century for pioneering scholars like Nurit Bird-David to reclaim and refresh the term as ‘new animism’ and [argue](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.1086/200061) that animism did not describe a ‘failed epistemology’, but a relational one.

起初，维多利亚时代的人热衷于把所谓「落后」的欧洲乡村民众与他们眼中不文明的海外文化相提并论。然而，正如哈佛大学（Harvard University）宗教专业博士候选人、草药师兼野生动物康复学徒娜塔莉亚·施维恩·斯科特（Natalia Schwien Scott）告诉我的那样，一旦殖民主义变得种族化，这套说辞便迅速改换：如今，作为人与动物之间谱系亲缘的泛灵论，被划归给黑人与棕色人种文化，成为他者化他们的手段。爱尔兰人自己受够了英国人的非人化对待，对这个词避之不及。这种警惕可以理解——而且，倘若历史可为殷鉴，它也完全有其道理。

Initially, the Victorians were thrilled to equate so-called ‘backwards’ rural Europeans with those cultures abroad that they deemed uncivilised. Yet, as Natalia Schwien Scott, a herbalist, wildlife rehabilitation apprentice, and PhD candidate in religion at Harvard University told me, once colonialism became racialised, the language quickly changed: now animism as genealogical kinship between humans and animals was delegated to Black and brown cultures as a means of othering them. The Irish themselves, sick of British dehumanisation, wanted nothing to do with the term. Such leeriness is understandable – and, if history is anything to go by, completely warranted.

在种族政治之外，泛灵论的切身体验——尽管它素有顺应时代精神、一团和气的名声——其实颇为复杂。「当你不是泛灵论者时，泛灵论是很容易喜欢的，」德国海德堡大学（Heidelberg University）的人类学家圭多·斯普伦格（Guido Sprenger）告诉我。2021年，斯普伦格发表了一篇[论文](https://www.jstor.org/stable/48746706)，标题颇为俏皮：「泛灵论能拯救世界吗？」他是目睹泛灵论「从一个令人兴奋的分析概念，滑落为一个指涉与环境之道德关系的万金油术语，而几乎不顾那些真正实践它、并因此陷入种种道德困境与危险的人」之后，才写下这篇文章的。他补充道：「泛灵论可不是那种漂亮、平和、与非人类卿卿我我的韵事。」

Beyond racial politics, the lived experience of animism – for all its zeitgeisty, Kumbaya reputation – is a complicated one. ‘Animism is easy to like when you’re not an animist,’ Guido Sprenger, an anthropologist at Heidelberg University in Germany, told me. In 2021, Sprenger published a [paper](https://www.jstor.org/stable/48746706) cheekily titled ‘Can Animism Save the World?’ He’d written it after watching animism slip ‘from being an exciting analytical concept to a catch-all term for moral relations with the environment, without much regard for those who actually practise it and find themselves in all sorts of moral conundrums and dangers.’ He added: ‘Animism isn’t that pretty, peaceful, smoochy, nonhuman affair.’

如果人类例外论的边界被消解，每只动物都是家庭成员，那么你该如何进食？

If the bounds of human exceptionalism are dissolved, and every animal is a family member, then how do you eat?

与许多其他人类学家一样，斯普伦格起初因这个词的殖民起源与含混用法而对它敬而远之；然而，当泛灵论开始在环境人类学中崭露头角时，他重新考虑了自己的立场。如今他担心，泛灵论的原罪正在卷土重来：出于为晚期资本主义和人类例外论寻找替代方案的迫切心情，这个词再一次被稀释到无法使用的地步。「一切不认同我们那套自然—文化二分的东西，不知怎么都成了泛灵的，因而也更好，」他说。「而这是退回到泛灵论那种漫无边际、理论单薄的用法。」他担心，这种对泛灵论骤然而起的热情，意味着回避它一些更为棘手的现实。

Like many other anthropologists, Sprenger was initially put off by the term’s colonial origins and nebulous usage; yet, when animism started to gain traction in environmental anthropology, he reconsidered his stance. Now, he worries that the original sin of animism is returning to dilute the term, once again, past the point of usability, out of a desperation to find alternatives to late-stage capitalism and human exceptionalism. ‘Everything that’s not us, with our nature-culture divide, is somehow animist and therefore better,’ he says. ‘And this is a step back into that diffuse and theoretically thin use of animism.’ He worries that this sudden enthusiasm for animism means sidestepping some of its trickier realities.

斯普伦格[长期相处](https://rai.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-8322.2006.00448.x)的泛灵论者，是老挝高原上的尔米特人（Rmeet）。一些生态哲学家主张与自然精灵建立更紧密的联结，以应对我们的生态危机；但在尔米特人看来，「这会是疯子的念头。」精灵是尔米特人生活中的既成事实，却不是仁慈的那种。正如斯普伦格告诉我的，在尔米特人的观点里：「谁想跟精灵打交道？谁想靠近它们？关键恰恰在于，你要与它们保持良好关系，因为你指望它们不要来搅扰你的生活。它们只是存在于那里的实体。它们不是一个规范性的范畴。它们跟一棵树一样无所谓规范——树可能砸在你头上，也可能给你荫凉。精灵就是在_那里_。」

The animists with whom Sprenger [spends](https://rai.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-8322.2006.00448.x) his time are the Rmeet people of the Laotian highlands. Some eco-philosophers argue for a closer connection with the nature spirits to address our ecological crisis but, for the Rmeet, ‘this would be the idea of a madman.’ Spirits are a fact of life for the Rmeet, but not a kind one. As Sprenger told me, from the Rmeets’ point of view: ‘Who wants to deal with spirits? Who wants to get close to them? The whole point is that you want to be on good terms with them because you want them not to interfere in your life. They are just entities that are there. They are not a normative category. They are just as normative as a tree – a tree can fall on your head, or it can give you shade. The spirits are just _there._’

他的话表明，亲密与安全并非一回事。例如，萦绕泛灵论宇宙观的一个难题便是[食人](https://aeon.co/essays/anthropophagy-art-flips-the-cannibal-worries-of-colonialism)。如果人类例外论的边界被消解，每只动物都是家庭成员，那么你该如何进食？一些文化靠在动物之间做出区分来化解这一两难（放过海豹，捕取鲑鱼）；另一些文化则借助仪式行为——比如烹煮——把动物从「人」转化为「肉」。还有一些文化则一头扎进这个难题。一个动物家庭成员或许会允许自己落入猎人之手，以这种自我牺牲让自己的亲族得以存活。

His words suggest that intimacy and safety are not the same. For example, one issue that besets animist cosmologies is [cannibalism](https://aeon.co/essays/anthropophagy-art-flips-the-cannibal-worries-of-colonialism). If the bounds of human exceptionalism are dissolved, and every animal is a family member, then how do you eat? Some cultures resolve this dilemma by distinguishing between animals (leave the seal, take the salmon); others by using ritual acts – cooking, say – to metamorphose animals from person to meat. Still others fly straight into the conundrum. An animal family member might allow itself to be caught by hunters, an act of self-sacrifice that allows its kin to survive.

在泛灵论的宇宙观里，爱与暴力永远纠缠不清，这让我不禁思忖：身为海豹的后裔究竟有什么要紧？为什么要把一个如此悲伤、如此令人不安的家族故事紧握不放？朱迪斯·巴特勒（Judith Butler）在《暴力、哀悼、政治》（Violence, Mourning, Politics，2003）中[论证](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/15240650409349213)：正是我们与他人的关系，使我们面临暴力的风险——然而放弃关系，便是放弃为人。「丧失与脆弱，」巴特勒写道，「似乎源于我们是社会性地构成的身体：依恋着他人，可能失去这些依恋；暴露于他人，并因这种暴露而面临暴力的风险。」简言之，我们的依恋使我们身处险境，而卸下依恋，则可能失去某种对我们安身于万物秩序之中而言根本的东西。

Love and violence are perpetually entangled in animist cosmologies, which makes me wonder: why does it matter to be descended from a seal? Why hold onto a family story so fundamentally sad and troubling? In ‘Violence, Mourning, Politics’ (2003), Judith Butler [argues](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/15240650409349213) that it is our relations with others that put us at risk of violence – and yet to forfeit being in relation is to forfeit being human. ‘Loss and vulnerability,’ they write, ‘seem to follow from our being socially constituted bodies, attached to others, at risk of losing those attachments, exposed to others, at risk of violence by virtue of that exposure.’ In short, our attachments put us in jeopardy, while shedding them risks losing something fundamental to our place in the order of things.

泛灵论——或者至少是宣称一种世界观，在其中我们视自身为融入超人类世界（more-than-human world）而非凌驾其上——并不提供安全。非但如此，它恰恰背道而驰。与超人类世界建立关系，认真对待我们在严格人类之外的那些联结，意味着更多的暴露，也意味着更多的牵挂。正如海豹人（selkie）故事所印证的那样，它把我们直接置于更多暴力的必经之路上。我们杀死一头鲸，也就杀死了我们的祖母。我们冒着房子被点燃、心被一头愤怒的海豹击碎的风险。但我们依然如此行事，因为正是这一点使我们成为人——而一旦失去它，我们便失去一切。

Animism – or at least claiming a worldview in which we see ourselves as integrated into the more-than-human world rather than lording over it – does not offer safety. If anything, it does the opposite. To be in relationship with the more-than-human world, to take seriously our connections beyond the strictly human, is to become more exposed and also more attached. It puts us, as the selkie stories confirm, directly in the path of more violence. We kill a whale and kill our grandmother. We risk having our house set alight and our heart broken by an angry seal. But we do it because it is what makes us human – and if we lose that, we lose everything.

我们能否从海豹人亲缘关系的种种纠葛中学到什么，系于一个显而易见的问题：当真有人相信自己身上有一半海豹血统吗？直到20世纪，爱尔兰海滨乡村社群如何看待他们自己的世界，如今已难以言明，尤其是在他们已经历数百年文化与语言抹除的情况下。然而，正如施维恩·斯科特所指出的：「这些材料写得清清楚楚：『她母亲是海豹人』，或者『她祖母是海豹人』。我们凭什么假定当时的人并不真的相信？」

W hether we can learn from the fraught relationships of selkie kinship rests on an obvious question: did anyone believe they were part-seal? It’s difficult to say what rural seaside communities in Ireland thought about their world up until the 20th century, particularly when they had already undergone centuries of cultural and linguistic erasure. Yet, as Schwien Scott notes: ‘The material very clearly says: “Her mother was a selkie,” or “Her grandmother was a selkie.” Why would we assume that wasn’t actually believed?’

1938年，一位来自戈尔韦（Galway）的女佣玛丽亚·尼·舒莱博因（Máire Ní Shúilleabháin）告诉女儿，她认识一些岛民，他们是_海洋少女_（_mhaighdean mhara_）的孩子。_海洋少女_是相当常见的现实，她的叔祖就亲眼见过一位；就在前一年，康尼马拉（Connemara）的一次目击引来了悬赏捉拿这位海洋女子的告示，赏金高达4,000英镑（她逃脱了）。科克（Cork）一位学龄采集员诺拉·奥沙利文（Nora O'Sullivan）在笔记本里写道，她父亲给她讲过一个故事：一个男子娶了美人鱼，他们的女儿后来嫁进了沙利文家（Sullivans）——也就是她自家的家族。她的老师还写了张小字条，证实这个故事确有其事。1882年，一位多兰女士（Ms Dolan）声称，她亲眼认识奥韦尔湖（Lough Owel）一位名叫马洪（Mahon）的男子与其湖中生物新娘所生的三个孩子。「她是个非常出色的主妇，那些湖里人个个如此，」多兰赞许地写道。「她把他的家打理得漂漂亮亮。」1937年，米歇尔·麦克·科纳吉尔（Micheál Mac Conagail）讲述说，他认识一位_海洋少女_，她的儿子后来成了神父。此类证言比比皆是。

One Galwegian domestic worker, Máire Ní Shúilleabháin, told her daughter in 1938 that she knew islanders who were children of _mhaighdean mhara_. The _mhaighdean mhara_ were a common-enough reality that her great-uncle had seen one; the year before, a sighting in Connemara led to a £4,000 bounty for the marine woman’s capture (she escaped). One school-age collector in Cork, Nora O’Sullivan, wrote in her notebook that her father told her a story of a man who married a mermaid whose daughter then married into the Sullivans – that is, her family. Her teacher wrote a small memo affirming the story. In 1882, one Ms Dolan claimed that she personally knew the three children of a Lough Owel man named Mahon and his lake-creature bride. ‘She was a very good housewife, as all those lake people are,’ Dolan noted approvingly. ‘She kept his house beautifully.’ In 1937, Micheál Mac Conagail recounted that he knew of a _mhaighdean mhara_ whose son became a priest. Such testimonies abound.

更有甚者，许多渔村社群基于人与海豹之间的血缘关系，形成了猎杀海豹的禁忌。其经济影响不可小觑。这些渔民不但不能捕猎海豹，还不得不咽下海豹作为捕捞竞争者的存在——在爱尔兰乡村赤贫的煎熬之中，这是沉重的一击。综合这一切，我们有理由推断：对海豹人的字面信仰曾广泛流传，并一直延续到在世者的记忆之中。而这种信仰有着真切的现实后果——没有一样是轻松的。

What is more, many fishing communities had taboos on killing seals based on blood relations between humans and seals. The economic implications of this were significant. Not only were these fishermen unable to harvest seals, they also had to swallow the seals’ presence as fishing competition – a huge blow amid grinding rural poverty in Ireland. Given all this, it’s reasonable to adduce a widespread literal belief in selkies that has lasted through living memory. And this belief had real-world implications – none of which were easy.

肖恩·奥·穆尔切蒂（Seán Ó Muircheartaigh）在他的故事里讲述道：燕麦收获时节来临，农夫着手清理阁楼——他与海豹妻子以及半海豹人的孩子们就住在那栋屋子里。一时大意，他把妻子的海豹皮斗篷扔了下来。她立刻一把抓住，把它悄悄藏了起来。

S eán Ó Muircheartaigh recounted in his story that, when the oat harvest came, the farmer set about clearing out the loft of the house he lived in with his seal wife and their half-selkie children. In a moment of carelessness, he threw down his wife’s sealskin cloak. She grabbed it immediately and secreted it out of sight.

当天夜里，她等丈夫出门消磨夜晚，随后她为孩子们沐浴、梳理头发，一如她自己被掳走的那晚梳理自己的头发那样。然后，她拿起斗篷，一去不返。

That night, she waited for her husband to step out for the evening, then she washed her children and brushed their hair, just as she had brushed her own the night she was kidnapped. And then she took her cloak and was gone.

在这个本已令人心碎的故事里，最摧折人心的面向之一在于：它讲的是一个抛弃孩子的女人。偶尔，海豹人离去时会留下一句祝福或一个诅咒，但多数时候，她走得迅速而无声。她几乎从不带孩子走：而在奥多德家（O'Dowds）的故事里，她把自己的骨肉变成了石头。她很少道别。在一小部分海豹人故事中，这位变形的母亲即便回到大海之后，仍会继续探望她陆地上的家人；在其中一个故事里，她回来为孩子梳头。但最常见的情形是，她就那样消失了。

One of the most devastating aspects of an already devastating story is that it is about a woman who abandons her children. Every so often, the selkie departs with a blessing, or a curse, but mostly she goes quickly and in silence. She almost never takes her children: and in the case of the O’Dowds, she turns her progeny into stone. She rarely says goodbye. In a small subset of selkie stories, the shapeshifting mother continues to visit her terrestrial family even after she has returned to the sea, in one, returning to comb her children’s hair. But most often she just disappears.

对海豹人自己而言，丧失是双重的。她的人类丈夫似乎对新娘的前尘往事漠不关心，讲故事的人却偶尔让我们窥见一斑。伊夫琳·尼·康丘博尔（Eibhlín Ní Chonchubhair）讲过一个海豹人的故事：她在重返大海之前驻足，向人类丈夫道别。丈夫茫然失措，问孩子们没了她可怎么办。她的回答辛辣：「我有比这些更亲爱的孩子。」在这个故事里，一如在其他故事中，她在水中受到海豹丈夫的迎接，后者喜极而呼，将她拥入怀中。海豹人奔回大海的背后，是一道可怕的算术：回到一个家庭，便意味着失去另一个。绑架与虐待令局面愈发复杂，而孩子们既是无辜者，也是强制婚姻的副产品。

For the selkie herself, the loss is doubled. While her human husband appears incurious about his bride’s former life, storytellers offer occasional glimpses. Eibhlín Ní Chonchubhair tells of a selkie who pauses before her marine return to bid farewell to her human husband. Bewildered, he asks what the children will do without her. Her reply is acid: ‘I have children who are dearer to me than these.’ In this story, as in others, she is met in the water by her selkie husband, who embraces her with cries of joy. Behind the selkie’s flight back to sea is a terrible calculus, where a return to one family means the loss of the other. The situation is complicated by kidnapping and abuse, with children who are innocents and also byproducts of forced marriage.

海豹人的故事，是一份穿越那些无法修复之关系的路线图

The selkie story is a roadmap for navigating relationships that cannot be repaired

家族萦绕着我们。民俗学家博·阿尔姆奎斯特（Bo Almqvist）在其[论文](https://www.jstor.org/stable/20522356)《美人鱼与婚姻》（Of Mermaids and Marriages，1990）中指出，他和诗人谢默斯·希尼（Seamus Heaney）都曾把海豹人故事视为父母自杀的对应物——那种选择，在被留下的人看来往往无从索解：也就是说，一个女人为孩子们梳理好头发，随后走进大海，消失不见。在许多方面，海豹人故事是一则鬼故事，只不过故事里的当事人碰巧还活着。

Family haunts us. In his [paper](https://www.jstor.org/stable/20522356) ‘Of Mermaids and Marriages’ (1990), the folklorist Bo Almqvist notes that both he and the poet Seamus Heaney have considered the selkie story as a parallel for parental suicide – a choice that is often incomprehensible to those left behind: to wit, a woman brushes her children’s hair then walks into the sea and vanishes. The selkie story is in many ways a ghost story whose participants just happen to still be alive.

我曾参照朱迪斯·巴特勒的说法提出：处于关系之中，便是把自己置于丧失的风险之下。但海豹人故事在[复杂性哀伤](https://aeon.co/ideas/its-complicated-why-some-grief-takes-much-longer-to-heal)的灌木丛间穿行漫游时，又更进了一步：它探索的是，与一个已然离去、又曾给你带来巨大痛苦的所爱之人_依然_保持关系，是怎样一种体验。在某些方面，海豹人故事是一份穿越无法修复之关系的路线图——在那里，没有和解的希望，也没有和解的欲求。首先，海豹人故事承认：我们并不总能选择自己与谁共处关系之中——而且，有时候，一段关系中最健康的举动，恰恰是打破它。

I’ve suggested, pace Judith Butler, that to be in relationship is to place ourselves at risk of loss. But the selkie story, in its peregrination through the undergrowth of [complicated grief](https://aeon.co/ideas/its-complicated-why-some-grief-takes-much-longer-to-heal), takes things one step further: it explores what it is like to _remain_ in relationship with a loved one who is gone and who has caused you immense pain. In some ways, the selkie story is a roadmap for navigating relationships that cannot be repaired, where there is no hope or desire for reconciliation. For one thing, selkie stories acknowledge that we don’t always get to choose who we’re in relationship with – and that, sometimes, the healthiest move within a relationship is to break it.

当我们面对气候紧急状态所伴随的不可避免的物种消亡，当我们掂量这颗星球上那些地方——在那里，最伟大的爱的举动，也许正是决定放弃人类自身的侵入——海豹人故事提供了一种范式，用以处理那些以缺席为底色的有意义的关系。丧失与萦绕经久不散：在爱尔兰，许多个人与社群在一场跨物种婚姻过去数代之后，仍视自己与海豹纠缠相连。海豹人故事承认那些关系的真实——关系中一方已骤然退出对话，以及在那场丧失之前种种的残忍与爱。它确认：你可以爱你的孩子并离开他们，也可以爱你的孩子并把孩子们变成石头。这些真相可以并肩而立，清一清喉咙，不安地低头望向地板。爱是杂乱的，而且并不总是良善的。

As we navigate the inevitable species loss attendant on the climate emergency, reckoning with those places on the globe where the greatest act of love may be to decide against human intrusion, the selkie story offers a model for processing meaningful relationships that are defined by absence. The losses and hauntings endure: many individuals and communities in Ireland continued to see themselves as entangled with seals even generations after an interspecies marriage. The selkie story acknowledges as real those relationships where one half of the duo has abruptly left the conversation, as well as the cruelty and love that led up to the loss. It affirms that you can love your children and leave them, or love your children and turn them into stone. These truths can rub shoulders with each other, clear their throats, look down uncomfortably at the floor. Love is messy and not always good.

海豹人叙事之所以有力，在于它正视事情出了差错之后的余波，并设定了这样一个世界：在那里，暴力并不意味着关爱的终结。它经营的是艰难的真相。然而，它同时也是一个允许人们眺望大海、看见表亲与手足、失去的父母与收获的家人的故事。它允许一个人望着一只海豹，感到亲缘。去想：她在那儿。那边游过去的，是我的家人。

The selkie narrative is powerful because it considers the aftermath of things going wrong and posits a world in which violence doesn’t mean an end to care. It deals in difficult truths. Yet it is also a story that allows people to look out to sea and see cousins and siblings, parents lost and family gained. It allows a human to look at a seal and feel kinship. To think: there she is. There goes my family.
