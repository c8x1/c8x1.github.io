每个星期四，同一个问题

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年11月4日·3分钟阅读

「月底就是公证处那趟。」电话里建安的声音很平。「房子的字要她签，你留了三年的同一个问题，也一起问了吧。一个下午的事，姐。」
「她上星期四还纠正我，说我买错了酱油。」
「她上个月管我叫二姨夫。你再挑日子，往后问谁去？」
「爸走那晚，最后半个小时，病房里只有她一个人。那句话，天下就她一个人听全了。」
「那就别挑了。」他停了停。「你那份广州的调令，再拖下去就要换人上。你自己算。」

"The notary appointment is at the end of the month." Jian'an's voice on the phone was flat. "The papers need her signature, and while you're there, ask that same question you've been keeping for three years. It's one afternoon, Sis."
"Last Thursday she corrected me for buying the wrong soy sauce."
"Last month she called me her brother-in-law. Keep picking your day, and who is left to ask?"
"The night Dad died, she was alone in that room for the last half hour. She's the only person alive who heard all of it."
"Then stop picking." He paused. "And that transfer of yours — drag it out again and Guangzhou will put someone else in the post. Do your own math."

那个星期四，粥坐在炉上，收音机咿咿呀呀地唱。她先开的口：「院里的梅树打苞没有？」
「打了。」她点点头，隔了好一阵才接：「你爸走那年，也打苞。」
我把话送到嘴边：「妈，那年冬天，医院走廊尽头，你进去跟爸……」
「粥要凉了。」她起身往厨房去，「你脸色不好，厂里又熬夜了？」
我扶着桌沿，没有追上去。话原样收回口袋，我还替她找了个理由：她是怕提爸。下个星期四再说。

That Thursday the porridge sat on the stove and the radio mumbled through an opera. She spoke first: "Has the plum tree in the yard budded yet?"
"It has." She nodded, and only after a good while added, "It budded the year your father died, too."
I brought the words up to my lips. "Ma, that winter, at the end of the hospital corridor, when you went in to Dad — "
"The porridge will go cold." She rose for the kitchen. "You look pale. Night shifts again at the factory?"
I stood there holding the edge of the table and did not follow. The question went back into my pocket whole, and I found her an excuse: she cannot bear to speak of Dad. Next Thursday, then.

入了秋，我往她那儿带的东西多起来：相片，爸的旧手绢。每一样她都伸手接，接过去就往回走：相片走到一九五八年，手绢走到一间她从没对我说起过的屋子。走到哪一站，她就跟哪一站的人说话。
「妈，这个是我。」我指着相片上并排的两个人。
「这是我跟我姐。」她笑了，「你长得像她。」
公证那天，我把纸摊开在桌上。她高高兴兴拿笔，一气签得很顺，落下的却是二姨的闺名。搁了笔她问：「这上头的门牌，是咱们新搬的家吗？」
那张纸，后来没有成。

Once autumn set in, I began carrying more things over to her: the photograph, Dad's old handkerchief. She took each one in her hands, and each one walked her back down the road — the photograph to 1958, the handkerchief to a room she had never once mentioned to me. Whichever station she reached, she talked with the people of that station.
"Ma, this one is me." I pointed at the two figures standing side by side in the photograph.
"That's me and my elder sister." She smiled. "You do look like her."
On the notary day I spread the papers out on the table. She took up the pen, happy to do it, and signed in one smooth stroke — her sister's girlhood name. Setting the pen down, she asked, "Is this house number our new home?"
Those papers never went through.

电话里，我跟建安讲梅树，讲粥。
「姐，」他说，「对我来说，她是分家那年冬天走的。打那以后，她没有一句话到过我这里。你讲的这些星期四，都是你的。」
「她还在。」
「在你的星期四里在。」他顿了顿。「房子的事，纸没签成，往后就一家一半，别再领她去折腾了。」
年底，广州的名单下来，上头没有我。我在传达室把回执折了折，塞进抽屉，下午照旧去坐星期四那趟车。

On the phone I told Jian'an about the plum tree, about the porridge.
"Sis," he said, "for me she left the winter we split the family. Not one word of hers has reached me since that year. These Thursdays you keep telling me about — they're yours."
"She's still here."
"Still here in your Thursdays." He paused. "As for the house, the papers died, so from now on it's half and half — stop dragging her through any more of it."
At year's end the Guangzhou roster came out, and my name was not on it. In the mail room I folded the receipt, pushed it into a drawer, and that afternoon took the Thursday bus as usual.

往后的一个星期四，我终于把话问出了口：「妈，那年冬天，医院走廊尽头那半个小时，你跟爸说了什么？」
她看着我，看了很久，久到收音机换了一折戏。然后她拍了拍我的手背：「立冬还没到，你就穿这么薄。粥在炉上，喝一碗再走。」
答得又软又顺，只是落在了别的话上。回程的车上我才明白过来：我等的那个知会，天上地下都没有人发。她不会摔一跤，也不会闭一夜眼，好让所有人指认说，就是这一天。她站的那个地方，往前去的路一条一条都朝里开。我问得再响，也只是送她往里再走一程。
字签不成了，那句话也等不来了。可她那边好好的：粥在炉上，戏还唱着。

On a Thursday after that, I finally asked it out loud: "Ma, that winter, that half hour at the end of the hospital corridor — what did you say to Dad?"
She looked at me for a long time, long enough for the radio to move on to another scene. Then she patted the back of my hand. "Winter hasn't even come and you're dressed this thin. The porridge is on the stove — have a bowl before you go."
The answer came soft and smooth; it just landed on different words. Only on the ride home did it come clear to me: the notice I kept waiting for, no one under heaven would ever issue it. There would be no fall, no long night for everyone to point to and call the one. Where she stands, every road that runs forward opens inward. However loudly I ask, I only walk her one more stretch along.
The signature was gone, and so was that sentence. Yet on her side all was well: the porridge on the stove, the opera still going.

最后那年，她的话来得一年比一年迟：我正月里问的冷暖，她立夏才答，答话的腔调还是她四十岁那年的腔调。桂嫂在门口对我说：「她昨天还认得我，喊我的名字呢。」我点了点头。屋里炉子照旧，粥照旧，收音机还是那个台，没有人把她拽进任何地方去。开春的一个早晨，她睡了过去，脸朝着窗，粥还没坐上炉子。
料理完后事，建安回来住了两晚。夜里他问：「她最后，还说话吗？」
「说，」我说，「说到最后一个星期。」
收拾五斗柜，我在针线笸箩底下摸到一张纸。是她的字，没有日子，只一行：「跟他说，粥温在炉上。」我说不上这个「他」是谁，也说不上是哪一年写的；可能留给爸，可能写给家里随便哪一个人，已经没有人能问了。
我把纸折好，收进大衣内袋。往后每个星期四，我还是坐那趟车，在梅树底下站一会儿，把一个星期的事说给她听。她那一头会有什么答话，我不等了。

In that last year her words arrived later and later: a question about the cold that I asked at New Year got its answer at the start of summer, still in the voice of her forty-year-old self. Gui-sao said to me at the door, "Yesterday she still knew me — called me by my name." I nodded. Inside, the stove was the same, the porridge was the same, the radio still on the same station; no one had dragged her anywhere. One morning in early spring she slept on past waking, her face toward the window, the porridge not yet set on the stove.
After the funeral was seen to, Jian'an came back and stayed two nights. In the dark he asked, "At the end — was she still talking?"
"She was," I said. "Into her last week."
Clearing the dresser, I felt a sheet of paper under the sewing basket. Her handwriting, no date, one line only: "Tell him the porridge is kept warm on the stove." I cannot say who that "him" was, or in which year it was written; it might have been left for Dad, it might have been for anyone in the family. There is no one left to ask.
I folded the sheet and put it away inside my coat. Every Thursday after that, I still took that bus, stood a while under the plum tree, and told her the news of my week. Whatever answer might come from her side, I stopped waiting for it.
