外圈

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年11月15日·3分钟阅读

前年秋，我挑着锔匠担子进石埭镇。这镇上行当各有一条谱：谁家师父传谁家徒弟，铺面、脚力、主顾，都按谱分派。我的手艺是跟师父在路上学的，师父自己是流徙的人，没在哪个镇的谱上落过名。我在正街口支起炉子，第三天，谱差把炉子抬进了廊下——正街生火，要谱内人作保。我挨家去问。笔墨铺、皮匠摊、脚店，问到第三家就不再问了，话都一样：「保你这事我担不起。保你一个，我谱上就得挪一处，挪谁家的都不好。」三天期满，没人保我。我把担子挑齐，推着炉子出了北门。镇上没有人对我说过一个重字。

In the autumn of the year before last, I carried my mender's pole into Shidai Town. Every trade here keeps a register of lineage: which master passed the craft to which apprentice, and shops, porters and customers are all portioned out along it. My craft I learned on the road from a master who was himself a drifting man, never entered in any town's register. On my third day at the mouth of the main street, the register clerks carried my furnace into the corridor — a fire on the main street wants a bondman from inside the register. I went door to door. The stationer, the cobbler's stall, the inn; by the third I stopped asking, for the words were all one: "Standing bond for you is beyond me. Bond for one outside, and something of mine must move in the register, and whichever house moves will not take it quietly." When the three days ran out, no one had stood bond. I gathered my load and pushed my furnace out the north gate. No one in the town had spoken a harsh word to me.

北门外有一片乱砖场，早年烧砖起坯的地方，窑早废了。谱外的手艺人都落在那里。我到的时候，修伞的佟家已经在了，纸扎匠两口子在西边占了半面坡，弹棉花的占着窑口背风处。同是谱外人，彼此并不因此多一分亲近——头一个月，我担子放的位子偏了半丈，佟家当家的就和我红过一回脸。各人是各人的营生，各揣各的手艺，谁也没想过要合在一起。

Beyond the north gate lay a field of broken brick, an old brick-kiln yard where the kiln had gone cold years since. Craftsmen outside the register all came to rest there. When I arrived, the Tong umbrella-mender was already in place; the paper-effigy couple held half the west slope; the cotton-fluffer kept the sheltered side of the old kiln mouth. Being fellow outsiders made no warmth between us — my first month, I set my stall half a rod too far over, and the eldest Tong and I had sharp words over it. Each kept his own trade, each hoarded his own craft, and no one thought of joining anything.

头一年冬天，我们各摆各的。乱砖场宽，我的摊子在东头，佟家在西头，中间隔着两车碎砖。难处出在「口子」上：每家的摊子都朝着镇子开一个口，摊面朝北门，过路人才看得见。口子一多，谱差挨着口子查过来，一天能查到三回：查炉照，问手艺来历。各家各自应付各自的日子。开春后，摊位不知不觉挪了位。先是弹棉花的窑口塌了半边，他把摊子挪到纸扎匠的坡下；接着我把担子往佟家那边挪了半里——那年秋粮紧，盘查更密，我看明白一件事：挨着摆的摊子，谱差一圈只过一遍；散着摆的，一家挨一家还是三回。没人召集，也没人商量。摆得拢的口子少，查得省，日子长；散着摆的，一个月一个月把摊子挪了进来。到第二年入夏，乱砖场上朝镇子的口子，只剩朝北门的一排。

That first winter we all set out separately. The brickfield was wide; my stall stood at the east end, the Tongs at the west, two cartloads of broken brick between. The trouble was in the mouths: every stall opened one toward the town, the stall face turned to the north gate so that passersby could see it. With mouths everywhere, the register clerks walked them one by one and could reach a stall three times a day, checking the fire license, asking after the craft's history. Each of us swallowed his own days. After New Year, the stalls drifted without anyone saying so. The cotton-fluffer's kiln mouth caved in at the side, and he moved below the paper-effigy couple's slope; then I shifted my load half a li toward the Tongs — grain had tightened that autumn, the searches thickened, and I saw one thing plain: stalls pressed together got a single round of the clerk a day; a stall set apart still got its three. No one called a gathering, and no one negotiated. Stalls pressed together kept fewer mouths, cheaper searches, longer days; the scattered ones, month by month, shifted in. By the second summer, the mouths the brickfield opened toward the town had shrunk to one row, facing the north gate alone.

如今乱砖场有了形状。朝着北门的外圈，修起了门面：佟家的伞铺先起，纸扎匠两口子挨着起，弹棉花的花仓再挨过去，门脸朝着街，做镇上的生意——谱查到门面上，规规矩矩，一户一照。门面背后围出一个场院，各家的后窗都开向院里。八月节，纸扎匠在场院里扎起一挂走马灯，弹棉花的家蒸了糖糕挨家送，我搬个小炉子在灯下锔一只白天送来的碗。院里说的话，街上听不见。镇里也有走向另一头的：箍桶的谭家小子，前年被缪木匠收作徒弟，入了谱，如今在正街开铺面，见了我们点头，我们叫他一声小谭师傅。

The brickfield has a shape now. Along its outer ring, facing the north gate, shopfronts have gone up: the Tong umbrella shop first, the paper-effigy couple beside it, the cotton warehouse next along, faces turned to the street and doing the town's trade — when the register reaches a shopfront, all is in order, one license to a door. Behind the fronts the stalls enclose a yard, and every house opens its back window onto it. At the mid-autumn festival the paper-effigy couple raised a trotting-horse lantern in the yard, the cotton-fluffer's household steamed sugar cakes and carried them door to door, and I set my little furnace under the lantern and mended a bowl brought in that day. What is said in the yard, the street does not hear. One boy went the other way: the hoop-maker's son, Tan, taken the year before as apprentice by the carpenter Miao, entered in the register, shopfront on the main street now; he nods when he passes us, and we call him little Master Tan.

我如今守着外圈东头第二间门面。白天对着街，炉火一起，锔钉叮叮地响，过路的把破碗缺锅送进来；天黑上了排门，转身进院，各家窗纸透着灯。有时我坐在炉边，想起头一年正街那三天：廊下没有我的炉子，也没有一个肯挪谱的人。挪谱的难处，我如今懂了——佟家作保收过一回学徒，画押那天，他谱上三家的名次重排了一遍，谁上谁下，前后吵了一个月。腾位置给人，就要动人家的名次；这镇子护得住的，从来只是排得下的人。护不住的，就由得我们自己在北门外长成一片。

I keep the second shopfront from the east end of the outer ring now. Days, I face the street; the fire lit, the mending rivets ring, and passersby bring cracked bowls and holed pots in. At dark I board the door, turn into the yard, and every house's window shows paper-light. Sometimes, sitting by the fire, I think of those three days at the mouth of the main street: no furnace of mine in the corridor, and no one who would move in the register. The difficulty of moving I understand now — the Tong house stood bond once for an apprentice, and on the day the paper was signed, three houses of his line had their places re-ranked; the quarrel over who stood above whom ran a month. To make room for a man is to move other men's places; what this town can shelter has only ever been the men who fit the ranking. The rest it could not hold, and it let us grow into one patch beyond the north gate.

注解：这镇子待我们，正像水待化不开的东西——没有恨，只有护不住。谱要维持自己的秩序，腾位置就得动人家的名次，位置便从来排不下我们，正如水护它的氢键网络，容不下处不来的分子。我们在北门外聚成一片，起头不为彼此投缘——是摊子拢起来朝着镇子的口子最少，各自省下的盘查最多，散摆的那些日子一个月一个月被磨掉了。这就是疏水效应（the hydrophobic effect）：被排除的彼此靠拢，把接触的口子缩到最小；外圈一层做买卖、里头过日子，正像双分子层的形状。至于化得开的，像谭家小子那样搭上谱的，镇子自会把他接进去。

Note: The town held us the way water holds what it cannot take in — no hatred in it, only no room. The register must keep its own order; making room means moving someone's place, and so no place was ever going to be ours, just as water keeps its web of bonds and cannot hold molecules that will not fit it. We grew into one patch beyond the gate not for any love between us — but because stalls pressed together opened the fewest mouths toward the town and saved each of us the most searching, and the scattered days were ground away month by month. This is the hydrophobic effect: what is excluded draws together and shrinks its face of contact to the smallest; a layer facing outward to trade and a life kept within — the very shape of a double layer. And what the town can take in, like the Tan boy who caught onto the register, it takes in.
