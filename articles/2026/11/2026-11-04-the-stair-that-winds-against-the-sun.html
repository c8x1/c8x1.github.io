逆着日头拧的梯

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年11月4日·3分钟阅读

关口上那座塔还在，旋梯还逆着日头拧。赶脚人路过，总爱伸手摸一把柱子，笑逄家的石匠把梯子拧反了：天底下的塔，梯子都顺着日头拧，独这一座反着来。塔里的人从不辩。塔里生的孩子开蒙，先学左手使刀；柱身上的旧疤一道一道留着，漆也不盖。外乡人问急了，塔主只回一句：疤，是给右手的孩子们看的。

The tower at the pass still stands, and its stair still winds against the sun. Mule drivers passing by like to lay a hand on the newel and laugh that the Pangs let their mason wind the stair backwards: every tower under heaven winds its stair with the sun, and this one alone runs the other way. The people of the tower never argue. Children born inside it are first taught the blade in the left hand; the old scars on the newel are left as they are, unvarnished. When a stranger pressed too hard, the tower master gave one answer: the scars are there for the right-handed children to see.

柱上的疤是那年冬天添的。夜里摸进来五个外路人。攀塔是他们的营生，做了半辈子，攀过九座，九座的梯子都顺着日头拧，肩膀上记住的全是柱子贴右肩。进这座塔，肩头先觉出不对：柱子换了肩，练熟的步子乱了半拍。混战里，塔主把刀交在左手，贴外墙那一面扫得开，一个人堵住了梯口；守在下面三级的小郎一慌，起的是右手，刀口磕在柱上，小臂挨了一刀，他把刀换回左手，才算站住那一级。天亮时，外路人拖着两个伤号走了，塔没破。老蒯后来听人讲起这一夜，只问了一句：小郎的伤，在右手吧？

The scars on the post came that winter. Five outsiders slipped in at night. Climbing towers was their trade, half a lifetime of it; they had taken nine, and all nine stairs wound with the sun, so their shoulders remembered the post riding the right shoulder. Entering this tower, the shoulder knew first: the post had changed shoulders, and their drilled steps fell half a beat wrong. In the melee the tower master carried his blade in the left hand, and along the outer wall it swung clear; alone he held the stair-head. Three steps below, the young master panicked and led with his right; the blade rang off the post, he took a cut on the forearm, and only when he passed the blade back to his left hand did he hold his step. At dawn the outsiders dragged off two wounded men, and the tower stood. When word of that night reached old Kuai, he asked one thing: the boy's wound, was it on the right arm?

往回说。定拧向那天的事，塔里老人还讲得出。老蒯把烟锅搁在案上，只问：正拧，反拧？逄塔主那年三十一，左手刀，小时候家里请武师掰过三年，没掰过来。他爹老逄死在自家梯上：那夜外墙上来了人，老逄退到梯口，堵着往下迎；正拧的梯，迎下去的人左臂贴柱，他的刀偏偏在左手，施展不开，人折在第七级。这笔账他在心里过了半晌，末了交代两件事：梯子反拧；守夜的人从明早起，先练左手刀。老蒯没劝，只问：那小郎呢？塔主说：他八岁，两把手都还嫩。

Go back a way. The day the winding was chosen, the tower's old people can still tell it. Kuai set his pipe on the table and asked one question: with the sun, or against it? The tower master was thirty-one that year, a left-handed blade; as a boy his family had hired a drill master for three years to bend him right, and it never took. His father died on his own stair: men came over the outer wall that night, the old man fell back to the stair-head to hold it from above, and on a stair wound with the sun a man facing down carries his left arm along the post; the old man's blade was in his left hand, it could not come clear, and he fell on the seventh step. The master turned that account over for half the morning, and in the end gave two orders: the stair would wind against the sun, and from tomorrow the watch would drill the left hand first. Kuai did not argue; he asked only, and the boy? The master said: he is eight. Both his hands are still soft.

再往回，是那天上午。案上摆着两只木模子，一拃高，一只顺着日头拧，一只逆着。小徒拿着比对了一晌午，横着转，竖着立，翻过来，倒过去，两个拧劲始终顶着，怎么摆也摆不到一道去。案头一盆清水，把顺的那只搁上去，水里现出来的正是逆的那只。水里这条捞不出来；真要它现身，得照着逆模子另打一只，两只本是一块料、同一副刨子出来的，谁也不多一分木脚。末了老蒯领着人去旧塔走梯，一人一根木棍，从梯顶往下迎：小徒的右手甩在外头，塔主的左手一路蹭着柱皮。同一条梯，梯子没换，是手换了。小徒嘀咕：这梯子匀净，四十级一块样板，怎么就偏了一边？老蒯说：匀是匀，拧劲在。

Further back still, that same morning. Two wooden models stood on the table, a hand-span tall, one wound with the sun, one against it. The apprentice matched them against each other the whole noon, turning them sideways, standing them upright, flipping them over, standing them on their heads; the two twists stayed opposed, and no arrangement ever laid them into one. A basin of still water sat at the table's edge; hold the sunwise one over it, and the stair that shows in the water is the other one, exactly. That one cannot be lifted out of the water; to make it appear you must carve it after the opposite model, and the two came from one block and one plane, neither carrying a shaving more of wood. At the end Kuai walked them up the old tower, each carrying a stick, coming down from the top: the apprentice's right arm swung free along the outer wall, and the master's left hand scraped the post the whole way. One stair; the stair did not change, the hand did. The apprentice muttered: this stair is even, forty steps off one template; how does it lean to one side? Kuai said: even is even. The twist is in it.

最早的事在石场。关口要起一座新塔，塔基是一整块生岩，圆的，塔身便也圆，里头的井筒窄得只容一架梯。老蒯先提的是直梯：直上直下，哪只手都不偏，连它映在水里的影子，都跟它自己一条一线。可直梯要一段直的空当，圆井筒里腾不出。梯只能旋着上；旋，就得先定拧向。老蒯在抹平的沙地上绷线，弹下第一道弧。弧一落地，这条梯偏哪只手就定了；往后四十级，只是把这道弧一层一层抬上去。

The first of it was in the stone yard. The pass wanted a new tower; the footing was a single shelf of raw rock, round, so the tower would be round, and the well inside it narrow enough to hold one stair only. Kuai's first proposal was a straight flight: straight up and down, leaning toward no hand, and even its shape in still water lies in a line with the thing itself. But a straight flight needs a straight run of air, and a round well cannot spare it. The stair had to climb turning, and to turn is to choose the twist. On the levelled sand Kuai strung his line and snapped the first arc. Once that arc was struck, the hand this stair would lean to was settled; the forty steps after were only that arc lifted course by course.

注解：这个故事讲的是手性（chirality）。顺着日头拧与逆着日头拧的两条旋梯互为对映体（enantiomorph），像左手与右手：无论怎样转、怎样翻，两条梯子永远合不拢，而在石料、工价这些寻常处毫无分别；只有当另一只同样偏着一边的手或刀与梯相遇，分别才显出来。边界：故事里偏哪只手是刀口上的输赢，手性本身只问一物与它的镜像能否重合；同一套规矩也长在分子和光上，那是这条梯没有走到的地方。

Note: This story is about chirality. The stair wound with the sun and the stair wound against it are enantiomorphs, like a left hand and a right: no turning or flipping will ever lay one on the other, and the two are alike in stone and price; the difference shows itself only when another thing that leans one way, a hand or a blade, meets the stair. The boundary: in the story, which hand a stair favors is settled on the edge of a blade, while chirality itself only asks whether a thing can be brought to coincide with its mirror image; the same rule also governs molecules and light, ground this staircase never reached.
