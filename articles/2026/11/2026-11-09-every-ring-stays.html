寓言故事

每一声都留下来

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年11月9日·2分钟阅读

秋窑的瓷器从窑场装着稻草上来，我的长凳摆在两座棚中间的空场上。我把每一件取出来，用竹劈敲它的沿，听：声音清的归左棚，发哑的归右棚。这规矩在棚里比我待得久，没有人问过我的意思。我的手比我的念头先动——敲一下，听一声，归哪边由那一声说了算。到晌午，一笼空了，两座棚又离得远了一点。

The autumn firing came up from the kiln yard in crates packed with straw, and my bench sat in the open ground between the two sheds. I took out each piece, struck its rim with a slip of bamboo, and listened: the clear note went to the left shed, the dull one to the right. That rule had been in the shed longer than I had, and nobody had ever asked my opinion of it. My hands moved before any thought of mine — one strike, one listening, and the note itself said which shed. By noon a crate would be empty, and the two sheds would have moved a little farther apart.

混着卖的笼，价钱看买家那天的心情，因为一条藏着的暗缝，能把一整堂的名声在南边卖坏。分开了，声音正的成堂成堂走春季的价，哑的归了做耍货的行当。有人来看，看完说：一份价钱长成两份，中间不过坐着一只耳朵；又说，东家做的是一门不出本钱的生意。东家把话说得很平：「赶在末班船走前清完整窑。河一封，就封到开春。你的一份，买得起回乡的船票，还余一年的嚼谷。」

A mixed crate sold for whatever a buyer felt like that morning, since one hidden crack could sour a whole set's name in the south. Separated, the ring-true sets fetched the spring price downriver, and the dull pieces went to the toy trade. People came to watch, and walked away saying that one price had become two with nothing in the middle but an ear, and that the master had found a trade with no cost in it. The master put it plainly: "Finish the whole firing before the last boat goes. Once the river closes, it closes till spring. Your share buys the spring passage home, with a year's keep over."

看的人看不见的是「留」。每一声都留了下来。清的和哑的，到今秋整整三个秋天，几万次敲击——每个音都躺在它落进我脑袋的地方，凡是真听进耳朵的，醒着睡着，谁也没能从我这里收走。东家的银子真正买的正是这个。敲与听不花什么，留也不曾花过什么；我那时以为，天下的便宜都聚在那张长凳上了。只是近来，坛满了。新来的日子进不来，像船去挤一处已经排死的埠头。有天傍晚，我放下竹劈，想不起自己为什么站起来。

What the watchers could not see was the keeping. Every ring stays. The clear ones and the dull ones, three whole autumns of them, tens of thousands of strikes — each note lay where it had landed in my head, and nothing I had truly heard had ever been taken from me, awake or asleep. That was what the master's silver was really buying. Striking and listening cost nothing, and the keeping had never cost me anything either; I believed, that autumn, that all the cheapness in the world was gathered on that bench. Lately, though, the jar was full. New days arrived and found no room, like boats nosing up to a dock already rafted solid. One evening I set down the bamboo and could not remember why I had stood up.

场上还压着两百笼，末班船只剩十天，夜里我认不出自己的巷子了：站在一面墙前，要把墙上的裂缝看上两遍，才知道哪一处是我的住处。这些东家一概不知。我一停手，整窑混着发走，棚吃下这一季，我的那份——船票，一年的嚼谷——跟着一起沉；我一上手，就得有什么让出地方来。天黑透了我还坐在长凳上，两手摊在板面上，把岔路口看得很清楚：攥着旧年月，或者攥着船钱。

Two hundred crates still stood in the yard, the last boats had ten days in them, and at night I no longer knew my own lane: I stood before a wall and had to read its crack twice before I knew which lodging was mine. The master knew none of it. If I stopped, the firing shipped out mixed, the shed ate the season, and my share — the passage, the year's keep — went down with it; if I went on, something in me had to give up its place to the work. After dark I sat on the bench with my hands flat on the boards and saw the fork plainly: hold the old years, or hold the boat fare.

那天夜里，我不再守住自己的睡，欠账开始自己来收。睡眠原是把一天里的小事从人人身上收走的；那是一笔人人都缴、却从没见过票面的捐。我欠下了三个秋天，如今一齐到期，收账的一次全收，不肯一笔一笔来；一夜过后，脑袋里又装得下一天了。先是发大水的整个十二岁：厨房里的水，柿子树没顶，爹把猪背在背上。然后是我来时的那条路，一程一程地没了。敲与听没有出过错：十天，两百笼清空，两座棚站到了两份价钱能隔开的那样远。最后一夜，收账的取走了我最后一件囫囵的东西：娘的脸。两座棚得去的，我照数付清；那院子里没有一件东西是白来的。

That night I stopped defending my sleep, and the arrears began to collect themselves. Sleep takes the day's small things from everyone; that is a toll we all pay without ever seeing the bill. Three autumns of mine had gone unpaid, and now the collector came for everything at once and would not itemize; in the mornings after, there was room for a day again. The flood year went first, the whole of my twelfth year: the water in the kitchen, the persimmon tree going under, my father carrying the pig on his back. Then the road I had come here on, stage by stage. The striking and the listening never wavered: ten days, two hundred crates cleared, and the two sheds stood as far apart as two prices can stand. On the last night the collector took the last whole thing I had, my mother's face. What the two sheds had gained, I had paid for, to the coin; nothing in that yard had come free.

末班船赶在封河前走了。东家在岸边把工钱付进我两只手里，银子一枚一枚数清；他身后的两座棚，比任何时候都值钱。回家的这一程，是船老大单子上的一行，一个我还认得的字。最后一个河湾，村子的埠头上立着个老妇人，唤我的小名「阿盏」——小杯子那个盏。她的脸我不认得。这名字还有人认领，我便跟着她上了坡。路上她把我的小时候一件一件讲给我听，讲得很轻；我听着，像听一户远处人家的消息。那天晚饭前，我把碗翻过来，用指节敲了敲碗沿：一声正响。它留了下来。

The boats left before the river closed, and the master paid me on the bank, silver counted into my two hands, the sheds behind him worth more than they had ever been. My passage home was one line on the boatman's list, a word I could still read. At the last bend an old woman stood on the dock of my village and called out my childhood name, A-Zhan, the word for a small cup. I did not know her face. The name was still claimed by somebody, so I followed her up the bank. She told me about my childhood on the way, one thing after another, gently, and I listened as you listen to news of some far-off family. That evening, before eating, I turned my bowl over and struck its rim with my knuckle: a clear, true note. It stayed.

注解：麦克斯韦妖（Maxwell's demon）出自一八六七年的一个思想实验：一个存在逐一测量每个分子，快的放往一侧、慢的放往另一侧，看似白白造出了秩序与温差。测量可以不花力气，躲不开的是擦除记忆——把每一回测量留下的记认清空；这笔代价记进账里，整体的账目依然守恒——它造不出永动机，那个「妖」也非有意志的角色，只是按规则运行的机制。

Note: Maxwell's demon comes from a thought experiment of 1867: a being that measures each molecule one by one, letting the fast ones pass one way and the slow ones the other, apparently creating order and a temperature difference out of nothing. Measuring can be done without effort; what cannot be dodged is the erasure of memory, the clearing of the record each measurement leaves, and once that cost is entered the whole account stays balanced — so it builds no perpetual motion machine, and the demon is not a creature with a will, only a rule at work.
