这无关政治：德黑兰的年轻时尚达人们

Parisa Khoshnami·Aeon·2026年11月18日·9分钟阅读·原文

雅萨曼*（Yasaman）是伊兰沙赫尔街（Iranshahr）的常客之一，这条街是德黑兰（Tehran）新兴的时尚中心。大多数傍晚，她都会在人行道上支起小摊，出售自己画的漫画与动画角色画像。在社交媒体上，她展示着一套属于自己的漫画风造型：粉色头发、粉色唇妆，还有晕染到眼角之外的粉色眼影。她告诉粉丝，每次出门前，母亲都会说：「妆化淡一点。」「妈，讲点道理，」她答道，「我要去的是伊兰沙赫尔。这打扮在伊兰沙赫尔根本不算什么。我要去的可是巴黎时装周，你知道的，那儿人人都在走秀。」

Yasaman* is one of the regulars on Iranshahr, the new fashion hub of Tehran. Most evenings, she sets up a stall on the pavement and sells her drawings of manga and anime characters. On social media, she sports her own manga-inspired look: pink hair, pink mouth and smudgy pink eyeshadow extending beyond the corner of each eye. She tells her followers that, before she leaves her house, her mother says: ‘Put on less makeup.’ ‘Mum, be reasonable,’ she answers. ‘I’m going to Iranshahr. For Iranshahr this is nothing. I’m going to Paris Fashion Week. You know everyone there is doing a catwalk.’

伊兰沙赫尔的人行道上挤满了年轻人，他们穿着最时髦的行头招摇过市，拍摄其他年轻有型的人，发到自己的社交媒体上。时尚达人们就聚集在这里，泡在咖啡馆和画廊里，在人行道上往来交际。几乎看不到一块头巾。几年前的街道还全然不是这副模样，但自今年二月美国与以色列的袭击开始以来，伊斯兰共和国（Islamic Republic）一直在为生存而战：领导层大半被杀，霍尔木兹海峡（Strait of Hormuz）遭到围困，邻国中不少已与敌方合谋，于是「管人们穿什么」在它的优先事项清单上一路下滑，排到了非常靠后的位置。

Iranshahr’s sidewalks teem with young people parading the latest fashions and filming other young, stylish people for their social media feeds. This is where the fashionistas congregate, hanging out in cafés and galleries, and mingling on the sidewalks. Barely a headscarf is visible. The streets looked nothing like this a few years ago, but once the American-Israeli attacks began in February this year, the Islamic Republic has been fighting for its survival: with much of its leadership killed, the Strait of Hormuz under siege, and many of the country’s neighbours in league with the enemy, policing what people wear has slipped a long way down its list of priorities.

头巾法自1983年起便写入伊朗法律，由所谓的「风纪警察」（morality police）在全国执行。违反它意味着罚款、鞭刑或监禁。但后来，一次违抗改变了一切。2017年12月，三十多岁的女子维达·莫瓦赫德（Vida Movahed）爬上德黑兰恩格莱布街（Enghelab Street）的一个公用设施箱，摘下白色头巾，用木棍挑起举在空中。虽然她随即被捕，后来还被判一年监禁，但她的举动被证明极具感染力。其他女性也爬上同一条街上的设施箱和临时搭起的台座，把头巾在空中挥舞。人们开始称她们为「恩格莱布街的女孩们」（Girls of Enghelab Street）。她们同样被捕了。如今已经很难传达她们的举动需要多大的胆量。多年来，女性们一直悄悄把头巾往后挪，但在大白天、在车流之上、在警察和所有人一样能清楚看见她们裸露头顶的地方摘下头巾——自1979年和1980年的抗议以来，几乎没有女性在公开场合做过这样的事。

The hijab law has been codified in Iran since 1983, enforced nationwide by the ‘morality’ police. Disobeying it meant fines, flogging or imprisonment. But then one act of disobedience changed everything. In December 2017, Vida Movahed, a woman in her 30s, climbed onto a utility box on Enghelab Street in Tehran, took off her white headscarf and held it up on a stick. Although she was arrested and later sentenced to a year in prison, her action proved contagious. Other women climbed up onto boxes and makeshift plinths in the same street, waving their headscarves in the air. People began calling them the Girls of Enghelab Street. They were arrested too. It is hard to convey now how much nerve their actions took. Women had been edging their scarves back for years, but taking one off in daylight, above the traffic, where the police could see their naked heads as clearly as everyone else, was something very few women had done in public, since the protests of 1979 and 1980.

随后，2022年9月，玛莎·吉娜·阿米尼（Mahsa Jina Amini）在风纪警察的羁押中死亡。与站在箱子上的那些女性不同，她并不是活动人士：22岁，库尔德族，从德黑兰以西600公里的城市萨盖兹（Saqqez）前来探亲，被拦下的原因只是她松垮的头巾没能盖住足够多的头发。她的死把数千人带上街头，悲痛与愤怒倾泻而出。女人们烧掉头巾，在镜头前剪去头发。她们的抗议催生了「女人、生命、自由」（Woman, Life, Freedom）运动，并持续数月之久。始于为一名女性讨还公道的诉求，变成了对强制头巾的挑战，继而是对体制本身的挑战。当局以逮捕、处决和一场从未真正停歇的镇压作为回应。死者的葬礼又轮番变成新的抗议。

Then, in September 2022, Mahsa Jina Amini died in custody of the morality police. Unlike the women on the boxes, she was not an activist: aged 22, a Kurdish woman visiting from Saqqez, a city 600 km west of Tehran, she’d been stopped because her loose headscarf was not covering enough of her hair. Her death brought thousands of people out on to the street in an outpouring of grief and anger. Women burned their headscarves. Cut their hair in front of cameras. Their protests spawned the Woman, Life, Freedom movement, and continued for months. What began as a demand for justice for one woman became a challenge to the compulsory hijab, then to the system itself. The authorities responded with arrests, executions and a repression that has never really stopped. The funerals of the dead became protests in their turn.

真正的变化在于：此后数月，风纪警察大规模地从街头消失了，而且没有任何公告宣布巡逻已经终止。那些从小到大都预期自己会被拦下的女性，如今没有被拦下，而她们知道原因何在：2022年的教训已经让政府明白，仅仅一次逮捕可能让它付出怎样的代价。

What changed was that the morality police largely disappeared from the streets for months afterwards, with no announcement that the patrols had ended. Women who had grown up expecting to be stopped were not being stopped, and they knew why: the government had learned in 2022 what a single arrest could cost.

德黑兰女子阿扎黛（Azadeh）告诉我，2025年6月与以色列的十二日战争期间，这座城市弥漫着末日将至的气息。美国总统唐纳德·特朗普（Donald Trump）发文说德黑兰民众应当撤离之后，人们抛下一切逃离家园，城市为之一空。检查站越设越多，政府等着一场始终没有到来的起义。但在袭击过后笼罩全城的那种奇异氛围中，阿扎黛注意到了另一个变化——一份无人签署的契约：「国家开始放任人们按自己的喜好生活，只要他们不为此走上街头。」

Azadeh, a woman from Tehran, tells me that, during the 12-day war with Israel in June 2025, the city felt apocalyptic. After the US president Donald Trump posted that people in Tehran should get out of the city, they fled their homes, leaving everything behind, and the city emptied. Checkpoints multiplied as the government waited for an uprising that never came. But in the strange mood that settled on the city following the attacks, Azadeh noticed another change – a bargain that nobody signed: ‘The state began leaving people alone to live as they like, as long as they do not come out into the street about it.’

如今在德黑兰发生的，是人们悄悄丢弃那些支配了日常生活数十年的规则。过去，露出几寸头发就能构成整场争端，但这已不再是问题所在。全城上下的女性正摘下头巾，脱下曼托（manteau）——那种她们此前在公开场合必须罩在衣服外面的宽松及膝外套——转而拥抱最新的潮流：七分裤、短款紧身的衣服、裸露的手臂、中长半身裙、裸露的双腿。她们享受着这份新的自由，沿着伊兰沙赫尔招摇而行（颇为贴切的是，这条街的名字取自古波斯语中「伊朗人的土地」一词），而沿街那些过去卖曼托和头巾的店铺，如今改卖女式衬衫和T恤了。

What is happening in Tehran now is the quiet discarding of rules that have governed daily life for decades. A few inches of visible hair used to be the whole argument, but it is no longer the point. Women up and down the city are removing their scarves and manteaus – the loose, knee-length coats they were required to wear over their clothes in public – and adopting the latest trends: capri trousers, cropped and close-fitting clothes, bare arms, midi skirts, bare legs. Enjoying this new freedom, they parade along Iranshahr (rather aptly, the street takes its name from the old Persian word for the land of the Iranians), where shops that used to sell manteaus and headscarves have switched to blouses and T-shirts.

一位我姑且称之为米娜（Mina）的年轻女子，把这种转变说得更直白。「这已经不是可选项了，」她说，「你没法穿着随随便便的衣服出门，必须打扮得精致利落。」她不再戴头巾，和她一起出门的人也都不戴。她告诉我，在那些人们专门去「被看见」的街上，几乎没人戴。在德黑兰，买得起的人穿最新季的设计师服装；而在伊朗的小城镇，本地作坊把巴黎和纽约的最新潮流仿制得以假乱真，以至于如今无论什么阶层的人，都能从头到脚穿上一身「香奈儿」（Chanel）。

A young woman whom I’ll call Mina puts the shift in blunter terms. ‘It isn’t optional anymore,’ she said. ‘You cannot leave the house in ordinary clothes. You have to be put together.’ She does not wear a headscarf anymore, and nor does anyone she goes out with. On the streets where people go to be seen, she tells me, hardly anyone does. In Tehran, those who can afford it wear the latest designer clothing. But in small Iranian towns, local workshops turn out such good copies of the latest Parisian and New York trends that anyone can now dress head to toe in ‘Chanel’, whatever their class.

同样来自德黑兰的西玛（Sima）说：「在穿着自由的女性之间存在一种团结。没有人谈论它，但人人都能感觉到。」回忆起自己第一次摘下头巾，西玛说：「我感觉自己像秃了头……我以为所有人都在看我，我一遍遍地回头确认。」她告诉我，她当时吓坏了，所以在包里备着一块头巾，一旦有什么风吹草动就能立刻掏出来戴上。这种感觉过了一阵子才消退。每次出门，她看到的都是更多不戴头巾的女性，这给了她勇气。她知道自己并不孤单。

Sima, also from Tehran, says that: ‘Among the women who dress freely there is a kind of solidarity. Nobody talks about it, but everyone feels it.’ Recalling the first time she removed her headscarf, Sima says: ‘I felt bald … I thought everyone was looking at me, and I kept checking.’ She was frightened, she told me, so she kept a headscarf in her bag ready to whip out if anything should happen. It took a while for the feeling to pass. Each time she went out, she saw more women without headscarves and that gave her heart. She knew that she was not alone.

对于这些变化，尤其是在伊朗之外，最省事的解读是：这是反抗——把服装当作对抗国家的武器。这种叙事并非全然不对，但它错过了街头正在发生的大部分事情，而身处其中的人也不会这样描述自己正在做的事。当我问米娜，穿衣打扮是否感觉像一种政治行为时，她停顿了一下，然后说不是。「我打扮只是为了自己，」她告诉我，「我不是在搞什么政治行动。」那么，伊兰沙赫尔街上究竟在发生什么？

The easy read on these developments, especially outside Iran, is that this is defiance: using clothing as a weapon against the state. The framing is not entirely wrong, but it misses most of what is happening on the street, and the people living it do not describe what they are up to in this way. When I asked Mina if getting dressed felt like a political act, she paused, then said no. ‘When I dress up, it’s only for myself,’ she told me. ‘I’m not doing some political act.’ So what is happening on Iranshahr?

伊朗社会学家阿塞夫·巴亚特（Asef Bayat）多年来一直在追问：在没有正式组织、也没有常规政治运动的情况下，普通人如何改变自己的社会。在《作为政治的生活：普通人如何改变中东》（Life as Politics: How Ordinary People Change the Middle East，2010）一书中，他提出，人们靠的与其说是抗议，不如说是单纯地按自己的意愿生活——当这样做的人数足够多、时间足够长，规则便不得不绕着他们弯折。巴亚特把西玛、米娜和阿扎黛所描述的现象命名为「在场之艺」（the art of presence）。它指的是「不顾一切艰难也要主张集体意志的勇气与创造力，去绕开束缚，利用手头可用的东西，发现新的空间，在其中让自己被听见、被看见、被感受、被实现」。这是软实力的一种形式。

The Iranian sociologist Asef Bayat has spent years asking how ordinary people can change their societies in the absence of formal organisations or conventional political movements. In Life as Politics: How Ordinary People Change the Middle East (2010), he argues that they do it less by protesting than by simply living as they wish to, and in enough numbers and for long enough that the rules have to bend around them. Bayat’s name for what Sima, Mina and Azadeh are describing is ‘the art of presence’. It denotes ‘the courage and creativity to assert collective will in spite of all odds, to circumvent constraints, utilising what is available and discovering new spaces within which to make oneself heard, seen, felt, and realised.’ It is a form of soft power.

挤满伊兰沙赫尔街人行道的女性们，不过是点着咖啡，为朋友的镜头调整一下太阳镜。她们从不喊口号，只是闲逛，欣赏彼此的造型。每个人所做的，无非是决定穿什么出门，再无更戏剧化的成分。但当成千上万的年轻人做出同样的决定，在同一段人行道上，一夜又一夜，一连数月，这笔账就不一样了。一场抗议或许一夜之间就能被清场，但面对成千上万径直按自己的意愿继续生活的人，政府要对付起来就难得多了。

The women filling the sidewalks of Iranshahr Street are just ordering coffee and adjusting their sunglasses for a friend’s camera. They never chant slogans. They just hang out and admire each other’s looks. Each person is doing nothing more dramatic than deciding what to wear out of the house. But when thousands of young people make the same decision, on the same stretch of pavement, evening after evening, for months, the arithmetic changes. A protest might be cleared in a night. But a government has a harder time dealing with thousands of people who simply go on living as they want.

风纪警察并没有消失，但他们的手法变了。如今在公开场合拦下一名女性代价高昂：「女人、生命、自由」抗议已经表明，这可能导致数月的示威，于是压力从个人转移到了女性聚集的场所，转移到了她们手中的手机上。整个夏天，伊兰沙赫尔和萨奈伊街（Sanaei Street）——德黑兰另一处热门聚集地——的几家最有名的咖啡馆，奉司法和警务官员之命被关停。给出的关停理由之一，是接待了未戴头巾的顾客。八月，全国至少有42家商户被勒令歇业，70多个社交媒体账号被封禁或收缴。去年，德黑兰警察局长还曾暗示，便衣警员就混在咖啡馆和演唱会现场之中。

The morality police have not gone away, but their methods have changed. Stopping a woman in public is expensive now: as the Woman, Life, Freedom protests showed, it can end in months of demonstration, so the pressure has moved away from individuals to the places where women gather, and to the phones in their hands. Over the summer, several of the best-known cafés on Iranshahr and Sanaei Street, another popular hangout spot in Tehran, were shut down on the orders of judicial and police officials. The reasons given for the closures included serving customers who were not wearing the hijab. At least 42 businesses were shuttered across the country in August, and more than 70 social media accounts were blocked or seized. Last year, Tehran’s police chief intimated that plainclothes officers were working inside cafés and at concerts.

不过，国家也学到了一课：它并不总需要亲临现场。一个女人只要认定自己身处的咖啡馆正被监视，她的举止就会真的像被监视一样。尽管如此，眼下的压力仍比国家理想中想要施加的轻。九月，一位高级官员抱怨说，「战争状态」使当局无法按自己的意愿充分执行头巾法。

However, the state has also learned that it doesn’t always need to be there: a woman who assumes the café she is sitting in is being watched behaves as though it is. Still, the pressure is lighter than the state would ideally like to exert. In September, one senior official complained that ‘war conditions’ were preventing the authorities from enforcing the hijab as fully as they wanted.

2026年2月美以袭击一发动，政府便抢先占据公共空间，唯恐再掀起一波受特朗普感召的抗议浪潮。每天傍晚，政府都组织自己的支持者举行集会，主要地点在瓦利阿斯尔街（Vali-Asr）——位于伊兰沙赫尔以西几个街区。于是，两种版本的公共生活同时上演：政府动员人群出场，以显示公众依然站在它一边；而在伊兰沙赫尔，年轻人挤满街道，为的是看看谁来了，以及大家的造型如何。

As soon as the American-Israeli attacks were launched in February 2026, the government moved to occupy public spaces, fearing another wave of Trump-inspired protests. Every evening, it organised demonstrations of its own supporters, mainly on Vali-Asr, a few blocks west of Iranshahr. Two versions of public life were thus running at once. The government was turning out crowds to show that the public was still with it, while on Iranshahr young people filled the street to see who had shown up and how they looked.

头发正处于这两种公共生活版本的交汇点上。几十年来，它被排除在公共视野之外，一件私事变成了法律义务；而如今它处处展示于人前，并且是刻意为之。为什么是头发？大概因为在公共场合，它是最显眼的女性特质标志。1936年1月，礼萨汗（Reza Shah）——1979年革命之前统治伊朗的最后一个王朝的缔造者——要求妻子塔吉·莫卢克王后（Queen Tadj ol-Molouk）和女儿们不戴面纱公开露面。他让人给她们拍照，照片中的她们不戴面纱、身着西式服装，并将这些照片散发到全国各地；与此同时，他的警察走上街头，把仍穿着恰多尔（chador）罩袍的妇女身上的罩袍扯了下来。

Hair sits at the crossroads of these two versions of public life. For decades, it was kept out of public view, a private fact turned into a legal obligation, but now it is on display everywhere and deliberately so. Why hair? Probably because it is the most visible sign of femininity in public. In January 1936, Reza Shah, founder of the last dynasty to rule Iran before the revolution of 1979, asked his wife Queen Tadj ol-Molouk and their daughters to appear unveiled in public. He had them photographed, unveiled and wearing Western clothes, and distributed the images around the country, while his police went into the streets and pulled the chador off any woman still wearing one.

礼萨汗的妻子塔吉·莫卢克王后与女儿沙姆斯（Shams）和阿什拉芙（Ashraf）不戴面纱出席一场公开典礼，摄于1936年1月8日。图片来源：维基百科（Wikipedia）

Reza Shah’s wife Queen Tadj ol-Molouk and their daughters Shams and Ashraf participating in a public ceremony unveiled, 8 January 1936. Courtesy Wikipedia

伊朗的神职人员将这一举动解读为直接的挑战。几十年后，正是这批神职人员领导的革命，把遮盖头发变成了强制义务。头发成了政治战场。本世纪以来，无论哪个政府掌权，女性的头发和她们行走的街道，始终是伊朗国家借以展示自己还握有多少控制力的地方，因为这是人人一眼就能看出的权力量尺。

Iran’s clergy read the move as a direct challenge. Decades later, the revolution led by that same clergy made covering the hair compulsory. Hair became a political battlefield. Whichever government has held power this century, women’s hair and the streets they walk in have been where the Iranian state shows how much control it still has, because it is the one measure of its power that everybody can see at a glance.

从2025年12月底延续至2026年，要求终结现政府的政治抗议遭到致命武力镇压，而伊朗沙赫尔街的街头文化照常延续。这并不是两个互不相干的人群。我在伊朗沙赫尔街上交谈过的人，自己就曾身处那些抗议之中。两群人都在违抗国家，方式却截然不同；而若从思考「人群」究竟是什么入手，这种差异会更容易看清。

From late December 2025 and into 2026, political protests demanding the end of the government were put down with lethal force, while the street culture of Iranshahr went on. These are not two separate populations. The people I spoke to on Iranshahr had been in those protests themselves. Both crowds were defying the state, but in very different ways, and this difference is easier to see if you start by considering what a crowd actually is.

In Crowds and Power (1960), Elias Canetti argued that a crowd is born at a single moment he called ‘the discharge’:

在被冠以德黑兰「绯红之冬」（Crimson Winter）之名的抗议中，抗议者正是这样的人群。他们的示威始于巴扎集市，导火索是货币崩溃和万物价格飞涨，12天之内便蔓延到一百多座城市。画面显示，主要的抗议人潮逐小时壮大，直到德黑兰最长的一条大道从一端到另一端都被占满。那很可能就是卡内蒂所描述的「释放」——一种积压多年、终于爆发的压力。在随后的致命镇压中，人权监督机构核实了7007人死亡，尽管一些估计认为死亡人数超过3万。

The protestors in what has been dubbed Tehran’s Crimson Winter were this kind of crowd. Their demonstrations started in the bazaars over the collapse of the currency and the rising price of everything, and within 12 days they had spread to more than 100 cities. Footage showed the main protest growing by the hour, until one of Tehran’s longest boulevards was full, end to end. It may well have been the discharge Canetti describes, of a pressure, years in the building, finally exploding. In the lethal crackdown that followed, human rights monitors verified 7,007 deaths, though some estimates put the toll at more than 30,000.

伊斯兰共和国始终试图控制自己的形象，尤其是自身权力的形象。从它指缝中溜走的东西，只要没人看见，就能被悄悄收拾干净。而一场蔓延全国、高喊政府必须下台的人群，是无法被悄悄收拾掉的。于是政府必须清空街道，以证明自己的权力仍能延伸至此。

The Islamic Republic has always tried to control its image and, above all, the image of its own power. What slips out of its hands can be tidied away, as long as nobody is looking. A crowd spreading across the country and shouting that the government must go cannot be tidied away. And so the government must clear the streets to prove its power still has reach.

伊朗沙赫尔街上聚成的群体也是一群「人群」，但它做的并不是冬季人群所做的事。街上没有人向政府索求任何东西。相反，他们以国家无法容纳的生活方式挑战其权威，只不过这种方式更安静、更缓慢，却也难以压制得多。正是在这里，巴亚特的「非运动」（non-movement）概念显出了用处。「你在德黑兰街头所描述的现象，或许可以用非运动来解释：这些年轻人正在展示一种挑战主流规范的生活方式，在伊朗的情形下，挑战的甚至是不成文的法律。」他在写给我的信中说。

The Iranshahr collective is a crowd too, but it is not doing what the winter crowd did. Nobody on Iranshahr is asking the government for anything. Instead, they defy its authority by living in a way it cannot accommodate, but which is quieter and slower and much harder to suppress. This is where Bayat’s idea of the non-movement becomes useful. ‘What you are describing in the streets of Tehran might be explained in terms of non-movement, in the sense that these young people are displaying a kind of lifestyle that defies dominant norms, and in the case of Iran, even unwritten laws,’ he wrote to me.

巴亚特在书中将非运动定义为「非集体行动者的集体行动」。这在很大程度上是一个关乎自觉意图的问题：「在非运动中，」他告诉我，「行动者并不一定把自己的行为看作蓄意的违抗或抵抗。他们所追求的，是实现另一种存在与行事的方式，一种与主导方式相对立的方式。」非运动所描述的，是大量普通人碎片化、即兴式的活动，这些人很少受某种意识形态或某个领导层的引导，却依然触发了社会变革。大多数决定走上街头、挑战规范的人，都会像米娜那样，把自己的选择描述为纯属个人选择。

In his book, Bayat defines non-movements as ‘the collective actions of noncollective actors’. It’s largely a question of conscious intent: ‘In non-movements,’ he told me, ‘actors do not necessarily see their acts as deliberate defiance or resistance. What they aim for is the realisation of an alternative mode of being and doing things that stands counter to the dominant ones.’ Non-movements describe the fragmented, improvised activities of large numbers of ordinary people who are rarely guided by an ideology or a leadership, but that trigger social change all the same. Most of the people making the decision to show up and defy norms, would, as Mina did, describe their choices as personal.

正如巴亚特所写，非运动的能动性「立足于庞大人数的力量」，面对这种力量，国家可能会发现自己缺乏重夺控制的手段。但更重要的是，非运动之所以难以管控，是因为它提供了「逃离、空间和不受控制的漏洞……可被普通行动者填补和占用」。正是这些小型逃离的平凡特质，让非运动得以悄然累积成形。

The agency of non-movements ‘rests on the power of big numbers’, as Bayat writes, against which a state may find it lacks the means to reassert control. But more importantly, the non-movement is difficult to police because it offers ‘escapes, spaces, and uncontrolled holes … that can be filled and appropriated by ordinary actors.’ It is the very ordinariness of these small escapes that allows the non-movement to quietly accrete.

伊朗政府最近自己发现了这一点。2023年，司法机构起草了一部「头巾与贞洁」法案（Hijab and Chastity bill），该法案后来获得通过，却从未付诸实施。事实上，是伊朗最高国家安全委员会（Supreme National Security Council）要求立法机构不要实施它。属于改革派阵营的伊朗总统马苏德·佩泽希齐扬（Masoud Pezeshkian）在国家电视台上承认，对于这部将由他本人负责颁布的法律，他「有很多保留意见」。「因为这部法律，我们可能会毁掉社会中的许多东西。」他说。他所指的，是社会信任的侵蚀。执行这部法律，将要求人们就头巾问题相互举报：朋友与家人将彼此反目；出租车司机若不举报一位未遮盖头发的乘客，就会被罚款。

The Iranian government made this discovery for itself recently. In 2023, the judiciary drafted a Hijab and Chastity bill that was later passed but never brought into force. Indeed, the Supreme National Security Council of Iran told the legislature not to implement it. Iran’s president Masoud Pezeshkian, who belongs to the reformist camp, admitted on state television that he had ‘many reservations’ about a law he would himself be responsible for promulgating. ‘We risk ruining a lot of things in society because of this law,’ he said. What he was referring to was the erosion of social trust. Enforcing the law would have required people to report each other over the hijab. Friends and families would have turned on one another; a taxi driver who failed to report an uncovered passenger would be fined.

用巴亚特的话说，这就是自下而上的变革可能呈现的样子。面对女性持续不断摘下头巾，政府从未承认失败。废除现行的强制头巾法将无异于宣告失败，于是这部法律仍留在法典上，无人执行，而执法行动则转移到政治代价较小的地方。被守护的，是一个依然掌控全局的国家形象。如果宗教真如强硬派希望的那样是真正的关切，那么无论付出什么代价，这部法律都会被执行；但如今的优先事项是政权及其存续。这就是为什么一个摘下头巾却对政府不发一言的女性，会被放任不管。对伊朗人民而言，并不存在一场运动所能带来的那种单一、正式的胜利，也没有一个可供纪念的日子。但变化终究在那里，就在一条面貌已与几年前迥然不同的街道上。

In Bayat’s terms, this is what change from below can look like. In response to women persistently removing the hijab, the government never admitted a defeat. Repealing the existing law on compulsory hijabs would be a declaration of defeat, so the law stays on the books, unenforced, and the enforcement moves to where the political costs are lesser. What is being protected is the image of a state still in charge. If religion was the real concern, as hardliners want it to be, the law would be enforced whatever the cost, but the priority now is the regime and its survival. That is why a woman who removes her headscarf yet says nothing about the government is left alone. For the people of Iran, there has been no single, formal victory of the kind that a movement delivers, and no date to mark it. But the change is there anyway, in a street that looks nothing like it did a few years ago.

伊斯兰共和国无疑还会不时展示实力，用一把挂锁、一次巡逻或一杆枪来伸张自己，但当它遭遇民众持续不断的抵抗时，又会再度退让。每退让一次，它的权威便流失一分，直到有一天，它也许会发现自己已然变成了另一种东西。

The Islamic Republic will no doubt put on a show of strength from time to time, asserting itself with a padlock, a patrol or a gun, but then it will pull back when it meets with people’s ongoing resistance. Each time it pulls back, a little more of its authority is lost, until one day it may find out that it has become something other than it was.
