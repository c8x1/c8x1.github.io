封秤之前

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年11月9日·3分钟阅读

腊月廿一，年关大集开在镇北的空场，四乡的货都往这儿汇。关屠户的猪拴在场心，一口整猪，毛重两百斤出头；闺女正月出门子，从头到脚的布，算下来要整整两匹。宋机户的布担就歇在猪旁边，白布、蓝布码得整齐；他要米——家里到年根只剩两升，大人娃儿要吃到来年麦口，至少得籴四石。齐粮贩的骡车停在布担外头，四石米装在麻袋里，袋口的封线都是好的；这一年他缺的偏偏是肉——他爹的新坟在村东，头一个年，刀头肉和年席上的荤腥，都得从一口整猪上出。三人要的，偏都在另两人手里。

On the twenty-first of the twelfth month, the year-end market opened on the empty ground north of the town, and goods from all four quarters of the countryside flowed toward it. Butcher Guan's pig stood tethered at the heart of the ground, one whole pig, a bit over two hundred catties. His daughter marries out in the first month, and the cloth for her trousseau, sheets to shoe uppers, came to two full bolts. Weaver Song's carrying-pole load of cloth rested beside the pig, white cloth and blue cloth bundled in good order; he wanted rice — his household was down to two sheng to the year's end, with mouths to feed clear to the next wheat, four shi at the least. Grain-dealer Qi's mule cart stood outside the cloth, four shi of rice sewn in hemp sacks with the mouth-threads still sound; what he lacked this year was meat — his father's grave lay new in the village's east ground, its first new year, and the offering meat and the feast meats all had to come off one whole pig. Each man wanted what the other two held.

这集上没有银子说话的份。腊月里银根比命根紧，铺户挤兑，私债收不回，谁见现银谁眼红，货换物反倒省心。只是年中有过一桩白纸案：南乡一个贩子收了半船笋干，画押的字据拿到衙门竟是张白纸——从那以后，官牙定的规矩只剩一条：一手换。你的货搭我的手，我的货搭你的手，两手同撒，牙人看见了才作数。想叫牙人从头到尾押一桩买卖也行，两成佣，三家都嫌贵，那是半年的活钱。至于谁先单独撒手，把货交进一个还没交货的人手里——去年吃过白纸亏的镇上，没人肯。

Silver carried no voice at this market. In the twelfth month credit was tighter than breath: the shops were squeezed, private debts went uncollected, and any man flashing cash drew every eye, so goods for goods sat easier in the mind. But there had been the blank-paper affair that summer: a dealer from the south took half a boatload of dried bamboo shoots, and the receipt he had signed turned up at the yamen a clean white sheet — since then, the licensed broker kept one rule only: hand for hand. Your goods touch my hand, my goods touch your hand, both hands open at once, and the broker must see it. To have the broker walk a trade from end to end cost two parts in ten — half a year's working money, and all three grudged it. As for letting go first, putting goods into the hands of a man who had not yet delivered — a town schooled by the blank paper would have none of it.

三个人就在场心里站了下来。猪拴在桩上，布担歇在脚边；米袋是齐粮贩自己一袋一袋扛下车的，码成齐齐一座。买猪的散客来了两拨，问割半扇行不行——杀开的猪再卖不出整猪的价，关屠户摇头。问布的婆子前后摸了三回，要扯六尺给孙子做袄，宋机户的布只整匹出，摇头。米行的小伙计来籴一斗回去熬粥，齐粮贩的封线一根本没动。晌午过后，日影从猪背挪上布担，又从布担挪上米袋。三个人的眼都在别人脚边，自己的货踩在自己脚下，场心里一杆官秤，一天没响过一声。

The three men took their stands at the heart of the ground. The pig was tethered to its post, the cloth load rested by a pair of feet; the rice sacks Grain-dealer Qi had carried down from the cart himself, stacked into a small wall. Scattered buyers came twice for the pig, asking after half a carcass — a pig cut open was never worth a whole pig, and Butcher Guan shook his head. A granny handling the cloth three times over, wanting six feet cut for a grandson's jacket — Weaver Song sold bolts whole, and shook his head. The rice shop's boy came for one dou to boil into porridge — Grain-dealer Qi never touched a sack-thread. Past noon the pig's shadow walked from the pig onto the cloth, and from the cloth onto the rice. All three men's eyes rested on the goods at the other men's feet, while under their own feet each kept what he had; one official scale stood in the ground, and it gave no sound all day.

年关集的规矩跟白纸案一样老：日落封秤。牙行把官秤抬回棚里，贴上封条，集就散了；下一集在正月十五以后，中间隔着一整个年。关屠户算着日头：猪出不了手，被面就没影，闺女的日子定在正月十六，误了这头猪就误了那头轿。宋机户算着家里那两口空缸：布换不成米，他家的年从腊月廿三起就得掺野菜。齐粮贩的账最短：除夕前一天必须上坟，他爹的坟头一个年，席面上没有刀头肉，那炷香就白烧了。三家的急都真，三家的手都攥着，急换不来攥，攥也换不来急。

The year-end market kept a rule as old as the blank-paper affair: at sunset the scales close. The brokers carry the official scale back into the shed, paste the seal across it, and the market is done; the next market waits past the fifteenth of the first month, and a whole new year stands between. Butcher Guan reckoned by the sun: no pig handed over, no cloth for the trousseau, and his daughter's day fixed for the sixteenth of the first month — miss this pig and you miss that bridal chair. Weaver Song reckoned by his two empty vats: no cloth into rice, and his household's new year would run on wild greens from the twenty-third on. Grain-dealer Qi's account was the shortest: the day before New Year's Eve he must go to the grave, his father's first new year at rest, and a feast table without offering meat made the incense burnt for nothing. Three real emergencies, three closed fists; no emergency yet had ever traded for a fist.

日头压上场边骡马棚的脊时，齐粮贩先动了。他把四只袋口的封线挨个撕开——线不撕，米倒不出来。他吆喝宋机户把空筐挪过来，一袋一袋把米倒净，话说得很糙：「坟上的事等不得。布随后送到我家，我认你这个人。猪还没影子，你也照这个样把布先撒给关家——都到这一步了。」宋机户收了米，布担没落回肩上，转身朝关屠户吆喝。关屠户把缰绳从桩上解下来，塞进齐粮贩手里，才弯腰去接那两匹布。猪过秤，两百零七斤；布过尺，两匹不短寸；米过斗，四石平。日头落进骡棚后头，牙行的封条贴上官秤——场心三个摊位都空了，三家的货各自上了路。

When the sun pressed onto the ridge of the mule sheds at the ground's edge, Grain-dealer Qi moved first. He tore the sewn thread from the four sack mouths, one after another — unopened, rice pours from nowhere. He called Weaver Song over with his empty baskets and poured the rice out sack by sack, till not a grain held back, his words coming out rough: "The grave's business cannot wait. Bring the cloth round to my house after; I take your word as the man. The pig isn't in sight yet, and you hand the cloth over to the Guans first, just the same — we have all come this far." Weaver Song took the rice, and the load did not go back onto his shoulder; he turned and called to Butcher Guan. Guan unknotted the tether from the post, pushed the rope into Grain-dealer Qi's hands, and only then bent to receive the two bolts. The pig crossed the scale at two hundred and seven catties; the cloth crossed the yardstick, two bolts short of nothing; the rice crossed the dou, four shi level. The sun dropped behind the mule sheds, and the broker's seal went onto the official scale — the three stalls at the heart of the ground stood empty, and three households' goods were on their way home.

那个年，关家的闺女正月十六上轿，被面簇新；宋家的两口缸从腊月满到麦口。齐家的年席摆得最远，摆在村东的坟前，刀头肉蒸得冒气——先撒手的那家最后一个收货，头一个开了席。从晌午站到日头压上骡棚的脊，三个人的脚底下各钉着自己的货：货从来不缺，缺的只是一处先松的手。

That new year, the Guan daughter went to her wedding chair on the sixteenth of the first month, her trousseau cloth new from the bolt. The Song household's two vats stayed full from the twelfth month to the wheat. The Qi family's feast was laid the farthest out, before the grave in the east ground, the offering meat steaming — the house that had let go first took its goods in last, and opened the feast first. From noon to the sun on the mule-shed ridge, the three men had stood with their own goods nailed under their own feet: the goods had never lacked a thing; what had been missing was one hand to loosen first.

注解：死锁（deadlocks）说的是这样一种僵局：几个人各自攥着旁人正要的东西，又都等自己要的先落进手里，只要这「等」首尾接成一圈，谁也动不了。解开它不必人人变得慷慨，圈上有一处先松手，东西转起来，一圈下来各家都收得齐。僵在圈里的也未必是谁存心；只是年市上更靠得住的，是事先把换货的次序立死，或请牙人过秤作保——等不来每一回都有等不起的人。

Note: A deadlock is a stand-off of just this kind: several people each hold what another needs, and each waits for what he wants to land in his hands first; so long as the waiting joins itself head to tail into a circle, no one moves. It does not take everyone growing generous to undo it — one hand on the circle lets go, the goods start round, and every house collects its own by the time the circle closes. No one in the circle means to be stuck; still, what a market can truly rely on is fixing the order of trade beforehand, or standing the broker's scale between the two hands — for it cannot wait, every time, on a man who can no longer wait.
