墙头一口气

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年11月18日·3分钟阅读

如今这处宅子，外人一眼看不出它曾是两院。老墙东边是井棚，西边是柿子架；墙头搭着一块走成酱色的木板，安稳得鸡站上去也不惊。有汉子挑着满担从板上过，脚不停，担不换肩；孩子们追着跑过去，又追着跑回来。刚过门的新媳妇看不分明，问墙根阴凉里打盹的老人：这墙是几时拆的？老人眼皮也不抬：「墙还在，好好在那儿。」

No one coming to this compound now would guess it had ever been two yards. The well shed stands east of the old wall, the persimmon racks west; across the wall's top lies a plank worn dark as old sauce, so settled that hens roost on it unstartled. A broad-backed man crosses with a full shoulder-load, never breaking stride, never shifting the pole; children chase each other over it and back. The young wife, newly married in, cannot make it out, and asks the old man dozing in the wall's shade: when did they take this wall down? He answers without lifting his eyelids: "The wall is still there, sound as ever."

把话退回大旱那年。西院的涝坝干成一片裂壳，吃水全指着东院那口老井，中间隔一道两人高的墙，是分家那年爷爷亲手垒的。墙这样高，翻得过去的只有几个空着手的年轻人：天不亮跃过去，灌满了再整桶整桶从墙头扔回来；半路泼掉一半是常事，井台上少不了东院的骂声。西院人人知道，能过活的日子在墙东头；知道归知道，一年两年，一户也没有挪。墙头高出一拃，过得去的十成里去九成；再出一拃，连那一成也留不住——爷爷当年把墙垒到两人高，图的正是谁也别过去。粮足的秋后，年轻人翻得利索；青黄不接的春天，连他们也只有趴在墙根喘的份。

Set the account back to the year of the great drought. The west yard's pond had dried into a shell of cracked mud; every drop of drinking water depended on the old well in the east yard, and between the two ran a wall two men high, laid by the grandfather's own hands the year the family was divided. At such height only a few young men could get over, and only empty-handed; before dawn they vaulted across, and heaved the filled buckets whole back over the wall-top, with half the water spilled on the way and curses waiting at the well-stone. Everyone in the west yard knew that a livable life lay east of that wall; knowing was all they had, and for a year, then another, not one household moved. Let the wall-top rise one hand's-breadth and nine of every ten who could cross are gone; one more, and the last tenth is gone too — the grandfather had built it two men high for exactly that. In fat autumns the young men vaulted briskly; in the lean gap of spring they lay panting at the wall's foot themselves.

春深时节，西院的水生烧得起不来床，他爹在外头替盐商拉脚未回，他娘一日三回求到墙根下。翻墙的年轻人肯搭手，工钱拿粮食抵：整桶的水从墙头那一边抛过来，桶摔豁了两口。东院的老太爷受够了天不亮满院生人，把秦木匠请去，抬出一封银子：这墙，再加三尺。秦木匠沿着墙根走了一个来回，量过来，量过去，天黑把银子原封退了回去。老太爷把银子掼在桌上，撂下一句话：东院往后没有他的活。

Deep in that spring, Shuisheng of the west yard lay burning too weak to rise; his father was away hauling for a salt merchant, and his mother begged at the foot of the wall three times a day. The vaulting young men would lend a hand, their wages paid in grain: whole buckets heaved over from the far side, and two buckets already staved in on the stones. The old master of the east yard had had his fill of strangers in his courtyard before daylight; he sent for Carpenter Qin and set out a wrapped ingot of silver: this wall, three feet higher. Qin walked the wall's length and back, measured it one way and the other, and at nightfall carried the silver back untouched. The old master flung it on the table and left him one sentence: from that day on, the east yard would give him no work.

第二天擦黑，他卸下自家门板，扛到墙下。老墙走到中段有一处早年沉下去的洼，整道墙数那里最矮，从两边院子里看过去，那里仍是过墙这一路的最高一步。他把门板搭上洼口，自己先上去走，西边两回，东边两回。头两天没人敢先上；第三天清早，水生娘等不得别人，两桶水挂上肩，一步一步上了板。到墙头那一步，担子整个顶在头顶，东院在眼前，西院在背后，而她两处都不在——那一步长不过一口气，想停也停不住；身子一晃，人已经落在井台上。水生的烧，那一礼拜就退了。

At dusk the next day he took his own door off its hinges and carried it to the wall. Midway along its length the old wall had a stretch sunk in the wet years; of the whole wall that place was the lowest, yet from either yard it remained the highest step of any crossing. He set the door plank over the sag and walked it himself first, twice from the west side, twice from the east. For two days no one dared be first; on the third morning Shuisheng's mother could wait for no one else — two full buckets hung on her shoulder pole, she went up the plank step by step. At the step on the wall-top the whole load rode above her head, the east yard before her eyes and the west yard at her back, and she was in neither of the two places. That step lasted no longer than one breath; there was no stopping on it; her body tipped, and she was already standing at the well-stone. Shuisheng's fever broke within the week.

那年秋里，西院一户一户挪了过去。没有谁召集，也没有谁劝说，哪家的腿攒够了劲，哪家便过。一个月下来，西院只剩空屋和晒柿饼的架子。天不亮，板子上往西去的是东边的人，去摘柿子、侍弄晒架；晌午又从板上担着东西回来，一天里两头走。老太爷到死没再理秦木匠，头一茬柿饼的簸箕，年年是他婆娘从板子上担过去的；他孙女学走路，就是在那块板上学的。后来有人问起西院的人，是哪一天定下搬的；谁也说不出一个日子，只说板子搭上之后，走路这件事忽然是能做的了。

That autumn the west yard moved over, one household at a time. No one called anyone together, no one argued anyone round; whichever family's legs had gathered strength enough, that family crossed. Within the month the west held nothing but empty rooms and the drying racks. Before daybreak the plank carried east feet going west, to pick persimmons and tend the racks; at noon it carried them back, loaded; it walked both directions all day long. The old master went to his grave without another word for Carpenter Qin, yet the trays of each year's first drying persimmons were carried across on that plank by his own wife, and his granddaughter learned to walk on it. Years later someone asked the west yard people on which day they had decided to move; no one could name a day — they only said that once the plank was up, walking across became a thing a body could do.

新媳妇跟着老人沿墙根走了一遭。两人高的墙还在原处，当年要添的三尺一块也没添上；门洞也从来没凿过一个。整道墙上与别处不同的，只有中段那一拃宽的洼，和洼上那块酱色的板。她问两家的和气是几时讲下的；老人说，没讲过。她问如今这起挑担的汉子是几时开始过墙的；老人朝井台那边抬了抬下巴——那年春上烧得只剩一把骨头的，就是他；他娘一日三回过墙挑的水，头一口喂的是他。秦木匠后来没再接过东院一天活，两院的门槛条凳倒都指着他，白做的活计，换些柿饼柿干。板子只管好走；人歇在哪一头，向来是井说了算。

The young wife walked the wall's length with the old man. Two men high it stood, exactly where it had always stood; not one of the promised three feet had been added, and no gate was ever cut through it. The only place the whole wall differed from any other was that hand-span of sag midway, and the dark plank lying over it. She asked when the two households had made their peace; the old man said it had never been made. She asked when the load-carrying men had begun to cross; he lifted his chin toward the well-stone — the boy who had burned down to a handful of bones that spring is the man now crossing without shifting his pole, and the first mouthful of the water his mother carried three times a day had gone to him. Carpenter Qin never took another day's work from the east yard; the door sills and stools of both yards still wait on his hands, work done for nothing, paid in dried persimmons. The plank only ever made the walking easy; where people came to rest was always the well's word.

注解：这面墙、那块板和那口井，对应过渡态理论（transition state theory）：变到另一头，先得攒够力气翻过路上最高的那一步；那一步两边都不属于，一口气即过。墙头高出一拃，快慢便差出十倍；板子是催化剂，垫稳了最高那一步，两头共用，一块不少，却从不决定人归哪一头——归哪一头，由井在哪一头（两头的落差）说了算。至于现实里并没有木匠，只有高度与冷热自己在算账；极少数没攒够力气也照样过了墙的（量子隧穿），寓言里没画。

Note: The wall, the plank, and the well stand for transition state theory: to change into the other state you must first gather the strength to get over the highest step of the road, a step that belongs to neither side and is gone within one breath; let the wall rise one hand's-breadth and the pace falls off tenfold. The plank is the catalyst — it steadies that highest step so that both directions go many times faster, it is shared by both sides and never one plank less, yet it never decides which side anyone ends on; that is settled by where the well stands, the drop between the two ends. For honesty's sake: there is no carpenter in reality, only height and heat keeping the accounts, and a rare few cross without ever mustering the strength (quantum tunneling), which the fable does not draw.
