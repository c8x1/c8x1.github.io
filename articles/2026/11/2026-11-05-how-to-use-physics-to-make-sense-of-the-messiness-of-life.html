如何用物理学理解生活的乱局

Zahaan Bharmal·Aeon·2026年11月5日·11分钟阅读·原文

那位资深合伙人开门见山，没有半句铺垫。「很抱歉，扎汉（Zahaan），」他说，「公司不再需要你的服务了。」我们有时在背后管他叫「汉堡王」（Burger King），因为他每天都会在办公桌前吃一份皇堡套餐（Whopper Meal）。此前我从未和他说过话，此后也再无机会了。

The senior partner didn’t waste any time. ‘I’m sorry, Zahaan,’ he said, ‘your services are no longer required at the firm.’ We sometimes called him the Burger King behind his back, because he ate a Whopper Meal at his desk every day. I’d never spoken to him before, and now I never would again.

那是2001年夏天。我刚大学毕业，在伦敦做管理咨询，那是我的第一份工作，入职刚九个月。父母允许我搬回原来的房间，我常常在午夜过后许久才下班回家，一进门便瘫倒。职场生活严酷磨人，但我倾尽了全力。有那么一小段时间，我觉得自己在飞：第一次绩效评估时，他们告诉我，我有「合伙人潜质」。

It was the summer of 2001. I was fresh out of university and nine months into my first job as a management consultant in London. My parents had allowed me to move back into my old room, where I routinely collapsed after getting home from the office well after midnight. Professional life was punishing, but I gave it my all. For a brief time, I felt like I was flying: at my first performance review, I was told I had ‘partner potential’.

可随后我接到一个电话，说一位美国籍资深合伙人要见我。这有些不寻常；不过转念一想，我工作那么卖力，反馈一直那么好，想必不会是什么坏事。我被领到二楼的一间办公室，比我平日办公的楼层低了好几层。

But then I got a phone call, telling me that one of the senior American partners wanted to meet me. That was unusual; but then again, I’d been working so hard and getting such good feedback, I figured it couldn’t be anything too bad. I was directed to an office on the first floor, several levels below where I usually sat.

他把我炒掉之后，我耳朵嗡嗡作响，胸口发紧；其余的事记不太清，只记得有人告诉我，我甚至不必再回自己的工位。后来我才明白，会面地点之所以那样选定，是因为从那里只需走一段楼梯，便能直下地面层。这样就没人会看见我，我也不必和任何人说话。

After he fired me, my ears buzzed and my chest tightened; I don’t remember much else, except being told that I shouldn’t even return to my desk. It became apparent that the location of the meeting had been chosen because it would allow me to take a single flight of stairs down to the ground floor. No one would need to see me, and I wouldn’t need to speak to anyone.

这个决定显得残忍而不公。但更甚于此的是，它似乎违背了多年来我借以理解世界的那套法则：物理定律。因果，作用与反作用。接下来是一段出人意料的旅程——那些我以为早已留在大学的方程，竟意外成了伴我穿过人生最混乱地带的同伴。

The decision felt cruel and unfair. But more than anything, it seemed to violate the laws that for many years had been my way of making sense of the world: the laws of physics. Cause and effect, action and reaction. What followed was an unlikely journey – one in which the equations I thought I’d left behind at university became, unexpectedly, a companion through the messiest parts of life.

在工作之前、上大学之前，我在伦敦一所竞争激烈、声名显赫的男校读书。尽管我会永远感激那段教育赐予我的机会，但对一个孤独而焦虑的少年来说，日子依然艰难。那所学校的社交通货是自信、伶牙俐齿的反击，还有一种我始终没能练成的、毫不费力的酷。我常常觉得自己站在边缘旁观，努力破译一场别人都凭直觉就能领会的游戏规则。

Before work, before university, I attended a competitive and prestigious all-boys school in London. While I’ll be forever grateful for the opportunities my education afforded me, life still felt tough for me as a lonely and anxious teenager. The school’s social currency was confidence, quick comebacks, and an effortless cool I never quite mastered. I often felt like I was watching from the edges, trying to decode the rules of a game that everyone else grasped intuitively.

唯一支撑我熬过来的，是物理学。作为诸科学中最基础的一门，物理学代表着一个完美有序的世界。它提供结构、规则与可预测性。没有社交上的揣度算计，我只需要努力，去把事情想明白。问题与解答，方程与结果。我受不受欢迎无关紧要——要紧的是我是否理解这个系统。渐渐地，世界的混沌生出了形状与结构。

The only thing that got me through was physics. The most fundamental of the sciences, physics represented a world that was perfectly ordered. It offered structure, rules and predictability. There was no social speculation; I could just work hard and try to figure things out. Problems and solutions, equations and outcomes. It didn’t matter how popular I was – what mattered was whether I understood the system. Slowly, the chaos of the world developed a sense of form and structure.

那份清晰感一直伴着我，从中学直到大学。我在牛津（Oxford）读物理学，很长时间里都笃定自己想成为科学家。这条路并不总是好走：有深夜苦读，有与习题集的苦苦缠斗，也有怀疑自己到底能不能开窍的时刻。可一旦豁然贯通，便妙不可言：熵、量子力学、宇宙学，所有那些支配着宇宙的深邃而奇异的定律。物理学仿佛自有其目的，我以为我们会一直走下去。

That clarity stayed with me, through school and into university. I read physics at Oxford, convinced for a long time that I wanted to become a scientist. It wasn’t always easy; there were late nights, bitter struggles with problem sets, moments I wondered if I’d ever get it. But when it clicked, it was wonderful: entropy, quantum mechanics, cosmology, all those deep, strange laws that govern the Universe. Physics seemed like it had a purpose, and I thought we’d go the distance.

但在大学最后一年的某个时候，事情开始起了变化。物理学仍让我着迷，但我发现自己也被别的学科吸引——政治、经济、商业。我想理解的不只是宇宙如何运转，还有人如何运转。于是毕业后，我决定把物理学留在身后，去看看那些别的兴趣会把我带向何处。

But at some point in my final year at university, things started to change. Physics still fascinated me, but I found myself drawn to other subjects too – politics, economics, business. I wanted to understand not just how the Universe worked, but how people did. So after graduating, I decided to leave physics behind and see where those other interests would take me.

后来才发现，当我抽身离开物理学时，许多其他人也离开了。在世纪之交我大学毕业后的那些年里，物理学开始在英格兰的课堂上淡出。2006年，物理A-level（英国16岁以上阶段的资格考试）报考人数较1991年下降了百分之三十七。此后绝对数字有所回升——但并非人人受益。如今，性别差距依然悬殊：2026年A-level物理报考者中仅有百分之二十三来自女生，这一数字几十年来几乎没有变动。而在英格兰，超过半数的GCSE物理课程（14至16岁学生修读）由非专业教师执教，全国物理教师缺口约有3500人。

As it turns out, when I stepped away from physics, many others did too. In the years after I left university at the turn of the millennium, physics began to fade from view in the English classroom. In 2006, physics A-level entries (post-16 qualifications in the UK) were down 37 per cent from where they were in 1991. The absolute numbers have since rebounded – but not for everyone. Today, the gender gap remains stark: only 23 per cent of A-level physics entries in 2026 came from girls, a figure that hasn’t budged much in decades. And more than half of GCSE physics classes in England (taken by 14- to 16-year-olds) are taught by a non-specialist, with a shortage of some 3,500 physics teachers nationally.

探究学生为何选择或拒绝物理学的研究发现，它被视为抽象、艰深、与现实生活脱节——即便报考人数已经回升，这种认知却纹丝未动。与此同时，一些较新的领域——计算机科学、行为经济学，甚至数据新闻——显得更直接、更可用。它们说的是「当下」的语言。

Research looking at why students choose or reject physics has found that it’s seen as abstract, difficult, and disconnected from real life – a perception that’s remained steady, even as enrolment has recovered. Meanwhile, newer fields – computer science, behavioural economics, even data journalism – have felt more immediate, more applicable. They speak the language of now.

奇怪的是，这种认知问题的出现，恰与物理学开始在一些出人意料之处崭露头角发生在同一个历史时刻。

What’s strange is that this perception problem has happened at the same historical moment in which physics has begun popping up in other surprising places.

多米尼克·卡明斯（Dominic Cummings）——脱欧运动的幕后战略家、后来又成为鲍里斯·约翰逊（Boris Johnson）的首席顾问——把竞选成功的相当一部分功劳记在了物理学头上。在他某期广受欢迎的通讯里，他转述理论物理学家理查德·费曼（Richard Feynman）的话：「最重要的是不要欺骗自己，而你正是最容易欺骗自己的人。」再者是埃隆·马斯克（Elon Musk），他把众多商业冒险的成功归功于物理学与「第一性原理思维」（first-principles thinking）：抛开惯例，从基本事实出发。而在金融界，传奇投资人查理·芒格（Charlie Munger）数十年来持同样的主张，说物理学的「逻辑与力量」帮了他极大的忙。

Dominic Cummings, the strategist behind Brexit and later Boris Johnson’s chief adviser, gave physics a fair share of the credit for the success of his campaigns. In one of his highly popular newsletters, he paraphrased the theoretical physicist Richard Feynman: ‘The most important thing is not to fool yourself and you are the easiest person to fool.’ Then there’s Elon Musk, who credits the success of his many business ventures to physics and ‘first-principles thinking’: discard convention, start from fundamental truths. And for decades, the legendary investor Charlie Munger made the same case in finance, saying that the ‘logic and power’ of physics had helped him enormously.

多年来头一次，物理学似乎站上了文化风潮的中心，受到其决定影响着数百万人生活的公众人物的称许。但凑近细看，图景要复杂得多。

For the first time in years, physics seems to be at the heart of the cultural zeitgeist, praised by public figures whose decisions affect millions of lives. But look closer, and the picture is more complicated.

首先，亿万富翁与政治操盘手算不上体面的代言人；卡明斯、马斯克和芒格或许才华横溢，但也极具分裂性。不过，比起他们是谁，更让我感兴趣的是他们如何让物理学派上用场——他们是真的在用这门学科思考，还是只把它当修辞的道具挥舞。「物理学」是否只是劈开复杂性的速记、把问题剥到最基本零件的简称？还是它确实能提供别的科学与思维方式给不了的东西？

In the first instance, billionaires and political operatives make for awkward ambassadors; Cummings, Musk and Munger might be brilliant, but they’re also extremely divisive. But more than who they are, what interests me is how they’re putting physics to work – whether they’re truly thinking with the discipline, or just brandishing it for rhetorical effect. Is ‘physics’ simply a shorthand for cutting through complexity, for stripping down problems to their most basic parts? Or does it genuinely offer something distinctive that other sciences and ways of thinking cannot?

哲学家把第一种进路称为「还原论」（reductionism）：只要把任何系统拆成足够小的组件，并追溯得足够远，最终都会抵达物理学。这种观点颇具威力，也解释了它为何吸引如此多的人。

Philosophers call the first approach ‘reductionism’: the idea that if you break any system into small enough components and trace them far enough, you eventually arrive at physics. That view can be powerful, and explains why so many people find it attractive.

而且它战绩辉煌。当19世纪的物理学家证明热不过是原子的运动——温度这个看似自成一类的东西，其实只是粒子跑得更快——一整门科学就此坍缩进了物理学。多年之后，同一路径帮助粒子物理学家把物质拆得越来越小，直到抵达某种显然无法再分的东西。从还原论的本能里，诞生了夸克（quark）、轻子（lepton）与希格斯玻色子（Higgs boson）。它们的目录蓝图——标准模型（Standard Model）——至今仍是人类历史上最伟大的智识成就之一。

It’s also been spectacularly successful. When 19th-century physicists showed that heat was simply the motion of atoms – that temperature, seemingly its own phenomenon, was actually just particles bouncing faster – an entire science collapsed into physics. Years later, that same approach helped particle physicists break matter into smaller and smaller pieces until arriving at something apparently irreducible. From the reductionist instinct came quarks, leptons and the Higgs boson. Their catalogue blueprint, the Standard Model, remains one of the greatest intellectual achievements in human history.

但还原论有其极限。拿政治来说：一个激进的还原论者可能会说，政治归根结底也是物理学，因为政治关乎人群的行为；人群由个体组成；人由原子构成；原子受物理学支配。可这难道意味着，我们能靠研究每一位登记选民脑中单个原子的行为，去预测一场全国大选的结果吗？不太可能。且不论可行性问题，这样做又有什么意义？经济学家已有经济学，心理学家已有心理学，政治家自有政治理论。物理学唯有在有所增益、有所照亮时，才具有价值。

But reductionism has its limits. Take politics: a radical reductionist might say that this too comes down to physics, because politics is about the behaviour of groups of people; groups are made of individual people; people are made of atoms; atoms are governed by physics. But does this mean that we could predict the outcome of, say, a national election by studying the behaviour of individual atoms inside the brains of every registered voter? Unlikely. Putting aside issues of practicality, what would be the point? Economists already have economics. Psychologists already have psychology. Politicians have political theory. Physics has value only if it’s additive and illuminating.

在我看来，物理学的真正价值从来不是把东西捣碎成最小碎块的大棒，而是一套驾驭系统的框架——识别模式，拷问假设，检验我们赖以依靠的模型是否真如预期那般运作。纵观科学史，处处可见这种思维方式。1854年霍乱席卷苏荷区（Soho）时，医生约翰·斯诺（John Snow）并不知道病因。但他把病例绘入地图，认出其中的模式，把疫情追溯到了宽街（Broad Street）上的一口水泵。这位如今所谓流行病学的奠基人，正是靠构建并拷问世界的心智模型、追问其中的假设、追随数据去往它们指向的任何地方来开展工作的。

For me, the real value of physics had never been as a cudgel for smashing things up into their smallest bits, but as a framework for navigating systems – for recognising patterns, probing assumptions, and testing whether the models we rely on actually work as they’re meant to. We see this way of thinking throughout the history of science. When cholera swept through Soho in 1854, the physician John Snow didn’t know its cause. But he mapped the cases, identified the pattern, and traced the outbreak to a single water pump on Broad Street. The founder of what we now know as epidemiology, Snow worked by constructing and interrogating mental models of the world, probing their assumptions, and following the data wherever they led.

因此，问题不只在于物理学能否被借来充当修辞，而在于它是否真能帮助我们更清晰地看世界，并以补益其他学科的方式行之。至少，在我不再正式研读物理学之后，留存于我心中的便是这些。不是方程，也不是教科书，而是一种思维方式——每当我的世界分崩离析，我发现自己总会向它伸手。

The question, then, is not simply whether physics can be borrowed as rhetoric, but whether it really helps us to see the world more clearly, in ways that complement and benefit other disciplines. That, at least, was what stayed with me after I stopped studying it formally. Not equations or textbooks, but a way of thinking that I found myself reaching for when my world was falling apart.

被解雇便是这样一个时刻。我那样努力，却不知怎么还是失败了。我所理解的关于因果的一切都告诉我：这本不该发生。可我偏偏落到了这里——连月拼尽全力，却拿不出一点成果。

Getting fired was one of those moments. I had worked so hard, yet somehow I’d still failed. Everything I understood about cause and effect told me this shouldn’t have happened. But here I was, after months of working flat out, with nothing to show for it.

有些人也许会去寻求心理治疗或职业辅导，我却转回物理学，试着让自己走出来。最直接的原因已足够清楚：互联网泡沫破了，涟漪荡过全球经济，最终拍上我的办公桌。各家开始勒紧腰带，而这种境况下的严酷法则往往是「后进先出」。

While some might have turned to therapy or career counselling, I turned back to physics to try to move on. The immediate cause was clear enough. The dot-com bubble had burst, sending ripples across the global economy that eventually reached my desk. Belts tightened, and the harsh rule in such circumstances tends to be last in, first out.

互联网泡沫的破灭，既非此类事情的头一遭，也非最后一遭。2008年，次贷危机来了；2011年，欧元区崩盘；2013年，卢比暴跌；2015年，中国股市动荡；2020年，则是新冠疫情（COVID-19）。这些危机有两个共同点。其一，几乎无人预见它们的到来——至少没有精确到何时、何地、以何种方式。其二，它们并非由某场惊天动地的大崩塌触发，而是由一些看似无害的小失效层层级联，滚成远为庞大的东西。例如，次贷危机的导火索，不过是一串原本可能无人留意的小事件——比如南加州一家本地房贷机构的倒闭。

The dot-com bust was not the first nor last time something like this would happen. In 2008 came the subprime mortgage crisis; in 2011, the eurozone crashed; in 2013, the rupee tanked; in 2015, there was turbulence on the Chinese stock market; and in 2020, the COVID-19 pandemic. All these crises had two things in common. First, almost no one saw them coming – at least not precisely when or where or how. Second, they weren’t triggered by some enormous dramatic collapse, but by small, seemingly innocuous failures that cascaded into something far larger. For example, it just took a few small incidents that might otherwise have passed unnoticed – like the failure of a local mortgage lender in Southern California – to precipitate the subprime mortgage crisis.

所有这些事件，都是物理学家可能称之为混沌系统的实例——理论上由决定论支配，实践中却对初始条件如此敏感，以至于微小的偏差能盘旋成截然不同的结局。一个著名的例子是「三体问题」（three-body problem），它因刘慈欣2008年的同名小说以及2024年网飞（Netflix）的改编剧集而广为人知。两颗行星相互绕转时，你可以写下简洁的方程，永远预言它们的轨迹。可添上第三颗行星，数学便骤然炸成不可预测。三个天体依旧受明确的定律支配，但三者初始位置一丁点无穷小的偏移，便足以让整个系统失衡。

All these events are examples of what physicists might call chaotic systems – deterministic in theory, but in practice so sensitive to initial conditions that tiny variations can spiral into radically different outcomes. A well-known example is the ‘three-body problem’, popularised by Liu Cixin’s 2008 novel of the same name and a 2024 Netflix adaptation. If you have two planets orbiting each other, you can write down neat equations to predict their paths forever. But add a third planet, and suddenly the maths explodes into unpredictability. The three bodies are still governed by clear laws, but an infinitesimally small shift in starting positions for the three objects can throw the whole system off balance.

1961年，数学家爱德华·洛伦兹（Edward Lorenz）在运行天气模拟时发现了类似的现象。他第一次输入的初始值四舍五入到小数点后六位，第二次却只保留三位。结果竟分歧得如此剧烈，以致他起初怀疑是计算机出了故障。实际上，是初始条件的细微差异级联成了全然不同的天气系统。大气就像经济、就像一份事业：原则上遵循决定论，实践中却无从预测。

The mathematician Edward Lorenz discovered something similar in 1961 when running weather simulations. His first attempt entered starting values rounded to six decimal places. His second try was rounded only to three. Yet the results diverged so dramatically that he initially suspected it was a computer fault. In fact, tiny differences in initial conditions had cascaded into entirely different weather systems. The atmosphere, like the economy, like a career, was deterministic in principle and unpredictable in practice.

理解这场崩盘是一回事，物理学也帮我想明白了接下来的路。我曾相信，投入会换来成比例的产出；照那个世界的逻辑，想出人头地，就该尽最大可能地努力。但物理学教给我们，混沌系统另有规则。它们需要的是浪涌容量（surge capacity）——一种让系统吸收冲击的额外缓冲。缺了它，再小的失败也可能招致全面崩塌。

Understanding the crash was one thing, but physics also helped me think about what came next. I had believed that inputs led to proportional outcomes; logically, in that world, the way to get ahead was to work as hard as possible. But physics teaches that chaotic systems play by different rules. They call instead for surge capacity, an extra buffer that allows them to absorb shocks. Without it, even small failures can lead to total collapse.

想想电网。多数时候，它平稳运转，嗡嗡作响，毫无问题。可在突如其来的负荷激增面前——比如热浪来袭、数百万台空调同时开启——系统可能过载停摆。为防这种局面，工程师在设计电网时会留出系统冗余，配备应对意外尖峰的额外容量。缺了这些，一次小小的扰动便能级联成一场大停电。

Think of a power grid. Most of the time it hums along without problem. But during an unexpected surge, such as a heatwave when millions of air conditioners are switched on at once, the system can overload and shut down. To prevent this, engineers design grids with slack in the system and extra capacity to handle unexpected spikes. Without it, a minor disruption can cascade into a blackout.

那正是我的失误。刚毕业那阵子，我一直过着没有浪涌容量的生活。说实话，我们大多数人都是如此。我们把一切优化到极致——从日程表到待办清单——笃信只要再逼自己一把，就不会有事。可我们越是在极限上运转，就越发脆弱。

That was my mistake. As a young graduate, I had been living without surge capacity. In truth, most of us do. We optimise everything from our schedules to our to-do lists – convinced that if we just push a bit harder, we’ll be fine. But the more we operate at our limits, the more fragile we become.

物理学给出的是另一番教益。韧性不在于无休止的最大化，而在于构筑承受异常事件的能力，以备其不期而至。那可能意味着时间上的松弛——在生活中留出恢复的空间，而不是把每一分钟都排满；也可能意味着职业上的松弛——发展新技能、副业与人脉，而不是押注单一路径。浪涌容量未必能阻止危机，却必定能帮你熬过危机。

Physics provides a different lesson. Rather than relentlessly maximising, resilience is about building the capacity to withstand outlier events when they come. That might mean time slack, leaving space in our lives to recover rather than scheduling every minute. It might mean career slack, developing new skills, side projects and networks, rather than relying on a single path. Surge capacity might not prevent a crisis, but it sure helps survive one.

在咨询业那段短暂而伤人的历练之后，我加入了公务员队伍，二十多岁剩余的岁月大多在政府工作中度过。我热爱那份使命感、那些同事，以及参与塑造政策的荣幸。然而说实话，我心里始终怀着一块小小的毒疮，由对昔日咨询同事的怨恨与嫉妒酿成。

After my brief and bruising spell in consulting, I joined the civil service and spent most of the rest of my 20s working in government. I loved the sense of purpose, the colleagues and the privilege of shaping policy. Yet, if I’m honest, I still carried a small canker of resentment and jealousy towards my former consulting colleagues.

与英国全国平均水平相比，我知道自己当公务员的薪水不低。可朋友们个个拿着六位数的年薪，我总觉得自己在某件事上败下阵来。他们买得起更好的衣服、汽车与公寓。更有那套点钱成金的戏法——买入资产，让钱生出更多的钱——进一步拉开他们的银行账户与我的差距。刺痛我的不是不平等本身，而是境况中令人感到的不公。我觉得自己同样拼命，却仍被甩在身后。不平等与不公平之别，从此对我变得重要。

Compared with the UK national average, I know I was paid well as a civil servant. But with friends on six-figure salaries, it felt like I was failing at something. They could afford nicer clothes, cars and apartments. And then there was the magic trick of turning money into more money by buying assets, further widening the gap between their bank balances and mine. What stung wasn’t the inequality itself, but the perceived unfairness of the circumstances. I felt like I’d worked just as hard and still fallen behind. That distinction between inequality and unfairness became important to me.

心理学家已经证明，我们打小便天生会做这个区分。在一项耶鲁（Yale）研究中，孩子们被要求根据两个人在一项任务上的表现，把10枚硬币分给他们。两人表现相当时，孩子们五五分——均等而公平。其中一人表现更好时，孩子们六四分——不均等，却依然公平。有趣的结果出现在孩子们被告知两人表现相当、却要分11枚硬币的时候。既然无法维持公平，孩子们能怎么办？他们决定干脆扔掉一枚硬币。一次又一次，孩子们都把公平放在第一位。

Psychologists have shown that we’re wired to make this distinction from a very young age. In one Yale study, children were asked to split 10 coins between two people based on their performance on a task. When the two people performed equally, the children split the coins five-five – equal and fair. When one person did better, the children split the coins six-four – unequal but still fair. The interesting result happened when the children were told that both people had performed equally, but they now had to split 11 coins. With no way to maintain fairness, what could the children do? They decided to simply throw away one coin. Time and again, the children prioritised fairness.

这个区分在物理学中同样清晰可见。不平等——就其字面意义的不均而言——织入了宇宙的肌理。最初，宇宙平滑而均质，是一团均匀的雾。直到微小的瑕疵出现——量子涨落让物质在某些地方聚得比别处更多——星系、恒星与行星才得以浮现。没有那份不均，便不会有结构，不会有生命诞生的可能。在这个意义上，不平等未必总是缺陷。有时，它正是生长与多样性的条件。

This distinction is also evident in physics. Inequality, in the literal sense of unevenness, is built into the fabric of the Universe. In the very beginning, the cosmos was smooth and equal, a uniform fog. Only when tiny imperfections appeared – quantum fluctuations that allowed matter to clump in some places more than others – did galaxies, stars and planets emerge. Without that inequality, there would have been no structure, no possibility of life. In that sense, inequality is not always a flaw. Sometimes it is the condition for growth and diversity.

同样的原则也见于自然系统。河流分出大小不等的支流。肺与血管演化成这样的网络：几条大动脉滋养着许多更细的分支。那种尺寸上的不均，不是腐败，也不是贪婪，而是一种效率。它让流动成为可能。然而流动一旦受阻——比如河道淤塞、血管堵塞——系统便告失灵。换言之，问题不在于不平等本身，而在于不公平：流动被阻断，规则被层层堆叠，机会被封死。

We see the same principle in natural systems. Rivers branch into unequal tributaries. Lungs and blood vessels evolve into networks with a few large arteries feeding many smaller ones. That inequality of size isn’t corruption or greed, but a kind of efficiency. It allows flow. Yet when the flow is blocked, like when a river silts up or a vessel clogs, the system fails. The problem, in other words, is not inequality itself, but unfairness: the obstruction of flow, the stacking of rules, the blocking of opportunity.

我想，在人类系统中也是如此。一个有人更富的社会，只要机会与资源仍能自由流动，或许尚可容忍，甚至不失健康。可在机会被封堵、规则被层层堆叠、不公当道的社会里，人们会本能地抗拒。一如当年我望着朋友们的咨询生涯，暗想自己为何被逐出门墙。物理学由此提示：真正的问题是正义的缺失，而非不平等本身。只要相信游戏没有被动过手脚，人们便能接受一个有人拥有更多的世界。

I think this is true in human systems, too. A society where some are richer than others may be tolerable, even healthy, so long as opportunities and resources can still move freely. But a society where opportunity is blocked, where the rules are stacked, where unfairness rules – people reject that instinctively. Just as I did when I looked at my friends’ consulting careers and wondered why I’d been cast out. Physics, then, suggests that a lack of justice is the real problem, rather than inequality per se. People will accept a world where some have more, so long as they believe the game isn’t rigged.

蓦然间，我在目光所及之处都看见了这种围绕公平的动态。2007至2008年的金融危机，不只是银行下了高风险的赌注，还在于人们感到：普通人付出了代价，银行家却带着分毫未损的奖金扬长而去。欧元区危机不只是债务问题，还在于南欧国家被自己未曾参与制定的规则挤压。「占领」运动（Occupy）、阿拉伯之春（Arab Spring）、脱欧、唐纳德·特朗普（Donald Trump）——以各自不同的方式，都是对不公的反叛。

Suddenly I saw this dynamic about fairness everywhere I looked. The financial crisis of 2007-08 wasn’t just about banks making risky bets, but about the perception that ordinary people paid the price while bankers walked away with bonuses intact. The eurozone crisis wasn’t just about debt, but about southern European countries being squeezed by rules they hadn’t set. The Occupy protests, the Arab Spring, Brexit, Donald Trump – all, in their different ways, were rebellions against unfairness.

透过物理学家的透镜看世界，这些时刻酷似相变（phase transition）。你可以缓缓给水加热，一切似乎纹丝不动——直到越过某个临界阈值、某个沸点，整个系统骤然从液态变为气态。人类社会同样能承受一段时间的张力。可一旦不公显得系统化，压力便会不断累积，直至系统翻覆。

Looking at the world through a physicist’s lens, these moments resemble phase transitions. You can heat water gradually and nothing seems to change – until you reach a critical threshold, a boiling point, and suddenly the whole system transforms from liquid to gas. Human societies, too, can bear strain for a while. But once unfairness feels systemic, the pressure builds until the system flips.

于是不足为怪：在这个动荡与转型的年代，物理学找到了新的拥趸——尤其是在那些想要催化剧变的人中间，无论其后果是善是恶。马斯克谈第一性原理，卡明斯谈复杂性。他们的例子表明，物理学的工具能够塑造实验室之外的广阔思维。可在他们手中，物理学更像一件武器而非框架，一种以清晰之名铲平细微差别的手段。对我们其余的人而言，挑战不只是借来物理学的外衣与光环，更是承续它的心态——那种把谦逊看得与胆识同等重要、把好奇看得与确定同等重要的心态。

Small wonder, then, that in an age of tumult and transition, physics has found new admirers – especially among those who want to catalyse radical change, for better or worse. Musk speaks of first principles, Cummings of complexity. Their examples show how the tools of physics can shape thinking far beyond the laboratory. But in their hands, physics can feel more like a weapon than a framework, a way of bulldozing nuance in the name of clarity. For the rest of us, the challenge is not simply to borrow the garb and glamour of physics, but to carry forward its mindset – one that values humility as much as boldness, and curiosity as much as certainty.

我想，那正是物理学仍能作出最大贡献之处。不是靠递给我们现成的答案，或「一切皆注定」的抚慰性幻觉，而是靠训练我们质疑自己，并与由此而来的不确定性共处。公共话语浸透了物理学，但我们更需深入领会它真正教给我们的东西：如何提出更好的问题，如何检验自己的盲点，如何承认自己所不知道的。随着新技术只会加剧伸手拿捷径的诱惑，这一需要显得愈发迫切。

That, I think, is where physics can still make its greatest contribution. Not by giving us ready-made answers or comforting illusions of inevitability, but by training us to question ourselves and live with the resulting uncertainty. Public discourse is saturated with physics, yet we need a deeper grounding in what it really teaches us about how to ask better questions, to test our own blind spots, and to admit what we don’t know. That need feels more urgent as new technologies only increase the temptation to reach for shortcuts.

那么，要让这种转变生根，需要做些什么？我与物理学的旅程始于少年时代，而我相信，如今我们必须从那里着手。我们太常把物理学干瘪而技术化的面目呈现给年轻人。我们教他们方程，却不教框架；教事实，却不教惊奇。我们很少告诉他们，物理学能帮上生活里最混乱的那些部分——它们无不与不确定、韧性与抉择缠绕在一起。

So what would it take to embed this shift? My journey with physics began when I was a teenager, and that’s where I believe we need to start now. Too often, we present physics to young people as dry and technical. We teach them equations but not frameworks, facts without wonder. We rarely tell them that physics can help with the messiest parts of life, bound up as they are with uncertainty, resilience, decision-making.

在英国，我们还在雪上加霜：学生被迫只选三四个科目，修读中学最后两年。实践中，这往往逼着人在「理科」路线与「文科」路线之间作切割。这种虚假的二元对立，恰恰剥夺了我们最需要的那种多学科思考者：懂得系统的艺术家，会讲故事的科学家，以及能把两者兼收并蓄的领导者。

In the UK, we make things worse by forcing students to choose just three or four subjects to study for the final two years of school. In practice, this often forces a split between being on the ‘science’ track or the ‘arts’ track. That false binary deprives us of exactly the kind of multidisciplinary thinkers we need: artists who understand systems, scientists who can tell stories, leaders who can hold both.

如今去学校探访时，我总设法把物理学呈现为一种超能力。我想让年轻人感到，物理学是他们可以使用的工具，而不是把他们拒之门外的门槛。最重要的是，我想让他们感到：物理学属于他们。

When I visit schools now, I try to show physics as a kind of superpower. I want young people to feel that physics is something they can use, not something that excludes them. More than anything, I want them to feel it’s theirs.

我动笔写这篇文章时，是一个被上司扫地出门的年轻人。收笔时，却落在一个全然不同的地方：物理学成了一种理解生活最混乱部分的方式。但其实，我一直在缓缓逼近的，是一个关于某些来之不易的心智习惯如何帮我们在现实的混乱与无常中找到意义的故事。对我们这些并非职业科学家的人来说，真正的任务也许不是不加批判地热爱物理学，也不是漫不经心地挥舞它，而是明智地运用它。物理学不该是通往坚如磐石之确定性的捷径，而更应是一种让我们在没有确定性时活得更有温度的方式。

I started this essay as a young man being shown the door by a superior. I ended it somewhere else entirely, with physics as a way of making sense of the messiest parts of life. But, really, what I’ve been edging up to is a story about how certain hardwon habits of mind can help us find meaning in the messiness and unpredictability of reality. For those of us who are not professional scientists, maybe the real task isn’t to love physics uncritically, or wield it carelessly, but to use it wisely. Physics shouldn’t be a shortcut to rock-solid certainty, so much as a way to live more humanely without it.
