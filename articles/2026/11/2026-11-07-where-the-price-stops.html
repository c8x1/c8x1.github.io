价钱停住的地方

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年11月7日·3分钟阅读

我家的河鲜行开在青浦埠，是爹手里传下来的。这行当没什么讲究，只有一门日课：每天开门头一桩事，把当日行价记在门后挂着的水牌上。爹交行那年嘱咐我一句，看行市别看风，看水牌。风天天刮，行价多少年都咬在一吊三上下。埠上二十几条船天天靠岸，馆子、盐坊、镇上人家把鱼收走；收走的跟送来的，就咬合在这个数上。

My family's river-fish shop stands at Qingpu Wharf, handed down from my father's hands. The trade has no refinements, only one daily rite: first thing after the doors open, write the day's price on the slate hanging behind them. The year he passed the shop on, my father told me one thing — watch the slate, not the wind. The wind blows every day, yet for years the price held its bite around one string three. Some twenty-odd boats land at the wharf each day; the restaurants, the saltery and the town households carry the fish away, and what leaves and what arrives bite together on that number.

官道通车那年，头一桩让我不敢信的事来了。城里的馆子坐马车到埠上，开口把行价抬到一吊八。我当夜睡不着，第二天去钱庄画了押：加盖两间鱼棚，添四个帮工，雇一条船专管进城送货——我算的是二吊。头两个月价钱也真往上走。走到一吊六，外乡的钓客闻着味来了，埠上的船多出十一条；馆子掌柜的嫌贵，改收塘鱼。入冬，价钱停在一吊五，不动了。棚子空着一间半，钱庄的利钱一个月四百文，送鱼的船还跑，只是进城一趟挣不回从前两趟的利。

The year the high road opened, the first thing arrived that I could not bring myself to believe. City restaurants came out to the wharf by carriage and bid the price up to one string eight. I could not sleep that night; the next morning I put my mark at the money house — two new fish sheds, four extra hands, a hired boat to run deliveries into the city — for the figure I was counting on was two strings. For two months the price did climb. At one string six the outside anglers came following the smell, and eleven more boats worked the wharf; the restaurants' stewards grudged the price and switched to pond fish. By winter the price stopped at one string five and would not move. One and a half of my sheds stood empty, the money house's interest came to four hundred cash a month, and the delivery boat still ran — only a trip to town no longer earned what two trips used to.

第二年城里塘鱼大熟，放出来的塘鱼照着河鱼的价踩。价钱一路往下掉，掉到一吊一我就慌了，贱价出清了囤的一半咸鱼，琢磨着裁人。可它掉到一吊一就站住了：镇上人家平日舍不得的鱼上了桌，盐坊趁贱多腌了两船，外乡的钓客歇了船，埠上回到二十几条。转过年，盐坊腌足了货，反过来问我要鱼——我出清的那一半，贱在了正底下。

The next year the town's pond fish came in heavy, and the pond fish let loose stepped on the river-fish price. It fell and fell; at one string one I panicked, sold off half my salted stock cheap, and thought about letting hands go. But at one string one it stood its ground: town households set on their tables the fish they usually grudged, the saltery took advantage of the cheap price to pickle two extra boatloads, the outside anglers tied up their boats, and the wharf fell back to twenty-odd. When the new year turned, the saltery had pickled its fill and came round asking me for fish — the half I had sold off, I had sold at the very bottom.

这中间没有一个人是冲着行价来的。钓客跟着味走，馆子跟着便宜走，各人算的是各人的小账。可就是这些各算各的，把每一阵风磨去一截，让价钱停在挪过去一点的地方。它从来不回到一吊三：一吊五停过，一吊一停过，停住的地方回回不一样，回回都比风刚吹来时近。

Not one of these people was acting on the price's behalf. The anglers follow the smell, the restaurants follow the cheap, each keeps his own small ledger. Yet it is precisely this everyone-for-himself arithmetic that grinds a length off every wind and lets the price come to rest a little way past where it stood. It never goes back to one string three: it has rested at one string five, it has rested at one string one; the resting place is different every time, and every time it lies nearer than where the wind first drove it.

炳叔又让我看明白一桩。他在埠上替两头搭话，嘴快，买卖经他的手，晌午前就定得完，往常要到擦黑。头几天我高兴，多备了两倍的鱼。日落一算，当日上岸的、当日收走的，跟炳叔没来时一样多，水牌上的价一分没挪。帮工问他图什么，我说他挣他的嘴钱；买卖到得早了，停的地方还是那个地方。

Uncle Bing taught me another thing. He brokers between the two ends at the wharf, a fast tongue; deals that pass through his hands are settled before noon, where they used to take until dusk. For the first days I was glad, and laid in twice the fish. At sunset I reckoned it up: what landed that day and what was carried off matched what it had been before Uncle Bing ever came, and the slate had not moved by one cash. A hand asked what he was in it for; I said he earns his talking money — the deals arrive sooner, and the place where they stop is still the same place.

今年封河前，行价又停在一吊一。棚子空着的那间半，开春租给了盐坊，利钱付得起；帮工一个没裁。镇上传开春官道要加一道关税，说什么的都有，有的说要上天，有的说要见底，我没接话。风来的时候，价钱让半步，让完停住；等它停住了，我再画押。水牌还挂在门后，我天天记。

Before the river froze this year, the price stopped at one string one again. The one and a half empty sheds I rented out to the saltery come spring; the interest is payable now, and not one hand was let go. The town is saying the high road will take a tariff come spring — some say the price will fly to the sky, some say it will drop through the floor; I did not join in. When the wind comes, the price yields half a step and then stops; when it has stopped, then I will put my mark on paper. The slate still hangs behind the door, and I write on it every day.

注解：勒夏特列原理（Le Chatelier's principle）：两头进出长久咬合的行市，哪头被推一下，各家照自己小账的寻常进出，就把这推力磨去一部分，价钱停在一个挪过的新数上——只磨一部分，回不去原数。这新数谁也算不出，只能看出往哪边挪；这条理只对咬得住、停得住的行市作数，散了的市面它管不着。

Note: Le Chatelier's principle: in a trade whose two ends have long meshed, whichever side gets a push, the ordinary comings and goings of each house keeping its own small ledger grind away part of that push, and the price comes to rest at a shifted new number — only part of it, never the old number back. The new number cannot be reckoned, only the direction seen; and the rule holds only for a trade that stays meshed and can come to rest — a market coming apart is beyond it.
