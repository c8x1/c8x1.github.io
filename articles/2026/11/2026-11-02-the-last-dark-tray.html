最后那盘没照过的蛋

AI Parable (inspired by Amanda Askell)·每日精选·2026年11月2日·3分钟阅读

涂家炕坊改作粮棚的那年，梁上还留着一只空壳——当年一双雏鸡从同一只壳里钻出来过，如今看一眼要收一枚铜钱。街口的人说，这条街败在运气走空：山客不来，春夜不聚，三代人围着猜的炕，说凉就凉了。

The year the Tu family's hatch-house was turned into a grain shed, one empty shell was still left hanging from the beam — the shell that two chicks had once broken out of together — and a look at it now cost a copper. People at the mouth of the street said the street had died of its luck running dry: the mountain traders stopped coming, the spring nights stopped gathering, and a hatch three generations had wagered around went cold, just like that.

倒数最后一个春夜。那年的灯比雏鸡先出炕：万老板开春就买齐了每盘蛋的来历，又把老涂的灯整季包下，一盘一盘照过去，报出实价。山蛋与熟户的蛋同价，谁也多赚不着一文；开炕那晚大厅里只剩账桌先生，壳里钻出什么，都与价钱无关，买定的人回家睡觉。没人喊，没人押，没有一只蛋值得等。

Count back to the last spring night. That year the lamp came out before the chicks did: Boss Wan opened the season by buying up the provenance of every tray, then took old Tu's lamp for the whole spring, candled tray after tray, and named the true price of each. Mountain eggs and home-flock eggs sold at one price, and nobody cleared an extra coin; on hatching night the tally clerk was the only man left in the hall, whatever stepped out of a shell had nothing to do with the price, and the buyers had settled up and gone home to bed. Nobody shouted, nobody wagered, and not one egg was worth waiting for.

再倒回去，到还肯吵的年头。山客挑来的杂盘集了十户的种，壳底下什么都摸不准，那样的夜，厅里挤得转不开身；熟户的准炕蛋只消一句「照旧」，卖得同米价一样稳。这条街有句老话：话可以拣短的讲，短不过壳底下那点没底。撑船的严三那年把船押在两盘杂盘上，老涂开出一夜的灯价——照过的盘心里有数，只买价钱压低了的那两盘，船就稳了。他付了。

Count back again, to the years when the street still loved a quarrel. The mixed trays the mountain men carried in held the seed of ten households; nothing under those shells could be called beforehand, and on such nights the hall was too packed to turn around in. The home flocks' steady eggs sold on a single word, "same as ever," quiet as the rice trade. The street kept an old saying: you can pick your words as short as you like, but no words run shorter than what a shell holds under it. That spring the boatman Yan San staked his boat on two of those mixed trays, and old Tu offered him a night of the lamp — see what you are buying, take only the trays priced under their worth, and the boat is safe. He paid.

两盘孵出来的，同他夜里在灯下看见的分毫不差；赚回灯钱，添了三分薄利。那盏灯的价钱，够把那夜最后的一盘黑蛋买上四十回——山客赶夜路到得晚，灯油熬尽了，最后那盘没照过的蛋黑着入了夜；厅里的钱都押光了，半大小子阿豆用一枚银毫把它端走。

The two trays hatched out exactly what he had seen under the lamp; he made back the lamp fee and a thin three percent on top. The price of that lamp would have bought the last dark tray of the night forty times over. The mountain trader had come in late off the night road, the lamp oil had burned to its end, and that last tray, never candled, went into the night dark; every coin in the hall was already wagered down, and the boy Adou, with one silver dime left in his pocket, carried it off.

后半夜一只壳裂开，钻出来的竟是两只雏，满厅的喊声震得梁上落灰。一只寻常的雏，只值一只雏的钱；一只壳里走出两只，就值一头骡。万老板一辈子只买灯见过的货；那对雏是自己走出壳的，天亮替灯作了保，他便用骡价从阿豆手里买定，空壳赏给老涂，挂上了梁。严三在门槛外站了一夜，没进厅。

Past midnight one shell cracked, and out of it stepped two chicks; the shouting shook dust off the beam. An ordinary chick is worth the price of an ordinary chick, but two chicks out of one shell is worth a mule. Boss Wan, who all his life bought only what the lamp had seen, bought the pair at the mule's price — the chicks had walked out of the shell on their own, and daylight vouched for them — and gave the empty shell to old Tu to hang on the beam. Yan San stood outside the threshold all night and never came into the hall.

万老板那条规矩，是更早一年记下的。那年狐狸把山里的公鸡拖净了，满街的蛋照出来一色空壳，论斤当吃蛋卖，春天连一夜开炕都没攒起来。账桌小伙计出身的他，把那年的灯记成了铺子的本事。双雏那夜之后，他把这条用成了买卖：先买来历，知道哪户的种，盘就好叫——来历从没把哪盘叫得更野过；再把老涂的灯整季包下。黑盘就此绝了种。

The rule Boss Wan kept was written down in a still earlier year. That year foxes dragged off every rooster in the hills, and every egg on the street candled out an empty shadow; the eggs went by weight as eating eggs, and the spring never got a single hatching night together. A tally clerk back then, he took that year's lamp for the shop's whole cleverness. After the night of the twin chicks he turned the rule into a trade: first he bought provenance, since knowing which household's seed a tray held only ever made the tray easier to call, never wilder; then he took old Tu's lamp for the whole season. Dark trays stopped existing.

人们说运气走空了。运气其实一直趴在几百只壳底下，只是开春就被一盏一盏灯点掉。这条街不是败在稀罕事不再来——是败在再没有一只壳，能黑到开炕那晚。梁上那只空壳如今最红：一枚铜钱，看一件已经孵完、再也不会有人猜的事。

People said the luck ran out. The luck was lying under those hundreds of shells all along; it was lamped away, one lamp at a time, at the start of each spring. The street did not die because rare things stopped coming — it died because not one shell was left dark enough to reach hatching night. And the shell on the beam is the busiest thing on the street now: one copper, to look at a thing already hatched, that nobody will ever have to guess again.

Note: Information entropy measures how hard a thing is to call, on average, before it breaks open: the rarer the outcome that lands, the more the news it brings is worth, and when the outcomes run neck and neck, the call is hardest of all. Once a matter is settled there is nothing left to wager on it; it has nothing to do with the warmth of a hatch-house, measuring only how deep the unknown under the shell runs.
