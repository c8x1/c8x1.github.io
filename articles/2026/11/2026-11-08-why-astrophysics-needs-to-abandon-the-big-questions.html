天体物理学为何需要放弃「大问题」（Big Questions）

Dylan Jow·Aeon·2026年11月8日·11分钟阅读·原文

在宇宙历史的宏大故事中，最重要的配角之一是尘埃。在早期宇宙里，随着星际气体云冷却、凝聚，尘埃颗粒吸收并重新辐射能量，最终促成了恒星的形成。后来，尘埃又提供了行星得以形成与成长的原材料。尘埃颗粒甚至可能正是最早的复杂有机分子得以形成的表面。然而，如果你去问大多数宇宙学家，他们会告诉你：尘埃纯粹是个干扰项（nuisance）。

In the story of cosmic history, one of the most important supporting characters is dust. As clouds of interstellar gas cooled and condensed in the early Universe, dust grains absorbed and re-emitted radiation, eventually forming stars. Later, dust provided the raw material from which planets could form and grow. Dust grains may even have been the surfaces on which the first complex organic molecules formed. Yet, if you ask most cosmologists, they’ll tell you that dust is a sheer nuisance.

在天体物理学中，「干扰项」是一个半技术性的术语。凡是妨碍天体物理学家研究其真正想研究对象的现象，都会被他们冠以「干扰项」之名。尘埃颗粒会发出偏振光，这种光可以模仿大爆炸（Big Bang）余晖——也就是宇宙微波背景（cosmic microwave background）——所发出的微弱信号，而宇宙学家已经为此花了几十年时间和数十亿美元。宇宙学家研究尘埃，不是为了揭示它在塑造宇宙中扮演的角色，而是为了把它扣除掉——「扫到地毯下面」这个说法再贴切不过了。

In astrophysics, ‘nuisance’ is a quasi-technical term. Astrophysicists call any phenomenon that gets in the way of the thing they actually want to study a ‘nuisance’. Dust grains emit polarised light that can mimic subtle signals from the afterglow of the Big Bang (the cosmic microwave background) that cosmologists have spent decades and billions of dollars trying to detect. A cosmologist studies dust not to uncover its role in shaping the cosmos, but to subtract it – the term ‘sweeping under the rug’ couldn’t be more apt.

「信号」（signal）与「干扰项」之间的这种二分，遍布天文学的许多子领域。研究一种称为「弱引力透镜」（weak gravitational lensing）现象的天文学家，利用引力造成的微弱光线畸变来绘制不可见的暗物质（dark matter）地图。对他们来说，构成我们所见一切的普通物质反而是干扰项。脉冲星计时天文学家则把称作脉冲星（pulsar）的快速自转中子星当作极其精确的时钟，用以探测引力波（gravitational waves）在星系中的经过。对他们来说，干扰项是电离星际气体——它会散射和频散脉冲星发出的光。

This dichotomy between ‘signal’ and ‘nuisance’ is found across many sub-fields in astronomy. Astronomers who study a phenomenon called ‘weak lensing’ use the subtle distortions of light caused by gravity to map invisible dark matter. For them, the ordinary matter that makes up everything we can see is a nuisance. Pulsar timing astronomers use rapidly spinning neutron stars called pulsars as extraordinarily precise clocks to detect the passage of gravitational waves through the galaxy. For them, the nuisance is ionised interstellar gas, which scatters and disperses the light from pulsars.

然而，一位天文学家的干扰项，可能是另一位天文学家的宝藏。正如激光束能揭示暗室中烟雾的复杂涡旋，脉冲星也照亮了星际介质（interstellar medium）的精细结构——星系之间那片不见灯火的空间。如果你想研究星际介质及其在塑造我们星系演化中的作用，这种散射光的气体便是无价的资源。

Yet one astronomer’s nuisance is another’s treasure. Just as a laser beam reveals the intricate swirls of smoke in a dark room, pulsars illuminate the fine structure of the interstellar medium, the unlit space between galaxies. If you want to study the interstellar medium and its role in shaping our galaxy’s evolution, the light-scattering gas is an invaluable resource.

一个现象究竟是干扰项，还是本身就值得研究的东西，显然取决于研究者及其兴趣。但在天文学这样高度协作的学科中，必须建立起共同的优先事项。伽利略（Galileo）和其他孤身在塔楼里、凭好奇心把自制望远镜随意指向天空的科学家的时代，已经一去不复返。现代天文学是一门高度专业化的学科，以大型合作为标志。这一行的工具——望远镜——由横跨多家机构的数百名科学家和工程师设计、建造和维护。但你不能就这么径直走进去要求使用。研究者必须为这些先进仪器的观测时间展开激烈竞争。为了指导这种资源如何分配，研究共同体发展出了一整套共同的优先事项。其中一些问题被抬升为「大问题」（Big Questions），其余所有问题便不可避免地沦为从属。

Whether a phenomenon is a nuisance or something worth studying in its own right clearly depends on the investigator and their interests. But in a discipline as collaborative as astronomy, shared priorities must be developed. Gone are the days of Galileo and other lone scientists in the tower, pointing telescopes built by their own hands wherever curiosity dictates. Modern astronomy is a highly specialised discipline, marked by large collaborations. The tools of the trade – telescopes – are designed, built and maintained by hundreds of scientists and engineers across many institutions. But you can’t just stroll in and ask to use one. Investigators must fiercely compete for time with these advanced instruments. To guide how that access is allocated, the research community has developed a set of shared priorities. Some questions are elevated to the status of ‘Big Questions’ to which all others inevitably become subordinate.

但这些「大问题」就一定是正确的问题吗？信号与干扰项之间、大问题与小问题之间的界限，由谁来划定？研究领域是由它试图回答的问题来定义的。如果我们想理解现代天体物理学，仅仅理解其理论的科学内容与数学内容是不够的。我们还需要理解那些塑造问题本身的人的力量——正是它们使某些问题获得优先，而另一些问题被视作无关紧要，或仅仅沦为通往更大目标的手段。

But are these Big Questions necessarily the right ones? Who decides where to draw the boundaries between signal and nuisance, or big and small? Fields of enquiry are defined by the questions they seek to answer. If we want to understand modern astrophysics, it is not enough to understand the scientific and mathematical content of its theories. We also need to understand the human forces that shape the questions themselves and cause some questions to be prioritised, and other questions to be viewed as irrelevant or merely means to those bigger ends.

要找出主导这一领域的「大问题」，只需看看那些将在未来几十年塑造这门学科的旗舰项目。它们包括平方公里阵列天文台（Square Kilometre Array Observatory）、詹姆斯·韦布空间望远镜（James Webb Space Telescope）、薇拉·C·鲁宾天文台（Vera C Rubin Observatory）、南希·格雷斯·罗曼空间望远镜（Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope），以及激光干涉空间天线（Laser Interferometer Space Antenna）。这些项目每一个在其生命周期内都将耗资数十亿美元。

To identify the Big Questions that dominate the field, you need look no further than the flagship projects set to shape the discipline for decades. These include the Square Kilometre Array Observatory, the James Webb Space Telescope, the Vera C Rubin Observatory, the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope, and the Laser Interferometer Space Antenna. Each of these projects will cost several billion dollars over its lifetime.

当我们审视这些项目公开宣称的科学目标时，一些一致的主题浮现出来。例如，位于英国焦德雷尔班克（Jodrell Bank）的平方公里阵列天文台，是一座将在可预见未来定义射电天文学领域的巨型射电阵列。它的网站上列有一份科学目标清单。在这份清单上，早期宇宙、引力波、暗能量（dark energy）与宇宙学都排在恒星、星际介质和生命起源之前。备受媒体关注的詹姆斯·韦布空间望远镜的官方页面，同样把早期宇宙——「宇宙黎明」（cosmic dawn）——排在恒星、行星和尘埃这类更为平淡的事项之前。这类优先次序常常反映在媒体对这些项目的报道中，进而塑造着年轻研究者、政界人士、资助方和普通公众关于哪些问题最重要的认知。

When we look at the stated science goals of these projects, consistent themes emerge. For example, the Square Kilometre Array Observatory at Jodrell Bank in the UK is a massive radio array that will define the field of radio astronomy for the foreseeable future. Its website contains a list of science goals. On that list, the early Universe, gravitational waves, dark energy and cosmology are all listed before stars, the interstellar medium and the origins of life. The official page for the much-covered James Webb Space Telescope similarly lists the early Universe – the ‘cosmic dawn’ – before more prosaic matters such as stars, planets and dust. Such priorities are often reflected in media coverage of these projects, which further shape perceptions among young researchers, politicians, funders and the general public about which questions matter most.

大问题与小问题之间的界限，往往正好落在「基础」物理学与「凌乱」天体物理学的分界线上。这一点体现在许多研究暗物质的宇宙学家的措辞中。几十年来，暗物质研究一直聚焦于最大的物理尺度——巨大的星系团——在这些尺度上，暗物质的引力效应可以直接推断出来。而在小尺度上，在星系内部及周围，暗物质的效应与星际气体的复杂动力学纠缠在一起。用宇宙学家普遍采用的术语来说：小尺度上暗物质的物理学，被星际气体的天体物理学所遮蔽。这种表述暗含着一种观念：天体物理学凌乱而复杂，最终从属于干净的基础物理学。

The boundaries between the big and small questions often fall along the lines between ‘fundamental’ physics and ‘messy’ astrophysics. This is reflected in the language of many cosmologists who study dark matter, for example. For decades, the study of dark matter has focused on the largest physical scales – giant clusters of galaxies – where the gravitational effects of dark matter can be directly inferred. On small scales, within and around galaxies, dark matter’s effects become entangled with the complex dynamics of interstellar gas. To use the terminology that is widely adopted by cosmologists: the physics of dark matter on small scales is obscured by the astrophysics of interstellar gas. Implicit in this framing is the idea that astrophysics is messy and complicated, and, ultimately, subordinate to clean, fundamental physics.

年轻研究者在踏进实验室之前很久，就已领教过这种物理学凌驾于天体物理学之上的所谓优势。像许多孩子一样，我从小就热爱太空。父母给我买了一台业余望远镜，我在后院度过了许多个夜晚，熟悉了冬季的夜空（夏季星空更难捉摸，因为它要到我上床睡觉之后才升起来）。但到进入研究生院时，我童年早期对星空的那份热爱已基本淡忘。和当时的许多同龄人一样，我想学的是理论粒子物理。

Young researchers encounter this supposed predominance of physics over astrophysics long before they set foot in a laboratory. Growing up, I loved space, as many children do. My parents bought me an amateur telescope, and I spent many nights in the backyard becoming familiar with the winter night sky (the summer sky was more elusive because it appeared after my bedtime). But, by the time I entered graduate school, I had largely forgotten about my early childhood passion for the heavens. Like many of my peers at the time, I wanted to study theoretical particle physics.

粒子物理学在流行文化中享有某种神话般的地位，被视为自然科学的顶峰。毕竟，粒子物理学家宣称的目标不只是关于粒子的理论，而是万物理论（a theory of everything）。当然，这样的理论只在有限的意义上才是关于「万物」的——它描述所有物质在最基本（微观）层面上如何行为；姑且不论由于数学上的复杂性，这种描述就连基本的化学反应都无法在功能上加以刻画。但当「基本」被抬到其他一切之上时，就不难理解为什么一颗易受影响的年轻心灵会被它俘获了。

Particle physics enjoys a kind of mythical status in popular culture as the apex of the natural sciences. After all, the purported goal of particle physicists is not just a theory of particles, but a theory of everything. Of course, such a theory would be a theory of ‘everything’ only in the limited sense that it would describe how all matter behaves on a fundamental (microscopic) level – never mind the fact that such a description would be functionally useless to describe even basic chemical reactions due to the mathematical complexity. But when the fundamental is elevated above everything else, it’s easy to see why an impressionable young mind might be captivated.

最终，我在研究生阶段并没有去学理论粒子物理。竞争太激烈了——班里几乎所有人都在争夺那寥寥无几的名额。于是，我重新燃起对天文学的热爱，决定改学天体物理学。但说实话，我当时多少把天体物理学看作一个安慰奖。

In the end, I did not study theoretical particle physics as a graduate student. The competition was intense – almost all of my classmates were vying for the few positions available. So, I rekindled my love of astronomy and decided to study astrophysics. But the truth is, at the time, I viewed astrophysics as something of a consolation prize.

这种把基础物理学置于一切之上的等级秩序，是一种历史的偶然。在整个20世纪下半叶，天文学从一门独立学科转变为物理学的一个子学科。在历史上，天文学是一门测绘、编目和观测的科学。但随着世纪中叶核物理学的成就、物理宇宙学的兴起，以及暗物质与暗能量的发现，一个将两门学科融合起来的进程开始了。

This hierarchy, which ranks fundamental physics above all else, is a historical contingency. Over the course of the second half of the 20th century, astronomy transformed from a distinct discipline into a sub-discipline of physics. Historically, astronomy was a science of mapping, cataloguing and observation. But the successes of mid-century nuclear physics, the rise of physical cosmology and the discovery of dark matter and dark energy began a process that fused the two disciplines.

广义相对论（general relativity）——它把引力描述为时空结构的弯曲——的发现，提供了一个新框架，使人们得以对宇宙整体的动力学进行定量研究。宇宙学，这门此前属于哲学家的领域，成了物理学家的领域。在20世纪的最后几十年里，这一框架带来了暗物质与暗能量的双重发现——两者都是粒子物理学标准模型（standard model of particle physics）无法解释的神秘物质。关键在于，如果没有天文学家的观测，标准模型中的这些缺口本会完全无人察觉。

The discovery of general relativity , which describes gravity as a warping of the fabric of space and time, provided a new framework that enabled the quantitative study of the dynamics of the Universe as a whole. Cosmology, which had previously been the domain of philosophers, became the domain of physicists. In the closing decades of the 20th century, this framework enabled the dual discovery of dark matter and dark energy – both mysterious substances unaccounted for by the standard model of particle physics. Crucially, these gaps in the standard model would have gone completely undetected without the observations of astronomers.

于是，天空从一种本身即值得研究的对象，变成了检验基础物理学的新实验室。这一转变保留在我们的词汇里：「天文学」（astronomy）一词被降级，让位于更显赫的「天体物理学」（astrophysics）。

The heavens, then, transformed from an object of study in its own right to a new laboratory for testing fundamental physics. This shift is preserved in our vocabulary: the term ‘astronomy’ was demoted in favour of the more prestigious term ‘astrophysics’.

天体物理学的崛起，在一定程度上是由冷战（Cold War）的地缘政治推动的：物理学的经费出现前所未有的激增，物理学在公众想象中的文化声望也随之上升。各国政府向物理学巨型工程投入巨资，巩固了一种致力于基础高能物理研究的大型合作文化。那是粒子加速器（particle accelerator）的时代——亚原子粒子被加速到极高的能量，以检验只在那些能量尺度上才会显现的物理学理论。但随后冷战结束，资金流断了。1993年，在已经花费数十亿美元之后，美国国会取消了位于得克萨斯州的超导超级对撞机（Superconducting Super Collider）项目。

The rise of astro physics was, in part, fuelled by the geopolitics of the Cold War, which saw an unprecedented surge in funding for physics, in addition to a rise in the cultural prestige of physics in the public imagination . Governments invested large sums of money in physics mega-projects, cementing a culture of large collaborations dedicated to the study of fundamental, high-energy physics. This was the era of the particle accelerator , in which subatomic particles are accelerated to extremely high energies to test theories of physics that manifest only at those energy scales. But then the Cold War ended, and the cash flow stopped. In 1993, the US Congress cancelled the Superconducting Super Collider project in Texas, after billions had already been spent.

一夜之间，整整一代粒子物理学家发现，自己在地球上失去了检验理论的实验工具。而天文学提供了一个颇具吸引力的替代方案。宇宙证明了自己才是终极的、免费的粒子加速器：其中充斥着超新星、碰撞的黑洞和大爆炸之类的剧烈事件，达到的能量尺度是任何地面加速器都永远无法企及的。只要能把真实天体物理环境中的种种复杂因素建模并处理掉，天空就会成为物理学的完美新实验室。

Suddenly, a generation of particle physicists found themselves without the experimental tools to test their theories on Earth. Astronomy, however, provided an attractive alternative. The Universe revealed itself to be the ultimate, free particle accelerator: full of violent events, such as supernovae, colliding black holes and the Big Bang, reaching energy scales that no terrestrial collider ever could. If only the complications of real astrophysical environments could be modelled and accounted for, then the heavens would be the perfect new laboratory for physics.

恰恰是这套逻辑，把我这个初出茅庐的研究生说服进了天体物理学。当时，我把天文学视为达到目的的手段，是通往万物理论道路上的一块垫脚石。我对天文学中那些凌乱的复杂问题感兴趣，仅仅是因为理解了它们，我们便可以把它们弃置一旁。

It was precisely this logic that sold me on astrophysics as a new graduate student. At the time, I saw astronomy as a means to an end, a stepping stone on the path to a theory of everything. I was interested in the messy complications of astronomy only insofar as understanding them would enable us to disregard them.

今天，天文学的这种「物理学化」已在这门学科的文化规范上留下深深的印记。现代天体物理学的特征，是庞大的、高度中央化的国际合作，以及一种往往心照不宣的信念：「基础」物理学居于科学金字塔的顶端，而真正的「天体物理学」问题不过是些干扰项。

Today, this ‘physicisation’ of astronomy has left a deep imprint on the cultural norms of the discipline. Modern astrophysics is characterised by massive, hyper-centralised international collaborations, and an often tacit belief that ‘fundamental’ physics sits at the top of the scientific pyramid, while the questions of real ‘astrophysics’ are mere nuisances.

这种把「基本」抬升于「具体」之上的意识形态，清晰地体现在现代大数据天文学的架构之中。过去几十年的标志，是实验产出海量数据。然而，这些数据处理流水线并非中立的资料采集机器；事实上，它们的设计本身就反映并强化着一种制度性偏见——把天体物理学视为通往基础真理的障碍。其逻辑是：只要数据足够多，那些在特定宇宙环境中呈现的丰富多样的天体物理现象，就可以被统计建模并平均掉，只留下最基本的东西。

This ideological elevation of the fundamental over the particular is evident in the architecture of modern big-data astronomy. The past few decades have been characterised by experiments yielding massive volumes of data. Yet, these pipelines are not neutral data-gathering machines; indeed, their very design reflects and reinforces the institutional bias towards astrophysics as a barrier to fundamental truth. The logic is that, with enough data, the rich and diverse astrophysical phenomena that manifest in particular cosmic environments can be statistically modelled and averaged away, leaving only what is fundamental.

以弱引力透镜现象为例：天文学家测量引力如何使遥远星系的形状发生畸变，并借此绘制宇宙中暗物质的分布图——否则暗物质并不可见。然而，弱引力透镜对宇宙中所有物质的引力效应同样敏感——既包括暗物质，也包括「普通」物质。支配普通物质的物理相互作用，使人们难以推断暗物质背后的分布。现代弱透镜巡天的数据分析流水线，把天体物理环境的复杂物理压缩成一组「干扰参数」（nuisance parameters），而只要数据足够多，这些参数就可以被平均掉。丰富的天体物理现象，从研究一开始、在巡天设计本身之中，就被框定为通往基础真理的统计障碍。

Consider, for example, the phenomenon of weak gravitational lensing, where astronomers measure how gravity distorts the shapes of distant galaxies. They can use this to build a map of the dark matter in the Universe, which is otherwise invisible. However, weak gravitational lensing is equally sensitive to the gravitational effects of all matter in the Universe – the dark matter and the ‘ordinary’ matter. The physical interactions that govern ordinary matter complicate inferences of the underlying dark matter distribution. Data analysis pipelines for modern weak lensing surveys flatten the complex physics of astrophysical environments to a set of ‘nuisance parameters’, which, with enough data, can be averaged away. Rich astrophysical phenomena are, from the very outset and in the survey design itself, framed as statistical obstacles to fundamental truths.

转向人工智能（AI）的趋势进一步强化了这一范式。现代天文台产出的拍字节级数据，与训练深度学习架构所需的海量数据集之间，存在着一种天然的共生关系。在炒作的驱动下，只要一个项目承诺使用人工智能，就有助于吸引经费和计算资源的配置——即便它真正做出发现的能力仍不明朗。

This paradigm is further reinforced by the turn towards artificial intelligence (AI). A natural symbiosis exists between the petabytes of data generated by modern observatories and the massive datasets needed to train deep learning architectures. Driven by the hype, if a project promises to use AI, it helps attract funding and allocation of computational resources, even when its capacity for genuine discovery remains unclear.

人工智能基础模型（Foundation AI models）被奉为消化海量数据集的最佳方式——这些数据集大到无法进行细致的人工分析——并有望直接从原始的天空图像中自动推断出复杂的天体物理过程。但是，这些技术学到的统计模式，与产生观测结果背后的物理机制之间，存在一种根本性的脱节。训练人工智能辨认猫的样子时，你可以用一组已知是猫和非猫的图像；而对于星系图像，却不存在哪一组图像的相关天体物理是全部已知的。于是，模型只能用取自天体物理模拟的合成数据来训练。然而，计算能力的限制迫使这些模拟对研究对象本身的物理做出简化近似。结果，人工智能模型继承了未经检验的假设，而这些假设关乎它们本想阐明的物理过程。

Foundation AI models are heralded as the best way to digest datasets too massive for detailed human analysis, promising to automatically infer complex astrophysics from raw images of the sky. But there is a fundamental disconnect between the statistical patterns these technologies learn and the underlying physical mechanisms that generate the observations. Whereas you might train an AI to learn what a cat looks like with a set of images of known cats and non-cats, there is no set of galaxy images where all the relevant astrophysics is known. Instead, models need to be trained with synthetic data drawn from astrophysical simulations. However, computational limitations force these simulations to rely on simplified approximations of the very physics in question. As a result, AI models inherit untested assumptions about the physical processes they aim to illuminate.

如果你的目标只是处理掉海量数据中的复杂天体物理因素，从而推断其背后的基础物理学，这并不是问题。毕竟，这些模型在识别并修正数据中微妙的相关性方面出类拔萃。但如果你的目标是理解天体物理现象本身的复杂物理，好处就没那么明显了。

This is not a problem if your goal is simply to account for complex astrophysics in massive data sets to infer the underlying fundamental physics. These models are, after all, exceptional at identifying and correcting for subtle correlations in the data. But if your goal is to understand the complex physics of the astrophysical phenomena themselves, the benefit is less obvious.

于是，现代天体物理学似乎朝着一个范式倾斜：对海量数据集建模，以揭示基本常量。这一范式与制度上获得优先的问题、以及用来回答这些问题的工具相互强化。然而，这种强化循环可能制造出一种全领域已就「最重要的问题是什么」达成共识的假象，从而遮蔽许多一线天文学家的日常现实。

Thus, modern astrophysics appears to be oriented towards a paradigm of modelling massive data sets to uncover fundamental constants. This paradigm is mutually reinforced by the questions that are institutionally prioritised and the tools used to answer them. However, this loop of reinforcement can create an illusion of field-wide consensus around what the most important questions are, which obscures the daily realities of many working astronomers.

这一点在围绕「哈勃张力」（Hubble tension）的讨论中体现得最为明显——即测量宇宙膨胀速率（哈勃常数，Hubble constant）的不同方法给出了相互矛盾的结果。在各种白皮书和科普文章中，哈勃张力都被渲染为物理学的一场深刻危机。如果这一差异是真实的（而非统计上的偶然涨落），那么它可能是关于暗能量本质的重要线索——暗能量正是驱动宇宙膨胀的东西。

This is nowhere more visible than in the discourse surrounding the ‘Hubble tension’ – the fact that different methods of measuring the Universe’s expansion rate (the Hubble constant) yield contradictory results. Across white papers and popular science articles, the Hubble tension is touted as a profound crisis in physics. If the discrepancy is real (as opposed to a statistical fluke), then it could be an important hint about the nature of dark energy, which drives the expansion.

然而，在天体物理学研究的第一线，态度截然不同。在我参加过的几乎每一场关于哈勃张力的报告会上，一线天体物理学家的反应都出奇地平淡。如果这个差异真是现代宇宙学的「大白鲸」（great white whale），为什么这么多研究者只报以耸肩？

And yet, in the trenches of astrophysics research, attitudes are markedly different. At nearly every talk I have attended on the Hubble tension, the reaction from working astrophysicists is surprisingly muted. If this discrepancy is the great white whale of modern cosmology, why do so many researchers respond with a shrug?

现实是，大多数科学家都是充满热情、近乎偏执的生物，会把人生的许多年头投入别人可能很难在意的高度冷门课题。这样的人自然而然地觉得自己的工作本身就有趣味，而不只是通往某个更大问题的一级台阶。当然，科学家的动机是复杂的，这些动机既受制度环境塑造，也塑造着他们职业生涯的长期轨迹。但在日常层面，许多天文学家确实就是非常在乎尘埃、气体动力学，或者宇宙磁场。

The reality is that most scientists are passionate and obsessive creatures, devoting many years of their lives to extremely niche topics that others might struggle to care about. Such people naturally find their work intrinsically interesting, not just as a step towards some bigger question. Of course, scientists have complex motivations that are shaped by their institutional environments and that shape the long-term trajectory of their careers. But, on a day-to-day basis, many astronomers really do just care a lot about dust, or gas dynamics, or cosmic magnetic fields.

就我自己而言，离纯物理学越远，我就越是重新发现对天文学本身的热爱。无论天文学能否挖掘出新的物理学基本定律，关于我们在宇宙中的位置、以及身为人类意味着什么，它都有很多话可说。自哥白尼（Copernicus）以来，天文学逐渐把人类在宇宙中的位置降格：从占据万物名副其实的中心，变为只是栖身于一颗不起眼的行星，围绕一颗不起眼的恒星运转，身处一个可能无限的宇宙中一个不起眼的星系。与此同时，随着已知宇宙边界的扩展，我们越来越理解生命在宇宙中的稀有与脆弱。因此，天文学在人类这个物种的故事中扮演着关键角色。我仍然希望天文学能把我们引向新的基础物理学，但那种希望已不再是我仰望星空的驱动力。我怀疑，我的许多天文学家同行也是如此。

For myself, the further away I got from physics proper, the more I rediscovered my love of astronomy for its own sake. Whether or not astronomy unearths new fundamental laws of physics, it has much to tell us about our place in the Universe and what it means to be human. Since Copernicus, astronomy has gradually demoted humanity’s position in the cosmos, from occupying the literal centre of everything, to merely occupying an unremarkable planet, orbiting an unremarkable star, in an unremarkable galaxy in a potentially infinite Universe. At the same time, as the bounds of the known Universe expand, we have increasingly understood the rarity and fragility of life in the cosmos. Astronomy, therefore, plays a critical role in the story of the human species. While I remain hopeful that astronomy can lead us to new fundamental physics, that hope is no longer the driving force of my interest in the heavens. I suspect this is the case for many of my fellow astronomers.

尽管如此，研究者仍不得不玩一场修辞游戏，在预定的「大问题」框架内为自己高度专门的研究辩护。当你申请学术职位时，得到的忠告是：一定要、一定要、一定要把你的工作与「大问题」挂钩。由于招聘或评审委员会通常由专业背景各异的教员组成，申请人无法指望委员会恰好对自己的冷门课题怀有内在兴趣。把你的科学研究放到与「大问题」的关系中来辩护，申请更有可能成功——哪怕委员会成员个人对这些大问题并无特别的热情。

Despite this, investigators are required to play a rhetorical game, justifying their deeply specific research within the context of the predetermined Big Questions. When you apply for academic jobs, the advice is: Always, always, always link your work to the Big Questions . Since hiring or grant committees typically comprise faculty with a diverse range of expertise, an applicant cannot rely on the committee sharing an intrinsic interest in their niche topic. Justifying your science in relation to the Big Questions is more likely to lead to a successful application, even if individually none of the members of the committee are particularly passionate about those questions.

科学期刊也在强化这场修辞游戏。最有声望的期刊要求其论文代表该领域的重大进展。换句话说，它们要求所发表的研究在预先定义的「大问题」上取得进展。研究者因此受到激励，在这一框架下包装自己的论文。我自己就写过这样的论文：把研究动机表述为朝解决哈勃张力迈出的一步。严格说来这也没错，但这样的表述歪曲了我当初投身那项研究的缘由，以及我觉得它真正有趣的原因。

Scientific journals reinforce this rhetorical game. The most prestigious journals demand that their articles represent significant advancements in the field. In other words, they require that the research they publish make progress on the pre-defined Big Questions. Investigators are incentivised to frame their papers in this context. I have personally written papers that motivate the work as a step towards resolving the Hubble tension. While technically true, this framing misrepresents the reasons I embarked upon that research and the real reasons I find it interesting.

这并不是说在研究共同体内拥有共同的优先事项有什么错——事实上，这不可避免。但重要的是认识到，这些优先事项是历史的偶然，而且在某种程度上是任意的。当研究者玩这场为研究辩护的修辞游戏时，只要把工作与某个物理学基本问题挂上钩，游戏便宣告结束。但为什么到此为止？寻找暗能量或暗物质，为什么就天然比研究星际尘埃的生命周期更正当？「基本」问题在内在正当性上，并不比挡在它们路上的干扰项更多，也不更少。毋宁说，哪些问题本身就是目的、哪些问题还需要进一步辩护，是我们自己选择的结果。

This is not to suggest that having shared priorities across a research community is somehow wrong – indeed, it is inevitable. But it is important to recognise that these priorities are historically contingent and, to some degree, arbitrary. When investigators play the rhetorical game of justifying their research, the game ends when they link their work to a fundamental question in physics. But why stop there? Why is the search for dark energy or dark matter inherently more justified than the lifecycle of interstellar dust? The ‘fundamental’ questions are no more or less intrinsically justified than the nuisances that get in their way. Rather, we choose which questions are ends in themselves and which require further justification.

许多评论者已大做文章，渲染当今高能物理学面临的危机。地面上的对撞机实验未能显示它们为之而建的「新物理」的任何迹象，而天体物理学能就基础物理学告诉我们什么，也很可能正接近极限。即使哈勃张力得到证实，我们也可能永远无法确定关于暗能量的正确物理理论；即便我们能更好地刻画暗物质的引力行为，它的粒子本质或许永远是个谜。「大问题」们开始失去光泽，一些人开始追问，投向大型项目的数十亿美元是否正在收益递减。但如果这对高能物理学是一场危机，对天文学却并无危机可言。天文学家将继续探究宇宙所呈现的一切古怪而奇妙的事物。

Many commentators have made a great deal of the crisis that faces high-energy physics today. Terrestrial collider experiments have failed to reveal any signs of the new physics they were built to find, and we may well be reaching the limit of what astrophysics can tell us about fundamental physics. Even if the Hubble tension is confirmed, we may never settle on the correct physical theory of dark energy. Even as we better characterise the gravitational behaviour of dark matter, its particle nature may remain a mystery forever. The Big Questions are beginning to lose their lustre, and some are beginning to ask whether the billions of dollars spent on mega-projects are diminishing in their returns. But if this is a crisis for high-energy physics, there is no crisis for astronomy. Astronomers will continue to investigate all the weird and wonderful things the Universe has to offer.

如果你是一位读到这篇文章的天文学家——或者任何愿意参与这场思想实验的人——我请你设想如下情形：如果明天你可以揭开暗能量的本质，或者发现外星生命，你会选择哪一个？两者都能稳获诺贝尔奖，但哪一个在感觉上才是更重大、更有意义的发现？虽然我只能代表自己发言，但我怀疑许多天文学家——甚至包括正牌的天体物理学家——都会选择后者。

If you’re an astronomer reading this – or anyone who would like to play along – I invite you to consider the following hypothetical. If, tomorrow, you could uncover the nature of dark energy, or discover alien life, which would you choose? Both would guarantee a Nobel, but which one actually feels like the bigger, more meaningful discovery? Although I can speak only for myself, I suspect many astronomers – even bona fide astrophysicists – would choose the latter.

天文学在自然科学中占有一个独特的位置。它的直接实用效益有限——离地球越远越是如此。很难想象对星系演化的细致理解，会在任何可预见的时间跨度内直接带来某种技术上的好处。然而，天文学激发公众想象的能力，没有哪门科学能及。这不是因为天文学有可能在高能量尺度上揭示新物理，而是因为作为一个物种，我们始终仰望天空，试图理解自己在宇宙中的位置。

Astronomy has a unique place among the natural sciences. Its immediate practical benefits are limited – especially the further away from Earth you go. It’s hard to imagine a detailed understanding of galaxy evolution directly leading to some technological benefit in any foreseeable timeline. Yet astronomy excites the public imagination like no other science. It’s not because astronomy has the potential to uncover new physics at high energy scales. It’s because we, as a species, have always looked up to try to understand our place in the Universe.

孩提时代透过后院的望远镜仰望时，我对基础物理学毫无概念。但望着猎户座星云（Orion Nebula）那团朦胧的雾霭，意识到自己正在见证恒星的诞生——那种惊叹之情，实在无以言表。如果天文学必须依照某些「大问题」来为自己正名，那就让它们是这两个吧：我们从何而来？我们在宇宙中的位置是什么？

Peering through my backyard telescope as a child, I had no concept of fundamental physics. But looking at the fuzzy haze of the Orion Nebula and realising that I was watching the birth of stars – well, there are no words to describe the sense of wonder I felt. If astronomy must justify itself according to some Big Questions, let them be these: where did we come from, and what is our place in the cosmos?

归根结底，一个领域的优先事项不是不可更改的事实，而是一种选择。优先事项的转变不必被视为危机，反而可以当作一个契机，让我们追问自己当初为什么要研究所研究的东西——一个做出不同选择、使选择更好地契合那些理由的机会。这样来看，天文学家有机会把这一领域的优先事项从物理学的还原论视角上扭转过来，重新收回「干扰项」的价值。

Ultimately, the priorities of a field are not immutable facts but a choice. Shifting priorities need not be seen as a crisis, but as an opportunity to ask ourselves why we study what we study in the first place – an opportunity to make different choices that better align with those reasons. Astronomers, then, have an opportunity to reorient the priorities of the field away from a reductionist view of physics, and to reclaim the value of the nuisance.
