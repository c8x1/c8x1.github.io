为什么存在着某物而非一无所有：一个新答案

Andrew T Brenner·Aeon·2026年11月14日·11分钟阅读·原文

1714年，戈特弗里德·威廉·莱布尼茨（Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz）发问：「为什么存在着某物，而不是一无所有？」哲学家们早就想知道宇宙为何存在，但世人公认，正是莱布尼茨以如此精确的字眼框定了这个问题。认真思量之下，这个问题令人头晕目眩。世界这个再明显不过的事实——事物存在——可以从看似平淡琐碎，转变为非常惊人，甚至彻头彻尾的怪异。路德维希·维特根斯坦（Ludwig Wittgenstein）在《逻辑哲学论》（Tractatus，1921年）中写道：「神秘的不是世界是怎样的，而是世界存在着。」这句话正道出了这种惊异。1818年，阿图尔·叔本华（Arthur Schopenhauer）称这个问题是「一个深不可测且始终令人不安的谜」。而世上现存最古老的宗教文本之一《梨俱吠陀》（Rigveda）则暗示，就连宇宙的「监督者」（adhyakṣaḥ）也未必知道答案。纯粹的存在本身就令人困惑。

Writing in 1714, Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz asked: ‘Why is there something rather than nothing?’ Philosophers have long wondered why the Universe exists, but Leibniz is credited with framing the issue in precisely these terms. The question is head-spinning when properly considered. This very obvious fact about the world – that things exist – can shift from seeming mundane and trivial to very surprising, even downright weird. Ludwig Wittgenstein expressed this astonishment when he wrote in his Tractatus (1921): ‘Not how the world is, is the mystical, but that it is.’ Arthur Schopenhauer in 1818 called the question ‘an unfathomable and always disquieting riddle’. And the Rigveda, one of the world’s oldest surviving religious texts, suggests that even the ‘overseer’ (adhyakṣaḥ) of the cosmos might not know the answer. Sheer existence is baffling.

时至今日，哲学家们仍在讨论这个问题，但对答案并无任何共识。有人提出，这类问题根本不可能有答案。1973年，德国哲学家卡尔·亨佩尔（Carl Hempel）写道：

Philosophers continue to discuss the question today, but there isn’t any consensus about the answer. Some have suggested that it’s the sort of question that couldn’t possibly have an answer. In 1973, the German philosopher Carl Hempel wrote:

他的想法是：假如你想解释的是为什么世界上竟然存在企鹅，那么你的解释就不能诉诸某只企鹅——毕竟那只企鹅正是你要解释的东西之一。同理，如果我们要寻找的是「为什么竟然存在着任何东西」的解释，那么这个解释就不能诉诸某个存在物，因为那个存在物正是我们试图解释的东西之一。但在亨佩尔看来，对「为什么存在着东西」不可能有任何其他类型的解释，因为任何关于「为什么某物存在」的解释都必须诉诸某个别的存在物。

His idea is that if you were trying to explain why, for instance, any penguins at all exist, then you couldn’t appeal to a penguin in your explanation – after all, that penguin is one of the things you are trying to explain. Similarly, if we’re looking for an explanation for why anything at all exists, then our explanation cannot appeal to an existing thing, since that existing thing is one of the things we are trying to explain. But Hempel thinks that there couldn’t possibly be any other sort of explanation for why anything exists, since any explanation for why something exists has to appeal to some other existing thing.

对亨佩尔这一疑虑的一种回应，是索性去做他声称绝无可能做到的事：为「为什么存在着某物而非一无所有」提供一个「不预设某物存在」的解释。许多哲学家已经尝试这样做了。但这些解释存在一个问题。

One response to Hempel’s concern is to go ahead and do what he claimed can’t possibly be done: offer an explanation for why there is something rather than nothing, an explanation that ‘does not assume the existence of something’. Many philosophers have attempted to do just that. But there is something wrong with these explanations.

首先，我们为什么会期待「为什么存在着某物而非一无所有」有一个解释？莱布尼茨认为这个问题必定有答案，因为照他的说法，「无」比起「有」要「简单且容易」得多。正是这一想法，在很大程度上解释了人们为何面对「存在着某物而非一无所有」这一事实时深感惊异。

Why might we expect there to be an explanation for why there is something rather than nothing in the first place? Leibniz thought there must be an explanation for why there is something rather than nothing because, as he put it, nothing would have been so much ‘simpler and easier’ than something. It is this thought that lies behind much of the astonishment people have expressed at the fact that there is something rather than nothing.

倘若世界处于我们按惯常标准衡量最有可能的状态，那么一个空无一物的世界正是我们所应预期的。我这里想到的是奥卡姆剃刀（Ockham's Razor）：对世界的较简单解释，比较复杂的解释更可能正确。举例来说，看看现今存活的各物种之间共享的所有那些性状。鲸和人类有相似的骨骼结构，尽管它们生活在迥异的环境中。查尔斯·达尔文（Charles Darwin）的一个关键洞见是：尽管可以设想，一位造物主恰好想要这些各不相同的物种——尽管环境与生活方式不同——拥有相似的性状，但假定它们共享这些性状是因为这些性状源自某个共同祖先，就要简单得多。而这一解释本身的简单性，就给了我们一个极佳的理由去认为那些共同的性状源自共同祖先。我们不断依赖这样一个假定：与证据相容的最简单的世界说明最可能正确。这一点，我们在科学中如此，在日常生活中亦然。

A world in which nothing exists is how we should expect the world to be if it takes what would, by our usual criteria, be its most probable state. What I have in mind here is Ockham’s Razor. Simpler accounts of the world are more likely to be correct than complex ones. For example, consider all the traits shared among the species alive today. Whales and humans have similar bone structures, despite the fact that they live in very different environments. One of Charles Darwin’s key insights was that, while it might conceivably have been true that a creator just happened to want these various species to have similar traits, despite their different environments and lifestyles, it is so much simpler to suppose that they share those traits because those traits are derived from a common ancestor. And the simplicity itself of that explanation gives us an excellent reason to think that the common traits are derived from a common ancestor. We constantly rely on the assumption that the simplest account of the world compatible with our evidence is most likely to be correct. This is something we do in science as well as in everyday life.

过去人们对「为什么存在着某物而非一无所有」提出的许多解释，都没有正视这个显见的事实：「无」本会比「有」「简单且容易」得多。这些解释说，存在着这样那样的理由，说明为什么有某物而非一无所有，却完全不去处理下面这个想法：我们本应预先期待一无所有，因为倘若曾经一无所有，实在就会简单得多。

Many of the proposed explanations for why there is something rather than nothing that have been offered in the past do not address the apparent fact that nothing would have been so much ‘simpler and easier’ than something. The proposed explanations say that there is such-and-such a reason for why there is something rather than nothing, but they do nothing to address the thought that we should antecedently expect there to be nothing because, if there had been nothing, then reality would have been so much simpler.

即便什么都不存在，也仍会存在大量非本体论真理

There would have been a whole lot of non-ontological truths even if nothing had existed

我想在此一石二鸟：为「为什么存在着某物而非一无所有」提供一个解释，同时消解「无本会比有简单得多」这一疑虑。而且，就我所知，这恰好还是一个全新的解释——考虑到这个问题之古老，新解释实在难得一见。

What I’d like to do here is kill two birds with one stone: to offer an explanation for why there is something rather than nothing, which simultaneously dissolves the suspicion that nothing would have been so much simpler than something. This also happens to be, as far as I can tell, a new explanation for why there is something rather than nothing, and new explanations are hard to come by given the antiquity of the question.

我想从一个重要的洞见出发：有一些真理，并不是关于存在物的真理。既然它们不是关于存在物的真理，我们姑且称之为「非本体论」真理。与之相对的是「本体论」真理——关于存在物的真理，比如「地球绕太阳运行」，或者「世上树比狗多」。存在非本体论真理，这一观察也曾被其他为「为什么存在着某物而非一无所有」所困扰的哲学家表达过。例如，哲学家约翰·莱斯利（John Leslie）在多篇论著中主张：之所以存在着某物而非一无所有，仅仅是因为「存在着某物而非一无所有」是好的。姑且不论这是否算一个成功的解释，请注意它诉诸的是一个非本体论真理：即便一无所有，也仍然存在着「实在不如它本可以的那样好」这一真理。而这确实显得可信。

I would like to start with the important insight that there are truths that are not truths about existing things. Since they are not truths about existing things, we’ll call them ‘non-ontological’ truths. Contrast these with ‘ontological’ truths – truths that are about existing things, like the truth that Earth orbits the Sun, or that there are more trees in the world than there are dogs. The observation that there are non-ontological truths is an insight expressed by other philosophers puzzled by the question of why there is something rather than nothing. For example, in a number of publications, the philosopher John Leslie has argued that there is something rather than nothing simply because it is good for there to be something rather than nothing. Setting aside whether this is a successful explanation, note that it appeals to a non-ontological truth: even if there was nothing, then there would have still been the truth that reality was less good than it could have been. And that does seem plausible.

一旦注意到存在非本体论真理，稍加反省便可以看出：即便什么都不存在，也仍会有许许多多这样的真理。下面举几个例子：

再说一遍，这些只是几个例子，我还可以列出许多别的。诚然，我们如何知道这些非本体论真理，颇为神秘。既然它们不涉及存在物，我们的认知官能竟能「把握」它们，就令人惊讶了——认知官能能把握那些我们可与之互动的对象（比如我眼前这条我能用眼睛看到、用手抚摸的狗），倒是顺理成章的事。

Again, these are just a few examples. I could list many others. Admittedly, it’s mysterious how we know about these non-ontological truths. Since they don’t concern existing things, it’s surprising that our cognitive faculties can ‘get a grip’ on them, in the way that they can get a grip on objects with which we can interact (eg, the dog in front of me that I can see with my eyes and pet with my hand).

可以设想有一些可能世界——实在的各种可能样子——它们并不仅仅是事物可能如此的方式

There can conceivably be possible worlds – possible ways for reality to be – that are not merely ways things could be

一个直接的反对意见是：如果真的什么都没有，那么也不会有任何非本体论真理。一个真正空无的世界将一无所有，连任何种类的真理都没有。哲学家泰伦·戈德施密特（Tyron Goldschmidt）在《存在之谜》（The Puzzle of Existence，2013年）中写道，「为什么竟然存在着存在者」这一问题是：

An immediate objection is that if there were truly nothing then there would not be any non-ontological truths either. A truly empty world would have nothing at all, including truths of any sort. The philosopher Tyron Goldschmidt expresses this thought when he writes in The Puzzle of Existence (2013) that the question of why any beings at all exist is:

但我不认为这种看法正确。这种观点假定：问「为什么竟然存在着任何东西」，就是在问「为什么竟然有真理」（用戈德施密特的话说，「为什么有某个世界实现」）。我们问的不是这个。我们寻找的是专门针对存在的解释。我们并不是在问「为什么有真理而非没有真理」。正如戈德施密特本人所指出的，那会是一个问得混乱的问题，因为「有真理」没有任何替代可能，连不可能的替代都没有。「一个什么都不是真的的情形」并没有描述任何情形，连不可能的情形都没有描述，因为对情形的描述告诉我们的是：倘若该情形发生，什么是或将会是真的。借助非本体论真理这个概念，我们还可以看到：可以设想有一些可能世界——实在的各种可能样子——它们并不仅仅是事物可能如此的方式。在那些空无的世界里，会有真理，却没有事物。

But I don’t think this is right. This view assumes that, in asking why anything at all exists, we are asking why there are any truths at all (in Goldschmidt’s terms, ‘why any world obtains’). That is not what we are asking. We are looking for an explanation for existence specifically. We are not asking why there are truths rather than no truths. That would, as Goldschmidt himself notes, be a confused question to ask, since there isn’t any alternative to there being truths, not even an impossible alternative. ‘A situation in which nothing is true’ doesn’t describe a situation, not even an impossible situation, since descriptions of a situation tell us what is or would be true were that situation to happen. With the concept of non-ontological truths, we can also see that there can conceivably be possible worlds – possible ways for reality to be – that are not merely ways things could be. In those empty worlds there would be truths, but no things.

许多非本体论真理即便在什么都不存在的情况下也依然为真，因为它们只要为真，就必然为真。例如，回想一下肯定前件式（modus ponens）：无论是否有东西存在，这条逻辑规则都不可能不真。其他许多非本体论真理也是如此，比如关于何者可能、何者不可能的真理、数学真理，以及道德真理。

Many non-ontological truths would still be true even if nothing existed because they are necessarily true, if true at all. For example, recall modus ponens. That logical rule couldn’t help but be true, whether or not anything exists. The same holds for many of the other non-ontological truths, such as truths regarding what is possible and impossible, mathematical truths, and moral truths.

这些非本体论真理中，有些比另一些更有争议。但你不必照单全收每一条非本体论真理，也能看出：一个什么都不存在的可能世界，会比乍看之下更为复杂。想到复杂性时，我们通常想到的是事物及其属性和行为的复杂性。但还有一整类更为抽象的复杂性：就非本体论真理而言的复杂性。

Some of these non-ontological truths are more controversial than others. But you don’t have to buy into every non-ontological truth to see that a possible world in which nothing exists would be more complex than it seems at first glance. When we think of complexity we normally think of the complexity of things and their properties and behaviours. But there is this whole other sort of complexity that is more abstract: complexity with respect to non-ontological truths.

即便一个什么都不存在的可能世界可能比我们最初想的更复杂，也推不出莱布尼茨说错了——他说的是，什么都不存在比有某物存在更简单。毕竟，你可能会想：如果存在某物，我们就不仅要面对上述那些非本体论真理，还要面对关于存在物的本体论真理。但如果设定一个新东西能解释许多原本得不到解释的东西，那么设定这个新东西反倒可以简化我们对实在的整体图景。

Even if a possible world in which nothing exists might be more complex than we initially thought, it doesn’t follow that Leibniz was wrong when he said that it is simpler for nothing to exist than for something to exist. After all, you might think that if something exists this would leave us with all those non-ontological truths cited above as well as ontological truths about the things that exist. But postulating something new can simplify our overall picture of reality if the new thing can explain a great deal that would otherwise remain unexplained.

如果存在物中有一个或多个能够解释那些非本体论真理为何为真，那么一个有某物存在的可能世界就很可能使事情变得更简单。这类似于达尔文通过设定某个我们先前并不相信其存在的东西——共同祖先——简化了我们对生物实在的说明。因此，如果存在某个合适类型的东西，而且这个存在物能解释许多非本体论真理为何为真，那么实在就可能比什么都不存在更简单。这个存在物越简单，它能解释的非本体论真理的范围越广，我们对实在的整体说明就越简单。可是，什么样的存在物能担此重任呢？有三种可能性自然而然地浮现，尽管我并不声称这三个选项已穷尽一切。

A possible world in which something exists may very well simplify matters if one or more of the existing things could explain why the non-ontological truths are true. This is similar to how Darwin simplified our account of biological reality by postulating the existence of something we didn’t previously believe in: a common ancestor. So if something of the right sort exists, this might make reality simpler than if nothing at all exists, if the existing thing can explain why many of the non-ontological truths are true. The simpler that existing thing is, and the wider the range of non-ontological truths it can explain, the simpler our overall account of reality will be. But what sort of existing thing might do the trick? Three possibilities naturally suggest themselves, though I don’t claim that these three options are exhaustive.

首先，上帝是一个理所当然的候选者。我这里想到的是世界各大一神教中的那种上帝：全能、全知，等等。就我们的目的而言，有神论最重要的特征是这样一种观念：上帝的本性和决定被认为最终解释着实在的诸多方面。正是上帝作为实在之解释基石的地位，使上帝成为解释各种非本体论真理的天然候选者。例如，如果上帝存在，那么上帝的决定就能解释各种否定性存在真理：机械哥斯拉（Mechagodzilla）不存在，是因为机械哥斯拉压根不在上帝想要创造、或想要允许其被创造的东西之列。

First, God is a natural candidate. Here I have in mind the sort of God we find in the world’s major monotheistic religions, all-powerful, all-knowing and so on. The feature of theism most important for our purposes is the idea that God’s nature and decisions are supposed to ultimately explain many aspects of reality. It is God’s status as reality’s explanatory bedrock that makes God a natural candidate for explaining various non-ontological truths. For example, if there is a God, then God’s decisions would explain various negative existential truths. Mechagodzilla doesn’t exist because Mechagodzilla just isn’t one of the things God wanted to create, or allow to be created.

神圣命令论者，如奥卡姆的威廉（William of Ockham，即「奥卡姆剃刀」的那位奥卡姆），用上帝的命令来解释道德真理——一个行为之所以是善的或恶的，是因为上帝命令它如此。勒内·笛卡尔（René Descartes）曾有一项广受非议的主张：是上帝钦定了哪些逻辑真理和数学真理为真。这些真理不可能另有模样——它们是必然的——但这一点也只是因为上帝决定它们不能另有模样。还有一种观点主张，上帝是一种格外简单的存在者。如果这种观点成立，那么只要上帝能解释许多非本体论真理为何为真，我们对实在的整体说明最终就可能很简单。这方面的传统学说是「神圣单纯性」论，其拥护者包括著名犹太思想家迈蒙尼德（Maimonides）、基督教思想家托马斯·阿奎那（Thomas Aquinas）和伊斯兰思想家伊本·西那（Ibn Sina）。按照这种观点，上帝内部毫无分别——无论是上帝的各种属性之间，还是上帝与其属性之间。过去几十年间，哲学家理查德·斯温伯恩（Richard Swinburne）细致论证了一种更为温和的理解：上帝是一个非常简单的存在者，因而有神论是一个非常简单的假说。其基本想法（我没有篇幅详加讨论）是：上帝的许多属性（全能、全知、全善等）最终可由上帝的一两个属性推导出来，而这些属性以干净利落的方式彼此契合。

Divine command theorists such as William of Ockham (of Ockham’s Razor fame) explain moral truths in terms of God’s commands – an act is good or bad because God commands it to be. René Descartes notoriously claimed that God decrees which logical and mathematical truths are true. These truths could not be otherwise – they are necessary – but that too is only because God decided they can’t be otherwise. There is also a case to be made that God is a particularly simple being. If that’s right, then our overall account of reality might end up being simple if God explains why many non-ontological truths are true. There is the traditional doctrine of divine simplicity, endorsed by prominent Jewish thinkers such as Maimonides, Christian thinkers such as Thomas Aquinas, and Islamic thinkers such as Ibn Sina. According to this view, there are no distinctions at all within God, either between God’s attributes, or between God and God’s attributes. Over the past several decades, the philosopher Richard Swinburne has argued in detail for a more modest way of understanding the idea that God is a very simple being, and so theism is a very simple hypothesis. The basic idea, which I don’t have the space to discuss in detail, is that many of God’s attributes (omnipotence, omniscience, perfect goodness, etc) are ultimately derivable from one or two of God’s attributes, and those attributes fit together in a neat and tidy manner.

如果上帝解释不讨你喜欢，这里还有第二种选择。许多非本体论真理或许可以由一个自然的（也就是非超自然的）对象来解释，这个对象说明了宇宙为何具有它的诸多属性。假设宇宙起源于一个宇宙奇点——一个密度无穷大的对象。鉴于自然律规定着宇宙如何随时间演化，初始奇点的状态将能解释各种否定性存在真理为何为真。这是因为：宇宙的初始条件加上自然律，将使宇宙的历史走上这一条而非那一条进程，而这条进程或者包含、或者不包含各种特定事物的存在。即便是非决定论的定律，在给定宇宙初始条件的情况下，也可能规定某些事物的存在极不可能。

Here is a second option if the God explanation doesn’t appeal. Many of the non-ontological truths might be explained by a natural (that is, non-supernatural) object that explains why the Universe has many of the properties it has. Suppose that the Universe originated in a cosmic singularity, an object of infinite density. The state of the initial singularity would, given how the laws of nature dictate the Universe evolves over time, explain why various negative existential truths are true. This is because the Universe’s initial conditions plus the laws will result in the Universe’s history taking one course rather than another, one that either does or does not include the existence of various particular things in it. Even non-deterministic laws may dictate that the existence of certain things is very improbable, given the Universe’s initial conditions.

就我们所知，这个充满存在物的现实世界，可能就是最简单的可能世界

The actual world, though filled with existing things, may for all we know be the simplest possible world

有些哲学家认为，就连最广义上关于何者可能、何者不可能的真理，也可以由初始奇点所具有的属性来解释。其想法是：可能与不可能奠基于——在自然律所规定的宇宙演化方式之下——宇宙的哪些状态能够从初始奇点的演化中产生。如果宇宙的某个状态原则上能够从初始奇点加自然律中产生，它就是可能的；如果它不能由此产生，它就是严格不可能的，就像圆的方一样不可能。事实上，按照这种观点，这正是圆的方之所以不可能的原因。但初始奇点能为我们对各种非本体论真理的解释带来格外的简单性吗？哲学家昆廷·史密斯（Quentin Smith）主张，初始宇宙奇点简单得无以复加：初始奇点没有空间延展，没有质量（至少在某些模型中如此），也不受任何物理定律支配。所以它当然是一个候选者。

Some philosophers think that truths regarding what is possible and impossible in the broadest sense are also explained by the properties had by an initial singularity. The idea is that what’s possible and impossible is grounded in which states of the Universe can result from the evolution of the initial singularity, given how the laws of nature say the Universe will evolve over time. If some state of the Universe can in principle result from the initial singularity and the laws, then it’s possible, but if it can’t result then it is strictly impossible, as impossible as a round square. In fact, on this view that’s what makes round squares impossible. But would an initial singularity give us a particularly simple explanation for various non-ontological truths? The philosopher Quentin Smith argued that an initial cosmic singularity is as simple as it gets, since an initial singularity would have had no spatial extension, no mass (at least on some models), and would not have been governed by any physical laws. So it’s certainly a candidate.

第三种可能是：许多非本体论真理之为真，是由我们人类造成的。而且，前面提到的许多非本体论真理，都曾以不同程度的可信性被某位哲学家归因于我们的决定、约定、实践之类的东西。例如，道德反实在论者如莎伦·斯特里特（Sharon Street）认为存在道德真理，但其为真是由我们的态度、约定或实践（或我们在理想条件下会持有的态度）造成的。逻辑约定主义者如贾里德·沃伦（Jared Warren）主张，逻辑真理是我们的语言实践的结果。数学直觉主义者如数学家布劳威尔（L E J Brouwer）认为，数学真理是心智建构出来的。

A third possibility is that we humans make many of the non-ontological truths true. And, with various degrees of plausibility, many of the non-ontological truths cited earlier have been attributed by some philosopher or other to our decisions, conventions, practices or whatever. For example, moral anti-realists such as Sharon Street think that there are moral truths, but that they are made true by our attitudes, conventions or practices (or the attitudes we would have in ideal conditions). Logical conventionalists such as Jared Warren contend that logical truths are a result of our linguistic practices. Mathematical intuitionists such as the mathematician L E J Brouwer think that mathematical truths are mentally constructed.

可能性当然不止这三种。但若把注意力限于这三种选项——上帝、奇点、人类——其中之一是否明显优于其余？这很难说。非本体论真理有很多，某些非本体论真理或许比另一些更能被某个存在物可信地解释。其中一些解释将如何运作，很难看出；我从未见过有人声称初始奇点可以解释哪些道德真理为真。而要对这三种选项作出完整评估，就需要细致考察各种现有解释的相对优劣。我们得逐一审视现有的各版本神圣命令论和以人为中心的道德反实在论，看看其中是否有哪种经得起细究。话虽如此，就简单性而言，前两种选项（上帝、奇点）确实似乎比第三种表现更好。它们主张诉诸一个存在物——一个可能非常简单的存在物——来解释大量非本体论真理。相比之下，如果试图诉诸人类来解释各种非本体论真理，我们似乎得不到一个很简单的解释。人类是复杂的对象，而且数量庞大。非本体论真理是由其中一些人解释的，还是由所有人解释的？面对可能导致相互矛盾的非本体论真理的人类态度或实践上的冲突，我们该如何调和？很难看出我们如何能找到一种优雅或简单的方式来交代这一切本该如何运作。

There are surely more possibilities than these three. But, confining our attention to these three options – God, singularity, human beings – is one of them clearly preferable to the others? It’s difficult to say. There are many non-ontological truths, and some non-ontological truths may be more plausibly explained by an existing thing than would other non-ontological truths. It is difficult to see how some of these explanations would work; I’ve never seen anyone claim that an initial singularity might explain which moral truths are true. And a full evaluation of the three options would require a detailed examination of the relative merits of the various explanations on offer. We would have to take a look at each of the versions of divine command theory and human-centred moral anti-realism on offer, and see if any of them seem plausible on closer inspection. That being said, the first two options (God, singularity) do seem to fare better than the third option with respect to simplicity. They propose to explain lots of non-ontological truths by appealing to one existing thing, an existing thing that might be very simple. By contrast, if we try to explain various non-ontological truths by appeal to human beings, we don’t seem to have a very simple explanation. Human beings are complex objects. There are also a lot of human beings. Are the non-ontological truths explained by some of them, or all of them? How would we reconcile conflicting human attitudes or practices that might result in contradictory non-ontological truths? It’s hard to see how we might come up with an elegant or simple way of accounting for how that’s all supposed to work.

关于细节的进一步思辨可以留待他日。要点在于：如果我们能设定某个足够简单、又能解释足够广范围的非本体论真理的存在物，我们就很可能得到这样一种对实在的说明：其中有一样或多样东西存在，却比那个什么都不存在的说明更简单。事实上，尽管乍看之下有违直觉，但就我们所知，这个充满存在物的现实世界可能正是最简单的可能世界——只要它有一个足够简单的解释基础。若果真如此，莱布尼茨说「无」本会比「有」「简单且容易」得多，就说错了。如果我们曾因实在没有采取它最简单、表面上最可能的状态——一个什么都不存在的状态——而感到惊讶，那种惊讶感也应当烟消云散。

We can leave further speculation regarding the details to another occasion. The main point is this: if we can posit some existing thing that is simple enough, and capable of explaining a wide-enough range of the non-ontological truths, we might very well end up with an account of reality in which one or more things exist, and yet which is simpler than an account of reality in which nothing exists. In fact, despite how counterintuitive it seems at first, the actual world, though filled with existing things, may for all we know be the simplest possible world, if it has a sufficiently simple explanatory foundation. In that case, Leibniz would have been incorrect when he said that nothing would have been ‘simpler and easier’ than something. If we found ourselves surprised that reality didn’t take its simplest and seemingly most probable state, one in which nothing exists, that sense of surprise should dissolve.

假设如果存在某个合适类型的东西，那么这比什么都不存在更简单。这个事实能解释为什么存在着某物而非一无所有吗？想来只有当我们认为「实在之所以是如此这般，是因为它有多简单」时，它才能解释。换言之，我们必须认为实在以某种方式朝着简单而努力，以至于如果世界已经尽可能简单，那是因为它就是尽可能简单的。你不应当以「我们的世界非常复杂」为由反对这个想法，因为如我所强调的，那种复杂性或许可以由一个非常简单的解释性起点来说明。

Suppose that if something of the right sort exists, then that would be simpler than if nothing at all existed. Would this fact explain why there is something rather than nothing? Presumably it would only if we think that reality is the way it is because of how simple it is. In other words, we would have to think that reality is somehow striving toward simplicity, so that, if the world is as simple as it can be, this is because it is as simple as it can be. You shouldn’t oppose this idea on the grounds that our world is very complex, since, as I’ve emphasised, that complexity might be accounted for in terms of a very simple explanatory starting point.

一个更严重的忧虑是：即使我们认定某事为真的理由是它更简单（记得奥卡姆剃刀吧），也推不出这件事本身就是因其更简单而为真的。我们认为各个物种有共同祖先，理由是这个假说是对证据最简单的解释。但那种简单性并不是使「存在那个共同祖先」为真的东西。

A more serious worry is that, even if our reason for thinking that something is true is because it is simpler (remember Ockham’s Razor), it doesn’t follow that the thing itself is true because it is simpler. The reason we think the various species have a common ancestor is because this hypothesis is the simplest explanation of the evidence. But that simplicity isn’t what makes it true that there is that common ancestor.

我们应当质疑这个假定：简单性从不解释某个事态为何出现。「有时实在采取较简单的形式，正是因为它更简单」这一想法，比乍看起来更能自圆其说。它还有一个好处：能解释为什么简单性是真理的向导（也就是说，为什么我们应当接受奥卡姆剃刀这类原则）——这种在科学和日常生活中都被遵循的方法论实践，出了名地难以证成。此外，我们中许多人和莱布尼茨一样，对实在没有采取它乍看之下最简单的形式——其中什么都不存在——感到困惑。我们往往认为：如果什么都不曾存在，而且那确实是实在最简单的样子，那么之所以什么都不存在，恰恰就是因为「无」远比「有」简单。

We should question this assumption that simplicity never explains why some state of affairs comes about. The idea that sometimes reality takes a simpler form because it is simpler is more defensible than it might first seem. It would have the benefit of explaining why simplicity is a guide to truth (that is, why we should accept principles such as Ockham’s Razor), a methodological practice followed in both science and everyday life, but which is notoriously hard to justify. What’s more, many of us follow Leibniz in being puzzled at the fact that reality didn’t take what seems at first to be its simplest form, wherein nothing exists. We tend to think that if nothing had existed, and if this would have in fact been the simplest way for reality to be, then nothing would have existed precisely because nothing was so much simpler than something.

我的论证把这条推理思路整个颠倒了过来。我们应当预期实在是简单的，而就我们所能判断的，我们所发现的正是如此。如果「存在着某物而非一无所有」正是实在最简单的样子，那我们就不应为此感到惊讶。这并不能解释一切。比如，假如实在以某种方式追求简单，我们完全有理由追问为什么会如此。说「实在追求简单，是因为这是实在运作起来最简单的方式」是不行的，因为那会是一个循环解释。所以，即使我这个极度思辨性的提议是正确的，我们仍然会面对许多神秘之处。在实在的无数种可能样子中，为什么最终是这样？这仍是一个令人困惑的问题——即便我们得出结论说，实在的特征之一，即事物存在，可能并不像它最初看来的那么令人困惑。

My argument flips this line of reasoning on its head. We should expect reality to be simple, and for all we can tell that’s exactly what we find. There being something rather than nothing shouldn’t surprise us if that’s the simplest way for reality to be. That wouldn’t explain everything. Supposing that reality somehow strives for simplicity, for example, we might very well wonder why that’s the case. It won’t work to say that reality strives for simplicity because that’s the simplest way for reality to work, as that would be a circular explanation. So, even if my extremely speculative proposal is correct, we’d still be left with a lot of mystery. Out of the infinite number of ways for reality to be, why did it turn out this way? That’s still a baffling question, even if we conclude that one of reality’s features, that things exist, might turn out to be less baffling than it initially seems.
