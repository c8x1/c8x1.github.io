为何杂家是我们这个过度优化时代的反英雄

Jay Miller·Aeon·2026年11月2日·11分钟阅读·原文

为何偏偏要为杂家（dilettante）辩护？往好里说是个业余爱好者，往坏里说是个轻浮的浪子：一个自身兴趣从不过是一时爱好的人，凭什么让人对他感兴趣？这是个严肃的时代——一个对什么都不严肃的人，凭什么被严肃对待？

Why, of all people, defend the dilettante? An amateur at best, a flighty rascal at worst: why take an interest in someone whose own interests are never more than a passing hobby? These are serious times – why take seriously someone for whom nothing is serious?

韦氏词典（Merriam-Webster）把杂家定义为「对某一艺术或知识分支怀有肤浅兴趣的人」。Encyclopedia.com 说杂家是「没有真正的投入与学识」却培养着兴趣的人。剑桥词典（Cambridge）则满足于说其理解「不很深刻，也不很严肃」。柯林斯词典（Collins）给出的形象是「看似对某学科兴趣盎然……实则所知不多」。没有一条是恭维话。

Merriam-Webster defines the dilettante as ‘a person having a superficial interest in an art or a branch of knowledge’. Encyclopedia.com calls the dilettante someone who cultivates an interest ‘without real commitment or knowledge’. Cambridge settles for someone whose understanding ‘is not very deep or serious’. Collins gives us someone who ‘seems interested in a subject … but does not really know very much about it’. None of it is flattering.

显然，杂家不是什么令人向往的角色。没有哪所大学把自己宣传成培养下一代杂家的学府；没有哪本杂志做过「年度杂家」专题（也许那份出色的《闲人》杂志［The Idler］算个例外）。他——因为在历史上和大众想象中，杂家常常是个「他」——本质上是个业余爱好者，一个以兴趣之广与专长之窄为标志的角色。

Clearly, the dilettante is no aspirational character. No university markets itself as training the next generation of dilettantes; no magazine runs a ‘dilettante of the year’ issue (except, perhaps, for the excellent Idler publication). He – for the dilettante is, historically and in popular imagination, so often a ‘he’ – is in essence an amateur, a role defined by wide interests and narrow expertise.

这就是杂家的名声，它由一种文化所赋予并维系，而这种文化把对单一志业的一心一意奉为通往有意义人生的唯一道路。我们总被告诫：选定一件事，然后坚持到底。我要挑战的并非某场明目张胆的反杂家运动，而是那种弥散却又无休无止的压力：收窄兴趣，走向专业化，获取专长，为了深度而牺牲广度。它回荡在毕业演讲、自助指南以及种种同样带着企业腔的动员讲话里，化作「追随你的梦想」「找到你的热爱」的连珠炮式训令。在这套图景中，专家是英雄，杂家不过是一则警世故事：那个从不投入、从不完成、在任何一件事上都没能精进到足够好的人。

Such is the reputation of the dilettante, assigned and maintained by a culture that treats a single-minded devotion to one pursuit as the only route to a meaningful life. Pick something and stick with it, we are told. It’s not so much an explicit campaign against dilettantism I’m challenging, but rather the diffuse yet relentless pressure to narrow one’s interests, to specialise, to gain expertise, to forgo breadth for the sake of depth. We hear it in the barrage of imperatives to follow your dreams or find your passion uttered at graduation speeches, in self-help guides, and similarly corporate-sounding pep talks. Under this scheme, the specialist is the hero and the dilettante is but a cautionary tale: the person who never committed, never finished, never got good enough at any one thing.

可如果这套图景恰恰弄反了呢？如果杂家在一事与一事之间永不停歇的流转，并非胆气不足的失败，而是另一种站得住脚的存在方式——其探索的热忱，恰恰是一个高度专业化、过度优化又过分严肃的世界最需要的东西呢？那么，杂家最终显形的就不是反复无常的小丑，而是一种现代的反英雄。而这，正是我要论证的、值得为之辩护的杂家之道。

But what if the scheme has it backwards? What if the dilettante’s perpetual movement from one thing to another is not a failure of nerve but a different and defensible way of being, one whose exploratory zeal turns out to be just what a hyper-specialised, over-optimised and all-too-serious world needs most? Then the dilettante would turn out to be not a capricious buffoon but a kind of modern anti-hero. That, I argue, is a dilettantism worth defending.

首先，让我们考察「杂家」与它的几个概念近亲——业余爱好者（amateur）、浅尝辄止的涉猎者（dabbler）、万事通（jack-of-all-trades）等——之间的关系。其一，杂家在本性上是业余爱好者，亦即非专家、非职业者。但业余身份只是杂家之道的必要条件，而非充分条件。任何人都可以在任何一件事上做个业余爱好者：木工、观鸟、天文学，等等。杂家的业余性横跨多个兴趣领域，且往往围绕艺术与美的创作和（或）鉴赏展开。与万事通一样，杂家是多维度的业余爱好者；只不过杂家的业余性典型地属于审美一脉。

First, let’s consider how ‘dilettante’ relates to some of its conceptual cousins: the amateur, the dabbler, the jack-of-all-trades, etc. First, the dilettante is by nature an amateur, ie, a non-expert, a non-professional. But amateurism is a necessary though not sufficient condition of dilettantism. Anyone can be an amateur at any particular thing: woodworking, birdwatching, astronomy, etc. The dilettante’s amateurism is wide-ranging over several interests, and tends to orient around the production and/or appreciation of art and beauty. Like the jack-of-all-trades, the dilettante is a multi-dimensional amateur. But the dilettante’s amateurism is typically of the aesthetic variety.

再者，杂家并非浅尝辄止的涉猎者。涉猎者只在兴趣的表面刮一刮，而我要说，杂家能够也确实会深入其中——只是不把自我感锚定在任何一项兴趣上。这也把杂家与漫游者（flâneur）区分开来——波德莱尔（Charles Baudelaire）笔下那位都市游荡者，在城市呈现的万般景致间信步，样样浅尝，却无一倾心。杂家深深投入于兴趣之中，与其说是被动的看客，不如说是主动的参与者。

Further, the dilettante is no dabbler. The dabbler scratches the surface of interests, whereas the dilettante, I argue, can and does go deep – without, however, anchoring a sense of self in any one interest. This also sets the dilettante apart from the flâneur, Charles Baudelaire’s urban wanderer strolling through the city’s offerings, sampling everything, invested in none of it. The dilettante is deeply engaged in interests, and more an active participant than a passive spectator.

这个词最初指的是为了「愉悦」（delight）而追求一项兴趣——尤其是艺术兴趣——的人

The term originally referred to someone who pursues an interest, particularly an artistic one, for the sake of ‘delight’

就此而言，这个概念在精神上最接近「文艺复兴式通才」（Renaissance man），只是去掉其中的性别限定与历史语境。杂家为享受所动，而非为必需、职业义务或资历的追逐所动；他有意识地培育多种各自分立、往往带有审美性质的兴趣，而不满足于表面的熟悉。

In this respect, the concept is closest in spirit to the ‘Renaissance man’, minus the gender specification and historical context. The dilettante is one who is moved by enjoyment rather than necessity, professional obligation or the pursuit of credentials, and who purposefully cultivates a plurality of distinct, often aesthetic interests beyond mere surface familiarity.

因此，为杂家辩护的一个理由，是词义上的公道（lexicological justice）。这个词被不公正地压上了过重的贬义，而事情并非从来如此。源自巴洛克时代的意大利语 dilettante，这个词最初指为了快乐——即「愉悦」——而追求一项兴趣、尤其是一项艺术兴趣的人。即便词义有所流变，它大体上始终是中性的，用来把一项实践中凭热情参与的人与以此为业的人区分开来。我们今天熟悉的贬义是晚近才有的，它由我称之为「匮乏」模型的观念塑成：业余性被负面地看作有所缺失——缺技艺、缺严肃、缺专注、缺专长。这正是阿多诺（Theodor Adorno）这类文化守门人所赋予的含义，他在1929年轻蔑地把杂家斥为「无法确立任何标准」之辈。

One reason to defend the dilettante, then, is for lexicological justice. The term has been unfairly burdened with an overly negative connotation. It wasn’t always this way. From the Baroque-era Italian dilettante, the term originally referred to someone who pursues an interest, particularly an artistic one, for the sake of pleasure, or ‘delight’. Even as the term evolved, it stayed largely neutral, distinguishing enthusiasts from professionals in a given practice. The pejorative connotation familiar to us today is more recent, shaped by what I’ll call a ‘deficiency’ model, in which amateurism is viewed negatively as a lack of something: skill, seriousness, focus, expertise. This is the meaning ascribed by cultural gatekeepers like Theodor Adorno, who in 1929 dismissed the dilettante as one ‘unable to establish any standards’.

然而，更重要的目标，是把杂家之道展示为一种比我们以为的更丰饶的生活方式。那是一种从专业化、工具化与优绩主义的锱铢必较中解放出来的生活，也是从那种对「为快乐而快乐」心存疑忌的新教工作伦理中解放出来的生活。贬抑教育与职业领域专业化的论调早已广为人知，力挺全面发展与博雅教育之德性的论调亦然。这场对杂家之道的辩护虽在精神上与之相近，着眼的却不是学术或职业生活的目的与结果，而是完成一次心理上的切换：从「找到你的那件事并坚持到底」这种为社会所规训的压力，切换到对多元兴趣开放而富于探索的追求。收回这种快乐的实验主义，意在把杂家之道呈现为一种丰裕而非匮乏的样式。

The more important aim, however, is to show dilettantism as a way of living that offers more than we might think. It is a life liberated from specialisation, instrumentalisation and meritocratic bean-counting, as well as from a Protestant work ethic that is suspicious of pleasure for pleasure’s sake. Arguments denigrating specialisation in education and vocation are well known, as are those championing well-roundedness and the virtues of the liberal arts. Though similar in spirit, this defence of dilettantism is less about the purpose or outcomes of academic or professional life and more about making the mental switch away from the socially conditioned pressure to find your thing and stick with it to an open and exploratory pursuit of pluralistic interests. Reclaiming this sense of joyful experimentalism is intended to show dilettantism as a mode of abundance rather than deficiency.

一个合适的起点，是歌德（J W von Goethe）与席勒（Friedrich Schiller）合撰的文章《论杂家之道》（On Dilettantism，1799）。这个残缺不全、从未完成的项目，出自魏玛时期德国这对文学梦之队之手，为杂家之道画了一幅态度极为暧昧的肖像：这边称赞杂家「在人身上培育了某种严肃的东西」，那边又把他贬斥为「剽窃者」与「拙劣的匠人」。然而事后回望，它对杂家之道的「益处」（Nutzen）与「弊端」或「害处」（Schaden）近乎科学式的梳理，恰有助于我们清醒地审视杂家之道在今天的潜在美德与恶习。

A good place to start is the essay ‘On Dilettantism’ (1799) by J W von Goethe and Friedrich Schiller. This fragmentary, never-completed project by the literary dream-team of Weimar-era Germany paints a deeply ambivalent portrait of dilettantism, praising the dilettante over here for ‘cultivat[ing] something serious in man’ and condemning him over there as a ‘plagiarist’ and a ‘bungler’. Viewed retrospectively, however, its almost scientific treatment of the ‘advantages’ (Nutzen) and ‘disadvantages’ or ‘harms’ (Schaden) of dilettantism helps give a clear-eyed view of the potential virtues and vices of dilettantism today.

至关重要的是，歌德与席勒的分析牢牢安放在魏玛古典主义的文化语境之中。这意味着，首先，杂家问题（一如其在意大利语的原始用法中那样）完全是从艺术的角度生发的。他们把杂家当作一个不折不扣的审美形象：「一个艺术的爱好者，他不仅想凝观和享受艺术，还想参与艺术的创造。」这个审美维度（依我看，很遗憾）已随着时间从「杂家」的词义中淡去。但它也无须被狭隘地铭刻在18世纪的焦虑里——关于谁有资格、谁没有资格被恰当地称为艺术家的焦虑。艺术参与并非杂家之道的本质要素。但值得重申、甚至值得置诸前台的是这样一个意思：杂家之道的「愉悦」，在某种程度上包含着对审美愉悦——取其宽泛的理解——的体验。也就是说，杂家追求的是克尔凯郭尔（Søren Kierkegaard）所谓的一种「审美生活」：观察、珍视并参与艺术与美的持续不断的日常活动。

Crucially, Goethe and Schiller’s analysis is squarely situated in the cultural context of Weimar classicism. This means that, first, the question of the dilettante arises (as it does in its original Italian usage) exclusively in relation to the arts. They treat the dilettante as a distinctly aesthetic figure, ‘a lover of the arts who wishes not only to contemplate and enjoy them, but also to participate in their production.’ This aesthetic dimension has (regrettably, I think) faded from the meaning of ‘dilettante’ over time. But nor does it need to be narrowly inscribed in 18th-century anxieties about who can and cannot properly be called an artist. Artistic engagement isn’t essential to dilettantism. But it is worth reasserting, maybe even foregrounding, the sense in which the ‘delight’ of dilettantism involves, to some extent, the experience of aesthetic pleasure, broadly understood. The dilettante, that is, pursues what Søren Kierkegaard calls an ‘aesthetic life’: the ongoing, everyday activity of observing, valuing, and participating in art and beauty.

杂家穿着业余者的外衣安然自得。对杂家来说，每一晚都是卡拉OK之夜

尽管植根于古典主义的审美主义，歌德与席勒对杂家的某些批评在今天依然掷地有声。比如，他们担心杂家「跳过台阶」，在创作过程中滑行得过于轻巧，过早地宣告胜利。这话并非全无道理。正如王尔德（Oscar Wilde）在1897年的书信体文章《自深深处》（De Profundis）里对「感伤主义者」的忧虑——那种人「渴望享有情绪的奢侈而不为之买单」——两位作者对那些为追逐桂冠而乐于插队的人提出了一个合理的反对。进步是靠练习挣得的，我们这些下过苦功的人理应守护它，任何一位受过正规训练、对YouTube和TikTok上冒充心理疗法的江湖骗术忍无可忍的治疗师，都会乐意为此作证。

Though rooted in classicist aestheticism, some of Goethe and Schiller’s criticism of the dilettante still resonates today. They worry, for example, that the dilettante ‘jumps over the steps’, gliding too glibly through the creative process and claiming victory too early. There’s something to this. Similar to Oscar Wilde’s concern about the ‘sentimentalist’ in his 1897 epistolary essay, De Profundis, who ‘desires to have the luxury of an emotion without paying for it’, the authors voice a reasonable objection to those willing to cut the line in chasing laurels. Progress is earned through practice, and those of us who have put in the work are right to protect it, as any trained therapist, frustrated with the quackery that passes for therapy on YouTube and TikTok, will gladly testify.

但由于把「真正的艺术家」理想化为真理与美的显现者，歌德与席勒免不了把杂家看成志向与能力双缺的人。平心而论，没有哪项实践是完全的放任自流，有些实践也确实比别的更容不下杂家（谁想住进杂家建筑师盖的房子呢？）。但那种认为杂家像正规艺术家一样试图遵循艺术与美的「真正客观的规则」却力有未逮的观念，建立在过时且欧洲中心的预设之上，误判了艺术家与杂家各自究竟在做什么。换言之，一旦撤掉新古典主义的前设，杂家顿时显得不再像模仿者，而更像一个获得解放的创造者。问题不在于杂家在某项实践中有所不足，而在于他对该实践持有一种不同的取向。的确，一种过分以目标为取向的态度——尤其是在消遣与愉悦的领域——恰恰逆着杂家之道的精神而行。它的魅力之一，正在于其不设终局的气质，在于它愿意为愉悦而非成就去做一件事。杂家穿着业余者的外衣安然自得。对杂家来说，每一晚都是卡拉OK之夜。

But due to their idealisation of the ‘true artist’ as a manifester of Truth and Beauty, Goethe and Schiller cannot help but view the dilettante as lacking in both aim and ability. To be fair, no practice is a total free-for-all, and some practices are less permissive of dilettantism than others (who wants to live in a house built by a dilettante architect?). But the notion that the dilettante tries and fails to follow the ‘true objective rules’ of art and beauty the way legit artists do rests on outdated and Eurocentric assumptions about what both artists and dilettantes are actually up to. In other words, take away the neo-classicist presumption, and suddenly the dilettante appears less like an imitator and more like a liberated creator. It’s not that the dilettante falls short in any given practice but, rather, has a different orientation toward it. Indeed, an overly goal-oriented attitude, especially in the realm of pastimes and pleasures, cuts against the very spirit of dilettantism. Part of its appeal is its ethos of open-endedness, its willingness to do something for the sake of delight rather than achievement. The dilettante is comfortable in the garb of an amateur. For the dilettante, every night is karaoke night.

从我们的这两位德国人那里，我们约略看出了杂家的失足之处。但他们也能帮我们完成那场矫正性的心理切换：不再把杂家看作蹩脚的准从业者，而是看作一个主动选择以更实验、更探索的取向对待某项实践的人。

From our Germans, we glean a sense of where the dilettante goes wrong. But they can also help us make the corrective mental switch from seeing the dilettante as a wannabe practitioner to someone who chooses a more experimental, exploratory orientation toward a given practice.

要把杂家之道看作丰裕而非匮乏，关键在于领会它所供给的自由感。首先，它是一种消极自由，一种免于束缚的自由，那些束缚原本捆着职业从业者。更确切地说，它是在一项实践内部来去移动的自由。

The key to perceiving dilettantism as abundance instead of deficiency is to appreciate the sense of freedom it affords. It is, in the first place, a negative freedom, a freedom from the constraints that otherwise bind professional practitioners. More precisely, it is a freedom of mobility within a practice.

其一，杂家拥有更大的自由去参与一项既定的实践。在歌德与席勒看来，杂家之道最重要的「益处」之一，是它「激发出某种对艺术的趣味，并把它延伸到艺术家无力企及的地方」。把这一点稍加推广，其效力远不限于艺术：杂家可以在专家与外行之间充当联络人，弥合高深实践与日常好奇之间的沟壑，把原本闭门自守的从业者引见给更广大的受众，并向每一个原本自觉被拒之门外的人递上请柬。

First, the dilettante has greater freedom to engage in a given practice. For Goethe and Schiller, one of the most important ‘advantages’ of dilettantism is that it ‘stimulates a certain taste for art, and extends it to where the artist would not be able to reach’. Generalising a bit, the point holds well beyond art: the dilettante can act as a liaison between expert and layperson, closing the gap between high practice and everyday curiosity, introducing otherwise cloistered practitioners to wider audiences and extending an invitation to anyone who might otherwise feel shut out.

举一个自己的例子。几年前，我加入了「Mountain Haulers」，一个每周二晚间在北卡罗来纳州阿什维尔（Asheville）骑行的车队。这是一场「不掉队」（no drop）骑行：无论有人多慢、多吃力，骑手们都会沿途设点等所有人跟上，而每月的头一回骑行总是沿河的轻松骑。这种开放让车队有别于另一种骑行文化——那种以臭名昭著的狂热小圈子著称的文化，圈内人争相攀比谁对这项运动的膜拜更虔诚。欢迎经验更少、投入更浅的人，似乎才是更真诚地爱你所宣称所爱之物的方式。如果骑车真是你的心头好，你当然巴不得车座上多几个屁股。那些周二晚来骑车的杂家骑手，让骑行这件事变得更好了。

A personal example. A few years ago, I joined Mountain Haulers, a cycling group that meets on Tuesday evenings for a ride through Asheville, North Carolina. It is a ‘no drop’ ride: cyclists wait at points along the way for everyone to catch up, however slow or struggling, and the first ride of the month is always a chill ride along the river. This openness sets the group apart from a cycling culture of notorious cliques of devotees clamouring to outdo each other’s worship of the sport. Welcoming the less-experienced and the less-committed seems a more genuine way of loving the thing you claim to love. If biking is your thing, surely you want more butts on bike seats. The dilettante cyclists who show up for Tuesday-night rides make cycling better.

现代人「守好自己的车道」的告诫，源自关于把一项兴趣据为己有需要何等投入的预设

The modern admonition to ‘stay in your lane’ stems from assumptions about the commitment required to claim an interest as one’s own

杂家之自由的另一重含义，涉及在不同实践之间的移动——一种免于其间壁垒的自由。杂家在「我是谁」与「我在做什么」之间保持一份超然，或者说有意的分离，从而过起文化旅居者的生活。绘画、园艺、MIG焊接、养蜂、学外语、收藏漫画、跳舞、游泳、版画制作、力量举、匹克球、酸面团烘焙、手风琴演奏、烟熏牛胸肉烧烤——全是令人兴奋的游历与探索之地，却无须在任何一处申请国籍，甚至连永久居留也不必谋求。杂家是个跋涉型的世界主义者，他的自由感恰恰在于永不困守一地。

The further sense of the dilettante’s freedom involves mobility between different practices – a freedom from the barriers dividing them. The dilettante maintains a certain detachment, or intentional separation, between who one is and what one does, to live the life of a cultural itinerant. Painting, gardening, MIG welding, beekeeping, language learning, comic-book collecting, dancing, swimming, printmaking, powerlifting, pickleballing, sourdough baking, accordion playing, brisket barbecuing – all exciting destinations to travel and explore without seeking citizenship or even permanent residence in any one. The dilettante is a wayfaring cosmopolitan whose sense of freedom is found in never being stuck in one place.

然而，这种移动性也属于一种积极自由：满足人探索世界并在世上留下印记的基本需要。歌德与席勒称许杂家激活了这种指向好奇与创造的天性冲动，并把这种驱力比作孩子玩耍般模仿周遭环境的天然欲望。更进一步我要说，杂家之道不只满足了「创造之人」（Homo faber）的创制之痒；仅仅为了喜悦与好奇而去制作与探索，本身就是一种自由，它对自我发现的价值，丝毫不亚于任何一种投入或单一的志业。

And yet, this mobility is also part of a positive freedom to satisfy the basic human need to explore and make one’s mark on the world. Goethe and Schiller credit the dilettante for activating this natural impulse toward curiosity and creativity, a drive they compare with children’s innate desire to playfully imitate their surroundings. Going further, I would say that dilettantism offers not only the satisfaction of scratching the Homo faber itch; making and exploring for the simple sake of joy and curiosity is a freedom that is every bit as valuable to self-discovery as any commitment or singular pursuit.

然而，从外面看，移动性可能显得只是没有长性。这一点无疑助长了对杂家的常见指控——肤浅。起锚离港、从一项兴趣抽身而去，会被标记为蜻蜓点水。我在此处只是推测：这类批评似乎植根于一种工作伦理，它告诉我们，在兴趣上的投资理应生息返利：权威、信誉、进入文化守卫空间的许可，等等。正如新古典主义者想把「真正的艺术家」与装腔作势之徒区别开来，现代人对「选一条车道」、或（更咄咄逼人的）「守好自己的车道」的告诫，同样源自一种预设：要把一项兴趣据为己有，需要何等程度、何等时长的投入。

However, from the outside, mobility may look like mere lack of commitment. No doubt this drives some of the allegations of superficiality commonly lodged against the dilettante. Pulling up anchors and moving on from an interest gets flagged as surface-skimming. I’m speculating here, but such criticism seems rooted in a work ethic that tells us that investment in certain interests ought to yield dividends: authority, credibility, permission to enter culturally guarded spaces, etc. Just as the neo-classicist wants to set the ‘true artist’ apart from the poseurs, the modern admonition to ‘pick a lane’, or (more aggressive) ‘stay in your lane’, stems from assumptions about the level and length of commitment required to claim an interest as one’s own.

杂家之道拒绝这种准资本主义的心智。社交、学术与文化空间里常见的那种琐屑的地盘意识（想想唱片店、鸡尾酒吧、画廊、哲学课堂等等）不仅多余，更要紧的是，它与对事物本身的真兴趣背道而驰。从匮乏转向丰裕，关键一环是学会把杂家看作某项实践的资产，而非威胁。即便是阿多诺这位头号势利眼，也容许了一版类似的说法：他论证说，专业的批评家身边需要留着几个杂家，「好向他证明自己不是其中一员」。

Dilettantism rejects this quasi-capitalist mindset. The petty territorialism common to social, academic and cultural spaces (think record stores, cocktail bars, art galleries, philosophy classes, etc) is unnecessary but, more importantly, at odds with a genuine interest in the thing itself. A crucial part of the switch from deficiency to abundance is learning to see the dilettante as an asset to a given practice rather than a threat to it. Even Adorno, snob par excellence, allows a version of this, arguing that the expert critic needs the dilettante around ‘in order to prove to himself that he isn’t one’.

杂家的移动不止于逃离疾病，更是一场朝向生机与活力的奋不顾身的冲刺

More than simply escaping sickness, the dilettante’s movement is a headlong rush at vitality and vigour

同样不可或缺的是，在这场心理切换中看到：杂家的好奇与专家的好奇强度相当，只是路数不同。不妨把杂家想象成两位气质迥异的哲学人物的爱情结晶：一位是克尔凯郭尔《非此即彼》（Either/Or，1843）中的审美者，他发现并培育各种兴趣，其投入的热烈尚未凝固为义务便被弃置；另一位是尼采（Friedrich Nietzsche）笔下的「自由精神」，那个好奇心炽烈、脚步轻捷的游牧人，对智识与艺术珍宝永不止息的追寻，给了他一种深刻——尽管无根——的自由。从克尔凯郭尔那里，杂家得到了对无聊（「万恶之根」）的与生俱来的厌憎，并以他所谓的「轮作」来成全：不断更换「耕作的方法与作物的种类」。从尼采那里，杂家继承了拒斥常规加上永动不息，一种永久的「无家可归」感，推动着对诸般兴趣不受拘束的研读。热烈到足以深入，超脱到终将离去——这样的人物展示了这样一种可能：以各种方式强烈地投入认识、行动与创造，却绝不容许自我感在其周遭凝固得太牢。

Also crucial to making this mental switch is seeing the dilettante’s curiosity as equal in intensity to the expert’s, but different. Picture the dilettante as the love-child of two very different philosophical figures: Kierkegaard’s aesthete in Either/Or (1843) who finds and cultivates interests with an intensity that is abandoned before it hardens into obligation, and Friedrich Nietzsche’s ‘free spirit’, the fiercely curious, light-footed nomad whose restless pursuit of intellectual and artistic treasures provides a profound, if rootless, freedom. From Kierkegaard, the dilettante acquires an innate aversion to boredom (‘the root of all evil’) which is achieved by what he calls ‘rotation of crops’: perpetually changing ‘the method of cultivation and the kinds of crops’. From Nietzsche, the dilettante inherits a rejection of convention coupled with perpetual motion, a sense of permanent ‘homelessness’ that propels an unfettered perusal of interests. Passionate enough to go deep, detached enough to eventually leave, such a figure shows the possibility of being intensely invested in various ways of knowing, doing and making, but never content to let a sense of self form too firmly around them.

在斯派克·琼斯（Spike Jonze）的电影《改编剧本》（Adaptation，2002）中，有一场令人难忘的戏：记者苏珊·奥尔琳（Susan Orlean，梅丽尔·斯特里普［Meryl Streep］饰）正在写一本关于「兰花窃贼」约翰·拉罗什（John Laroche，克里斯·库珀［Chris Cooper］饰）的书；她一方面被他追踪一种罕见的致幻「鬼兰」的举动所吸引，另一方面又困惑地得知，这不过是他此生一连串痴迷中最新的一个：乌龟、史前化石、19世纪的荷兰镜子，等等。他怎么能对一件投入如此之深的事说断就断？他只能讲一桩旧事。一段深沉而吞噬一切的对热带鱼的爱，随着一个领悟戛然而止——这份热情已然走完了它的旅程。「去他妈的鱼，」他对她说，搅碎了她那个「爱一样东西就意味着永远守着它」的想当然。

In a memorable scene from the Spike Jonze film Adaptation (2002), the journalist Susan Orlean (Meryl Streep), who is writing a book about the orchid thief John Laroche (Chris Cooper), is at once intrigued by his hunt for a rare hallucinogenic ‘ghost orchid’ and baffled to learn that it is only the latest in a lifetime of obsessions: turtles, prehistoric fossils, 19th-century Dutch mirrors, etc. How can he detach so abruptly from something in which he’s invested so much? He can only offer an anecdote. A profound and consuming love for tropical fish ended suddenly with the realisation that the passion had simply run its course. ‘Fuck fish,’ he tells her, disrupting her assumption that loving something means sticking with it forever.

不同之处在于：与克尔凯郭尔的审美者不同，杂家的轮作并非出自绝望。活力、超脱与永不餍足的好奇，构成着杂家自由而充盈的主体性，而不是通往自我达成路上的一个阶段。杂家选择了审美生活，而不是克尔凯郭尔「非此即彼」的二分所摆出的那种委身的人生，并且全然担起这个选择。杂家也不像尼采的自由精神那样，只是正从某种生存之疾中复原。与其冒着复发——或更糟，陷入悲观主义这种「溃蚀性的恶习」——的风险，不如说杂家之道是对这份自由的健康拥抱；杂家的移动不止于逃离疾病，更是一场朝向生机与活力的奋不顾身的冲刺。

The difference is that, unlike Kierkegaard’s aesthete, the dilettante doesn’t rotate crops out of despair. Dynamism, detachment and insatiable curiosity are constitutive of the dilettante’s free and full subjectivity, rather than a stage on the way to achieving selfhood. The dilettante chooses aesthetic life over the life of commitment that Kierkegaard’s ‘either/or’ dichotomy presents, and fully owns that choice. Nor is the dilettante simply recovering from existential illness, as Nietzsche’s free spirit seems to be. Rather than running the risk of relapse – or worse, the ‘cankerous vice’ of pessimism – dilettantism is a healthy embrace of this freedom; more than simply escaping sickness, the dilettante’s movement is a headlong rush at vitality and vigour.

在杂家的基因里，写着一种追逐热情的驱力，又与一种拒绝深深耦合：拒绝把生活或身份永久地浇铸在任何一项热情周围。杂家拥有一种独到的能力：潜入一项兴趣的深水，然后浮出水面，再一头扎进下一项的深处；每一项都是一次探索、一场冒险，却不是终身以之、定义一生的工程。恰恰是为了给下一次侦察任务留出可能，杂家才拒绝把「坚持到底」奉为教条。有时候，杂家是个虔诚的爱好者，在一项兴趣上一驻数年；有时候，是个同时抛接好几件相互交叠项目的杂耍人；有时候，又是个追寻下一件新鲜事的连环狂热者——把这些模式拢在一起的，不是任何固定的时长或来去的节律，而是一种朝向永恒运动的秉性。杂家的才具、精力与热情，不断经由新的出口重新导流。杂家之道绝非匮乏，而是一场对丰裕的不懈追寻。

In the dilettante’s genes is a compulsion to pursue passions, deeply coupled with a refusal to shape a life or identity permanently around any one of them. The dilettante possesses a unique capacity to dive deep into an interest, then surface and plunge into the depths of the next, each one an exploration, an adventure, but not a lifelong, life-defining project. It is precisely to hold open the possibility of the next reconnaissance mission that the dilettante rejects any dogmatic principle of stick-to-itiveness. Sometimes, the dilettante is a devout hobbyist parked on one interest for years at a stretch; sometimes, a juggler of several overlapping projects at once; and sometimes, a serial enthusiast on the hunt for the next novelty – what holds these modes together isn’t any fixed duration or rhythm of arrival and departure, but a disposition toward perpetual motion. The dilettante’s talent, energy and passion are constantly rechannelled through new outlets. Far from deficiency, dilettantism is a constant quest for abundance.

如此描画的杂家生活，并不格外轻松。那是一种永远寻索、满足倏忽即逝的生活，其中的成就往往无人喝彩。在兴趣的广度获得更高的敬重之前，今天的杂家多半只能割舍公众的承认，安于一种由追寻与愉悦织成的私人生活。杂家很少作为杂家而广受称颂。想想苏珊·桑塔格（Susan Sontag）这样的人：小说家、批评家、政治活动家，她广泛涉猎的工作，既因对1960年代纽约智识生活不拘一格的贡献而赢得赞誉，也因缺乏严谨、缺乏实质而招致批评。换言之，杂家一栏里的「胜」，往往带着星号注脚——充其量是一场附有条件的胜利。

The life of the dilettante, so described, is not an especially easy one. It is a life of perpetual searching and fleeting satisfaction, in which accomplishment often arrives without accolades. Until breadth of interest is held in higher esteem, today’s dilettante will largely have to forgo public recognition and settle for a private life of pursuits and pleasures. Rarely is the dilettante widely celebrated as such. Consider someone like Susan Sontag, a novelist, critic and political activist whose wide-ranging work has earned both praise for its unconventional contributions to the intellectual life of 1960s New York and criticism for lacking rigour, for lacking substance. A ‘Win’ in the dilettante’s column, in other words, often comes with an asterisk – a conditional victory at best.

再者，杂家也不是合群的动物；无论他自觉多么融入一个群体，在其中他多半永远像个游客。走到任何围绕共同兴趣组织起来的社群里，杂家都必须安于一匹孤狼在其边缘的独行。

Further, the dilettante is also no pack animal; however much a part of a group one may feel, the dilettante will likely always be something of a tourist within it. The dilettante has to be comfortable travelling as a lone wolf at the margins of any community organised around a shared interest.

不，杂家之道并非人人适格。倘若宇宙召唤你去追求单一的热爱——恭喜你！坚持下去！但对我们这些更近乎被错失恐惧（FOMO）所驱使、贪恋这世界所能给出的纷繁与丰饶的人来说，杂家的道路可以带来一场实打实的生活品质升级。如果勇气意味着克服对新开始的恐惧——没有头衔、没有既往战绩可供安抚——那么杂家之道或许大有可为。毕竟，并非每一块田都要种成单一作物，为产量最大化而播种耕耘。我们中的一些人，需要靠轮作来打理自己的园圃。为杂家辩护，因而是一声召唤：循着兴趣分岔的小径走下去，走向一种以丰裕——而非声名——为意义之源的生活。

No, dilettantism is not for everyone. For anyone whom the universe has called to pursue a single passion – congratulations! Stay the course! But for those of us compelled by something closer to FOMO for the sheer variety and abundance the world has to offer, the dilettante’s path can lead to a genuine quality-of-life upgrade. If courage means overcoming the fear of new beginnings, without the reassurance of a title or a track record to show for it, then dilettantism may have much to offer. After all, not every crop is a monoculture, sowed and tilled to maximise output. Some of us need to cultivate our gardens through crop rotation. A defence of the dilettante is thus a call to follow the forked path of varying interests to a life made meaningful through abundance rather than acclaim.
